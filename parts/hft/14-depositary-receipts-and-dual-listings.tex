\chapter{Depositary Receipts and Dual Listings}\label{ch:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings}

A company's shares trade in London and its receipts in New York. For two hours both are open and one is a currency conversion of the other; for the
rest of New York's day one of them is priced by a market maker who can only estimate. In this chapter's simulation, the receipt's fair value is
known to 2 basis points while the home market is open and to 74 once it has closed, 94 without a proxy for the home market; a conversion through the
depositary pays only when the gap exceeds 29 basis points.

\section{Receipts, ratios and the depositary}

A depositary receipt (Book 1, chapter 17) is issued by a depositary bank against shares it holds in custody in the home market. Each receipt
represents a fixed number of shares.

\begin{definition}[Depositary receipt ratio]\label{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:ratio}
The \emph{depositary receipt ratio}\index{depositary receipt ratio} is the number of home-market shares one receipt represents (or its inverse,
as the depositary states it); with it and the exchange rate, a receipt's parity value is the home price times the ratio times the currency's price in
the receipt's currency.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Depositary receipt issuance]\label{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:issuance}
\emph{Depositary receipt issuance}\index{depositary receipt issuance} is the creation of new receipts by the depositary against shares delivered
into its custody; cancellation is the reverse, receipts surrendered for the shares they represent. Both carry the depositary's fee per receipt and
settle on both markets' settlement cycles.
\end{definition}

Issuance and cancellation play for receipts the role creation and redemption play for exchange-traded funds (chapter 12): they let an arbitrageur
move inventory between the two markets when the prices part. A dual listing, the same shares listed on two exchanges, needs no depositary: the
shares move between registers, with the same economic effect and its own frictions.

\begin{definition}[Pre-release]\label{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}
\emph{Pre-release}\index{pre-release} is the issuance of depositary receipts before the corresponding shares have been deposited, allowed under
depositary agreements only if the broker receiving them has an agreement with the depositary and the broker or its customer owns the
corresponding shares.
\end{definition}

\begin{dated}{2026-09}{Pre-release in enforcement, and a depositary's fees}\label{dat:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}
On 26 December 2018 the SEC announced that JPMorgan would pay more than \$135 million to settle charges of improper handling of pre-released
American depositary receipts: it had provided receipts to brokers in thousands of \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}{pre-release} transactions when neither the broker nor its customers
had the foreign shares needed to support them. The SEC said it had then held all four depositary banks accountable; its 2018 releases announced
settlements of nearly \$75 million with Deutsche Bank subsidiaries, more than \$54 million with BNY Mellon and more than \$38 million with Citibank. A
depositary's fee schedule, as NIO disclosed it in its annual report for 2018, charged up to \$0.05 per receipt issued and up to \$0.05 per receipt
cancelled.
\end{dated}

\section{Arbitrage in overlapping hours}

While both markets are open, the receipt is a portfolio of three prices: the home shares, the currency and the receipt itself. Gagnon and Karolyi
compared intraday receipt prices with synchronous home prices, currency-adjusted, for 506 cross-listed stocks from 35 countries: deviations from
parity averaged an economically small 4.9 basis points, but were volatile and could be large, and they rose with proxies for the holding costs that
impede arbitrage. A receipt market maker in the overlap quotes the receipt from the home market's live price (chapter 2's \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:cross}{cross-instrument fair price})
and hedges in the home shares and the currency; an arbitrageur takes the receipt when its gap to parity exceeds the round trip in three instruments.

In the chapter's simulation, with 2 basis points of noise in the home mid and 4 in the receipt's, the gap between the receipt and parity has a
standard deviation of 4.5 basis points and exceeds a 10-basis-point round trip in 2.7\% of the overlap's minutes: an arbitrage that exists, rarely,
and goes to whoever sees it first.

\section{Pricing across non-overlapping hours}

When the home market closes, the receipt keeps trading and its market maker needs a fair value without the home price. The last home price moved by
the currency is the naive answer; the last home price moved by a proxy that still trades (a futures contract on the home index, a fund on the home
market listed alongside the receipt) and by the currency is better; the receipt's own trading, and the prices of other receipts from the same country,
add information the proxy does not have.

The error grows with the square root of the time since the home close (\cref{fig:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:error}): the proxy
removes the market's part and leaves the company's own, which no proxy can carry. A market maker quoting the receipt in those hours widens its
spread with the time since the home close, skews with its forecast, and hedges the market part in the proxy.

Froot and Dabora studied a sharper case, companies that pool their cash flows but list twin shares in different markets: the twins' relative price
moved with the markets in which each traded most. A twin's relative price rose when the market on which it traded relatively intensively rose, which
no explanation they tested (dividends, expenditures, voting rights, currency, ex-dividend timing, taxes) fully accounted for.

\section{Conversion: issuance, cancellation and its frictions}

Conversion moves shares between markets at a cost: the depositary's fee per receipt, the spreads of the legs, the currency, and financing over the
settlement lag. For a \$25 receipt, a \$0.05 fee is 20 basis points; with 6 basis points of trading costs and two days' financing at 5\%, the gap must
exceed 28.8 basis points before converting pays (\cref{lst:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:conversion}). A market maker therefore converts
to move inventory it could not unwind by trading, not to capture the everyday gap. In markets with limits on foreign ownership, conversion into
receipts may be capped, and the receipt then trades at a lasting premium that no arbitrage can close.

\section{Strategy files}

\begin{strategyfile}{Receipt against ordinary shares in overlapping hours}\label{strat:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:overlap}
\sfield{Who pays you, and why} Investors in the receipt or the home shares who trade without watching the other listing, and liquidity providers
whose quotes follow the other market late.
\sfield{Instruments and venues} The receipt on a US exchange, the ordinary shares at home, the currency.
\sfield{Signal} The receipt's price against the home price times the ratio times the currency, beyond the three legs' round-trip cost.
\sfield{Sizing and execution} Take the dearer side and buy the cheaper; or quote the receipt from the home market and hedge fills there and in the
currency; hold until the gap closes or convert when inventory builds.
\sfield{Costs} Three spreads, fees on both exchanges, stamp duties at home where they apply, the currency's spread.
\sfield{How it dies} Gaps average a few basis points (4.9 in Gagnon and Karolyi's sample) and go to the fastest link between the two markets.
\sfield{Horizon, capacity, infrastructure} Milliseconds to minutes in the overlap; links between the two markets' data centres.
\sfield{Backtest honestly} Synchronous prices on one clock; the currency's executable price; the home market's settlement and stamp duty.
\sfield{Sources} Gagnon and Karolyi (2010).
\end{strategyfile}

\begin{strategyfile}{Pricing the receipt when the home market is closed}\label{strat:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:closed}
\sfield{Who pays you, and why} Investors who trade the company in their own hours and pay for immediacy when the home market cannot provide it.
\sfield{Instruments and venues} The receipt; a proxy for the home market (index future, country fund); the currency.
\sfield{Signal} The home close moved by the proxy and the currency, updated by the receipt's own trading and by other receipts from the same market.
\sfield{Sizing and execution} Quote with a spread that widens with the time since the home close; hedge the market part in the proxy; carry the
company's own part to the home open.
\sfield{Costs} The proxy's spread and basis; the company-specific risk overnight.
\sfield{How it dies} Company news while the home market is closed: in the simulation the error with the proxy still reaches 109 basis points at the
end of the session.
\sfield{Horizon, capacity, infrastructure} Hours; capacity from the receipt's volume and the market maker's appetite for overnight risk.
\sfield{Backtest honestly} The proxy's price at each quote; company announcements timed as released; the home open, not the close, as the exit.
\sfield{Sources} This chapter; Froot and Dabora (1999) on how the location of trade moves prices.
\end{strategyfile}

\begin{strategyfile}{Conversion arbitrage through the depositary}\label{strat:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:conversion}
\sfield{Who pays you, and why} Demand concentrated on one listing (index inclusion, a local fund flow) that pushes it away from the other.
\sfield{Instruments and venues} Receipts, ordinary shares, the depositary's issuance and cancellation, the currency.
\sfield{Signal} A gap beyond the conversion threshold: the fee per receipt as a share of its price, the legs' costs and financing over the
settlement lag.
\sfield{Sizing and execution} Buy the cheap listing, convert, deliver into the dear one; size to the gap's persistence, not its peak.
\sfield{Costs} The depositary's fee (up to \$0.05 a receipt each way in the disclosed schedule), custody, taxes on transfer, financing.
\sfield{How it dies} Ownership limits that cap conversion; and the temptation of \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}{pre-release}, which the SEC's 2018 cases show is a legal line.
\sfield{Horizon, capacity, infrastructure} Days; capacity from the depositary's processing and the gap's size.
\sfield{Backtest honestly} Settlement dates on both markets, the fee schedule in force, and the conversion's real completion time.
\sfield{Sources} SEC press releases on \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}{pre-release} (2018); NIO's annual report (fee schedule).
\end{strategyfile}

\section{Tutorial: two prices, one company}\label{tut:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:tut}

\begin{tutorial}
\textbf{Goal.} Measure how well a receipt market maker knows its fair value in open and closed hours, and when conversion pays. \textbf{End state:}
\cref{fig:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:error} and the numbers in the text.
\begin{tutsteps}
\item \textbf{The pair.} \texttt{firm.drarb.Pair}: 40 receipt sessions of 390 minutes, the home market open for the first 120; a receipt of two
shares at 1.25 dollars per unit of home currency; the company's value a market part (1\% a day) and its own part (1.2\%); the currency 0.6\%; a proxy
following the market part with a basis of 0.2\% a day.
\item \textbf{Fair values} from the last home mid, the currency and, after the home close, the proxy's move.
\omcode{code/firm/drarb/firm_drarb.py}{83}{87}{The receipt's fair value: last home mid times ratio times currency, moved by the proxy since the home close.}\label{lst:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:quote}
\item \textbf{Errors} against the true value, by minute of the session (\texttt{hf\_dr.errors}, \texttt{hf\_dr.by\_minute}).
\item \textbf{Conversion.} The threshold for a \$25 receipt with a \$0.05 fee.
\omcode{code/firm/drarb/firm_drarb.py}{40}{52}{The conversion threshold and decision: fee, trading costs, financing over the settlement lag.}\label{lst:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:conversion}
\end{tutsteps}
\textbf{What to change next.} Update the fair value from the receipt's own trades after the home close (chapter 2's filter); add other receipts
from the same country as a second proxy; model the overnight gap to the home open.
\end{tutorial}

The numbers: 2.0 basis points of root-mean-square error while the home market is open (the home mid's own noise), 74 after it closes with the proxy
and 94 without, rising to 109 and 137 at the end of the session. The proxy removes the market's share of the error and leaves the company's.

\section{Build: the receipt arbitrage module}\label{bld:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:drarb}

\begin{build}
\bfield{Purpose} Price a receipt across open and closed hours and decide conversions.
\bfield{Interface} \texttt{dr\_fair(home, ratio, fx)}, \texttt{closed\_fair(home\_close, ratio, fx, proxy\_move, beta)},
\texttt{threshold\_bp(dr\_price, fee\_per\_dr, cost\_bp, days, rate)}, \texttt{conversion\_edge(dr, home, ratio, fx, fee\_per\_dr, cost\_bp, days,
rate)}, \texttt{Pair(days, seed)} with \texttt{quote(method)} and \texttt{error\_bp(method)}.
\bfield{Rules} The currency is dollars per unit of home currency; conversion costs include financing over the settlement lag.
\bfield{Acceptance tests} \texttt{code/firm/drarb/tests/}: fair values by hand; threshold and edges for issue, cancel and nothing; the home
reference is the current minute while open and the close afterwards; the two methods agree while open; the proxy lowers the closed-hours error, which
is more than ten times the open-hours error.
\bfield{Stretch} The receipt's own trades as information; overnight gaps; ownership limits on conversion.
\end{build}

\section{Problem: Two Prices, One Company}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- two prices, one company}]\label{pb:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:1}
A market maker quotes a company's receipt in New York through the hours when its home market is open and closed.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The instrument.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Define the \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:ratio}{depositary receipt ratio} and parity.
\item Define \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:issuance}{depositary receipt issuance} and cancellation.
\item Define \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}{pre-release} and the condition that makes it proper.
\item Summarise the dated box.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- Prices.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item What did Gagnon and Karolyi measure, and find?
\item What did Froot and Dabora find about twin shares?
\item Write the closed-hours fair value.
\item What information does the receipt's own trading add after the home close?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The simulation.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Give the open-hours and closed-hours errors, with and without the proxy.
\item How large are the overlap's gaps, and how often do they exceed a 10-basis-point round trip?
\item What is the conversion threshold, and why is it so much larger than the everyday gap?
\item How should the market maker's spread change through the session?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- The verdict.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item State the \emph{named result}: the receipt market maker's quoting error in closed hours against open hours, and the conversion threshold after
fees.
\item When does conversion pay?
\item What do foreign ownership limits do to the arbitrage?
\item Which of the three strategy files carries overnight risk?
\item How would you hedge the company's own part overnight?
\item Why must the backtest use the home open, not the close, as the exit?
\item What would a second proxy (another receipt from the same market) add?
\item In one sentence: what does the receipt market maker sell in the hours after the home close?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
