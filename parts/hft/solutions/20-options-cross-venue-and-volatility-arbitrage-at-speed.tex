\begin{solution}{exo:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:4}
The best bid and offer across more venues are more extreme, so they cross parity more often; but each violation is a venue's noise that the next
refresh removes, and all scanners see the same quotes: only the fastest captures it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:5}
Treasuries carry a convenience yield (liquidity, collateral value, regulatory treatment) that lowers their yield; the box's rate, inferred without
it, is the risk-free rate before that premium: about 40 basis points above on average in van Binsbergen, Diamond and Grotteria.
\end{solution}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:conversion}.
\item Nine strikes, venues quoting 10 cents wide with 3 cents of noise, a cent a leg: violations in 0.7\%, 6.6\% and 26\% of snapshots for 1, 3 and 6
venues, worth one to two cents.
\item 6--8\% with half the noise kept, 43\% with 90\%: the quotes' persistence.
\item The reversal is short the stock and pays the borrow and dividends.
\item 4.02\%; one cent against a 4\% funding rate.
\item Treasury convenience yields of about 40 basis points, larger below three months, four times larger in the crisis.
\item See \cref{def:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:jelly}; 4.0\% carry between three and six months.
\item A dividend announcement or a borrow change between the expiries.
\item When the dividend exceeds the time value given up (the put's value at the strike); 13 cents in the example.
\item More than half of long positions that should be exercised were not, 1996--2006, costing holders over \$491 million.
\item Offsetting trades between market makers who exercise all longs and take most assignments; the volume it inflates, facilitated by exchanges'
fee limits.
\item $c/(2g)$: 1.7\%.
\item Zero below 1.7\%; \$0.27 per public contract at 5\%, \$1.05 at 10\%, \$3.04 at 20\%, \$10.02 at 50\%, capturing 42\% to 82\% of the failures'
value.
\item Holders who fail to exercise; by exercising, or by their brokers doing it for them.
\item Lower fees lower the break-even and raise the play's size.
\item One listing's quotes follow the other's with a delay; the trade takes the stale one.
\item The box, which ties capital and balance sheet for its whole term.
\item From open interest in calls that should be exercised and past ex-dates' exercise rates by holder type.
\item Notice and, where agreed, automatic exercise instructions.
\item The holders who forget, paid to whoever trades to take their assignments.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\iqlookfor{box-implied rates.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:3}
Most will be exercised and you will be assigned: short the stock the next morning without the dividend. Buy the stock or exercise offsetting longs;
the unassigned part gains the dividend less time value.
\iqlookfor{assignment and its position.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:4}
Maintain best bid and offer per series across venues incrementally; recompute parity only for series whose quotes changed; require sizes on all legs
and apply fees, borrow and dividends; send all legs together.
\iqlookfor{incremental, executable, all legs.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:5}
The borrow fee jumps or shares are recalled: the reversal loses its carry or must be closed; parity for that stock shifts and puts become dear.
\iqlookfor{borrow risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:options-cross-venue-and-volatility-arbitrage-at-speed:6}
The derivative of $aq/(P+2q)$ is $aP/(P+2q)^2$; setting it equal to $c$ gives $(P+2q)^2=aP/c$, so $q^\ast=(\sqrt{aP/c}-P)/2$.
\iqlookfor{the first-order condition.}
\end{solution}
