\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fair-value:4}
$q\Delta=0.005$: $\bar\Sigma=\tfrac12(-0.005+\sqrt{0.000025+0.002})=0.02$, $\bar K=0.025/0.125=0.2$. At one observation a second,
$q\Delta=0.05$, $\bar\Sigma=0.05$ and $\bar K=0.1/0.2=0.5$: rarer observations each count for more.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fair-value:5}
The filter assumes the sources' errors are independent white noise around the current efficient price. The mids of this market lag the efficient
price for seconds, so their errors are autocorrelated and shared; the filter weighs them wrongly and adds its own smoothing lag. The regression
learns the weights that best forecast the target it is judged on, whatever the error structure.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:fair-value:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An estimate of the efficient price from everything observed, updated on every message; a future's fair value is a no-arbitrage price from
spot, financing and dividends.
\item $w=(aQ^b+bQ^a)/(Q^b+Q^a)$: the larger bid queue is less likely to be exhausted first, so the next move is more likely up.
\item Average change of the mid over the horizon by imbalance bucket, on one-tick books, where the imbalance is informative and the mid is
well defined.
\item $-0.087,-0.007,0.022,0.091,0.220$; $99.5+0.091=99.59$.
\item 2.4\% lower error at a half-second lead (0.536 against 0.549 ticks).
\item Combines every venue's book by information; the NBBO is a regulatory construct of protected quotes, blind to staleness and depth.
\item 0.73 and 0.32; they sum to about one because both measure one price.
\item Uses related instruments' prices after translating them into the instrument's units (hedge ratio, conversion, currency, basis).
\item 0.74, 0.22, 0.18 at a half-second lead; 0.89, $-0.09$, 0.27 at two seconds.
\item $\Sigma\leftarrow\Sigma+q(t-t')$, $K=\Sigma/(\Sigma+r_j)$, $\hat P\leftarrow\hat P+K(y-\hat P)$, $\Sigma\leftarrow(1-K)\Sigma$.
\item See \cref{prop:hf:fair-value:steady}.
\item Its variance $\Sigma$ has grown with the time since the last update, so the gain is larger.
\item $q=0.047$ square ticks a second from the first hour's efficient-price variance; $r=0.104$, 0.123, 0.134 from each source's gap to the mid five
seconds later.
\item Its noise model (independent white errors) is wrong for mids that lag; the regression is fitted to the target.
\item Mid 0.549, microprice 0.536, cross-instrument 0.529 ticks; the leader brings 65\% of the improvement over the mid (the \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:consolidated}{consolidated fair
price} without it is at 0.542).
\item It grows with the lead: $-3.5\%$ at half a second, $-7.5\%$ at one, $-12.8\%$ at two.
\item Each point averages three hours whose errors are autocorrelated; the differences between neighbouring leads are of the size of the noise.
\item The mid follows the efficient price a tick at a time over seconds; the \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:cross}{cross-instrument fair price} moves as soon as the leader has.
\item By the adverse selection it saves per fill, times the fills of a year.
\item It uses information the mid does not contain: the queues, other venues, and above all the instruments that move first.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fair-value:1}
Close to the ask: the bid queue is far less likely to be exhausted first, so the next mid change is more likely up; how close is an empirical
question, answered by a microprice table.
\iqlookfor{direction and that the size of the adjustment must be estimated.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fair-value:2}
Score it as a forecast of observable future prices (the mid or the next trade some seconds later), out of sample, against the mid as a baseline;
check markouts of the fills it would have generated.
\iqlookfor{a forecasting target, out-of-sample evaluation, and a baseline.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fair-value:3}
Fixed point of $\Sigma\mapsto(\Sigma+q\Delta)r/(\Sigma+q\Delta+r)$: $\Sigma^2+q\Delta\Sigma-q\Delta r=0$,
$\bar\Sigma=\tfrac12(-q\Delta+\sqrt{q^2\Delta^2+4q\Delta r})$, $\bar K=(\bar\Sigma+q\Delta)/(\bar\Sigma+q\Delta+r)$.
\iqlookfor{the Riccati fixed point done cleanly.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fair-value:4}
Venue, instrument, exchange and receive timestamps, sequence number, prices and sizes. A silent feed's observations age: its weight must fall (a
filter does it through the elapsed time), and a gap in sequence numbers must mark its book invalid until recovered.
\iqlookfor{timestamps, sequence numbers, staleness handling.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fair-value:5}
The leader's source disappears: its weight must go to zero at the close, the quotes widen because the \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:fair}{fair price} becomes less precise, and the fund
is priced from its own books and whatever proxies remain open.
\iqlookfor{source availability, wider quotes when information drops.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fair-value:6}
Minimise $w^2r_1+(1-w)^2r_2$: $w_1=r_2/(r_1+r_2)$, variance $r_1r_2/(r_1+r_2)$. Ignoring the second venue is optimal only if its noise is infinite;
with correlated noises the optimal weight can be zero or negative.
\iqlookfor{inverse-variance weights and the correlated case.}
\end{solution}
