\begin{solution}{exo:hf:etf-market-making:3}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:etf-market-making:4}
The net asset value stood 557 basis points above the bonds' true value (staleness); the mid stood 5 basis points below the true value (the market
maker's discount). Only the second is tradable against the bonds, and only by someone who can sell the bonds at their true value, which in a freeze
nobody can at size.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:etf-market-making:5}
Its quote is 13.7 basis points from the true value on average, more than its half-spread: clients who see the proxy buy when its ask is below the
true value and sell when its bid is above, and the flow it gets is the flow its errors attract. It earns \$132\,100 a normal day against \$163\,600.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:etf-market-making:6}
Redeeming hands over bonds that must be sold at the freeze's spread, six times the normal: \$262\,000 over five days, for inventory that was already
hedged in the proxy and would have been unwound by the quotes after the freeze.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:etf-market-making:7}
The net asset value's worst staleness rises from 557 to 722 basis points, the \omterm{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}{pricing basket}'s error in the freeze from 13.8 to 16.5 basis points; the
basket quoter still earns \$3.48 million (against \$3.43 million) and the net asset value quoter loses \$96.9 million (against \$84.7 million). Staleness
hurts whoever quotes from stale prices; a proxy-driven basket degrades slowly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:etf-market-making:8}
The net asset value was stale: the bonds were worth less than it said. Redeeming delivers the bonds, which must be sold at their true value and at the
stress spread; most of the 5\% was the net asset value's error, not a profit. The arbitrage is against the bonds' executable value, not the published
number.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:etf-market-making:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}; $\hat P_{i,t}=P_i(1+\beta_i(G_t-G_{t_i}))$.
\item Every holding is stale: the close times one plus the overnight future's move times one plus the currency's move.
\item Deviations typically within about 200 basis points, larger for international and illiquid funds, still about 100 after controlling for stale
prices.
\item Net asset value 13.7 and 214 basis points; \omterm{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}{pricing basket} 3.5 and 9.9; last prints 2 and 11 trading hours old on average.
\item See \cref{def:hf:etf-market-making:fee}; \$1\,250 per order on a \$14.6 million unit, up to 3.0\% more for cash creations.
\item See \cref{def:hf:etf-market-making:custom}; Rule 6c-11, with written policies and procedures.
\item Create if $(P^{\mathrm{ETF}}-V)N>F+c_{\mathrm{buy}}VN$; redeem if $(V-P^{\mathrm{ETF}})N>F+c_{\mathrm{sell}}VN$.
\item The bonds received must be sold at the stress spread, and the fund may choose which bonds to deliver.
\item Net asset value quoter \$111\,600 (s.d.\ \$16\,000); basket quoter \$149\,100 (s.d.\ \$9\,300).
\item $-\$69.9$ million and $+\$3.55$ million; 95\% of the loss on trades with clients at prices above the bonds' value.
\item About \$14\,500 a normal day; a third off the day's standard deviation.
\item \$262\,000 in bond spreads.
\item The basket quoter earned \$3.2--3.6 million through the freeze while quoting as much as 532 basis points below the net asset value, of which 557
was staleness and $-5$ its own discount to the true value.
\item The market maker with a \omterm{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}{pricing basket} and a proxy hedge, at about 5 to 10 basis points below the true value.
\item A market maker that must stop buying lets the shares fall below the true value until other buyers arrive: the discount becomes a price for
balance sheet.
\item When the shares trade below the value at which the basket can be sold, after fees and the spread, and the redemption can be completed.
\item The age of the prices behind them, and a value adjusted for observable proxies.
\item Regress bond returns between prints on the proxy's return over the same interval, pooled by rating and maturity.
\item Fixed-income quoting on a stale value: it lives where staleness is largest.
\item Its own \omterm{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}{pricing basket}, backed by its hedge and its balance sheet.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:etf-market-making:1}
Buy the fund and sell the basket, or buy and redeem; everyone else does too, so the gap closes before a unit can be assembled unless it is inside the
costs of the basket's spreads and fees.
\iqlookfor{executable basket value and competition.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:etf-market-making:2}
Move each bond's last trade by its sensitivity to liquid proxies (Treasury futures, credit indices, similar funds) since the trade; use dealer quotes
and platform data where available; weight by the fund's holdings; check against similar funds.
\iqlookfor{a proxy-adjusted \omterm{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}{pricing basket}.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:etf-market-making:3}
The bonds' executable spreads, the fund's redemption terms and basket, the proxy's liquidity and basis, the balance sheet and its limits; usually
hedge in the proxy and hold, redeeming when the bonds can be sold.
\iqlookfor{costs of each exit and the hedge.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:etf-market-making:4}
In a stress the net asset value is stale: marking to it shows the book at a value nobody can realise, hides the loss and invites buying more. Mark
to a model value from the \omterm{def:hf:etf-market-making:pricing}{pricing basket}, and report the gap.
\iqlookfor{independent marks.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:etf-market-making:5}
Holdings files loaded before the open; a shared cache of instrument prices and proxy moves updated per tick; each fund's value updated incrementally
from the instruments that moved; stale-price models run on a timer; publish to quoting engines with timestamps and age.
\iqlookfor{incremental computation and shared state.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:etf-market-making:6}
If the holding's return since its print is $\beta\Delta G+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ uncorrelated with $\Delta G$, the least-squares forecast
given $\Delta G$ is $\beta\Delta G$. It is biased when $\beta$ is misestimated, when the proxy has its own basis, or when the holding's news is
correlated with the lack of trading.
\iqlookfor{the regression argument and its failure modes.}
\end{solution}
