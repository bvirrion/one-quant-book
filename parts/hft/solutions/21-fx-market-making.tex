\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fx-market-making:4}
The check rejects every informed request (their move exceeds the threshold) and a share of uninformed ones whose price happened to move for the
client. On the toxic tier the informed losses avoided exceed the good trades lost; on the others, with few or no informed requests, only the good
trades are lost.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fx-market-making:5}
Without skew the variance is quadratic in the unhedged share and the optimum $1-C/(2\lambda V)$ is 97\%; with the skew the variance is about linear
in it, so the objective is linear and the optimum is a corner: hedge nearly all or nothing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fx-market-making:6}
The reduction in triangular arbitrage opportunities came primarily from computers taking liquidity; the reduction in return autocorrelation, from
computers providing it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fx-market-making:7}
Uninformed tier-3 rejects rise from 18.3\% to 32.6\%: over a longer hold, prices move beyond the threshold more often.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:fx-market-making:8}
Internalisation pays only if the inventory it holds is offset quickly; a high rate may mean the provider is carrying risk it should hedge (or that
its skew is too aggressive for its clients). The comparison must include the inventory's risk and the skew's cost in worse prices to clients.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:fx-market-making:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \$9.6 trillion a day in April 2025; swaps \$4 trillion; spot up 42\% to 31\% of turnover; the dollar on 89\% of trades.
\item Algorithmic trading improved price efficiency; takers removed triangular arbitrage, providers reduced autocorrelation.
\item Inverse-variance weights with staleness; 0.26 against 0.30 basis point.
\item See \cref{def:hf:fx-market-making:cross}; 2.6 pips.
\item Three tiers at 0.3, 0.5 and 1.0 basis point with 30\%, 10\% and no informed requests; 5 ms hold; 0.1 basis point threshold.
\item Tier 1: 0.125 to 0.200 with 43.5\% rejects; tier 2: 0.453 to 0.395 (26.5\%); tier 3: 1.008 to 0.851 (18.3\%); all: 0.400 to 0.389 (33.4\%).
\item It also rejects moves in the provider's favour: 0.282 basis point and 48.8\% rejects over all tiers.
\item Rejections, the delay, and the information the provider gains from the rejected requests.
\item 20\,000 trades of \$1 million; a share $h$ hedged at 0.25 basis point; the skew raises the probability of a reducing trade with the inventory.
\item 95\%, 80\%, 50\% and 5\%.
\item \$588\,000 a day against \$300\,000 hedging everything.
\item With the skew the variance is about linear in the unhedged share, so the objective is nearly linear.
\item Capture 0.400 basis point a request without last look and 0.389 with it, 33\% rejected (0.125 to 0.200 on the toxic tier); internalising stops
paying when the inventory's half-life exceeds about 900 client trades, and above about 1\,150 the best policy hedges 95\%.
\item Last look for the toxic tier only; firm prices for the others.
\item From the decay of the inventory after large trades, with and without skew.
\item Thin direct markets, liquid legs: synthesis and internalisation across pairs.
\item Disclosed streaming with last look.
\item Natural offsets across pairs and clients, shortening the half-life.
\item Informed flow arriving together would make inventories one-sided, lengthening the half-life and favouring hedging.
\item Immediacy in a currency, priced by tier, hedged or offset.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fx-market-making:1}
The client's tier and history, the inventory already held, how fast the flow usually offsets, the primary venue's depth, and the market's
volatility; hedge the part that the offsetting flow will not absorb.
\iqlookfor{internalisation versus hedging with inputs.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fx-market-making:2}
Mark-outs of each client's fills at several horizons, by client and size; use them to tier, price and choose last-look treatment.
\iqlookfor{mark-outs to tiering.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fx-market-making:3}
One fair-price engine per pair, tier pricing as offsets from it computed incrementally, stream publishers per client connection, all fed from the
same market-data path with timestamps.
\iqlookfor{shared \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:fair}{fair price}, incremental tiers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fx-market-making:4}
Flow may have become more two-sided (good), or the skew more aggressive, or hedging thresholds looser (risk); check inventory sizes and their decay,
and the P\&L of the internalised positions.
\iqlookfor{rate versus risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fx-market-making:5}
It rejects moves in either direction, so clients are not rejected only when the price has moved in their favour; the provider also gives up the
trades that moved in its favour, lowering its capture (0.282 against 0.389 in the model).
\iqlookfor{symmetry and its price.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:fx-market-making:6}
Quadratic: $-C+2\lambda(1-h)V=0$, $h^\ast=1-C/(2\lambda V)$, interior when $C<2\lambda V$. Linear: the derivative $-C+\lambda V'$ is constant, so
$h^\ast=1$ if $\lambda V'>C$ and $0$ otherwise.
\iqlookfor{interior versus corner.}
\end{solution}
