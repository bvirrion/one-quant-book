\begin{solution}{pb:hf:automated-options-market-making:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:hf:automated-options-market-making:live}; the underlying moves the surface continuously, the smile is refitted on a slower clock.
\item Within 0.025 volatility points with 0.2 points of noise per quote.
\item See \cref{def:hf:automated-options-market-making:mass}.
\item 11 cents for the 30-day at-the-money call; from one cent to 20.
\item Notional, volume, count and percentage-of-quote parameters over 100 ms to a day; trading stops when one is reached.
\item A 1\% move, 150 stale quotes, one hit every 5 microseconds; ordinary orders every 5 seconds of 1 to 12 legs, \$4 a fill.
\item \$4,950 and 1,052; \$9,899 and 326; \$12,867 and 3; \$19,773 and 0.
\item The sweeper takes the largest mispricings first because the protection may stop it at any moment.
\item Short about 13,000 shares at a threshold of 13; the hedge stops further moves adding to the loss.
\item Inventory risk has a first-order effect on option prices, larger than asymmetric information in price impact.
\item See \cref{def:hf:automated-options-market-making:strike}.
\item \$16,500 unprotected; \$9,900 with a \$1,000 limit at 50 microseconds, \$6,700 at 5.
\item From \$1,980 at two executions to \$79,370 without protection, about \$1,000 an execution; thresholds below twelve lose 326 to 2,441 fills a day,
thirteen loses three.
\item Just above the largest legitimate multi-leg order: thirteen here.
\item A sweep on several exchanges at once meets several counters, each counting only its own executions.
\item The exchange counts executions, not risk: vega and gamma accumulate in ways a count cannot see.
\item Timed executions pay less than 40\% of conventional spreads.
\item Mass-quote making.
\item From the loss the firm accepts on a volatility jump of plausible size, and the enforcement speed.
\item Losses to informed sweeps against fills lost to ordinary orders.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:automated-options-market-making:1}
The surface moves with the underlying; every quote is recomputed and resent in \omterm{def:hf:automated-options-market-making:mass}{mass quotes}; until they land, the stale ones may be swept and the
protection may pull them; delta from any fills is hedged; the smile is refitted if the move changed it.
\iqlookfor{the sequence and the protection.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:automated-options-market-making:2}
Market data decode, surface update, 5,000 theos and widths (vectorised), message encoding in blocks per the exchange's limits, sending; most time goes
to computing and encoding, so precompute sensitivities and update linearly.
\iqlookfor{vectorisation and linear updates.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:automated-options-market-making:3}
Move the smile with the underlying by the rule, and refit when the market's quotes or trades deviate from the moved smile beyond their noise, or
after a set number of updates.
\iqlookfor{residuals against a noise threshold.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:automated-options-market-making:4}
Whether the trips were sweeps or legitimate multi-leg orders, the losses in each, the threshold against the largest orders, and the timing against
underlying moves.
\iqlookfor{separate informed from legitimate trips.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:automated-options-market-making:5}
Put buyers are more often informed or hedging crash risk, the put wing's vega is harder to hedge, and inventory there carries tail risk.
\iqlookfor{asymmetric risk and flow.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:automated-options-market-making:6}
The loss is $\sum_{i=1}^{\min(n,N)}e_i$ times size and multiplier for $N$ targets; with equal edges it is linear in $n$, with decreasing edges
concave.
\iqlookfor{partial sums of ordered edges.}
\end{solution}
