\begin{solution}{exo:hf:inventory-models:4}
At the horizon the inventory term vanishes and $(2/\gamma)\ln(1+\gamma/k)\to2/k$ as $\gamma\to0$ (since $\ln(1+x)\sim x$). A risk-neutral market maker
still trades off the capture per fill, which grows with depth, against the fill rate, which falls with it: $\delta Ae^{-k\delta}$ is maximised at
$\delta=1/k$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:inventory-models:5}
Between $q=3$ (ask depth 0.011) and $q=4$ ($-0.217$). A negative depth offers below the mid: in a real book it crosses the spread, i.e. the market
maker sells aggressively to the bid.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:inventory-models:6}
The inventory strategy skews: whenever it holds a position, one of its quotes is closer to the mid than half the average width, and that is the
side that fills, so it earns less per fill (it fills slightly more often, 97.0 times against 91.7, for less money). What it gains is variance:
6.49 against 14.00. For any positive risk aversion its certainty equivalent is the higher one, and that is what it maximises.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:inventory-models:7}
$q=0$: both depths 0.804. $q=3$: bid 1.550, ask 0.011.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:inventory-models:8}
Quotes behind the touch of a one-tick stock are almost never filled (the chapter's exploring quoter got none in 5,280 seconds): the fit is on zeros,
and $k$ is not identified. Depth cannot be chosen continuously in a large-tick book; the decision is whether to rest at the touch.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:inventory-models:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S_t=S_0+\sigma W_t$; quotes $S\mp\delta^{b,a}$; fills at $Ae^{-k\delta}$; maximise $\E[-e^{-\gamma(X_T+q_TS_T)}]$.
\item The rate at which a resting quote at depth $\delta$ is executed; $\lambda(\delta)=Ae^{-k\delta}$.
\item The indifference price of one more or one fewer unit; $S^{\mathrm{res}}=S-q\gamma\sigma^2(T-t)$.
\item $\gamma\sigma^2(T-t)$ (inventory risk) plus $(2/\gamma)\ln(1+\gamma/k)$ (monopoly).
\item $S^{\mathrm{res}}=99.40$; width $0.2+1.291=1.491$; bid 98.65, ask 100.15.
\item A cost $\phi\int q^2dt+\alpha q_T^2$ on held inventory; $\phi=\tfrac12\gamma\sigma^2$ charges the variance the position adds.
\item See the proof of \cref{prop:hf:inventory-models:cj}.
\item The value is linear in $s$ ($x+qs+h$), so the second derivative in $s$ is zero; risk enters only through the penalty.
\item 0.804 on both sides at $q=0$; bid 1.550 and ask 0.011 at $q=3$; beyond $q=3$ the ask is below the mid, a crossing order.
\item $S_0=100$, $T=1$, $\sigma=2$, steps 0.005, $k=1.5$, $A=140$, binomial mid, 1,000 paths.
\item $\gamma=0.01$: 68.45 (9.06) against 68.50 (13.91); $\gamma=0.1$: 64.83 (6.49) against 68.15 (14.00); $\gamma=1$: 31.44 (4.83) against 43.30
(10.34); the paper: 68.6 (8.7)/68.8 (12.8), 65.0 (6.6)/68.4 (12.7), 31.4 (5.0)/44.0 (11.0).
\item From $\gamma\approx0.1$: 5\% less profit at 0.1, 27\% less at 1; at 0.01 they are equal.
\item The exact penalised quotes dominate symmetric widths: $\phi=0.5$ gives 66.7 with sd 6.9 against 68.5 with sd 13.9.
\item Eight fills in 1,640 seconds at the touch and none in 5,280 seconds behind it: market orders here never walk past the touch.
\item $\partial_th-\phi q^2+\lambda\max(0,c+h(q-1)-h(q))+\lambda\max(0,c+h(q+1)-h(q))=0$; rest on a side when its bracket is positive.
\item From the symmetric Quoter's 241 fills on three calibration sessions: $\lambda=0.033$ lots a second per side, $c=0.139$ ticks a share; separate
sessions so that the comparison is not scored on the data that set its parameters.
\item On the simulated market: symmetric $-\$65.17$ with mean $|q|$ 2.51 lots; $\phi=10^{-3}$: $+\$11.00$ with 0.57. In the model at $\gamma=0.1$:
64.83 with sd 6.49 against 68.15 with sd 14.00.
\item The standard errors (about \$40 for the symmetric quoter) are larger than some of the differences, but every penalised session holds less
inventory and varies less.
\item The absence of information: fills are followed by adverse moves, which the model's martingale mid rules out.
\item Stop adding to the position and keep offering the other side, in proportion to how much the position costs to hold.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:inventory-models:1}
Both, down: a lower ask sells more readily and a lower bid buys less. In a one-tick market, keep the ask at the touch and pull or shrink the bid.
\iqlookfor{both quotes move with the \omterm{def:hf:inventory-models:reservation}{reservation price}; the large-tick version.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:inventory-models:2}
Maximise $\delta\,Ae^{-k\delta}$: $\delta^\ast=1/k$, earning $A/(ek)$ per unit of time.
\iqlookfor{the first-order condition and the value.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:inventory-models:3}
The risk of holding $q$ until the horizon is $q^2\sigma^2(T-t)$; it shrinks as the horizon approaches. A market maker that holds overnight has no
natural $T$: it uses a running penalty (a constant risk charge per unit of time) or a horizon equal to its typical holding period.
\iqlookfor{the source of the $T-t$ and a sensible replacement.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:inventory-models:4}
Underflow: $e^{-\alpha kq^2}$ and the matrix exponential's small entries become zero and $\ln0=-\infty$. Renormalise $\omega$ at each step (a
constant factor drops out of every depth) and floor the logarithm; or work in log space.
\iqlookfor{recognising underflow and why rescaling is harmless.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:inventory-models:5}
Choose $\phi$ so that the inventory the policy accepts, times the volatility over the holding period, fits the loss limit at a confidence level; check
it by simulating the policy's P\&L distribution, and cap inventory hard as well.
\iqlookfor{linking the penalty to a risk budget and verifying by simulation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:inventory-models:6}
The choice of which sides to show at the touch (and of size, and of when to cross). The state is $(t,q)$ and perhaps queue position; each side's
control is binary, the \omterm{def:hf:inventory-models:intensity}{fill intensity} is that of the touch, and the Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation has a maximum between resting and not.
\iqlookfor{binary controls and a discrete-choice HJB.}
\end{solution}
