\begin{solution}{exo:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:3}
The gap is \$0.20 (80 basis points), above the threshold: issue (buy shares, deposit, sell receipts). Edge $0.20-0.05-25\times(0.0006+0.00028)=\$0.128$
a receipt.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:4}
The unobserved moves accumulate as a random walk, so their standard deviation grows with the square root of the time since the last observation. The
proxy carries the market part; the company's own part (1.2\% a day here) remains.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:5}
By providing liquidity: the spread paid by investors on each side, with the other listing as a hedge; the average deviation is small because market
makers and arbitrageurs keep it so, and it is their spread that pays them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:6}
Receipts were provided to brokers when neither the broker nor its customers had the foreign shares: receipts were created without shares behind
them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:7}
The closed-hours error with the proxy falls from 74 to 38 basis points (from 109 to 57 at the end of the session); without the proxy, from 94 to 70.
Halving the company's own volatility nearly halves the error the proxy leaves.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:8}
At 21:00 London the home market is closed: the 40 basis points are measured against a stale home close. The receipt may be right and the parity
stale; the trade is a bet on the home open, with the company's news risk overnight, not an arbitrage.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:ratio}; parity is home price times ratio times the currency.
\item See \cref{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:issuance}.
\item See \cref{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}: the broker or its customer must own the shares.
\item JPMorgan paid more than \$135 million (December 2018) for \omterm{def:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:prerelease}{pre-release} without the shares; all four depositary banks were charged; fees up to
\$0.05 a receipt each way.
\item Intraday parity deviations for 506 cross-listed stocks from 35 countries: 4.9 basis points on average, volatile, larger with holding costs.
\item Twins' relative prices moved with the markets where each traded most.
\item The home close times ratio times the current currency, times one plus beta times the proxy's move since the close.
\item Buyers and sellers who know something about the company that the proxy does not carry.
\item 2.0 open; 74 closed with the proxy, 94 without; 109 and 137 at the session's end.
\item A standard deviation of 4.5 basis points; above 10 in 2.7\% of the overlap's minutes.
\item 28.8 basis points for a \$25 receipt; the fee alone is 20.
\item Narrow in the overlap, widening with the square root of the time since the home close.
\item 2 basis points while the home market is open against 74 after (with a proxy), and 28.8 basis points to convert.
\item When a gap persists beyond the threshold for longer than the settlement lag, typically from concentrated demand on one listing.
\item They cap conversion in one direction, so a premium can last.
\item Pricing the receipt when the home market is closed.
\item It cannot be hedged by a proxy: only by the home shares at the open, or by a smaller position.
\item The trade ends when the home market opens; the close is before the risk.
\item Part of the company-specific and sector moves the index proxy misses.
\item Immediacy in a company whose home price is unknown until morning.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:2}
Home close times the current exchange rate, moved by the home index future's change since the close times the stock's beta; updated by the
receipt's own trades and those of related receipts, with an error that grows with time.
\iqlookfor{proxy plus own-trade information, with an error model.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:3}
Conversion may be capped by ownership limits or suspended; the home shares may be hard to borrow; the costs (fees, taxes, settlement) may exceed 1\%.
\iqlookfor{limits to arbitrage.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:4}
That the desk or its customer owns the shares, that the depositary agreement allows it, and that the process is documented; the 2018 cases turned on
receipts issued with no shares behind them.
\iqlookfor{ownership and the SEC's cases.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:5}
Reference data (ratios, fees, hours), live home prices and currencies, proxies per home market, a model per receipt updated on each input with its
age, published with a confidence band to the quoting engines.
\iqlookfor{hours-aware inputs and staleness.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:depositary-receipts-and-dual-listings:6}
With daily variances $\sigma_m^2$ (market), $\sigma_i^2$ (company) and $\sigma_b^2$ (proxy basis), after a fraction $\tau$ of a day the error's
variance is $(\sigma_m^2+\sigma_i^2)\tau$ without the proxy and $(\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2)\tau$ with it, plus the home mid's noise; the root grows with
$\sqrt\tau$.
\iqlookfor{variance accumulation by component.}
\end{solution}
