$2+0.5-(0.5+1)=1.0$ basis point a fill.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:2}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:3}
Longs pay shorts when the rate is positive: it receives $150{,}000\times0.0001=\$15$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:4}
From the first second the limit fills at shallow discounts and the position then suffers the rest of the fall before it recovers; waiting until 40
basis points buys at deeper discounts and gains the whole recovery from there: \$20,800 against \$5,200.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:5}
Between requotes the \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:fair}{fair price} moves; when it moves by more than the half-spread plus the arbitrageurs' threshold, they take the stale quote at a
price already worse than the fair one. The spread is earned only on fills at fresh quotes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:6}
Capital controls and the difficulty of moving funds between countries, which kept arbitrageurs from closing cross-country gaps.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:7}
The 60-basis-point threshold earns \$17,800 from the cascade; the \$2 million limit from the first second loses \$600. Timing matters more than
capacity: the small early position is full before the discount is worth taking.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:8}
One account's daily order cap allows about 0.06 requotes a second per instrument across twenty; requoting each every second would be refused, and at
the rate allowed the quotes would be stale and picked off. The backtest must enforce the venue's limits.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:xmm} and \cref{def:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:hedge}.
\item Large, recurrent arbitrage opportunities across exchanges, larger across countries than within, kept open by capital controls.
\item 6,000 weight a minute, 100 orders per 10 seconds, 200,000 a day; funding at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC; \$19 billion liquidated on 10 October
2025.
\item A coin at \$60,000, 3\% daily volatility, clients every half second, arbitrageurs on stale quotes, 2 basis points either side, a spot hedge,
funding every eight hours, a cascade on day four.
\item The basis between perpetual and spot, and funding.
\item See \cref{def:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:absorb}.
\item \$600, \$5,200, \$20,800 and \$17,800.
\item A deeper cascade, the dislocation becoming the price, auto-deleveraging, the venue's solvency.
\item The daily cap: 1.16 requotes a second for one instrument.
\item \$275,400, \$270,800, \$173,000 and $-\$265,900$.
\item Both sides within a maximum spread with a minimum size; the inventory limits pulling a side.
\item On the quotes that are stalest relative to the \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:fair}{fair price}, and on fewer instruments.
\item Spread \$574,900, hedging $-\$464,300$, rebates \$143,700, funding \$300, cascade \$20,800: \$275,400; a budget of one requote every four
seconds gives up \$102,400 of it, every ten seconds all of it and more.
\item The spread is largest; the hedge's cost and the budget are the most fragile.
\item A second \omterm{def:hf:fair-value:fair}{fair price} and a second place to hedge, reducing slippage and venue risk.
\item \omterm{def:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:absorb}{Liquidation absorption}, which holds positions on venues during their worst moments.
\item Carry the side that receives across timestamps when the basis allows; flatten the side that pays before them.
\item Uptime and depth, as the programme defines them.
\item Compare the model's basis path and forced flow with the venues' records of that day.
\item Immediacy on one venue, priced from another.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:1}
Fees on both venues, whether funds and coins can be moved (withdrawal status, transfer time), the depth behind the prices, and whether one venue's
price is stale or halted.
\iqlookfor{transferability and executable depth.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:2}
One governor per venue and account, mirroring each limit as a window; a priority queue of requests by value; hedges before requotes; a safety
margin below the limit; react to 429 by pausing.
\iqlookfor{priorities and margins.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:3}
The rate is a time average of the premium index over the interval plus a clamped interest term; most of it is known before the timestamp as the
average accumulates.
\iqlookfor{the formula's averaging.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:4}
The coins and the positions hedged elsewhere against them; limits on balances per venue by the venue's risk, and hedges that do not depend on that
venue.
\iqlookfor{venue counterparty risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:5}
Only once the basis is deep and the forced flow visible, within a capacity set by the recovery's likely size; hedge on the deepest spot venue.
\iqlookfor{timing and capacity.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:crypto-high-frequency-trading-on-centralised-venues:6}
The price's change over $\tau$ has standard deviation $\sigma\sqrt\tau$; the chance it exceeds $h$ in size is $2(1-\Phi(h/(\sigma\sqrt\tau)))$,
so stale quotes appear at about that rate per requote interval.
\iqlookfor{diffusion over the requote interval.}
\end{solution}
