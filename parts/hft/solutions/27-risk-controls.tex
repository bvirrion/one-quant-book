\begin{solution}{exo:hf:risk-controls:4}
It limits how fast orders go out, not what they commit: the runaway continues at a third of its speed and loses a third as much. It protects the
venues and the firm's standing with them, and slows any runaway so that other controls have time.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:risk-controls:5}
The throttle slows the loss, so the \omterm{def:hf:risk-controls:loss}{loss limit}'s threshold is reached later; the loss at the stop is the same.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:risk-controls:6}
Cancel first: open orders can still fill and add to the position while it is being flattened; flattening a position that keeps growing is chasing it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:risk-controls:7}
\$1.5 million fires on no ordinary day of the 2,500 (\$1 million fires on 2\%); the runaway loses \$1.6 million before it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:hf:risk-controls:8}
The history is of days the algorithm behaved; a volatile day can exceed it without anything going wrong. In the model a \$500,000 limit fires on
19\% of ordinary days: its cost is the trading lost on those days and the flattening at bad prices.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:hf:risk-controls:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Its routing system sent millions of orders in about 45 minutes: over 4 million executions in 154 stocks, 397 million shares, \$3.5 billion
long and \$3.15 billion short, and a loss over \$460 million.
\item See \cref{def:hf:risk-controls:access}; controls against erroneous orders and orders beyond credit and capital thresholds, documented, reviewed,
certified.
\item Cancelling immediately any or all unexecuted orders on any or all venues, and knowing which algorithm and trader sent each order.
\item A \$58 million basket entered as \$444 billion; \$189 billion passed controls to an algorithm that sold \$1.4 billion; no hard block, an
overridable alert, slow monitoring.
\item See \cref{def:hf:risk-controls:pretrade} and \cref{def:hf:risk-controls:collar}.
\item See \cref{def:hf:risk-controls:loss} and \cref{def:hf:risk-controls:kill}.
\item Firm, desks, strategies, instruments; every desk within the firm's limits, every strategy on a known desk, every limit positive.
\item Exported with a gate section that Book 13's gate loads with its \texttt{from\_riskctl} adapter.
\item Throttle never (\$161 million); collar 600 s (\$106 million); capital 407 s (\$72 million); position 102 s (\$18 million); \omterm{def:hf:risk-controls:loss}{loss limit} 12 s (\$2.1
million); a person at 5 minutes \$53 million; none \$477 million.
\item The throttle on 10.2\% and the position limit on 7.6\%; the others on none.
\item Tighter thresholds stop the runaway sooner and fire on more ordinary days: \omterm{def:hf:risk-controls:loss}{loss limits} 2\% at \$1 million and 19\% at \$500,000; position
limits half of all days at \$150 million.
\item It measures what the controls protect, so for the same false alarms it stops sooner; the position limit catches a runaway that has not yet
lost.
\item \$477 million with none, \$161 million with a throttle, \$106 million with a collar, \$72 million with a capital threshold, \$18 million with a
position limit and \$2.1 million with a \omterm{def:hf:risk-controls:loss}{loss limit}, which stops it first, after 12 seconds.
\item A \omterm{def:hf:risk-controls:loss}{loss limit} and a position limit at every level, a collar and size checks on every order, a capital threshold, a throttle for the venues; the
\omterm{def:hf:risk-controls:loss}{loss limit} at \$1.5--2 million for this book.
\item Monitors automatically, and people on the desk and in risk, within seconds, on every venue at once.
\item Who, when, which level, which action, and why.
\item A position or exposure limit and a monitor of positions that do not match the strategy's normal behaviour.
\item Replay recorded days and planted faults through the gate (Book 13's runaway on the exchange simulator).
\item That the controls exist, are reasonably designed and are reviewed regularly.
\item A bound on how much can go wrong before anyone understands why.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:risk-controls:1}
Kill state, stale limits, reference price, collar, quantity, notional, worst-case position, open orders, gross exposure, rate, duplicates: cheapest and
most decisive first.
\iqlookfor{a complete, ordered list.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:risk-controls:2}
Without current limits the gate cannot know an order is safe; refusing is the safe error. The risk is an outage of trading from a lost heartbeat,
so the snapshot's publisher must be monitored and redundant.
\iqlookfor{fail closed and its cost.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:risk-controls:3}
Not necessarily: a limit is a bound on what can go wrong, not a forecast. Review whether the loss was the strategy's normal risk (limit too tight) or
a malfunction (limit worked).
\iqlookfor{limits as bounds, reviewed after firing.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:risk-controls:4}
From the distribution of its daily peak positions, set above a high quantile so it rarely fires, and below what the firm can lose at a stressed
price move.
\iqlookfor{history and stress.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:risk-controls:5}
Block new orders at every gateway at once, send mass cancels on every session (or rely on cancel-on-disconnect), then flatten; the state must be
shared so no gateway sends another order.
\iqlookfor{block, cancel, then flatten, everywhere.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:hf:risk-controls:6}
Expected cost per day $c\,P(D<-L)+\frac1N\ell\,\tau(L)$ with $\tau(L)=L/\ell$, so the second term is $L/N$; with $c$ the cost of a false alarm,
set $c\,\phi((L+\mu)/\sigma)/\sigma=1/N$ and solve for $L$.
\iqlookfor{marginal false-alarm cost against marginal runaway loss.}
\end{solution}
