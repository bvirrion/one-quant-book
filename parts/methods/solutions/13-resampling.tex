\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:qm:resampling} --- Resampling}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:1}
Each of the $n$ draws misses the day with probability $1 - 1/n$, so $(1 - 1/n)^n$: 0.3677 for $n = 1\,260$, and $e^{-1} = 0.3679$ in the
limit. A resample contains about 63\% of the distinct days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:2}
The block continues each day with probability $0.95$, so it lasts more than 60 days with probability $0.95^{60} = 0.046$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:3}
$5^5 = 3\,125$ ordered resamples; $\binom{9}{5} = 126$ multisets (choosing five draws among five values with repetition).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:4}
Let $S = \sum_j(X_j - \bar X)^2$ and $d_i = X_i - \bar X$. Removing $X_i$ leaves the sum of squares $S - \frac{n}{n-1}d_i^2$ about the new
mean, so $\hat\theta_{(i)} = \bigl(S - \frac{n}{n-1}d_i^2\bigr)/(n - 1)$. Since $\sum_i d_i^2 = S$, the average is $\bar\theta = (S - \frac{S}{n-1})/(n
- 1) = \frac{(n-2)S}{(n-1)^2}$. The corrected estimate is $n\hat\theta - (n - 1)\bar\theta = S - \frac{(n-2)S}{n-1} = \frac{S}{n-1} = s^2$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:5}
With $\gamma(k) = \gamma(0)\phi^{|k|}$: $G = 2\gamma(0)\phi/(1 - \phi)^2$ and $g(0) = \gamma(0)(1 + \phi)/(1 - \phi)$, so $G/g(0) = 2\phi/(1 -
\phi^2) = 4/3$, and $b = (4/3)^{2/3} \times 1\,260^{1/3} = 1.211 \times 10.80 = 13.1$ days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:6}
$1 - 0.99^{100} = 0.634$. The \omterm{def:qm:resampling:bootstrap}{bootstrap} maximum equals the sample maximum 63\% of the time: its law has an atom, while the true law of the
maximum is continuous, so the \omterm{def:qm:resampling:bootstrap}{bootstrap} is inconsistent for extremes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:7}
\texttt{spurious\_rejections()} with 199 permutations or shifts per pair: the \omterm{def:qm:resampling:perm}{permutation test} rejects in 21.5\% of the 200 pairs, four
times its nominal size, because both series are persistent and shuffling destroys that; the circular-shift test, which keeps each series'
dependence, rejects in 2.5\%.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:qm:resampling:8}
Three flaws. Resampling days independently ignores the clustering of the P\&L; for the chapter's volatility seller such intervals cover
the truth 68.5\% of the time, not 95\%. The \omterm{def:qm:resampling:bootstrap}{bootstrap} cannot produce losses larger than the worst day in the sample, so even block
intervals are too narrow for a strategy with rare large losses. And the percentile is a statement about this history's resamples, not a
probability about the true \omterm{def:qm:estimation:sharpe}{Sharpe ratio}; if the strategy was chosen among many, chapter~12's correction applies on top.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:qm:resampling:1}
It estimates the sampling law of a statistic by recomputing it on resamples drawn with replacement from the data, the empirical law standing
in for the true one. It fails for statistics driven by extremes (the maximum, tail quantiles), for dependent data resampled as if
independent, and for very small samples.
\iqlookfor{The plug-in principle, and at least two failure modes.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:qm:resampling:2}
Daily P\&L is dependent: volatility clusters, positions overlap, edges come in regimes. Independent resampling estimates the short-run variance,
and the interval is too narrow; in the chapter's example it covers 68.5\% instead of 95\%. Resample blocks (\omterm{def:qm:resampling:blocks}{stationary bootstrap}), and check
coverage by simulation.
\iqlookfor{Long-run versus short-run variance, block resampling, coverage as the test.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:qm:resampling:3}
The error of the variance estimate is minimised by a length growing like $n^{1/3}$ with a constant set by the autocovariances; estimate
it (Politis--White with the 2009 correction), then check sensitivity by plotting the result against the block length. Persistent,
heavy-tailed data need longer blocks than the rule suggests.
\iqlookfor{The $n^{1/3}$ rate, an automatic rule, and a sensitivity check.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:qm:resampling:4}
When the series are serially dependent: shuffling makes the signal exchangeable although it is not, and spurious correlation between two
persistent series is far more common than the shuffles show. In the chapter a \omterm{def:qm:resampling:perm}{permutation test} rejects 21.5\% of independent pairs at
5\%. Use circular shifts or block permutations.
\iqlookfor{\omterm{def:qm:resampling:perm}{Exchangeability} as the condition, and a resampling scheme that keeps the dependence.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:qm:resampling:5}
The \omterm{def:qm:resampling:jackknife}{jackknife}: $n$ deterministic leave-one-out recomputations, a bias correction that removes the $1/n$ term, and a \omterm{def:qm:estimation:estimator}{standard error} for smooth
statistics. The \omterm{def:qm:resampling:bootstrap}{bootstrap}: a whole distribution, hence intervals and tests, at the cost of randomness and more computation. Both need
blocks for dependent data.
\iqlookfor{Bias versus distribution, and the shared need for blocks.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:qm:resampling:6}
Resample days jointly across all two hundred strategies (keeping their correlation) with a \omterm{def:qm:resampling:blocks}{stationary bootstrap} (keeping time dependence),
centre each strategy's resampled mean on its sample mean, compute the maximum $t$-statistic per resample, and read the $p$-value of the
observed maximum from that distribution: White's \omterm{def:qm:testing-and-multiple-testing:rc}{Reality Check}, or Hansen's SPA with studentisation and recentring, or Romano--Wolf for
step-down.
\iqlookfor{Joint resampling, recentring to impose the null, the maximum as the statistic.}
\end{solution}
