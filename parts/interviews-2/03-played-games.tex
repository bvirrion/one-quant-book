\chapter{Played Games}\label{ch:iv2:played-games}

\begin{ivtrains}
Quoting prices out loud while new information arrives. Each game has rules and a score sheet here, and a script in its answer. Copy the score sheet, open the script and cover it with a sheet of paper. Slide the paper down only as far as the next stop line, write your answer, and then read on. Better still, ask a friend to read the script aloud as the interviewer, giving no hints.
\end{ivtrains}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund, crypto} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:played-games:1}
\textbf{Three cards and a better-informed player.} Ten cards numbered 1 to 10 are shuffled, and three are dealt face down: $A$, $B$ and $C$. You make a market on their sum. A lot you bought at price $x$ settles at the sum minus $x$, and a lot you sold at $x$ settles at $x$ minus the sum.

You quote four times, each time as a bid and an ask in steps of 0.5. Your ask must be exactly 2 above your bid in rounds 1 to 3, and exactly 1 above it in round 4. No card is face up in rounds 1 and 2. Card $A$ is turned over before round 3, and card $C$ before round 4.

Your only counterparty is another player who has looked at card $B$ and calculates perfectly. At each quote they work out the expected sum, given $B$ and the cards face up. They sell you one lot at your bid if your bid is above that value, buy one lot at your ask if your ask is below it, and otherwise pass. You know this rule. Take about 20 seconds over each quote, and try to finish with the most money.

\textbf{Options on two dice.} Two fair dice will be rolled one after the other, and $U$ is their sum. Three instruments settle on $U$: a future paying $U$, a call paying $\max(U - 8, 0)$ and a put paying $\max(8 - U, 0)$. You are a dealer. In round 2 you may trade futures with another dealer. Die 1 is rolled before round 3, when you may trade futures again. In round 4 the other dealer shows prices in all three instruments. Die 2 is then rolled, and in round 5 everything settles. Take about 30 seconds over each round.

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund, crypto} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:played-games:3}
\textbf{Bidding for a lot you only half know.} Two ten-sided dice, with faces 1 to 10, are rolled for each lot. You see die $A$, your rival sees die $B$, and neither sees the other's. The lot is worth $A + B$. Whoever wins pays a price and keeps the lot, so your profit is $A + B$ minus what you pay. If you lose, your profit is zero.

There are four rounds, with new dice each time. You bid in steps of 0.5, from 0 to 20, and a tie goes to your rival. Your rival follows the rule stated for each round, whatever you do, and you know that rule. Take about 30 seconds over each round, and try to finish with the most money.
\begin{itemize}
\item Round 1. Sealed bids. The higher bid wins and pays its own bid. Your rival bids $B + 1$.
\item Round 2. Sealed bids. The higher bid wins and pays the other player's bid. Your rival bids $2B$.
\item Round 3. A rising price. The price climbs from 0, and each of you names in advance the highest price at which you will stay in. The one with the lower limit drops out, and the other wins at that price. Your rival's limit is $2B$.
\item Round 4. As round 1, but before you bid, you may pay 2, win or lose, to see die $B$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR, Risk} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund, crypto} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:played-games:4}
\textbf{Five bets and a bankroll.} You start with 1,000. Each round is one bet at stated odds, and you say how much of your current bankroll to stake. You cannot borrow, and you can stake nothing. A stated payout is profit: a winning bet returns your stake as well. Your aim is the fastest growth over many such sessions, not the largest number at the end of this one. Take about 30 seconds a round.
\begin{itemize}
\item Round 1. A coin that wins 60\% of the time, at even money.
\item Round 2. A fair die. A six pays 4 times your stake. Anything else loses the stake.
\item Round 3. A bet that wins 45\% of the time and pays 2 times your stake.
\item Round 4. Two separate coins, each winning 60\% of the time at even money. You stake the same amount on each.
\item Round 5. A bet that wins 90\% of the time and pays your stake. The other 10\% of the time you lose 5 times your stake.
\end{itemize}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR, Analyst} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:played-games:5}
\textbf{Quotes on numbers you have not looked up.} You quote a bid and an ask on five numbers, with no calculator and about 30 seconds each. The interviewer knows each number. Score each round afterwards, with a calculator:
\begin{itemize}
\item You always score $-\log_2(\text{ask}/\text{bid})$, so doubling your width costs 1.
\item If the number is above your ask, you also score $-4\log_2(\text{number}/\text{ask})$.
\item If it is below your bid, you also score $-4\log_2(\text{bid}/\text{number})$.
\end{itemize}
The best possible score is 0. The numbers are: the count of primes below 1,000; the count of digits in $100!$; the count of five-card hands from a 52-card deck; the sum of the first 100 primes; and the sum of the decimal digits of $2^{100}$.

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR, LLE} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund, crypto} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:played-games:7}
\textbf{A contract, a coin and a faster reader.} A hidden coin is one of two kinds, each equally likely. A good coin lands heads 70\% of the time, and a bad coin 30\%. A contract pays 100 if the coin is good, and 0 if it is bad.

There are six rounds. In each, you may either pull your quote or show a bid and an ask 6 apart, centred on the fair value you state to one decimal place. If you show a quote:
\begin{itemize}
\item a customer trades one lot with you, and that locks in 3 for you against the fair value you stated, as long as your stated value is right;
\item then, if a flip is due that round, a faster reader sees it before you can change your quote. If the new fair value is outside your quote, they trade one lot at your stale quote. You close that lot at once at the new fair value.
\end{itemize}
The interviewer tells you each round whether a flip is due. Round 1 has none, and rounds 2 to 6 have one each. Your aim is the most money.

\textbf{Other players' quotes.} Four players each roll one ten-sided die with faces 1 to 10, and see only their own. A contract pays the sum of all four dice. You roll a 7. The other three follow fixed rules, and you know them:
\begin{itemize}
\item Players $A$ and $B$ quote once, centred on their own die plus 16.5, with a bid 0.5 below and an ask 0.5 above. Each trades one lot in the whole game, at that quote, and never reads anyone else.
\item Player $C$ quotes twice, centred on their die plus $n$ plus 16.5. Here $n$ is $-3$, 0 or $+3$, equally likely, and drawn afresh for each quote. Player $C$ trades one lot at each quote.
\end{itemize}
Quotes appear round by round. You may trade or pass at each one, and your only aim is money. After round 4, the dice are shown, and the contract settles.

\textbf{Buying six lots in four rounds.} You must buy exactly 6 lots over four rounds. Round 1's price is 100. In a round with price $P$, buying $k$ lots costs $kP + 0.5k^2$ in total, and this impact does not last into the next round.

At the start of each round, you are told the price and a forecast $f$. The next round's price is $P + f + \varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon$ equally likely to be $+1$ or $-1$. The forecasts of later rounds are unknown, and average zero. Your aim is the lowest expected total cost. Round 4 must finish the job.
