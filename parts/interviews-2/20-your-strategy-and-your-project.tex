\chapter{Your Strategy and Your Project}\label{ch:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project}

\begin{ivtrains}
Talking about your own work when the person across the table is looking for the weak point: how to walk through it, how to defend a number, and how to behave when you are wrong. Where an answer has no number, it gives the recommended shape and blanks for your own facts. Fill them with what you did, and nothing else.
\end{ivtrains}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, DS, QD, Dev, LLE} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:1}
Pick the project on your CV you know best, and walk me through it in two minutes.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{DS, QR} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:13}
Your fraud model has a test AUC of 0.95. One case in a hundred is a fraud. At your chosen threshold, it catches 80\% of frauds and flags 5\% of good cases. What share of flagged cases are frauds? In a batch of 1\,000 cases, what would I see?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, DS, QT} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:14}
You say you finished 10th of 2\,000 in a data competition. The public scoreboard used 2\,000 rows, you made 50 submissions, and your best public accuracy was 80\%. All your submissions had about the same true skill. How much of that 80\% is selection? What if they were correlated 0.9? How many test rows do you need to separate you from a rival 0.3 points behind, at 1.96 standard errors, with independent errors, and with errors correlated 0.8?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Risk} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:15}
Your strategy has a 20\% return and 15\% volatility, and you show me 5 years of it. What maximum drawdown do you expect in those 5 years? Yours was 12\%. Is that good news? What average drawdown would you expect on a normal day?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{LLE, QD, Dev} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:16}
You say your matching engine averages 1 microsecond a message. You measured it by sending a message, waiting for its reply, and then sending the next. Once a second it stalls for 10 milliseconds. What do your mean and 99th percentile show? What would a stream of 100\,000 messages a second see, arriving on their own schedule? What should you have done?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:17}
Your strategy made 60\% over 1\,000 days, with a daily volatility of 1\%. I notice 40 of the 60 points came from 5 days. What are the Sharpes with and without those days? How much would normal returns give for the top 5 days? What does this tell you?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, DS, QT} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:18}
Your thesis found a signal significant at the 1\% level. Suppose that 5\% of ideas in this field are true, and that your test detects a true one half the time. What is the chance that it's real? What would it be at a 5\% level and at a 0.1\% level? What do you say when I ask?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, DS} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:19}
You say your volatility model beat the benchmark. On 500 days, its average loss was lower by 0.05, and the daily loss differences had a standard deviation of 0.6. What $t$-statistic do you get? If the differences follow a first-order autoregression with coefficient 0.3, what is it then? How do you put this in one sentence?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:your-strategy-and-your-project:20}
Here is a one-page result that you're defending. Four years, net return 18\%, volatility 12\%, turnover 50\% of capital a day, cost 3 basis points per dollar traded, beta of 0.3 to a market with a 12\% return and 16\% volatility. The Sharpe is 2.2 in the first half and 0.8 in the second. Take the risk-free rate as zero. I ask, in order: (a) What is the $t$-statistic? (b) What if costs double? (c) What are your alpha and information ratio? (d) Is the second half a sign of decay? (e) After how many independent trials would the $t$-statistic stop being significant at 5\%? (f) So what do you believe, and what would you tell me to do?
\end{question}
