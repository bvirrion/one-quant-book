\chapter{Betting and Kelly}\label{ch:iv2:betting-and-kelly}

\begin{ivtrains}
Pricing a bet before sizing it, then sizing it: the growth-optimal fraction and fractions of it, what ruin looks like under each, what an estimated edge does to the size, what a limit on the stake changes, and when a target makes the right bet a different bet. You need a pencil and a rough logarithm.
\end{ivtrains}

\section{Pricing and first sizes}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:1}
A bookmaker pays 4 to 1 on the roll of a six with a fair die: if the six comes up you gain four times your stake, and otherwise you lose your stake. What is your expected profit per dollar staked, and what odds would be fair? A second bookmaker quotes both heads and tails on a fair coin at decimal odds of 1.90, meaning that a winning stake of 1 returns 1.90 in total. What is its margin if it takes equal money on both sides?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:2}
A match has two possible outcomes, $X$ and $Y$. One bookmaker offers decimal odds of 2.50 on $X$, so a winning stake of 1 returns 2.50 in total. Another offers decimal odds of 1.80 on $Y$. You have \$1,000. How do you stake it to lock in a profit whichever way the match goes, and how much is that profit?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:3}
You may repeatedly make a bet you win with probability 55\%, gaining your stake if you win and losing it if you do not. What fixed fraction of your wealth should you stake each time to make your wealth grow fastest in the long run? What is your growth rate per bet at that fraction?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:4}
A bet pays 2 to 1: you gain twice your stake if you win and lose your stake if you do not. You win with probability 40\%. What fraction of your wealth do you stake to make it grow fastest? What if you win with probability 30\%?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:5}
Each round your whole wealth rises 50\% or falls 40\%, with equal probability. What is your expected wealth after one round? What happens to a typical path over many rounds? Which of the two would you bet on, and why are they different?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:6}
You start with \$1. You make 10 bets in a row, each won with probability 60\%, and each time you stake all you have, doubling it or losing it. What is your expected final wealth, and what is the probability you finish with anything? Would you play this way?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:7}
An even-money bet is won with probability 60\%. Compare staking the growth-optimal fraction of your wealth each time with staking half of that fraction. What are the two growth rates per bet? How does the standard deviation of the change in log wealth compare?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:8}
A bet doubles your stake's value, a gain of 100\%, with probability one half. Otherwise you lose half your stake. What fraction of your wealth makes it grow fastest, and what is the growth rate per bet at that fraction? Why is the answer not the usual ``edge over odds'' with a loss of the whole stake?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:9}
Someone offers you one coin flip: you gain \$150 on heads and lose \$100 on tails. You value money by the logarithm of your wealth. What is the smallest wealth at which you accept? What is the expected profit of the flip, and why is it not enough on its own?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:10}
A strategy returns an average of 8\% a year above the rate you borrow at, with a volatility of 16\% a year. Treat its returns as normal and rebalance continuously. What leverage makes long-run wealth grow fastest, and what is the growth rate above the borrowing rate? Write both in terms of the Sharpe ratio, the average excess return divided by volatility.
\end{question}

\section{Beyond a single even-money bet}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:11}
An even-money bet is won with probability 60\%. Show that staking twice the growth-optimal fraction shrinks a typical path. At what fraction does long-run growth reach exactly zero? The usual rule of thumb says twice the optimum: why does it not hold exactly here?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:12}
A bet has three outcomes per dollar staked: you gain \$2 with probability 30\%, get your stake back with probability 40\%, and lose your stake with probability 30\%. What fraction of your wealth makes it grow fastest, and what is the growth rate? What does the ``money back'' outcome do to the answer?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:13}
Two independent even-money bets, each won with probability 60\%, settle at the same moment. You must decide both stakes before either settles. By symmetry you stake the same fraction of your wealth on each. What fraction makes wealth grow fastest? How does the growth per round compare with making the two bets one after the other at the usual Kelly fraction?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:14}
Two strategies each return 10\% a year above the borrowing rate with a volatility of 20\%, and their correlation is 0.5. Treat returns as normal, rebalanced continuously. What growth-optimal leverage goes on each, and what is the total? What are the leverage on each and the growth rate if the correlation is zero? What is the growth rate at 0.5?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:15}
A fair coin is tossed repeatedly, and before each toss you can bet at even money. A tipster tells you the coming result and is right 75\% of the time, independently from toss to toss. What fraction of your wealth do you stake on the tip each time, and what is your long-run growth per toss? What would that growth be with a tipster who is right every time, and one who is right half the time?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:16}
Three horses run a race, and exactly one wins. They win with probabilities 50\%, 30\% and 20\%. Every horse pays 3 for 1, meaning that a winning stake of 1 returns 3 in total. You stake all your wealth each race, split across the three horses as you choose. What split makes your wealth grow fastest, and what is the growth per race? Does the split change if the payouts become 2, 4 and 8 for 1?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:17}
An even-money bet is won with probability 51\%. The only stake allowed is \$10 or nothing, and you have \$100. If you bet, what is your growth rate per bet in log wealth? What is the smallest wealth at which a \$10 bet has positive growth? What is the smallest at which \$10 is exactly the Kelly stake?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:18}
You have \$1,000 and an even-money bet won with probability 60\%, but a table limit of \$50 per bet. You stake the limit every time, for as long as you have money. What is your expected gain per bet, and what is the probability you are ever wiped out? How is your wealth's path different from Kelly's once your wealth is large?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, Trader, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:19}
You have \$100 and need \$200 to pay a debt that is due. You may make as many even-money bets as you like, at any stake up to what you hold. How do you bet to maximise the chance of reaching \$200 if each bet is won with probability 40\%? And if each is won with probability 60\%? Give the chance for \$1 bets and for one all-in bet in both cases.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{Analyst, QT, Risk} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:20}
You have \$20,000 and a 1\% chance of losing \$5,000 this year. Insurance covering the whole loss costs \$60. You value money by the logarithm of your wealth. What is the expected loss? What is the largest premium you would pay? Do you buy? What changes if you have only \$10,000?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:21}
A coin flip gains you \$200 on heads and loses you \$100 on tails. A colleague refuses a single flip, but says they would accept 100 of them. Over the 100 flips, what are the mean and the standard deviation of the gain, and what is the probability of a loss? With what wealth would a log-utility investor accept one flip? Is your colleague being consistent?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:22}
A strategy has a Sharpe ratio of 1 a year, its average excess return divided by its volatility. It is run continuously with normal returns. At full Kelly leverage, how long does a typical path take to double, in terms of the growth of its logarithm, and what is the volatility of the leveraged strategy? What are the same numbers at half Kelly?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Risk} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:23}
A strategy returns 8\% a year above the borrowing rate with a volatility of 16\%, with normal returns, rebalanced continuously. The growth-optimal leverage is 3.125. Your lender caps leverage at 2. What growth rate above the borrowing rate do you get, and what share of the maximum is that? At what leverage does growth return to zero?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR, Analyst} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:24}
A coin is tossed until the first head. If that takes $k$ tosses, the game pays $2^k$ dollars. What is its expected payoff? What is the expected payoff if the house can pay at most \$1,048,576, which is $2^{20}$, and pays that for any longer run? What would someone with log utility pay to play the uncapped game with a wealth of \$1,000?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Risk} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:25}
You measure a Sharpe ratio of 1.5 a year on four years of data, and you size for full Kelly on that estimate. What is the standard error of the estimate, for roughly normal returns? What is your long-run growth if the true Sharpe ratio is 1, and if it is 0.5? What is it in each case at half the estimated Kelly leverage?
\end{question}

\section{Ruin, estimates and targets}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:26}
You stake the Kelly fraction of your wealth on an even-money bet won with probability 60\%. Show that the probability your wealth ever falls to a fraction $x$ of where it started is at most $x$. What is the same probability in continuous time, for full Kelly and for half Kelly? What are the chances of ever losing half?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, Risk, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:27}
A strategy with normal returns is run continuously at a constant multiple $c$ of its Kelly leverage. Your investors will leave if you ever lose half, and you want at most a 5\% chance of that, for any length of time. What is the highest $c$? What share of the maximum long-run growth does that keep?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:28}
You see a bettor win 60 of 100 even-money bets. Take a uniform prior on the win probability $p$. Your next bet is your last, and you maximise expected log wealth. What fraction do you stake, and why does your uncertainty about $p$ not shrink it? A colleague says that since 95\% of the posterior for $p$ is between about 0.50 and 0.69, you should stake the size implied by the lower end. What are the arguments for and against?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QT, QR} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:29}
You have \$1, and you need \$2 after exactly three even-money bets, each won with probability 60\%. You choose each stake after seeing the earlier results, and you cannot stake more than you hold. What is the highest chance of finishing with at least \$2, and what do you stake first? What chance does betting a Kelly 20\% each time give? What does betting everything each time give?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:30}
On an even-money bet won with probability 60\%, you stake 20\% of your wealth each time, and a rival stakes 30\%. Over 1,000 bets, who has the higher expected wealth? What is the probability you finish ahead of the rival? Reconcile the two.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Risk} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:31}
A trade makes 25 cents per dollar at risk with probability 90\%, and it loses the whole dollar with probability 10\%. What fraction of your wealth maximises long-run growth? What does the ``mean over variance'' rule give, and what is your growth at that size? What does this tell you about sizing a strategy that sells options?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:32}
A shuffled deck of 26 red and 26 black cards is turned over one card at a time. Before each card you may bet any part of your wealth on its colour, at even money. Starting with \$1, how do you bet to maximise your expected log wealth at the end, and what do you end with? Can any other strategy have a higher expected final wealth?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:33}
An even-money bet is won with probability 55\%. You want at least a 95\% chance of being ahead at the end. Using a normal approximation for log wealth, how many bets do you need at the Kelly fraction, and how many at half of it? What are the exact numbers from the binomial distribution?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:34}
A coin has an unknown probability $p$ of heads, with a uniform prior. You bet at even money on each of $n$ tosses. Before each toss you find your posterior mean for $p$, call it $m$, and you stake the fraction $|2m - 1|$ of your wealth on the likelier side. Show that your final wealth depends only on the number of heads, not on their order, and find it. For 7 heads in 10 tosses, what do you end with? What would someone who knew $p = 0.7$ and played Kelly end with? And with 10 heads in 10 tosses?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv2:betting-and-kelly:35}
You can trade continuously, without limit, a risky asset with a Sharpe ratio of 1 a year, following geometric Brownian motion, and cash at a zero rate. You start with \$1 and are paid only if you hold at least \$2 in one year. What is the highest achievable probability, and what strategy achieves it? Compare it with holding the Kelly leverage throughout, and with half Kelly. What do you hold if the asset has done badly late in the year?
\end{question}
