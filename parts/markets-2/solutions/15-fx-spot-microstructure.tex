\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:m2:fx-spot-microstructure} --- FX Spot Microstructure}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:1}
The provider earned the half-spread, USD~100, and lost the two-pip move of the
mid in the client's favour, USD~200: a \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-out} of $-\text{USD}~100$, or $-0.87$
basis points of the mid.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:2}
$0.01\sqrt{h}$: 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 basis points.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:3}
Whether, and how, price changes in either direction affect the decision to
accept or reject; the expected or typical time taken to decide; and the purpose
of \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{last look}. During the window it must not use the request to gather
information with no intention of trading, nor trade on it, by adjusting its
prices or hedging.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:4}
$0.05\,\varphi(0) = 0.0199$ basis points at 25 milliseconds, against 0.0399 at
100: a quarter of the hold, half the cost.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:5}
Down: it lowers both bid and ask by 0.1 pip, so that clients buying euros come
to it and clients selling go elsewhere. It pays a tenth of a pip on the trades it
attracts, less than the spread and market impact of selling EUR~20 million on a
venue, and reveals nothing to the market.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:6}
It rejects moves beyond the threshold in both directions, twice the region of
the asymmetric check. But the rejected moves in the client's favour are offset
by rejected moves against it, which the client would otherwise have had to
accept; the asymmetric check keeps those, and refuses only the good ones.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:7}
At 100 milliseconds: asymmetric, informed 92.68\% and uninformed 30.79\%
rejected, \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-out} 0.4491 basis points; symmetric, informed 93.46\% and
uninformed 61.76\%, \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-out} 0.3991.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:8}
The \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{reject rate} does not measure what is rejected. An asymmetric check that
rejects 5\% of requests, all of them those in which the price moved in the
client's favour, still transfers the value of those moves; and a \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{hold time}
without rejects costs the client the option the provider holds during it. Look
at the direction of the moves on rejected and accepted requests, the \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{hold times},
and the \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-outs}, not only at the reject count.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:1}
\textbf{1.} $0.01\sqrt{100} = 0.1$ basis points.
\textbf{2.} $1 - \Phi(0.5) = 30.9\%$.
\textbf{3.} $2(1 - \Phi(0.5)) = 61.7\%$.
\textbf{4.} Asymmetric, $0.1\,\varphi(0.5) = 0.0352$ basis points; symmetric,
zero.
\textbf{5.} $0.1/\sqrt{2\pi} = 0.0399$ basis points.
\textbf{6.} $0.0352 \times 10^{-4} \times 2 \times 10^9$, about USD~7\,041.
\textbf{7.} About USD~1.76 million.
\textbf{8.} The half-spread costs USD~80\,000 a day; the window adds 8.8\% to
it, hidden.
\textbf{9.} 0.0199 basis points, about USD~3\,989 a day.
\textbf{10.} The move over the hold has a standard deviation proportional to
$\sqrt h$, and the transfer is proportional to that standard deviation.
\textbf{11.} The direction of the price move on rejected requests (all in the
client's favour), the \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-outs} of accepted requests against rejected ones, and
\omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{hold times} that differ by direction.
\textbf{12.} It counts rejects, not their direction: a small share of rejects,
all on favourable moves, is exactly what a costly asymmetric check produces.
\textbf{13.} Under Principle~17, whether and how price moves in either
direction affect acceptance, the typical \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{hold time}, and the purpose; and
confirmation that the request is not used for trading during the window.
\textbf{14.} The market risk it wanted to shed, left with it at the moment the
price moved against it, and the cost of trading again at a worse price.
\textbf{15.} Its quotes may be tighter or larger, and a symmetric, short, well
disclosed check may be worth its lost fills.
\textbf{16.} That it was applied broadly and indiscriminately, without checking
for latency arbitrage, that it rejected only trades unprofitable to the bank,
and that clients were given misleading or no explanations.
\textbf{17.} It takes nothing on average, but it still refuses trades and leaves
the client with the risk; its fairness depends on a short hold and on
disclosure.
\textbf{18.} By wider spreads for clients with poor \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-outs}, smaller sizes,
tiers, faster price updates, and by refusing to stream to toxic flow at all.
\textbf{19.} Named result: \emph{the asymmetric window} of 100 milliseconds at
a 0.05-basis-point threshold takes 0.0352 basis points per request, about
USD~1.76 million a year on USD~2 billion of requests a day.
\textbf{20.} The value of the price moves in its favour that the provider refuses
to honour, plus the risk of every rejected trade.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:1}
A short window after a trade request arrives during which the provider may
accept or reject it at the quoted price. Providers use it to protect streamed
prices against stale-price and latency arbitrage, to check credit and validity,
and so to quote tighter to everyone; the cost falls on clients whose requests
are rejected.
\iqlookfor{the definition, the protection argument and its cost.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:2}
Record for every request the mid at the request, at the decision and after it.
Compare the distribution of the move during the hold for rejected and accepted
requests: with a symmetric check, rejects occur on large moves in both
directions; with an asymmetric one, only on moves in your favour. Test whether
reject probability depends on the sign of the move, and whether \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{hold times}
differ by direction.
\iqlookfor{conditioning on the direction of the move.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:3}
The provider's profit on a trade marked at the mid at a later horizon: the
half-spread minus the client-favourable move. Averaged by client over horizons
from milliseconds to minutes it shows who trades ahead of moves. Tier by it:
tight prices for clients with positive \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:markout}{mark-outs}, wider or slower for the
others, re-estimated regularly with confidence intervals.
\iqlookfor{the definition and a sensible tiering procedure.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:4}
It depends on the flow expected and the risk limits. If clients are likely to
buy euros soon, skew the price to attract them and internalise; if the position
is large against the limit or the market is fast, hedge part of it on a venue or
with an execution algorithm. Waiting without a skew is a view on the price,
which a market maker should take only deliberately.
\iqlookfor{inventory, expected flow and limits, not a single rule.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:5}
Transparency and disclosure of how \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{last look} works, including its symmetry and
timing; use only as a validity and price check; no information gathering and no
trading on the request during the window. It does not ban asymmetric checks or
fix \omterm{def:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:lastlook}{hold times}, and it is not law: adherence is voluntary, through statements of
commitment.
\iqlookfor{the substance of Principle 17 and its limits.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m2:fx-spot-microstructure:6}
One core computes the mid from venue feeds; per-tier spreads and skews are
applied in a precomputed table; quotes are published through a fan-out layer
with per-client throttles. Requests arrive on a separate path; the check
compares the current mid with the mid at the quoted time, stored with each quote
identifier, against a symmetric threshold, after a fixed short hold; the decision
and its inputs are logged for later analysis. No allocation on the hot path,
lock-free queues, and a replay test against recorded market data.
\iqlookfor{separation of pricing and acceptance paths, symmetric logic, logging
for audit.}
\end{solution}
