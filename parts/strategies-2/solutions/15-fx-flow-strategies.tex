\begin{solution}{exo:s2:fx-flow-strategies:4}
Foreign investors who hedge their US equities are short dollars in proportion to the holdings' value; when the holdings rise, they must sell more
dollars forward to keep the hedge ratio, and they do so at the month-end fix.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:s2:fx-flow-strategies:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A reference rate from trades in a short window; portfolios are valued and hedges settled at it.
\item Extraordinary volatility and negative serial correlation around it.
\item Information sharing on clients' orders and attempts to manipulate fixes and trigger stop-losses; \pounds1.1 billion of fines.
\item Mandated net orders at the fix, estimable in advance.
\item Hedged investors restore their hedge ratios after the month's asset returns.
\item Equity appreciation over the month predicts currency depreciation before the month-end fix.
\item Over the last two days, correlation $-0.18$ ($t$ = $-3.3$) and a trade of 11.4 basis points a month ($t$ = 3.4).
\item A forecast of the hedgers' currency trades from returns, holdings and hedge ratios.
\item Hedge ratios 0.5 and 0.3, a push of 12.2 basis points in standard deviation, half reversed; estimates with 30\% errors.
\item 12.2 basis points a month, Sharpe ratio 0.99.
\item From 0.2 in the smallest fifth to 21.8 in the largest.
\item 0.75 basis points a month after costs, Sharpe ratio 0.50.
\item \textbf{Named result.} The month-end flow trade earns 12.2 basis points a month ($t$ = 5.4), rising with the estimated flow from 0.2 in the
smallest fifth to 21.8 in the largest; the public-data version earned 11.4 ($t$ = 3.4).
\item 10.2 basis points a month ($t$ = 4.9).
\item Positioning for a central bank's predictable response; the bank can outlast any trader, or give up its line.
\item Public information, a flow large against depth, and acceptance of the months the estimate misses.
\item Holdings and hedge ratios known then, fix-time prices, costs.
\item Fix-window liquidity provision.
\item Both trade flows that follow from rules applied to past returns: leveraged products' rebalancing there, hedgers' here.
\item Investors whose mandates make them trade at a fixed time, whatever the price.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:s2:fx-flow-strategies:1}
The benchmark exchange rates set from trades in a short window around 4 p.m.\ London; portfolios, indices and hedges are valued and settled at it, so
large mandated orders concentrate there.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:s2:fx-flow-strategies:2}
From each market's returns over the month, estimates of foreign holdings by country (from public statistics and fund filings) and typical hedge
ratios by investor type; net the flows across investor groups and scale by the market's depth at the fix.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:s2:fx-flow-strategies:3}
Execute the client's order as agreed, with its interests first; hedge the desk's own risk as allowed by the firm's policy. Never share the order,
trade ahead of it for the desk's benefit, or coordinate with others to move the fix.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:s2:fx-flow-strategies:4}
The estimate is wrong in direction; other flows dominate; crowding by others trading the same estimate; spreads at the fix; a news event near month-end.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:s2:fx-flow-strategies:5}
Record every order and trade with timestamps around fixes; compare desk positions with client orders at the fix; flag trades ahead of large client
orders, messages mentioning client flows, and repeated moves in the window.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:s2:fx-flow-strategies:6}
With holdings $H$ worth $H(1 + R)$ after the month, the hedge needed is $hH(1 + R)$ against $hH$ held: a trade of $hHR$. If both $H$ and $h$ are
overestimated by $(1 + \epsilon)$, the estimate is $hHR(1 + \epsilon)^2$, an error of about $2\epsilon$ in proportion; its sign is right whenever
$R$'s is.
\end{solution}
