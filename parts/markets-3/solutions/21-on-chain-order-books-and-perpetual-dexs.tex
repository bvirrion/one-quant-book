\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs} --- On-Chain Order Books and Perpetual DEXs}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:1}
Arrival order: the IOC buys the ask at 101, the cancel finds nothing, the new ask rests at 103. Class rule: the new ask at 103 rests, the
cancel removes the ask at 101, and the IOC finds no ask at or below 101 and expires. Only the maker's quote at 103 is left in both cases.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:2}
Every quote and cancel would cost gas and wait up to a block: makers could neither afford to update quotes nor cancel before being picked
off, so they would quote wide or not at all; and the public \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:mempool}{mempool} would show every cancel before it executed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:3}
A \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:pow}{validator}, or a minority of stake, publishing a false price: the median ignores outliers, and moving it requires a majority of the stake to
move together. The weighted median over venues inside each \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:pow}{validator}'s price protects against one venue's price being moved.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:4}
Initial margin at 20 times is 5\%, so maintenance is 2.5\%; the vault takes over below two thirds of it, an equity of about 1.67\% of notional.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:5}
$4\text{ million} \times (1 - 0.111) = $ USD~3.56 million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:6}
In an oracle-priced pool the provider is the counterparty to every trade at the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle}'s price, and loses to anyone who knows the price better or
sooner (\omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} latency, manipulation), plus the traders' net P\&L. In a vault it bears the inventory of its market making and the tail of inherited
liquidations. Both concentrate the venue's risk on depositors; the pool's is continuous, the vault's comes in jumps.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:7}
Arrival order: 980 lots picked off and 1\,480 ticks lost; the class rule: none. The rule's protection does not depend on who arrives first
within the block, only on the maker's cancel being in the same block.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:8}
\omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:pow}{Validators} can vote to delist a market and settle it at a price they specify by rule, and protocol parameters (margins, caps, the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle}'s
sources) can change by governance. Decentralised means that no single company decides, not that nothing can change.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:1}
\textbf{1.} Half of the 33.3\% initial margin at 3 times: 16.7\%; at two thirds of that, 11.1\% of notional, about USD~1.11 million.
\textbf{2.} The book was too thin: a market order for USD~10 million would have moved the price far, and was sent in 20\% slices that did not
restore margin. \textbf{3.} USD~8.89 million at 100\%; USD~38.89 million at 400\%. \textbf{4.} He holds offsetting long positions in other
accounts or markets that gain from the rise, while the loss on the liquidated short stops at his margin. \textbf{5.} Move the spot price on
several external venues at once, since the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} is a weighted median over them: the cheapest venues to move set how much the attack costs.
\textbf{6.} $5/(5 - 0.111) \approx$ USD~1.02 million. \textbf{7.} USD~7.78 million. \textbf{8.} The squeeze the attacker can afford depends
on what it costs to move the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle}'s sources; thin external markets make large squeezes cheap. \textbf{9.} Higher margin tiers for large
positions, lower maximum leverage, total \omterm{def:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:oicap}{open-interest caps}, \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} sources and weights, and the size of liquidation slices. \textbf{10.} The
caps, tiers and \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} rules that bound its tail, and the governance process that can change them. \textbf{11.} All positions are settled at
the rule's price and orders cancelled. \textbf{12.} Those short the squeezed asset gain relative to a settlement at the squeezed price, the
attacker's longs lose; the vault is spared the continuing squeeze. \textbf{13.} Both: it is a collective decision by rule-holders, not by a
company, but it shows that rules can change during a position. \textbf{14.} By its own staff: raising margins, restricting the market,
delisting, or settling at a chosen price under its terms of service. \textbf{15.} As a discount or a limit on positions in markets where such
votes are plausible (thin, recently listed, vault-backstopped). \textbf{16.} Only with caps sized to the external markets' depth, or not at
all for \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} whose spot can be moved cheaply. \textbf{17.} Safer: custody on chain, public state, rules that apply to all; not safer:
\omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} dependence, public backstops that can be targeted, governance intervention, and smart-contract risk. \textbf{18.} Where the in-block
rule protects its quotes and the fees suit its volume, typically the \omterm{def:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:perpdex}{on-chain order book} with cancels first; the oracle-priced pool is a
counterparty, not a place to make markets. \textbf{19.} \emph{Named result:} a loss of USD~$10\text{ million} \times (s - 0.111)$ for a
squeeze $s$ (USD~38.89 million at 400\%), and a cap of about USD~1.02 million to keep it within USD~5 million at 500\%. \textbf{20.} Depositors'
capital that stands behind every liquidation, and so a target for anyone who can make a liquidation go wrong.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:1}
It removes the pick-off of stale quotes whenever the maker's cancel lands in the same block as the taker's order, lowering adverse selection;
makers can quote tighter and larger, and takers must beat the maker by a whole block to pick off a quote.
\iqlookfor{adverse selection moved from makers to takers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:2}
Messages are signed transactions with nonces; acknowledgements come per block; the state of orders is read from blocks, not from a private
stream; in-block ordering rules determine matching; gas or \omterm{def:m3:centralised-exchanges:ratelimit}{rate limits} per action; reconciliation against the chain's state; and key
management for signing wallets.
\iqlookfor{blocks as the clock, and signatures as the session.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:3}
A median ignores up to half the weight of outliers, so a manipulated or broken venue does not move it; a volume-weighted average follows every
source in proportion and can be moved by wash-traded volume. The median can jump between sources and lags when the market moves on the heavy
ones.
\iqlookfor{robustness against responsiveness.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:4}
Deposits in a vault or pool as a credit and tail exposure: a limit by venue, sized to stress losses (squeezes, \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} failures), lock-up periods,
monitoring of open interest and caps, and of governance proposals.
\iqlookfor{tail losses and liquidity of the deposit.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:5}
Attack: trade when the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} is stale (latency), move thin \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} sources, or open positions at a price known to be about to change. Defend:
robust multi-source \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracles}, delays or price-impact fees, \omterm{def:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:oicap}{open-interest caps}, and limits on position size relative to the sources' depth.
\iqlookfor{the gap between the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:contract}{oracle} and the true price.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:on-chain-order-books-and-perpetual-dexs:6}
Ingest blocks with their transactions in proposed order; implement the venue's in-block rule and matching exactly; replay from a known snapshot,
verifying the resulting book and positions against published state at checkpoints; keep the replayer deterministic (integer arithmetic, stable
sorting) and version it with the protocol's upgrades.
\iqlookfor{exact rules, snapshots and checkpoints.}
\end{solution}
