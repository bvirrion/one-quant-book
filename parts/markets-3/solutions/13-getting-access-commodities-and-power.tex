\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:2}
A position limit is a hard cap; an \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:accountability}{accountability level} is a threshold above which the exchange may ask for
explanations and order the trader not to increase the position.

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:3}

and deals only with categories 1, 2 and 4.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:4}

before the deadline; otherwise it will be settled as a long imbalance.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:5}
First case: $3 + \max(-1, 0) - 2 = $ EUR~1 million. Second: $3 + 0.5 - 2 = $ EUR~1.5 million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:6}
\omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}{REMIT} covers wholesale energy products, including derivatives on electricity and gas traded on organised
markets; the firm is a market participant that must register and report unless it trades only products
outside its scope.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:7}
Exchange: EUR~4.47 million of collateral, EUR~0.283 million a year; OTC: EUR~6.21 million of credit, EUR~0.118
million; physical: EUR~0.5 million, EUR~0.175 million. The exchange route costs as much as the OTC route when
$60\,000 + m \times 37.23\text{ million} \times 5\% = 118\,075$: a margin rate of 3.1\%.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:8}
Futures hedge the price, not the buyer's credit: if the buyer defaults after taking delivery, the firm loses
the unpaid energy and must replace the future deliveries at market prices while its futures hedge moves the
other way. Credit and price are separate risks.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:1}
\textbf{1.} 438\,000~MWh, EUR~37.23 million. \textbf{2.} EUR~4.47 million. \textbf{3.} Physical delivery
through the grid must be scheduled and its imbalances settled by a responsible party. \textbf{4.} EUR~6.21
million: payment follows delivery, and two months covers a billing cycle plus the time to stop deliveries and
replace the buyer. \textbf{5.} The buyer, whose bank issues it for a fee (about EUR~93\,000 a year at 1.5\%).
\textbf{6.} The firm posts EUR~4.97 million (margin and security); its buyer's bank adds EUR~6.21 million of
credit. \textbf{7.} EUR~248\,380 at 5\%. \textbf{8.} It roughly doubles, since the margin rate applies to a
larger notional, and variation margin flows with the price in between. \textbf{9.} It is a bank's promise to pay
the firm, obtained by the buyer, not cash the firm has posted. \textbf{10.} The balancing-group contract, a
metering and scheduling system, nomination procedures with the operator, and the regulator's registration.
\textbf{11.} Registration with a national regulator, which assigns an ACER code to put on its transaction reports.
\textbf{12.} Not if its commodity dealing is ancillary to a main business that is neither investment services
nor banking and meets the delegated regulation's tests; a pure trading firm must check this. \textbf{13.} It must
designate a representative in the Union and register in a member state where it is active. \textbf{14.} Its
wholesale energy contracts, including exchange trades (usually reported for it by the exchange) and bilateral
physical contracts. \textbf{15.} The exchange's position limits or \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:accountability}{accountability levels}. \textbf{16.} The
exchange route: collateral is cash but the operations are simplest; physical balancing can come once systems
and staff are in place. \textbf{17.} Liquidity: margin calls if prices move, before any revenue from the
physical sale. \textbf{18.} Capital, ownership and management, risk controls, the trading plan and its limits,
and the collateral it can post. \textbf{19.} \emph{Named result:} EUR~4.97 million posted by the firm (EUR~4.47
million of margin and EUR~0.5 million of security), EUR~6.21 million of credit from the buyer's bank, and
EUR~248\,380 a year of carry. \textbf{20.} Collateral, credit and compliance before any price risk is taken.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:1}
A virtual account with the grid operator that aggregates a portfolio's injections, withdrawals and trades and is
settled for its imbalances. Costs: security or guarantees, scheduling systems and staff working to deadlines,
imbalance costs, and fees.
\iqlookfor{operational and financial costs, not only the definition.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:2}
The buyer's bank undertakes to pay against conforming documents: the seller's credit risk moves from the buyer to
the bank, and payment does not depend on the buyer's cooperation once documents are presented.
\iqlookfor{documentary payment and the substitution of credit.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:3}
Futures: daily variation margin in cash and initial margin that rises with volatility, so large moves drain
liquidity before the physical position pays. OTC forwards: collateral under credit support agreements (or none),
more credit risk and less liquidity drain. The 2022 energy crisis showed the futures route's cash risk.
\iqlookfor{cash margin against credit risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:4}
A clearing relationship that will deliver and receive warrants, a warehouse or warrant account, cash to pay for
metal it takes, and a plan for lending if it holds a dominant position.
\iqlookfor{the physical settlement chain behind the contract.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:5}
\omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}{REMIT} registration (and ACER code) with a national regulator, transaction reporting arrangements, a check of
authorisation needs under MiFID II (the \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:aae}{ancillary activity exemption} rarely fits a fund), exchange and clearing
agreements, and, for physical trading, balancing-group and network contracts.
\iqlookfor{\omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}{REMIT}, MiFID II status, and the physical layer.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:6}
A position store by group, zone and MTU fed by all trade capture; forecast inputs; a nomination builder that
balances and matches per counterparty; validation against deadlines and operator formats; submission with
acknowledgement tracking and automatic resubmission after intraday trades; reconciliation with the operator's
matched schedules and with imbalance settlements.
\iqlookfor{balancing, matching, deadlines and reconciliation.}
\end{solution}
