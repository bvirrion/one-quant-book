\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues} --- Getting Access: Crypto Venues}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:1}
USD~100 million is on VIP~4 (0.040\%/0.052\%): $100\text{ million} \times (0.7 \times 4 + 0.3 \times 5.2)$ basis points, USD~43\,600.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:2}
Identity and incorporation, beneficial owners and directors, source of funds, licences and regulatory status, the trading strategy. The
venue bears the legal risk of serving a sanctioned or unlicensed client or laundering funds, and its own licences depend on these checks.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:3}
Ten a second for ten seconds is 100, but 200\,000 a day is only 2.3 a second on average: a strategy that quotes all day runs into the daily
rule long before it saturates the short one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:4}
USD~11\,040 a month: from 3.6 basis points on venue C's own schedule to 2.68 on the matched Binance tier.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:5}
USD~10\,500 a month; missing by one point loses all of it, since the rebate is paid only if the requirement is met.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:6}
Resilience and information: a venue can fail, suspend withdrawals or go down in a crisis, and prices and liquidity on each venue inform quoting
on the others; small allocations also keep the accounts, connections and relationships alive.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:7}
About 64.0\% at 10 basis points and 93.6\% at 20: at 10, the spread limit binds long before the depth rule does.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:8}
Trading between one's own \omterm{def:m3:centralised-exchanges:subaccount}{sub-accounts} is \omterm{def:m3:spot-markets:wash}{wash trading}; venues exclude it from tiers where they can detect it, it breaches their terms, and it may
be illegal manipulation. It also pays taker fees on half of it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:1}
\textbf{1.} USD~52\,320 (Binance), 28\,800 (Kraken), 43\,200 (C), 86\,400 (D) and 110\,400 (E): USD~321\,120. \textbf{2.} USD~80\,400 and
63\,000: USD~143\,400. \textbf{3.} USD~177\,720 a month. \textbf{4.} Binance moves from VIP~4 to VIP~5 and Kraken from the USD~100 million to the
USD~250 million tier; Kraken's maker fee is zero from USD~10 million, so 70\% of its volume pays nothing. \textbf{5.} It would cut C's cost by
USD~11\,040, making a three-venue allocation cheaper than spreading and closer to concentration. \textbf{6.} $300\text{ million} \times 70\% \times
0.5$ basis points: USD~10\,500 a month. \textbf{7.} 87.3\%: it does not qualify. \textbf{8.} Keeping full size up to 2.25 times normal volatility
instead of 2.0: 91.0\%. \textbf{9.} Full-size quotes in more of the volatile minutes, when adverse selection is highest; the rebate must exceed the
extra expected losses. \textbf{10.} Keeping quotes compliant in fast markets needs updates, which the order-count rules ration; the firm must budget
its messages for the moments the programme measures. \textbf{11.} Concentration puts USD~300 million a month through each of two venues, and the
collateral and balances there; spreading halves each exposure but adds three. \textbf{12.} Collateral held with a custodian and mirrored on the venues
reduces what each venue failure can take, making concentration of trading less of a concentration of credit. \textbf{13.} Liquidity and prices on
those venues, a fallback if one of the two fails, and the option to trade there later. \textbf{14.} By the realised cost of its own trades on each venue
(spread paid, slippage, markouts), not by fees alone. \textbf{15.} Monthly, and whenever a schedule, a programme or its volume changes. \textbf{16.}
It lowers the firm's costs and deepens two books, but it thins the others and concentrates the market's liquidity and risk. \textbf{17.} Rebates buy
the quoting behaviour the venue wants from the firms that can provide it; cutting fees for everyone buys nothing specific. \textbf{18.} Few: two or
three where it can reach good tiers, plus one kept small for resilience. \textbf{19.} \emph{Named result:} USD~177\,720 a month saved by concentrating
on two venues; the rebate requires 90\% uptime, which the strategy reaches (91.0\%) only if it keeps full size up to 2.25 times normal volatility,
against 87.3\% as run. \textbf{20.} Access to its customers' order flow, on terms that improve with the volume and the risk the market maker brings.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:1}
Fees fall with volume on each venue, so concentrating volume lowers the average fee; the cheapest allocation puts most volume where the schedule rewards
it, subject to resilience, liquidity and credit limits per venue.
\iqlookfor{marginal tier effects and non-fee constraints.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:2}
Its licences and jurisdiction, ownership, financial statements or \omterm{def:m3:centralised-exchanges:por}{proof of reserves} and liabilities, custody and segregation terms, withdrawal track
record, insurance, its treatment of client assets in insolvency, and the firm's credit limit and sweep policy for it.
\iqlookfor{credit analysis of the venue.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:3}
Measure uptime continuously as the venue does; prioritise quote updates on the instruments at risk of failing; coalesce updates; keep a message budget
for fast markets; widen within the allowed spread rather than cancel; alert before the monthly measure drops below the threshold.
\iqlookfor{measurement, prioritisation and a message budget.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:4}
Only if the rebate exceeds the expected adverse-selection losses of quoting through those periods, measured from the firm's own markouts in volatile
minutes; the answer differs by instrument.
\iqlookfor{rebate against measured tail losses.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:5}
Compare its volume with its \omterm{def:m3:spot-markets:depth}{quoted depth} and web or on-chain activity, test its trade sizes for rounding and Benford's law
(\cref{ch:m3:spot-markets}), check its prices' co-movement with the main venues, and trade small amounts to see the real depth.
\iqlookfor{independent evidence of real activity.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:getting-access-crypto-venues:6}
For each venue: volume and tier, fees and rebates earned, uptime against each programme, message usage against limits, balances and collateral
against credit limits, withdrawal and connectivity incidents, and execution quality; with alerts for threshold breaches and a monthly allocation
recommendation.
\iqlookfor{costs, obligations, limits and credit in one view.}
\end{solution}
