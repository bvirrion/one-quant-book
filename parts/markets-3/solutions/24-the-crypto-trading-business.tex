\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:m3:the-crypto-trading-business} --- The Crypto Trading Business}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:1}
It holds the lent \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} and owes the same number back: a long spot position and a short forward of equal size, whose price
moves cancel.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:2}
A \omterm{def:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:custody}{hot wallet}'s keys sit on connected systems and can sign at once; a \omterm{def:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:custody}{cold wallet}'s keys are offline and signing needs a
deliberate offline process. MPC splits the key into shares held by several parties who sign jointly, so that no single person
or machine ever holds the whole key.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:3}
\omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:stable}{Stablecoin} (asset-referenced and e-money \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{token}) titles from 30 June 2024; the rest, including CASPs, from 30 December 2024, with
existing providers allowed to continue until 1 July 2026 at the latest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:4}
About USD~0.171, with a delta of 0.60.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:5}
From 11.7\% to 41.8\% of the loan: the calls are out of the money, and the value of out-of-the-money options rises steeply with
volatility, so the fee depends mostly on a number no one can know for a \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{token} that has not traded.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:6}
The mid across venues; the depth it would consume across them and their fees; the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:stable}{stablecoin} and fiat conversion; the risk of the price
moving during the hedge (volatility times the square root of the hedging time, times size); the settlement and credit risk of the client
and the window; and a margin for the desk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:7}
About 444\,600 \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens}: the hedge falls from 678\,600 \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} short to 234\,000.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:8}
A volume obligation is met most cheaply by trading with oneself, which is \omterm{def:m3:spot-markets:wash}{wash trading}: the SEC's 2024 cases charged firms that sold exactly
that service. Obligations should be on spread, depth and presence, which serve users.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:1}
\textbf{1.} 2 million \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} at USD~0.50: USD~1 million. \textbf{2.} About USD~114\,052 (0.75), 89\,993 (1.00) and 60\,574 (1.50); USD~264\,619
in all. \textbf{3.} 26.5\%. \textbf{4.} Within 0.33\% (USD~263\,744) with 200\,000 paths. \textbf{5.} 11.7\%. \textbf{6.} About 992\,331 \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens}.
\textbf{7.} 49.6\%. \textbf{8.} At 1.00 with eleven months left the hedge is about 1\,400\,000 \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} short: the market maker sells another 408\,000 into
the rally. \textbf{9.} At 0.25 it is about 518\,000: it buys back 474\,000 into the fall. \textbf{10.} Half the loan is sold into a market that has just
opened, as the issuer's own supply: the market maker's hedging is a large seller at listing. \textbf{11.} Maximum spread, minimum depth on each side, presence
(share of time quoting), venues and reporting; avoid volume targets and anything that rewards a price level. \textbf{12.} Quote at most 0.5\% either side of
its fair value with USD~50\,000 on each side, drawing on the lent \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} for the offers and on its own \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:stable}{stablecoins} for the bids, and widen only within the
obligation. \textbf{13.} More supply at a known date: the price and volatility assumptions change, and the hedge and quotes must be re-sized ahead of it.
\textbf{14.} The calls' value as fee revenue at signing (and remeasured), the lent \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} as an asset with an equal liability to return them, and the hedge's
P\&L as position P\&L. \textbf{15.} Manipulating the price or volume (wash trades, pushing the price towards the strikes), or misleading investors about the
market's activity. \textbf{16.} It depends on volatility: at 120\% the calls pay the market maker USD~265\,000 for a year's quoting; if the issuer estimated
50\%, it thought it paid much less. \textbf{17.} A new project has \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} but little cash, and calls pay most when the listing succeeds. \textbf{18.} Measure
its quotes' spread and depth on the venues yourself, look for its trades against itself, and ask for its hedge and inventory reports. \textbf{19.}
\emph{Named result:} the calls are worth 26.5\% of the loaned \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens}' value, and the market maker sells about 992\,000 \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} (49.6\% of the loan) on day
one. \textbf{20.} Liquidity for a new market, paid for with an option on its success.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:1}
With call options on the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{token}, while the lent \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} supply its inventory; it sells the calls' delta at signing and rebalances, selling in rallies and buying
in falls, to earn the calls' value rather than bet on the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{token}.
\iqlookfor{the loan nets out; the calls are the pay; delta hedging.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:2}
Most assets in cold storage; \omterm{def:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:custody}{hot wallets} sized to daily needs; MPC or multi-signature approvals with separation of duties; address allow-lists; daily
reconciliation of on-chain balances to books; limits and sweeps for balances on venues; tested recovery procedures; independent audit.
\iqlookfor{defence in depth and reconciliation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:3}
From comparable \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:key}{tokens} at their listings (sector, float, unlock schedule), the realised and implied volatilities of peers, pre-market or perpetual prices if
any trade, and a wide range for stress; update fast from the first days' trading.
\iqlookfor{comparables and humility.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:4}
From the available depth across venues at that hour (thin), perpetual prices, the time to hedge and its price risk, the \omterm{def:m3:blockchains-for-traders:stable}{stablecoin} leg (fiat is closed), and
the client's settlement: wider than on a weekday, or partly on a risk basis with a hedge in perpetuals.
\iqlookfor{weekend liquidity and settlement.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:5}
Authorisation for the services it provides in the EU (custody, dealing), conduct and disclosure rules, reporting, and originator and beneficiary information on
transfers with other regulated providers, with procedures for transfers to unhosted wallets; venues it may use may change.
\iqlookfor{authorisation, conduct, and data with transfers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:m3:the-crypto-trading-business:6}
Pre-approved destination addresses per venue; a request with amounts and purpose; approval by several people through MPC quorums; test transfers for new routes;
amounts split by venue and chain; monitoring until credited; reconciliation; and alerts on anything outside the plan.
\iqlookfor{quorums, allow-lists, test transfers and reconciliation.}
\end{solution}
