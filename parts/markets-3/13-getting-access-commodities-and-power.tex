\chapter{Getting Access: Commodities and Power}\label{ch:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power}

A new trading firm wants to trade German power spreads. Before its first order it needs a
clearing member that will accept its exchange trades and post its margin, an admission to the
spot exchange, a balancing-group contract with a grid operator if it will ever hold physical
power at delivery, a registration with an energy regulator that gives it the identifier it
must put on every reported trade, and, for each physical counterparty that will not trade on
credit, a \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}{letter of credit} from a bank. Each of these takes weeks, some months, and most of
them take collateral. This chapter, the last of the commodities part, covers the access layer
of commodity and power markets: exchange and clearing membership, the metal exchange's
categories, \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:group}{balancing groups} and nominations, the credit and \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:tf}{trade finance} of physical trade,
and the registrations and exemptions a firm needs before it trades.

\section{Exchanges and clearing for commodity futures}

Commodity futures clear like other futures (One Quant Book 1, chapters 5 and 30): a firm either
becomes a clearing member of the clearing house, which few new firms can, or trades through a
clearing member (in the United States, a futures commission merchant) that stands between it and
the clearing house and posts its margin.

\begin{definition}[Designated contract market]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:dcm}
A \emph{designated contract market}\index{designated contract market} (DCM) is a board of trade
registered with the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission under section 5 of the Commodity Exchange
Act, which may list futures and options on any commodity for all types of traders, including retail.
\end{definition}

Access to a commodity exchange is the same as to any futures exchange, with two additions: position
limits and the physical delivery at expiry. A firm that holds positions near expiry must either be able
to make or take delivery (a pipeline account at Cushing, a warehouse account, a \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:group}{balancing group}) or close
before the delivery period; its clearing member will insist on one or the other.

\begin{definition}[Accountability level]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:accountability}
An \emph{accountability level}\index{accountability level} is a position size set by an exchange above
which a trader must, on request, explain its position and its purpose, and may be told not to increase
it; unlike a position limit (One Quant Book 1, chapter 27), it is not a hard cap.
\end{definition}

\begin{dated}{2026-09}{US position limits}\label{dat:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:limits}
The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's position-limits rule, published on 14 January 2021 and effective
on 15 March 2021, sets federal spot-month limits (at or below 25\% of deliverable supply) on 25 core
referenced futures contracts in agricultural, metal and energy commodities and their economically
equivalent swaps; exchanges set limits or \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:accountability}{accountability levels} on other contracts and outside the spot month.
\end{dated}

\section{The metal exchange's membership categories}

The London Metal Exchange is the one commodity exchange whose membership categories a trader must know
by name, because they decide who may trade in its open-outcry ring, who may issue its contracts and who may
deal with clients.

\begin{definition}[Ring dealing member]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:ring}
A \emph{ring dealing member}\index{ring dealing member} (Category 1) of the London Metal Exchange may trade
in the Ring, where the official prices are set, issue LME contracts to clients and clear them.
\end{definition}

\section{Balancing groups and nominations}

Physical power and gas are delivered through networks, and the network operator must know, for every
\omterm{def:m3:power-markets-design:mtu}{market time unit}, who will put energy in and who will take it out.

\begin{definition}[Balancing group, schedule nomination]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:group}
A \emph{balancing group}\index{balancing group} is a virtual account with a transmission system operator
that aggregates a set of injection and withdrawal points and the group's trades with other groups, and whose
net position the operator settles as the group's imbalance; its \omterm{def:m3:power-markets-trading:brp}{balancing responsible party}
(\cref{ch:m3:power-markets-trading}) signs the contract. A \emph{schedule nomination}\index{schedule
nomination} is the group's declaration to the operator, before a deadline, of its planned injections,
withdrawals and trades with other groups for each \omterm{def:m3:power-markets-design:mtu}{market time unit}.
\end{definition}

\begin{method}[Building a balanced nomination]\label{met:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:nominate}
For each MTU: (i) sum the group's forecast injections and withdrawals; (ii) add every trade with another
group, positive for purchases; (iii) check that the net is zero, and trade (intraday) or adjust forecasts
until it is; (iv) check that every trade matches the counterparty group's opposite nomination; (v) submit
before the deadline, and resubmit as intraday trades change the schedule.
\end{method}

\begin{dated}{2026-09}{Germany's standard balancing group contract}\label{dat:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:bkv}
The Bundesnetzagentur's standard contract (version BK6-23-102 of 23 November 2023) makes the group's manager responsible for a balanced
quarter-hourly account. Day-ahead schedules reach the transmission operator by 14:30 the day before; within Germany, intraday schedules can
change up to a quarter-hour before delivery, and may be temporarily unbalanced by up to 10\% of the group's declared maximum export, at most
50~MW in the last two hours.
\end{dated}

\section{Physical logistics: credit lines, letters of credit and trade finance}

Physical commodities are paid for after delivery, often thirty days after the \omterm{def:m3:physical-commodity-markets:bl}{bill of lading}
(\cref{ch:m3:physical-commodity-markets}). Between delivery and payment the seller is a lender to the buyer.

\begin{definition}[Letter of credit]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}
A \emph{letter of credit}\index{letter of credit} is an undertaking by a bank, issued at the request of a
buyer, to pay the seller a stated amount when the seller presents documents that conform to its terms (the
\omterm{def:m3:physical-commodity-markets:bl}{bill of lading}, invoices, inspection certificates): it replaces the buyer's credit by the bank's.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trade finance]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:tf}
\emph{Trade finance}\index{trade finance} is the bank credit that funds physical commodity trade: letters of
credit, borrowing against specific cargoes or inventory, and the credit facilities from which trading houses
draw them.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[What a letter of credit must cover]\label{ex:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}
A seller has delivered EUR~3 million of energy not yet paid, and the remaining deliveries under its contract
would cost EUR~1 million less to replace today than their contract price, so the buyer's default would cost
the seller nothing on them. With a \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}{letter of credit} of EUR~2 million, EUR~1 million is uncovered. Had the
replacement cost been EUR~0.5 million more than the contract price, the uncovered exposure would be
EUR~1.5 million.
\end{example}

\section{Licences and registrations before the first trade}

A paper trader that moves into physical energy meets regulation that applies to the commodity, not to the
instrument.

\begin{definition}[REMIT]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}
\emph{REMIT}\index{REMIT} is the European Union's regulation on wholesale energy market integrity and
transparency (Regulation (EU) No 1227/2011): it prohibits insider trading and manipulation in wholesale
electricity and gas markets and requires market participants to register with a national regulator, which
gives them an identifier (the ACER code), and to report their transactions and inside information.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ancillary activity exemption]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:aae}
The \emph{ancillary activity exemption}\index{ancillary activity exemption} lets a firm deal on its own
account in commodity derivatives or \omterm{def:m3:emissions-and-environmental-markets:cap}{emission allowances} without authorisation as an investment firm under
the European Union's markets-in-financial-instruments rules, provided that the activity is ancillary to a
main business, at group level, that is neither investment services nor banking.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Market-based rate authorisation]\label{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:mbr}
\emph{Market-based rate authorisation}\index{market-based rate authorisation} is the permission by the US
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission for a seller to make wholesale sales of electric energy, capacity and
\omterm{def:m3:power-markets-design:capacity}{ancillary services} at negotiated prices, granted to sellers that show they and their affiliates lack, or have
mitigated, market power.
\end{definition}

\begin{dated}{2026-09}{European registrations}\label{dat:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}
\omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}{REMIT} II (Regulation (EU) 2024/1106) extends reporting to storage and balancing contracts and to products with
potential delivery in the Union, requires market participants established outside the Union to designate a
representative in it, and makes LNG data reporting permanent; its new implementing regulation applies from 29
April 2026. The criteria of the \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:aae}{ancillary activity exemption} are set by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/1833,
applicable since 9 November 2021, without an annual notification.
\end{dated}

\section{Build: nominations and physical credit}\label{bld:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:nominate}

\begin{build}
\bfield{Purpose} The miniature firm's physical power book must produce correct, balanced, matched nominations
before every deadline, and know how much of its physical credit is covered.
\bfield{Interface} \texttt{Nomination(group).add\_trade(mtu, counterparty, qty)}, \texttt{.net(mtu)},
\texttt{.unbalanced(mtus)}; \texttt{counterparty\_mismatches(a, b, mtus)}; \texttt{in\_time(submitted,
delivery\_day, deadline)}; \texttt{uncovered\_exposure(unpaid, forward\_mtm, lc)}; \texttt{carry\_cost}.
\bfield{Rules} A group cannot trade with itself; a nomination is balanced within a tolerance; matching is per
MTU and counterparty; exposure counts only positive replacement values.
\bfield{Acceptance tests} \texttt{code/firm/nominate/tests/}: an unbalanced MTU found; a mismatch found;
deadlines; uncovered exposure; the carry of collateral.
\bfield{Stretch} Build nominations from \texttt{firm.balancing} (\cref{ch:m3:power-markets-trading}); the
operator's message formats; gas nominations by \omterm{def:m3:natural-gas-and-lng:gasday}{gas day} (\cref{ch:m3:natural-gas-and-lng}).
\end{build}

\section{Problem: First Trade in German Power}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- what a new entrant posts before trading}]\label{pb:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:1}
A new firm will buy 50~MW of German baseload for next year on the exchange and sell the same power to an
industrial buyer under a physical contract, as a \omterm{def:m3:power-markets-trading:brp}{balancing responsible party}. Take the illustrative inputs of
the chapter: \qty{85}{EUR/MWh}, 12\% initial margin, a EUR~500{,}000 balancing-group security, a \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}{letter of credit}
covering two months of deliveries at 1.5\% a year, and a 5\% funding rate.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The position.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item What volume and notional does the firm trade?
\item What initial margin does the exchange leg require?
\item Why does the physical leg need a \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:group}{balancing group}?
\item What \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}{letter of credit} does the firm require from its buyer, and why two months?
\item Who pays for that \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}{letter of credit}?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The collateral.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Give the total collateral and credit tied up.
\item Give its annual carrying cost.
\item What happens to the margin if prices double?
\item Why is the \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:lc}{letter of credit} not collateral posted by the firm?
\item What else must be in place before the first nomination?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The regulation.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Which registration does \omterm{def:m3:getting-access-commodities-and-power:remit}{REMIT} require, and what does it give the firm?
\item Does the firm need authorisation as an investment firm?
\item What changes if the firm is established outside the Union?
\item Which transactions must it report?
\item What position rules apply to the exchange leg?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- Judgement.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Which route would you choose for a first year, and why?
\item Where is the firm's largest risk in the first month?
\item What would a bank ask before issuing credit lines to the firm?
\item State the \emph{named result}: the collateral and credit the firm ties up before its first trade, and its
annual carry.
\item In one sentence: what does access to a physical market cost?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
