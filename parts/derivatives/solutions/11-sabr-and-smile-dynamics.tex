\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics} --- SABR and Smile Dynamics}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:1}
$\beta=1$, $\nu=0$: a lognormal forward with constant volatility $\alpha$, a flat Black smile at $\alpha$. $\beta=0$,
$\nu=0$: the Bachelier model, a flat normal smile at $\alpha$, which in Black volatility is a skew falling roughly like
$\alpha/\sqrt{FK}$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:2}
$\alpha=0.2\times0.03^{1-\beta}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}(0.025)=\alpha\times0.025^{\beta-1}$: 24.0\% for $\beta=0$,
21.9\% for $\beta=0.5$, 20.0\% for $\beta=1$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:3}
Each expiry is fitted with its own parameters, so the model never has to describe how volatility behaves across
expiries: mean reversion, which shapes a term structure, has nothing to do.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:4}
$dF=\alpha F^\beta dW^1$ and $d\alpha=\nu\alpha dW^2$ with correlation $\rho$: $\Cov(d\alpha,dF)=\rho\nu\alpha^2F^\beta dt$
and $\Var(dF)=\alpha^2F^{2\beta}dt$, so the slope is $\rho\nu/F^\beta$. The option's sensitivity to $\alpha$ times that slope
is the part of its $\alpha$-risk hedgeable with the forward: $\mathcal V\,\partial_\alpha\sigma_B\,\rho\nu/F^\beta$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:5}
From the historical co-movement of at-the-money volatility and the forward: regress the change of $\ln\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}$
on the change of $\ln F$; the slope estimates $\beta-1$. A wrong $\beta$ is compensated by $\rho$ in the fit, so prices are
unchanged, but the deltas are wrong by the difference in \omterm{def:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:backbone}{backbone} slope times \omterm{def:dv:greeks-and-the-hedging-pnl:greeks}{vega}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:6}
70.8, 59.9 and 55.0 basis points a year, against 28.8\%, 20.0\% and 15.8\% in Black volatility. A normal volatility is
roughly the Black volatility times $\sqrt{FK}$; much of the fall of the Black smile with the strike is the lognormal model's
own $1/\sqrt{FK}$ scaling, which the normal quote removes.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:7}
The minimum-variance and Black deltas give almost the same error (variance ratio 0.999); Hagan's is 10\% worse. With a
positive correlation the \omterm{def:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:backbone}{backbone} and the volatility's co-move cancel nearly exactly at the money.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:8}
The fit is to quoted strikes and expiries; a ten-year 1\% strike is outside them, where Hagan's formula can produce a
negative density (below about 1.6\% in the chapter's ten-year example), so its price there is not even arbitrage-free.
Use the arbitrage-free density or a shifted model, and a reserve for the extrapolation.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:1}
$dF=\alpha F^\beta dW^1$, $d\alpha=\nu\alpha dW^2$, correlation $\rho$. $\alpha$: level; $\beta$: \omterm{def:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:backbone}{backbone} (how at-the-money
volatility moves with $F$); $\rho$: skew around the \omterm{def:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:backbone}{backbone}; $\nu$: curvature of the wings.
\iqlookfor{the roles, and that $\beta$ is about dynamics.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:2}
The at-the-money volatility as a function of the forward, $\approx\alpha F^{\beta-1}$. Regress changes of
$\ln\sigma_{\mathrm{ATM}}$ on changes of $\ln F$ over a period of stable regime; the slope is $\beta-1$.
\iqlookfor{the definition and an estimation procedure.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:3}
$V(S,v)$; $dV=V_SdS+V_vdv+\dots$; regress $dv$ on $dS$: slope $\Cov(dv,dS)/\Var(dS)$; hedge $V_S+V_v\times$ slope. The
remaining term is uncorrelated with $dS$.
\iqlookfor{the regression argument.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:4}
\omterm{def:dv:local-volatility:model}{Local volatility} predicts that the smile moves opposite to the forward, while the rates market's smile moved with it; the
model's delta therefore had the wrong correction and its hedges could be worse than Black's.
\iqlookfor{dynamics in the wrong direction.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:5}
Hagan's formula is an asymptotic expansion; far from the money and at long expiries it implies non-convex call prices.
Detect it by computing the density, and price there with the arbitrage-free SABR density (a one-dimensional forward equation),
a shifted model, or a separate wing extrapolation.
\iqlookfor{the expansion's domain and a fix.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:dv:sabr-and-smile-dynamics:6}
Trades whose value depends on how the smile moves with the forward: delta-hedged options (through the delta), forward-start
options and spread options between tenors. Hedge by computing risks under several $(\beta,\rho)$ pairs fitted to the same smile
and holding a reserve for the spread of their P\&Ls, or by hedging \omterm{def:dv:greeks-and-the-hedging-pnl:greeks}{vega} by strike (bucketed) so that the model's dynamics matter
less.
\iqlookfor{dynamics-sensitive products and model-uncertainty management.}
\end{solution}
