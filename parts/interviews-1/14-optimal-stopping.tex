\chapter{Optimal Stopping}\label{ch:iv1:optimal-stopping}

\begin{ivtrains}
Deciding when to stop, by working backwards from the last decision. A threshold is the value of continuing, and most of these questions come down to finding that one number. The later ones ask what information is worth, and when no stopping rule can help at all.
\end{ivtrains}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:1}
You roll a fair die and are paid its value in dollars, but you may reject the roll and roll again, taking the new value. With one possible re-roll, when should you keep and what is the game worth? And with two possible re-rolls?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:2}
You draw a uniform number on $[0, 1]$ and may keep it or reject it for a new draw. What is the game worth with one possible rejection? With two? With three? What is the threshold at each stage?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:3}
A bag holds 100 tickets, numbered 1 to 100. You draw one and see it. You may keep it and be paid its number in dollars, or you may draw a second ticket from the remaining 99 and be paid that instead. What should you do, and what is the game worth?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:4}
You turn over the cards of a shuffled standard deck, one by one. At any moment you may say ``stop'', and you win if the next card is red; if you never speak you are forced to bet on the last card. What rule gives you the best chance of winning?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, DS} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:5}
You interview $n$ candidates in random order. After each interview you can compare the candidate only with those already seen, and you must hire on the spot or reject for good. You win only by hiring the best of all $n$. Find the best rule and the win chance for $n = 3$, then the best rule for $n = 10$, then the limit for large $n$.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:6}
You may draw uniform numbers on $[0, 1]$ as many times as you like, paying $0.02$ for each draw, the first included, and you keep the last number drawn. When do you stop, how many draws do you make on average, and what is the game worth? What happens as the cost rises and as it falls to 0?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:7}
You roll a fair 20-sided die for free, and you are paid the number shown. You may re-roll as often as you like, paying \$1 for each re-roll, and only the last number counts. When do you stop, and what is the game worth?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst, DS} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:8}
You are selling a block of stock. Each day one offer arrives, uniform on $[0, 1]$, and a rejected offer is gone. Money received a day later is worth $0.8$ times as much. Which offers do you accept, how long does it take on average, and what is the block worth just before the first offer arrives?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:9}
You roll a fair die repeatedly and add up the results. You may stop at any time and take the total in dollars, but if you roll a 6, the game ends and you get nothing. When should you stop? About what is the game worth?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:10}
A fair coin is flipped up to 4 times, and you may stop after any flip, including before the first. You are paid heads minus tails so far, which may be negative. What is the best rule, and what is it worth? How does the answer change if you are paid heads minus tails or nothing, whichever is greater?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:11}
A trade costs 40 and pays 100 if a deal closes, which you believe has a 50\% chance, and nothing otherwise. A research report will say ``yes'' or ``no'', and it is right 80\% of the time whichever way the truth lies. What is the most you would pay for this report? What would you pay for a perfect report?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, DS, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:12}
Coin A has a known heads chance of $1/2$, and coin B has an unknown heads chance, uniform on $[0, 1]$. You flip one coin at a time, choosing each time after seeing past results, and you are paid \$1 per head. With two flips, which do you flip first, and what is the game worth? Why flip a coin that has the same average chance as the other?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:13}
You draw uniform numbers on $[0, 1]$ and add them up. You may stop at any time and take the total, but if the total goes over 1 you get nothing. When should you stop, and what is the game worth at the start?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:14}
A deck is dealt face up, one card after another. Each red card pays you \$1, each black card costs you \$1, and you may stop at any time. Work out the value of a deck of 2 red and 2 black by hand, and describe the method for a full deck of 26 and 26. About how much is the full deck worth?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:15}
You drive along a long straight street towards a restaurant. Parking spaces lie one unit apart along the whole street, one of them exactly at the restaurant, and each is free with probability $0.1$, independently. You see whether a space is free only when you reach it, and you cannot turn back. If you park $x$ units from the restaurant, before it or past it, you walk $x$ units. Which free spaces do you take, and what is your expected walk? What is the rule for a general chance $p$ that a space is free?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, DS, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:16}
A coin has an unknown heads chance, uniform on $[0, 1]$. You will place one bet on the next flip, at even money and for \$1, on the side of your choice. Before that, you may see $n$ free flips. What is your expected profit for $n = 0$, 1, 2 and 3? What does the second flip add, and what is the limit as $n$ grows?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{market maker, hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:17}
Player A draws a uniform number on $[0, 1]$ and may reject it once for a new draw, which must then be kept. Player B sees A's final number, and then does the same, with one possible rejection. The higher number wins. When should A keep the first draw, and what is A's chance of winning?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, DS} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:18}
There are 30 independent trials in order, each a success with chance $0.1$. You watch them one at a time, and you may stop once, at a success. You win if that success turns out to be the last of the 30. Which rule is best, and what is the win chance? How is this linked to hiring the best of $n$ candidates?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:19}
You flip a fair coin as many times as you like, and when you stop you are paid the fraction of flips that were heads. There is no cost and no limit. Show that the game is worth at least $3/4$. Find what the rule ``stop the first time heads lead'' is worth in closed form. Would you ever stop with heads level with tails, or even behind?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:optimal-stopping:20}
Independent non-negative prizes $X_1, \dots, X_n$ with known distributions are shown in order. You can take a prize when it is shown, or lose it for good. A prophet, who sees all of them in advance, earns $E[\max X_i]$. Show that a single fixed threshold lets you earn at least half of this, whatever the distributions, and show by example that you cannot guarantee more than a half.
\end{question}
