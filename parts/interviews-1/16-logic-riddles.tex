\chapter{Logic Riddles}\label{ch:iv1:logic-riddles}

\begin{ivtrains}
Finding the one idea that makes a puzzle short. A weighing gives three outcomes, a bit encodes a yes or a no, a colouring or a parity is an invariant that no move can change, and what other people know is information. In an interview, say the idea first, then check it on a small case.
\end{ivtrains}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:1}
You have 8 identical-looking balls, one of them slightly heavier, and a balance with two pans. How many weighings do you need to find the heavy ball for certain, and how? How many balls could you handle with 4 weighings?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:2}
You have two ropes and a lighter. Each rope takes exactly 60 minutes to burn from one end to the other, but it burns unevenly, so half of a rope need not take 30 minutes. How do you measure exactly 45 minutes?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:3}
You have a 4-minute sand timer and a 7-minute sand timer, and you can turn either over at the moment when one of them runs out. How do you measure exactly 9 minutes, starting both at time 0?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:4}
There are 10 bags of coins. In nine of them each coin weighs 10 grams, and in one of them each coin weighs 11 grams. You have an accurate scale that gives a number, and you may use it once. How do you find the bag?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:5}
You must take exactly one pill of type A and one of type B each day. The pills look identical, and they are expensive. Today you tipped out one A and then, by accident, two B into your hand, and you cannot tell the three apart. How do you get today's correct dose without wasting a pill?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:6}
A gold chain has 7 links in a row. You must pay a worker one link's worth of gold at the end of each of 7 days, and you may take back earlier pieces as change. Cutting a link frees it as a single piece and splits the chain on either side of it. What is the fewest links you must cut, and which? With 2 cuts, what is the longest chain that you could pay out in this way, one link a day?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:7}
Two opposite corner squares are removed from an 8 by 8 chessboard. Can the remaining 62 squares be covered by 31 dominoes, each covering two neighbouring squares? What if you remove two squares of opposite colours instead, anywhere on the board?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:8}
Two people will each get a black or white hat at random. Each sees the other's hat only, and they guess their own hat colour at the same moment, with no signal. They may agree on a plan first. They win if at least one guess is right. Find a plan that always wins.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:9}
Three switches outside a closed room control three ordinary filament bulbs inside. You may set the switches as you wish, then enter the room once, after which you cannot touch them. How do you tell which switch controls which bulb?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:10}
Three boxes are labelled ``apples'', ``oranges'' and ``mixed'', and you are told that every label is wrong. You may take one fruit from one box of your choice without looking inside. Which box do you choose, and how do you then relabel all three?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:11}
Four people must cross a bridge at night with one torch. At most two can cross at a time, and any crossing needs the torch, which must be walked back. They take 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes to cross, and a pair moves at the slower one's pace. What is the shortest total time, and what is the plan?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:12}
You have a 3-litre jug, a 5-litre jug and plenty of water, and no other marks. How do you measure exactly 4 litres? Could you measure 3 litres with a 6-litre jug and a 10-litre jug? State the general rule.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:13}
On an 8 by 8 grid, one cell is black and all the others are white. A move flips all the cells of a row or all the cells of a column. Can you reach an all-white grid? What is the invariant?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:14}
A hundred people stand in a line, each with a random black or white hat, and each sees all the hats in front only. From the back, each in turn says ``black'' or ``white'', and everybody hears. They may agree on a plan first. How many can be sure of being right, and how?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD, Dev} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:15}
One of 1000 bottles of wine has been poisoned. A taster shows the effect exactly 24 hours after any sip, however small, and you must know the bottle in 24 hours. How few tasters do you need? How few would you need if you had 48 hours, and could use a second round with those still unaffected?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:16}
A table holds 100 coins, of which you are told exactly 10 show heads. You are blindfolded, and you cannot feel which side is up. Split the coins into two groups, flipping any coins that you choose, so that both groups have the same number of heads.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:17}
Can a knight start at one corner of an 8 by 8 chessboard, visit every square exactly once, and finish at the opposite corner? Give the argument. Could it finish at some other square, in principle?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:18}
One square is removed from a $2^n$ by $2^n$ board, any square at all. Show that the rest can be covered by L-shaped pieces of three squares. How many pieces are used on an 8 by 8 board?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:19}
An island has 13 red, 15 green and 17 blue chameleons. When two of different colours meet, both change to the third colour. Could all the chameleons ever be the same colour? What if the counts were 13, 16 and 16?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:20}
The numbers 1 to 10 are on a board. A move wipes out any two numbers $a$ and $b$ and writes $|a - b|$. After nine moves, one number is left. Can it be 0? Separately, if each move instead writes $a + b + ab$, what is the last number, and does the order matter?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD, Dev} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:21}
You have 100 distinct numbers. How many comparisons do you need to find both the largest and the smallest, if done cleverly, rather than the $198$ of two separate scans? Give the method, and the formula for $n$ numbers.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:22}
A census taker asks a woman the ages of her three daughters, as whole numbers. She says the product of the ages is 36, and the sum is the house number next door. The taker looks at that number and still cannot tell. She adds that the eldest plays the piano. What are the ages, and what must the house number be?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:23}
Three perfect logicians stand in a line, each seeing the hats in front. The hats were taken from a box of 3 red and 2 white. The one at the back says, ``I do not know my colour.'' The one in the middle says, ``I do not know either.'' The one at the front, who sees nobody, says, ``I know mine.'' What colour, and how?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:24}
There are 10 stacks of coins. Each stack is either all genuine, at 10 grams a coin, or all fake, at 11 grams a coin, and any number of stacks may be fake. You may use an accurate scale once. How do you find all the fake stacks? How many coins do you need in all?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:25}
You stand at a fork, with one road leading to safety and the other not. Two guards know the road. One always tells the truth and the other always lies, and you do not know which is which. With one yes-or-no question to one guard, how do you find the safe road? Show that your question works in all four cases.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Analyst} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:26}
A notebook has 100 numbered statements. Statement $k$ says, ``Exactly $k$ of the statements in this notebook are false.'' Which statements are true? Would your answer change if it said ``at least $k$''?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD, Trader} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:27}
You have 12 balls, of which exactly one is odd: heavier or lighter, you do not know which. With a balance, find the odd ball and whether it is heavy or light, in 3 weighings. Why is 13 balls impossible in this form, even though $3^3 = 27$ outcomes appear to be enough? Give a scheme of weighings fixed in advance.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:28}
Can a 10 by 10 board be covered exactly by straight 1 by 4 bricks? What about a 4 by 10 board, and what is the general rule for an $m$ by $n$ board with 1 by 4 bricks? Give the colouring that decides it.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD, Dev} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:29}
How many comparisons are needed to find the second-largest of 64 distinct numbers, compared with two separate scans? Give the method, and the formula for $n$ numbers. Why must every number but two lose a comparison to something other than the largest?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:30}
A group of $n$ people each get a hat, with any of $n$ colours, and repeats allowed. Each sees all the hats except their own, and they all guess at the same moment. They may agree on a plan first. Find a plan that guarantees that exactly one person is right, whatever the hats are.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:31}
Three people each get a random black or white hat. Each sees the two other hats, and at the same moment they guess their own colour or pass. The group wins if at least one guesses and all guesses are right. They may agree on a plan first. What win chance can they reach, and why does it not break the rule that each guess is right half of the time?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:32}
On an island live 100 people with blue eyes and 100 with brown, and nobody knows that those are the only colours possible. No one may discuss eye colour, there are no mirrors, and anyone who works out their own eye colour leaves at midnight of that day. A visitor announces to all, ``At least one of you has blue eyes.'' What happens? What new information did the visitor give, when everybody could already see blue eyes?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, DS, QD} \iqfirm{any} \iqlevel{junior, experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:33}
A hundred prisoners may plan once and are then kept apart. Each day, one prisoner chosen at random visits a room with a light switch, which starts off, and may toggle it. Anyone may declare ``all of us have visited''. If this is right they go free, and if it is wrong they all stay. Find a plan that is certain to work. How long does it take on average?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, QD} \iqfirm{hedge fund, market maker} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:34}
A chessboard has a coin on each of its 64 squares, heads or tails, in an arrangement chosen by a hostile jailer, who also points to one square. Prisoner A sees all this and must flip exactly one coin. Prisoner B then enters, sees only the coins, and must name the square the jailer chose. They may plan before. Find the plan, and say what is special about 64.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[$\star\star\star\star$ \iqroles{QR, QT, Trader} \iqfirm{hedge fund} \iqlevel{experienced}]\label{iq:iv1:logic-riddles:35}
A member of the audience picks 5 cards from a standard deck and hands them to an assistant. The assistant returns one of them to the audience, and lays the other 4 in a row, face up, in an order of the assistant's choice. A magician, who has seen nothing else, names the hidden card. How does the system work? Do a counting argument to show how far above 52 it could go.
\end{question}
