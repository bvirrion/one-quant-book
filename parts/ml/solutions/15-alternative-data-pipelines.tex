\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:ml:alternative-data-pipelines} --- Alternative-Data Pipelines}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:1}
The ratio is about 100, far outside $[0.5, 2]$: the delivery is held for review. A factor of 100 points to a change of
units (cents for dollars); other causes of a large ratio are a file delivered twice, a new partner's data added, or
cumulative totals sent instead of increments.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:2}
Weights $\begin{pmatrix}1.18 & 0.72\\ 1.45 & 0.89\end{pmatrix}$ (to two decimals), weighted counts
$\begin{pmatrix}35.5 & 14.5\\ 14.5 & 35.5\end{pmatrix}$: every row and column sums to 50. Raking matches the margins, not
the cells; many weightings do, and raking picks the one closest to the starting weights in a relative-entropy sense.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:3}
``BOREALIS 5120'' normalises to ``borealis'', which matches the first token of three retailers' names equally (Borealis
Retail, Stores and Outfitters) and none of their second tokens: score 0.79 for each, a tie in the review band. ``BOREALI RETA
STR 313'' normalises to ``boreali reta'', whose tokens are prefixes of ``borealis'' and ``retail'': score 1 for Borealis
Retail alone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:4}
Year-on-year growth compares a quarter with the same quarter a year earlier. For quarters 16 to 19 the current value comes
from the new panel and the earlier one from the old, so the change in who is in the panel is read as a change in sales, by
an amount that depends on each retailer's customers. From quarter 20 both sides come from the new panel, and the error
returns to the level of a stable panel (2.7 points).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:5}
It was delivered at launch one quarter after it ended, exactly like a live quarter, so its timestamps are indistinguishable
from live data. It did not matter because the vendor could not adjust it: the retailers had not yet reported its sales. It
would matter if the vendor had built it with any later information (a merchant map fitted afterwards, a panel cleaned of
later \omterm{def:ml:neural-networks-for-noisy-tabular-data:dropout}{dropouts}), which only the vendor's documentation or a comparison of vintages can reveal.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:6}
Repairing hides the vendor's problem and guesses the fix: a negative spend may be a refund, a sign error or a corrupted
row, and its absolute value is only one guess. Reject, count by reason, report to the vendor, and repair only with a rule
the vendor confirms, recorded with the data.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:7}
The raked error rises from 2.1 to 19.7 points on average. The dropped strings carried 18.4\% of the retailers' delivered
spend, but in a share that changes from quarter to quarter (each cell's spend is split at random across two or three of a
retailer's strings), so dropping them adds noise to every growth rate rather than a constant bias. Retailers and quarters
where the stem-only string happened to carry more spend suffer most.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:8}
Let the panel's cell counts be $n_{ar} = N u_a v_r$ and the population's shares $p_{ar} = \alpha_a\beta_r$. The weights
$w_{ar} = (\alpha_a/u_a)(\beta_r/v_r)$ give weighted counts $N\alpha_a\beta_r$, which match both margins, and raking's first
two steps reach them: the row step multiplies by $\alpha_a/u_a$, after which the column sums are $N\alpha_\cdot v_r$ and the
column step multiplies by $\beta_r/v_r$. These are the post-stratification weights $p_{ar}/(n_{ar}/N)$. When the population
has an interaction ($p_{ar}\ne\alpha_a\beta_r$), raking still matches the margins but not the cells, and a statistic that
depends on the interaction stays biased.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Missing fields, negative spends, panel counts as text, duplicated rows, and one delivery in cents. Validation rejects
121, 69, 64 and 111 rows, every planted defect.
\item The delivery in cents: every row is valid. The delivery check against the previous total holds it.
\item A repair is a guess about the vendor's error; rejection keeps the data honest and makes the vendor fix it.
\item The value with its valid time and the delivery time as knowledge time, so that every query can be asked as of a date
and backfill can be seen.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Exact matching on the normalised name: 300 of 600. The resolver: 500 matched automatically, all correctly, 100 to
review, none unmatched.
\item The strings with only a first word that up to three retailers share: a machine cannot tell them apart and a person
with store lists can.
\item None is matched; six go to review, four are rejected.
\item A wrong match moves spend between companies silently and biases both nowcasts; a string in review delays data and
costs a person's time.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Raw: 3.3 points (quarters 4--15), 22.5 (16--19), 2.7 (20--23). Raked: 1.7, 2.7, 2.6.
\item That population margins are known, that the panel covers every cell, and that within a cell panelists spend like the
population. The residual is sampling noise in small cells and any within-cell difference.
\item Quarters 0 to 10: their first-seen time is quarter 12, more than a quarter after they ended.
\item Raked: 0.480 backfilled, 0.419 live before the change, 0.412 after. Raw: 0.366, 0.391, 0.264.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{The panel that grew.} In the year after the partner change the raw nowcast misses growth by 22.5 points on
average, the raked one by 2.7; the backfilled history's IC is 0.480 against 0.419 live.
\item The panel's composition by period and the margins it is weighted to; partner and methodology changes with dates;
delivery timestamps for all history; the merchant map and its changes.
\item A break in composition (panel counts by cell), in the relation of panel totals to reported sales (Book~7's CUSUM on
residuals), and a jump in the share of new merchant strings.
\item Delivery checks, rejection rates, the review queue, panel composition, nowcast residuals against reported sales, and
the live IC.
\item None of the difference from live; treat backfilled history as an upper bound and plan on the live number.
\item On the review queue and on held deliveries: where the machine's uncertainty is concentrated and a person's knowledge
is cheapest.
\item The same stages with other entities (sites, vessels, apps) and other biases (cloud cover, device mix, store
openings); the composition question and the first-seen question do not go away.
\item That the panel still represents the population it represented when the model was fitted.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:1}
History delivered after the fact, often built with information unavailable at the time (a merchant map, calibration to
reported numbers). Detect it by recording \omterm{def:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:firstseen}{first-seen timestamps} at ingestion, asking the vendor for delivery dates and
vintages, and comparing the history's statistics with the live period's.
\iqlookfor{the definition, \omterm{def:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:firstseen}{first-seen timestamps}, and live-versus-backfill comparison.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:2}
Schema (fields, types, ranges, uniqueness), delivery-level checks (row counts, totals against the last delivery, coverage,
arrival time), and referential checks (unknown entities). On failure: reject rows with reasons, hold the delivery, alert,
never repair silently; store with knowledge times.
\iqlookfor{row and delivery checks, and a failure policy with no silent repair.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:3}
Normalise descriptors, block candidates by tokens, score by token and prefix similarity (or a trained matcher), accept
above a threshold, review a band, reject below; keep the mapping point in time. Measure precision and recall on a labelled
sample, stratified by descriptor pattern, and track the review queue and new-string rates.
\iqlookfor{thresholds with review, point-in-time mapping, measured precision.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:4}
Reweight the panel to population margins (raking or post-stratification) by age and other known dimensions. It fails when
cells are empty or tiny, when margins are unknown or stale, when panelists differ from the population within cells, and
when composition changes break year-on-year comparisons.
\iqlookfor{reweighting and its assumptions, including within-cell differences.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:5}
The data before the model: a change of partner or methodology, panel composition, the merchant map, delivery timing; then
the retailer (a new channel the panel does not see, such as online sales through another processor); only then the model.
\iqlookfor{panel and mapping changes before model changes.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ml:alternative-data-pipelines:6}
Split the trial at launch: the two live years are the evidence, with their standard error; the six backfilled years are an
upper bound. Check the vendor's backfill method, compare vintages, and shrink the estimate towards the live IC; expect further
decay as others buy the data (Book~8, chapter~16).
\iqlookfor{live-only estimation, skepticism of backfill, and expected decay.}
\end{solution}
