\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:1}
At \$2.3 million the credit support amount is \$0.3 million, below the minimum transfer amount: nothing. At \$10.37 million it is \$8.37 million,
rounded up to a call of \$8.4 million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:2}
An \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod}{event of default} is attributable to one party and allows termination of all transactions; a \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod}{termination event} is no one's fault and affects the
transactions it concerns. A \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:ate}{NAV trigger} is an \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:ate}{additional termination event}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:4}
The swap was in its counterparty's favour: terminating would have crystallised a payment, and withholding under section 2(a)(iii) deferred it.
The court held that the safe harbours protect termination and netting, not waiting; that a year of riding the market was unacceptable; that
Metavante had waived its right to terminate; and that it must perform.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:5}
That the payment obligation is suspended, not extinguished, for as long as the \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod}{event of default} continues, with no time limit, and that the clause
does not offend the anti-deprivation principle. Under the New York ruling, a non-defaulting party that waits too long loses the right to terminate
and must pay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:6}

million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:7}
Prime broker 2 now trips on day 151 on a 20\% fall over 63 days, but dealer B's trigger still trips first, on day 137.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:legal-documentation:8}
Triggers are measured over windows: a 12\% fall in a month tripped dealer B's while the fund was 17.5\% below its peak, and the tightest trigger,
not the average, decides when the first counterparty may terminate; cross-default can then spread it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:legal-documentation:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item All confirmations form one agreement, so on default every transaction is terminated and netted to a single amount, which prevents
cherry-picking.
\item See \cref{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod,def:fm:legal-documentation:ate}.
\item See \cref{def:fm:legal-documentation:ate,def:fm:legal-documentation:flawed}.
\item Each payment is subject to the condition precedent that no \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod}{event of default} or potential \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod}{event of default} with respect to the other party
has occurred and is continuing.
\item See \cref{exo:fm:legal-documentation:4}.
\item See \cref{exo:fm:legal-documentation:5}.
\item Whether to terminate, and on what date; promptly where US bankruptcy law applies.
\item Their qualified financial contracts must accept US resolution transfers and stays, and may not allow default rights triggered by an
affiliate's insolvency.
\item \$447.7 million on day 45, \$247.7 million on day 204, 44.7\%.
\item Dealer B day 137 (12\% in 21 days), prime broker 1 day 150 (15\% in 21 days), prime broker 2 day 177 (30\% in 63 days), dealer A day 180
(floor of \$280 million).
\item Dealer A 44 calls and 43 returns, dealer B 19 and 20, dealer C none: its threshold exceeds its exposure.
\item \$3.4 million.
\item See \cref{tab:fm:legal-documentation:closeout}: \$1.36 million owed to the fund with set-off; without, the fund pays \$4.64 million and claims

\item See \cref{def:fm:legal-documentation:pba}; margin terms and lock-up, rehypothecation, set-off and the \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:ate}{NAV triggers}.
\item Dealer B's 12\% monthly trigger, the tightest; then the prime brokers', since their trips stop financing.
\item A trip that is an \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:eod}{event of default} at one counterparty can become one at others, so the first trip can terminate all of them.

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:legal-documentation:1}
The smallest amount that is transferred; it avoids daily transfers of trivial amounts, at the cost of an uncollateralised gap up to that size.
\iqlookfor{operational cost against exposure.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:legal-documentation:2}
An \omterm{def:fm:legal-documentation:ate}{additional termination event} on a fall in the fund's NAV; it lets the counterparty exit before a shrinking fund cannot pay.
\iqlookfor{early exit from a deteriorating credit.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:legal-documentation:3}
Credit support amount $\max(E+IA-\text{threshold},0)$ less the haircut value held; zero below the MTA; round calls up and returns down. Edge cases:
ineligible collateral, exposure changing sign, a return that crosses the MTA only after rounding.
\iqlookfor{the order of operations and the sign conventions.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:legal-documentation:4}
Terminate and close out with netting and set-off, or suspend payments under section 2(a)(iii) where the governing law allows; in a US bankruptcy,
decide promptly.
\iqlookfor{the Metavante and Lomas contrast.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:legal-documentation:5}
With enforceable netting the exposure is the net of all trades under the agreement, not the sum of the positive ones, which lowers the exposure and
the capital.
\iqlookfor{net against gross exposure.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:legal-documentation:6}
Simulate NAV paths from the fund's return distribution, including fat tails and volatility clustering, and count the paths on which any window's
fall exceeds any trigger; include the effect of trips on funding.
\iqlookfor{path dependence and the tightest trigger.}
\end{solution}
