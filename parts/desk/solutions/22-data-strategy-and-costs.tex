\begin{solution}{exo:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:5}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Market power in benchmarks, ratings data and data vendors, and complex, multiple licences that add cost; a buyer has limited price leverage
and must negotiate terms.
\item Pre- and post-trade data on a reasonable commercial basis, non-discriminatory, free after fifteen minutes.
\item See \cref{def:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:budget}.
\item \$564\,000 market data, \$750\,000 terminals, \$460\,000 reference data and news, \$300\,000 alternative data: \$2.07 million.
\item See \cref{def:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:licences}.
\item 50 users.
\item It removes counting and the audit exposure for that product, and fixes the cost as headcount changes.
\item Definitions of use, derived-data rights, audit periods and interest, renewal caps.
\item See \cref{def:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:backbill}.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:audit}.
\item \$490\,000; \$146\,000 at 5\%; \$1.19 million at 30\%.
\item Reconcile the auditor's counts with the entitlement records, challenge what is not supported, negotiate interest and settlement, and fix the
counting.
\item Counts come from access actually granted, so under-reporting cannot arise from users' declarations.
\item By its expected contribution net of costs, decaying as others buy it; \$369\,000 a year over three years, against a \$300\,000 price.
\item \$933\,000 market making, \$622\,000 statistical arbitrage, \$311\,000 event-driven, \$207\,000 macro.
\item Which strategies' data costs exceed what the data earns them.
\item Products with unused entitlements and datasets whose trial contribution does not cover the fee.
\item Monthly against invoices and before every renewal.
\item 50 users; \$490\,000 including \$81\,000 of interest.
\item Count every licence from the entitlement system, buy enterprise terms where headcount exceeds the break-even and remove unused access before
renewals; price every dataset by what it earns and allocate the budget to the strategies that use it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:1}
Use by machines rather than people viewing a screen, such as algorithmic trading or risk calculation; exchanges charge for it separately, often
by category, and it is a large share of a trading firm's market-data fees.
\iqlookfor{machine use and separate fees.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:2}
Because above the break-even it is cheaper, and because it removes counting and audit exposure and lets headcount grow without new fees.
\iqlookfor{break-even and audit risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:3}
Trial it for incremental information over what the firm already has, estimate the return it adds net of costs and its decay, and compare the
break-even fee with the price.
\iqlookfor{incremental value and decay.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:4}
Grant access only through an entitlement system, log every grant and use by the provider's unit of count, reconcile with invoices monthly and keep
the records for the audit period.
\iqlookfor{counts from access, not declarations.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:5}
The unreported fees over the audit period plus interest (\cref{prop:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:audit}); verify, negotiate, pay, and fix the
counting.
\iqlookfor{the formula and the remediation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:data-strategy-and-costs:6}
By measured use (entitlements, messages, queries); it makes strategies weigh data costs against returns, and encourages dropping unused data,
but can also discourage sharing data that would benefit others.
\iqlookfor{allocation by use and its incentives.}
\end{solution}
