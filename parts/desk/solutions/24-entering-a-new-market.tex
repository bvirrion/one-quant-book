\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:1}
Clearing: $2+0.5+5+0.5=8$ months to limits, 9 with certification. Registration: $2+4+3=9$ months to membership, 10 with certification. The
registration chain is critical.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:2}
Market data licences (6 months) and connectivity (4 months): they can start later without delaying the first trade, which defers their costs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:3}
\$949\,000; the peak is \$170\,000 in month 5, when registration, connectivity, data, hires and software overlap.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:4}
Nine months; the clearing chain (entity, identifier, clearing agreement, limits, certification) becomes critical.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:5}
See the proof of \cref{prop:fm:entering-a-new-market:kill}. With noise, the firm sometimes stops a sound entry (losing its later profits) or
continues a weak one, so the kill point can lower the value if the threshold is set too high.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:6}
Stopped entries have already spent their set-up costs and some months of losses, so they still lose money, but much less than if they had run for
three years: the losses shrink rather than disappear.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:7}
The best threshold is \$250\,000, the running cost, adding \$200\,000; at \$400\,000 the rule stops almost half the entries and lowers the expected
value by \$138\,000.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:entering-a-new-market:8}
The critical path runs through registration and membership, which are two months longer; starting clearing first while registration waits delays
the first trade. Draw the network before choosing what to start.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:entering-a-new-market:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An edge that should transfer, or clients who trade there; Flow Traders' expansion into China, with ETP value traded in Asia up from
\euro114 billion to \euro152 billion.
\item See \cref{def:fm:entering-a-new-market:plan}.
\item Entity and identifier (Book 2), registration (chapter 17), clearing (Book 1, chapter 18), membership and certification (Book 13),
connectivity (Book 14), data (chapter 22), people (chapter 10).
\item Clearing after the identifier; certification after the clearing limits; membership after registration.
\item See \cref{def:fm:entering-a-new-market:cpm}.
\item See \cref{tab:fm:entering-a-new-market:tasks}; entity, registration, membership, certification: ten months.
\item Identifier, clearing agreement and limits 1 month; hires and software 2; connectivity 4; data 6: start them late.
\item \$949\,000, peaking at \$170\,000 in month 5.
\item $R(1-e^{-t/4})$ against \$250\,000 a month; $-\$3.55$ million, \$2.00 million and \$8.46 million.
\item See \cref{def:fm:entering-a-new-market:staged}.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:entering-a-new-market:kill}.
\item Mean \$2.52 million without, \$2.72 million with; 5th percentile $-\$3.57$ and $-\$1.92$ million; loss probability 38.6\% and 39.6\%.
\item Near the running cost, and written before entry.
\item Registration and clearing first; hires, connectivity and data as late as their slack allows.
\item The owners of the critical workstreams.
\item Progress on the critical path, the revenue estimate against the kill criterion, and the spending.
\item A faster, more reliable measure of the steady state, or a costlier running rate.
\item Uncertain durations, costs that depend on each other, competitors' response, and more than one kill point.
\item Ten months on the critical path; the kill point adds \$200\,000 to the expected NPV.
\item Start registration and clearing at once, the rest within their slack, for a first trade in ten months; stop after six months of trading if
estimated steady-state revenue is below the running cost.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:entering-a-new-market:1}
Earliest start of each task is the maximum finish of its predecessors; the project length is the largest finish; walk back to find latest
finishes and slack; the zero-slack tasks form the critical path.
\iqlookfor{forward and backward passes.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:entering-a-new-market:2}
A legal entity with an identifier, any required registration, a clearing arrangement with limits, membership or access, connectivity, market data
licences, certified software and staff.
\iqlookfor{the full checklist.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:entering-a-new-market:3}
As a real option: the expected value with the right to stop when an estimate falls below a threshold, minus the value without it, allowing for the
estimate's noise.
\iqlookfor{option value and noise.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:entering-a-new-market:4}
A revenue or capture level after a fixed period, on defined data, near the level at which continuing adds value; set before entry so that the
decision is not captured by sunk costs.
\iqlookfor{sunk-cost bias.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:entering-a-new-market:5}
Its edge may not transfer, set-up and running costs come before revenue, local competitors are established, and the ramp is slower than planned.
\iqlookfor{costs first, revenue uncertain.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:entering-a-new-market:6}
Simulate durations from distributions, recompute the critical path each time (it can change), and count the share of runs finishing by the date.
\iqlookfor{Monte Carlo over the network.}
\end{solution}
