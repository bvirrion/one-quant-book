\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:5}
A crystallised fee is never returned. Months of $+10\%$ and $-10\%$: monthly \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:class}{crystallisation} takes $0.2\times0.10=0.02$ of capital in the first
month; crystallised over the two months the fund ends at 0.99, below its mark, and pays nothing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:6}
Pro rata sells a slice of every bucket within a week, including the slow ones at a fire-sale cost, which the stayers bear without \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:gate}{swing pricing};
in exchange the remaining portfolio keeps its original shape and liquidity. Liquid first sells only cheap assets and leaves the rest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:7}
After the first 5\%: $(8-5)/95=3.16\%$ sellable within seven days. The second 5\% exceeds that and leaves nothing sellable within seven days.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:8}
Liquidity is a property of the pair and of the path: a first wave of redemptions met liquid first lowers the 60\% for the next; \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:side}{side letters} may
give some investors better terms; the 60\% may be an estimate from calm markets. Measure the ladder after a stressed redemption sequence, with
stressed selling costs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Feeders built for different investors invest in one master that holds the portfolio; the \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:gp}{general partner} manages and is liable, \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:gp}{limited
partners} contribute capital with limited liability.
\item The administrator computes NAV, keeps the register and computes fees; the \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:admin}{custodian} holds the assets. Independence stops the manager from
marking or using the assets itself.
\item See the definitions of the chapter.
\item A \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:ucits}{UCITS} redeems at any unit-holder's request and may suspend only temporarily and in exceptional cases.
\item Investors 9.0\%, portfolios 51.8\%.
\item No: the portfolios are more liquid than the investors' terms at every horizon; the ratio is smallest at 365 days (76.7 against 58.2).
\item 2.5\%, 2.2\% and 0.5\%.
\item Only 8\% of its assets could be sold within seven days against a four-day right to redeem; liquid assets had been sold disproportionately.
The 2025 decisions are referred to the Upper Tribunal and provisional; the corporate director settled with an up to £230 million redress scheme.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:liquidfirst}.
\item Liquid first: 0.04\% and 31.1\%. Pro rata: 5.39\% and 51.8\%.
\item Liquid first: 1.69\% and 0. Pro rata: 8.81\% and 8.0\%.
\item It pays 10\% and defers 20\%, leaving 46.4\% (hedge-fund ladder) and 0 (illiquid fund) sellable within seven days.
\item It puts the selling cost on the redeemers and removes the stayers' dilution; it does not create liquidity.
\item 51.8\% and 8\% of NAV.
\item Adjustments so that each investor pays performance fees on its own gain; B's free ride is $3.33-1.11=2.22$.
\item 11.54\%, 11.26\% and 10.78\% of starting capital over five years.
\item The side-letter investor can leave first and leave the others with a less liquid fund.
\item When investors may withdraw more within a period than the fund can sell within it at a normal cost.
\item Within seven days below a 1\% cost: 51.8\% for the Form PF ladder, 8\% for the illiquid fund; liquid-first dilution for a 30\% request
without \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:gate}{swing pricing}: 0.04\% and 1.69\% of the stayers' NAV.
\item Match the terms to the assets: longer notice, less frequent dealing or a gate in the documents, before any redemption arrives; and keep a
liquidity buffer instead of selling liquid assets first.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:1}
To pool investors with different tax positions in one portfolio; the master fund owns the portfolio, and the investors own the feeders.
\iqlookfor{the separation between the vehicle and the manager.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:2}
A cap on redemptions per dealing date; it prevents a run from forcing fire sales that would dilute those who stay.
\iqlookfor{the gate as protection for remaining investors.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:3}
With one share price and one mark, the new investor pays no performance fee until the mark is regained, although its own investment gained;
series accounting or \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:eq}{equalisation} charges it on its own gain.
\iqlookfor{one mark for many entry prices.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:4}
It leaves the stayers a less liquid fund, so the next redemption costs more and leaving early is rewarded
(\cref{prop:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:liquidfirst}).
\iqlookfor{the incentive to run.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:5}
It charges the cost of large net flows to the investors who cause them; it does not make the assets any easier to sell.
\iqlookfor{dilution against liquidity.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:6}
Compare, at each horizon, the share of NAV investors can withdraw (terms, gates, \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:side}{side letters}) with the share the portfolio can be sold within at
a stated cost, under stress; ask for the position-level liquidity ladder, the investor register by terms, and the \omterm{def:fm:the-asset-manager-and-the-fund:side}{side letters}.
\iqlookfor{both sides of the pair, and stress.}
\end{solution}
