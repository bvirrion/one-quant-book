\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:5}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:6}
Budget to date $60.5\times3/12=15.125$ against $55\times3/12=13.75$: \$1.4 million behind. The three-month standard deviation is
$42.2\sqrt{0.25}=21.1$: 0.065 standard deviations, noise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:7}
Budget 66: $\lambda=66/(55-0.674\times42.2)=2.49$; daily 99\% VaR $2.326\times2.49\times42.2/\sqrt{252}=15.4$, against 6.7 allowed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:8}
With a budget 10\% above expectation the desk misses with probability 55\% a year, and two years running with probability 30.5\% if the years are
independent. Two misses say little; compare revenue with expectation and the Sharpe ratio with its history.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:budget}.
\item 55, 42.2 and 1.30.
\item About \$20.5 million after costs and pay; 26\% that revenue falls short of the \$28 million of costs.
\item \$60.5 million.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:target}.
\item 44.8\% in closed form, 45.0\% in 20\,000 simulated years.
\item Its results cluster near the mean, so a target above the mean is further in units of its volatility.
\item \$30.0 million and \$50.4 million.
\item 2.28 times every position.
\item See \cref{def:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:appetite}; the upper bound is the firm's risk capacity.
\item $(55+20)/1.645=45.6$.
\item 6.7 a day; stops 8.8, 7.0 and 4.2.
\item By 8\% (45.6 against 42.2).
\item The appetite allows 45.6 of volatility; meeting the budget three years in four needs about 96.
\item See \cref{def:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:scope}; its pricing, booking and settlement are new and untested.
\item \$1.4 million, 0.065 standard deviations.
\item Add risk to catch up: the gap is noise, and adding risk after losses breaks the appetite.
\item The budget and the limits; the desk's Sharpe ratio is not a December decision.
\item $\Phi(S(1-k))$: 44.8\% at $S=1.3$, $k=1.1$; a risk scale of 2.28 to meet it with probability 75\%.
\item The budget is above the desk's expected revenue and so is met less than half the time on current limits; either set it at expectation, or
raise the limits by the factor it needs, which the firm's \omterm{def:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:appetite}{risk appetite} does not allow.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:1}
50\%, and $\Phi(-1)=15.9\%$ for a loss.
\iqlookfor{normal quantiles in Sharpe units.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:2}
A written statement of the losses and risks the firm accepts, in earnings, capital and liquidity; cascaded into volatility budgets, VaR limits and
stops by desk and business.
\iqlookfor{the top-down cascade.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:3}
At a Sharpe ratio of 1.3 the odds fall from 50\% to $\Phi(-0.26)=39.7\%$; ask for the limits the budget needs or a budget at expectation.
\iqlookfor{$\Phi(S(1-k))$ and the link between budget and risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:4}
When the gap is large relative to the volatility of revenue to date, which grows with the square root of time; a quarter in, most gaps are noise.
\iqlookfor{scaling the band with $\sqrt t$.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:5}
\omterm{def:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:scope}{New-product approval}: risk, product control, operations, legal, tax, compliance and technology review it and sign off, often with initial size
limits; its controls are the least tested.
\iqlookfor{the approval process and why.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-head-of-desks-job:6}
$P=\Phi(S(1-k))$ falls in $S$ when $k>1$ (a stretch budget) and rises when $k<1$ (a conservative one).
\iqlookfor{the sign of $1-k$.}
\end{solution}
