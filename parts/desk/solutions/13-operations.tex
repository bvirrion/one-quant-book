\begin{solution}{exo:fm:operations:5}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:operations:6}
With thirteen people few breaks wait; the rest of the late share is breaks whose work alone takes more than 2.5 hours, 2.35\% of them, which no
team size changes. Fewer breaks, or faster work on each, move the floor.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:operations:7}
Halving the breaks: \$1.42 million a year against \$1.50 million for faster work. Faster work lowers the floor ($e^{-2\times2.5}=0.67\%$) but the
offered load stays at 7.5 people, so it needs ten; halving the breaks halves the load, to five, but leaves the floor at 2.35\%.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:operations:8}
The team's size is set by the offered load, which the cycle does not change, and by the waiting it can remove; the window's effect beyond that is
the work-time floor, which people cannot remove. On the chapter's numbers one or two more people (11 to 12 or 13) meet the service level; the rest of
the cost must be taken out of the work.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:operations:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:fm:operations:offices}.
\item So that the \omterm{def:fm:operations:offices}{front office}'s trades and P\&L are checked by people not paid on them: segregation of duties.
\item Settlement no later than the first business day after the trade unless expressly agreed otherwise (since 28 May 2024); allocation,
confirmation and affirmation by the end of the trade date.
\item Nearly 95\% of transactions affirmed by the 9 p.m. ET cut-off (73\% in January 2024), and fail rates of 2.12\% and 3.31\% in July 2024,
consistent with T+2.
\item M/M/c with $\lambda=15$, $\mu=1.5$; stable if $c\mu>\lambda$, that is $c\ge11$.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:operations:late}; the floor holds because the wait is never negative.
\item 2.35\%.
\item 0.682, 0.449 and 0.174.
\item 11 under T+2 and 12 under T+1.
\item 8.37\%, 3.38\% and 2.69\% late; 1\,255, 508 and 403 fails; \$3.14, 1.27 and 1.01 million.
\item Thirteen people, \$2.57 million a year, against \$1.32 million under T+2.
\item \$1.42 million with half the breaks (seven people) and \$1.50 million with 30-minute work (ten people).
\item Breaks resolved within 30 minutes rise from 28.6\% to 50.3\%; those older than four hours fall from 1.3\% to 0.3\%.
\item Batch arrivals at the end-of-day reconciliation; they make late shares larger than the model's.
\item See \cref{def:fm:operations:discipline}; 1.0 basis point a day for liquid shares, 0.5 for illiquid ones, 0.10 for sovereign debt.
\item Regulation (EU) 2023/2845 made mandatory buy-ins a last resort, to be introduced only by a Commission implementing act.
\item See \cref{def:fm:operations:collateral} and \cref{exo:fm:operations:5}.
\item See \cref{def:fm:operations:kri}; breaks older than a day, the fail rate, the share affirmed on the trade date, disputed calls.
\item Eleven people under T+2 and twelve under T+1 for 95\%; going from eleven to thirteen people under T+1 avoids \$2.13 million of fail cost a year,
and \$1.01 million remains.
\item Add two people to cover the waiting, and spend the rest on the work itself: standing settlement instructions, electronic allocation and
matching on the trade date, and real-time reconciliation. Track breaks older than a day, fails and trade-date affirmation daily as \omterm{def:fm:operations:kri}{key risk
indicators}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:operations:1}
The \omterm{def:fm:operations:offices}{middle office} controls and reports on the \omterm{def:fm:operations:offices}{front office}'s activity (validation, P\&L, risk); the \omterm{def:fm:operations:offices}{back office} processes trades to settlement
(confirmation, clearing, settlement, cash and collateral).
\iqlookfor{control against processing.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:operations:2}
A difference between two records of the same trade, position or balance; wrong capture, missing or stale settlement instructions, missing
allocations, corporate actions applied differently, timing.
\iqlookfor{records that should agree.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:operations:3}
At least eleven, since the load is $15/1.5=10$ people; a stable queue still leaves breaks waiting, and a short window adds a floor from the work
itself, so the service level needs more, or less work per break.
\iqlookfor{load, stability and the service level.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:operations:4}
Pay or accept the undisputed amount, reconcile the trade population and the marks of the largest differences, agree or escalate, and track the
dispute's age as a \omterm{def:fm:operations:kri}{key risk indicator}.
\iqlookfor{undisputed amount first, then reconciliation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:operations:5}
It compressed allocation, confirmation and affirmation into the trade date and moved the breaks work into the evening; it rewarded automation and
penalised manual steps.
\iqlookfor{same-day affirmation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:operations:6}
Capture trades with complete reference data and standing settlement instructions, match and affirm electronically on the trade date, reconcile in
real time, route exceptions to a queue sized for the load, and track ageing and fails as \omterm{def:fm:operations:kri}{key risk indicators}.
\iqlookfor{prevention first, then queue sizing.}
\end{solution}
