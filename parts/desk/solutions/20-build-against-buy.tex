\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:fm:build-against-buy} --- Build against Buy}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:1}
$0.35\times1.3=\$0.455$ million; the first two years cost $7\times0.455=\$3.19$ million each.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:2}
$1.2\times1.05^6=\$1.61$ million; with a support engineer, \$2.06 million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:3}
Buying is cheaper over horizons of two to six years; building is cheaper over one year (the purchase's integration year is dear) and from seven
years on.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:4}
The build's costs come in the first two years and its savings later; discounting shrinks the later savings more than the early costs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:5}
A complete suite of agency services, including workflow technology, analytics and liquidity solutions, on Virtu's infrastructure, and about
\$123 million a year of expense savings plus \$125 million of capital synergies; about \$1.0 billion in cash, \$30.30 a share.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:6}
The option pays in the states where the price has risen; the more likely and larger the rises, the more it saves. It is worthless if \omterm{def:fm:build-against-buy:lockin}{switching
costs} more than any price rise could, or if the price cannot rise.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:7}
\$15.28 million without the option and \$13.79 million with it: the option is worth \$1.50 million, against \$0.56 million at a probability of 0.3.
Both exceed the build's \$11.04 million.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:build-against-buy:8}
Engineers on the payroll cost their wage whatever they do, and what they build instead is the opportunity cost: the chapter prices it at 30\% of the
wage. Building also commits the firm to the maintenance tail for the system's life.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:build-against-buy:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:fm:build-against-buy:bab,def:fm:build-against-buy:tco}.
\item Build what makes the firm different (quoting, risk checks, strategies); buy what is the same for everyone (payroll, email, much of post-trade).
\item Investment Technology Group, for about \$1.0 billion in cash, for agency services, workflow technology, analytics and cost savings.
\item It pays for the vendor's clients, people and other businesses as well as its software.
\item See \cref{exo:fm:build-against-buy:1,exo:fm:build-against-buy:2}.
\item \$11.04 million and \$11.30 million; \$14.2 million and \$14.9 million undiscounted.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:build-against-buy:wage}.
\item \$363\,000 loaded per engineer-year; seven years.
\item See \cref{def:fm:build-against-buy:lockin}.
\item At each renewal the price may rise 30\% with probability 0.3; \omterm{def:fm:build-against-buy:lockin}{switching costs} \$2 million. Expected cost \$13.46 million without the option, \$12.90
million with it.
\item See \cref{def:fm:build-against-buy:escrow}; it limits the damage if the vendor disappears, not the vendor's pricing power.
\item Data export in standard formats, termination assistance, price caps at renewal, escrow, short terms.
\item The licence and the wage, then the development effort.
\item The licence and a price cap at renewal.
\item Their full cost and what they would otherwise build.
\item See \cref{def:fm:build-against-buy:rfp}; requirements, service levels, pricing over the life, terms, references.
\item It makes the interface the firm's, so a new vendor or a build can replace the old one without rewiring.
\item At every renewal, and when the firm's engineer costs or the vendor's prices move.
\item \$363\,000 per loaded engineer-year over seven years; the option to switch is worth \$0.56 million.
\item Build the system, since at \$350\,000 per engineer-year it is cheaper over seven years and avoids the vendor's price risk, and specify the
interfaces so that the build could be replaced; if engineers cost more than \$363\,000, buy with a price cap and an exit plan.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:build-against-buy:1}
Whether the handler is part of the edge (latency the vendor cannot match), the full cost including maintenance and venue changes, and the cost of
switching if the vendor fails or raises its price.
\iqlookfor{edge, full cost and lock-in.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:build-against-buy:2}
The order-management system is the same for everyone; the algorithms are how the firm executes better than others.
\iqlookfor{differentiation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:build-against-buy:3}
As a real option: model the vendor's price at renewals on a tree, and compare the expected cost of staying with the cost under the best switching
policy, net of \omterm{def:fm:build-against-buy:lockin}{switching costs}.
\iqlookfor{backward induction on a price tree.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:build-against-buy:4}
Proprietary data formats, integrations to its interfaces, skills and contracts; design with the firm's own interfaces, standard formats and export
paths.
\iqlookfor{owning the interface.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:build-against-buy:5}
Price power, outages, security, insolvency and loss of support; manage with service levels, exit plans, escrow, a second supplier where it matters
and regular tests.
\iqlookfor{concentration and exit.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:build-against-buy:6}
Six engineer-years to build, then a maintenance tail of perhaps a third to a half of that each year, all at the loaded wage plus opportunity cost,
plus infrastructure; discount over the life, and compare with a vendor's full cost, not its licence.
\iqlookfor{the maintenance tail and the opportunity cost.}
\end{solution}
