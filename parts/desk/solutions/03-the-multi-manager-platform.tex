\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:7}

\begin{solution}{exo:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:8}
Track records are measured on survivors and in sample; the teams that will lose are the ones not yet replaced, and new teams' Sharpe ratios are
unknown. \omterm{def:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:netting}{Netting risk} depends on the Sharpe ratio of the pods as they will be, and on turnover: replacing losers restores it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A charge of the fund's actual costs, team pay included, to investors; the share of a team's own profit, net of costs and carried losses,
paid to the team.
\item A percentage of the team's own profit in the preceding year, marked to market, regardless of other teams and of the fund; losses are carried
forward, with generally no clawback.
\item Direct investments: hedges or contra trades against shared exposures, and trades that add to netted positions held by several teams.
\item Each team is paid on its own profit; winners are paid while losers' losses fall on the fund.
\item \$20.09 gross; \$4.27 team payouts; \$4.00 costs; \$2.38 manager; \$9.44 investor.
\item 9 of 400.
\item \$14.47, at \$5.62 of fees; the manager would pay \$8.27 of team pay and costs out of \$5.62 and lose \$2.65 per \$100.
\item 47.0\%.
\item The cost of paying teams on their own profit instead of on the total;
$p\sigma(\varphi(S)-S\Phi(-S))$ per pod when the fund is profitable.
\item 1.7\%, 7.9\% and 22.9\% of gross profit.
\item 1.3\% and 6.2\%.
\item 0.1\%: a team that stays must earn back its losses before it is paid again.
\item The quality of its teams (their Sharpe ratios) and how often it replaces losing ones.
\item Volatility 6.9\% and Sharpe ratio 2.9 without; 2.7\% and 7.5 with; all the shared risk is one tradable factor in the model.
\item 88.4\% cut and 26.6\% stopped; return 20.1\% to 13.4\%, volatility 6.9\% to 4.8\%, Sharpe ratio 2.92 to 2.77.
\item Every pod has a live edge, so stopping any pod only removes profit.
\item The centre's own trading with the fund's capital; the fund owns it, no team is paid on it.
\item Inside the same limits as the pods, since it can add to their concentration.
\item $p\sigma(\varphi(S)-S\Phi(-S))$ per pod, 1.7\% of gross profit at $S=1$ and 7.9\% at $S=0.5$; payouts plus costs take 41.2\% of gross profit.
\item How often teams are replaced and how good they are, which set the netting cost; and what the \omterm{def:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:centre}{centre book} does and is limited by.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:1}

\iqlookfor{that the fee follows the cost base, and who bears it.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:2}

\iqlookfor{carry-forward and its reset.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:3}
$\sum_i\max(X_i,0)\ge\max(\sum_i X_i,0)$; with a positive total the cost is $p\sum_i(\E\max(X_i,0)-\E X_i)=p\sigma(\varphi(S)-S\Phi(-S))$ per pod.
\iqlookfor{convexity of the payout and the closed form.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:5}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:the-multi-manager-platform:6}
Its P\&L against the pods' summed exposures: a pure hedge has negative correlation with the pods' shared factor P\&L and little stand-alone
drift; positions that grow with the pods' consensus add to it.
\iqlookfor{correlation with the netted book and position sizes.}
\end{solution}
