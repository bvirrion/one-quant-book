\begin{solution}{exo:fm:technology-strategy:8}
The waste is the industry's, not the firm's: a firm that stops upgrading in a strategy where others race loses the race revenue ($\alpha V/m$) and
keeps only its share of the rest. The right decision is strategy by strategy: race where $\alpha V/m$ exceeds the cost step, leave where it does not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:fm:technology-strategy:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item See \cref{def:fm:technology-strategy:tier,def:fm:technology-strategy:race}.
\item A cost that rose 19\% over seven years while revenue more than doubled and halved: a fixed commitment.
\item About a race a minute per symbol, 5--10 millionths of a second, a fifth of volume, six firms with over 80\% of wins and losses, a third of the
cost of liquidity.
\item \$0.29, \$1.35 and \$4.45 million a year per strategy; venue fees from Book 14's published schedules, the rest inputs.
\item $V(\alpha\,\mathbf 1\{\text{top}\}/m+(1-\alpha)/n)-c$.
\item See \cref{fig:fm:technology-strategy:profits}: tier 3 for futures market making, ETF arbitrage and options market making; tier 2 for the crypto
trade; tier 1 for statistical arbitrage.
\item No rival is faster than tier 1, so tier 2 wins all races for \$1.06 million more; tier 3 adds nothing.
\item A larger race share or pool, or cheaper speed.
\item See \cref{prop:fm:technology-strategy:count}.
\item Five at tier 3 in futures market making and ETF arbitrage (\$22.2 million each); one at tier 2 in statistical arbitrage (\$2.5 million); four at
tier 3 in options market making (\$18.1 million); two at tier 2 in the crypto trade (\$3.6 million).
\item 93.6\%, 93.6\%, 42.7\%, 92.1\% and 59.8\%; 89.6\% in total.
\item A firm that stops loses its race revenue to those who continue.
\item That continuous markets create arbitrage rents that competition does not remove, only raises the speed needed to capture, in a socially
wasteful race; they propose frequent batch auctions.
\item See \cref{def:fm:technology-strategy:teams} and \cref{fig:fm:technology-strategy:teams}.
\item See \cref{def:fm:technology-strategy:runchange}.
\item \$16.2 million run, \$4.0 million change: 80.2\% run.
\item Technology expenses of \euro70.6 million in 2025 against \euro66.6 million in 2024, beside net trading income of \euro485.8 million.
\item Every year, on the competitors' tiers, the race share measured from the firm's own fills and the tier prices.
\item Tier 3 for futures market making, ETF arbitrage and options market making, tier 2 for the crypto basis trade, tier 1 for statistical
arbitrage; 89.6\% of the industry's speed spending is waste against no one upgrading.
\item Race only where races decide most of a large pool, on a shared low-latency platform; review each strategy's tier yearly and account for every
tier as a run cost it commits the firm to.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:technology-strategy:1}
A \omterm{def:fm:technology-strategy:teams}{product team} builds and runs one business's trading system and is measured on its results; a \omterm{def:fm:technology-strategy:teams}{platform team} provides shared services such as
market data and gateways and is measured on their reliability and on the \omterm{def:fm:technology-strategy:teams}{product teams}' delivery.
\iqlookfor{ownership and measures.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:technology-strategy:2}
When the races it wins are worth less than the extra cost of speed, or when rivals are faster anyway and it wins no races.
\iqlookfor{race share times pool against cost.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:technology-strategy:3}
$\alpha V=27$; each fast firm gets $27/m$; $27/m\ge4$ for $m\le6$, so all five.
\iqlookfor{the counting argument.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:technology-strategy:4}
Measure the share of the strategy's revenue lost to faster competitors in races, estimate what hardware would recover, and compare with its full
cost including the engineers; decide on that, not on the latency alone.
\iqlookfor{revenue at stake against full cost.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:technology-strategy:5}
Each change project creates infrastructure, licences and support that must be run for as long as the business exists, so change becomes run.
\iqlookfor{commitments accumulate.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:fm:technology-strategy:6}
They would batch orders in short intervals so that tiny speed differences no longer decide who trades: $\alpha$ falls towards zero, fewer firms
race, and speed spending falls.
\iqlookfor{the race share as the lever.}
\end{solution}
