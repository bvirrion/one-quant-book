\begin{solution}{pb:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A public register with which companies and partnerships file their constitution, officers, owners and accounts;
the annual statements the law requires an entity to prepare, have audited unless exempt, and file.
\item Company registries; SEC EDGAR; listed firms' annual reports; prospectuses and offering documents; banks'
divisional and Pillar~3 reports.
\item About 75\% of about 2.2 million a year arrive electronically (\omterm{def:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:ixbrl}{inline XBRL} or XBRL); the rest are paper or image
documents without tags.
\item The periodic Form X-17A-5 report of a broker-dealer's financial condition, income and net capital, confidential;
the annual audited statement of financial condition, filed as a separate document, is public.
\item A bank's or large investment firm's public report on capital, risk and remuneration; it counts the individuals
paid \euro1 million or more, in bands.
\item The costs of all persons employed in the year; the monthly average of persons employed under contracts of
service; wages and salaries, social security costs, other pension costs.
\item Contractors, secondees from other group companies, and partners or members.
\item A parent and its subsidiaries presented as one entity, with intra-group transactions removed; what an LLP's
members receive: salaried amounts and automatic profit shares charged as an expense, plus profit divided at their
discretion.
\item Human-readable XHTML whose numbers are tagged with taxonomy concepts; the context gives entity, period and
dimensions, the unit the currency or measure, the scale the power of ten, the sign the sign, the format the separators.
\item Each period recurs in every later filing as a comparative, and a later filing may restate it.
\item Turnover \pounds140.6, 269.8, 778.6, 664.2, 588.2, 1\,224.8 million; \omterm{def:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:staff}{staff costs} \pounds116.7, 182.9, 409.0, 394.7,
408.0, 556.1 million; employees 68, 83, 98, 113, 143, 173.
\item Staff cost per head \pounds1.72, 2.20, 4.17, 3.49, 2.85, 3.21 million; turnover per head \pounds2.07, 3.25, 7.94,
5.88, 4.11, 7.08 million.
\item 83.0\%, 67.8\%, 52.5\%, 59.4\%, 69.4\%, 45.4\%: highest in fiscal 2020, when turnover was smallest, and lowest in
fiscal 2025, when it was largest; part of pay does not scale with turnover.
\item Turnover less administrative expenses plus other income with the tagged sign gives \pounds521.1 million against the
stated \pounds556.6 million; the other income is tagged negative although it is income.
\item Fair-value gains on investments below operating profit: \pounds233.9, 249.7, 350.7 and 178.3 million.
\item Dividends of \pounds1\,694.4 million against profit after tax of \pounds1\,718.4 million: 98.6\%.
\item Staff cost per head \pounds1.72--4.17 million and turnover per head \pounds2.07--7.94 million over fiscal 2020--2025;
98.6\% of six years' profit after tax distributed.
\item It is a mean over a skewed distribution; it includes the employer's social security and pension costs; it mixes
earlier years' deferred awards into this year's cost.
\item A partnership keeps its members' profit outside \omterm{def:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:staff}{staff costs} and may have corporate members; a group entity's
revenue is set by transfer pricing and its staff may be employed elsewhere in the group.
\item They tell her the firm's scale, how its costs and pay move with its revenue, and that it pays out almost all its
profit; they do not tell her what she, or a typical employee in her role, will be paid.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:1}
The value as tagged: 900 with no minus sign if the tag has none; the brackets are presentation. The sign attribute of
the tag, and the concept's balance (debit or credit) in the taxonomy, give the meaning.
\iqlookfor{separating presentation from data; knowing that expenses are usually tagged positive.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:2}
\omterm{def:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:consolidated}{Consolidated accounts} add the parent and its subsidiaries line by line and remove intra-group items; the parent's
accounts show the parent alone, with subsidiaries as investments. Compare consolidated with consolidated, at the same
date and in one currency.
\iqlookfor{elimination of intra-group items; like with like.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:3}
Download each daily file, stream its documents, keep those of companies in a list of trading firms (by SIC code and a
curated list), parse the facts, and store each with company, period, concept, value, source file and filing date.
Keep every version: a later filing that restates a period is a new row, and queries take the latest. Reconcile totals
and flag failures rather than fixing them silently.
\iqlookfor{provenance, versioning of restatements, streaming, reconciliation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:4}
A good year for the market or the strategy; a change of entity or of transfer pricing; staff moved to another group
company or reclassified as contractors or members; a change of fiscal period; a currency move; a one-off gain in
revenue; an acquisition consolidated late in the year.
\iqlookfor{entity and period effects before productivity.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:5}
Regulation (one entity holds the trading permissions and capital), legal form (a partnership suits profit sharing
with members), tax and transfer pricing across the group, and the ability to second staff to several group entities.
\iqlookfor{regulatory capital, partnership economics, transfer pricing.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:6}
Regress \omterm{def:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:staff}{staff costs} on revenue across years (Book~16, chapter~1): the slope estimates the variable share per unit of
revenue, the intercept the fixed part. Six points give a wide interval; headcount growth, deferred awards expensed over
several years, and one-off costs all bias it.
\iqlookfor{the regression, its uncertainty with few points, and the timing of deferred pay.}
\end{solution}
