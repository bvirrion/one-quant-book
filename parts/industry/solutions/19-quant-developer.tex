\begin{solution}{pb:in:quant-developer:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An engineer who turns research prototypes into production code and owns its performance and correctness; the
\omterm{def:in:quant-developer:research}{research engineer}, the \omterm{def:in:quant-developer:library}{library developer} and the \omterm{def:in:quant-developer:trading}{trading-system developer}.
\item Research infrastructure and production signals; library releases; the trading system.
\item Reproducibility and researchers' time; stability, speed and coverage; latency, uptime and correct behaviour. A result
nobody can rerun; every desk's numbers moving; a loss in seconds.
\item The \omterm{def:in:quant-developer:trading}{trading-system developer}.
\item In a research group; in a central analytics group (or the trading technology of an options market maker); in
trading technology.
\item Software developers and financial quantitative analysts; the first fits the \omterm{def:in:quant-developer:trading}{trading-system developer}, the second the
\omterm{def:in:quant-developer:research}{research engineer} and the \omterm{def:in:quant-developer:library}{library developer}.
\item The analysts' code is in the highest preparation band (graduate school), the software developers' in the band below
(a bachelor's degree).
\item The 2010 codes 15-1132 and 15-1133 were merged into 15-1252.
\item 72.2\% 13-2099.01, 14.8\% 15-1252, 13.0\% other codes.
\item Almost all the filings come from one or two employers per kind, and cells from fewer than three employers are
suppressed.
\item Independent bootstrap resamples of each population; the difference and ratio of the resampled medians give 95\%
percentile intervals.
\item \$5\,000, from $-\$372$ to \$5\,688.
\item $-\$11\,000$ ($-\$19\,038$ to $-\$6\,600$) and $-\$27\,092$ ($-\$37\,500$ to $-\$19\,900$).
\item The software code's filings are concentrated at level IV.
\item It becomes $-\$7\,789$.
\item 72.2\% analysts, 14.8\% software developers, 13.0\% other; software minus analysts \$5\,000 overall ($-\$372$ to
\$5\,688), $-\$27\,092$ at level IV.
\item No: the employer and the level decide the offer; the code only describes it.
\item A median of \$235\,000 over 12 filings from 3 employers; the other kinds' filings come from one or two employers.
\item What the job ships: research tools, a library or a trading system, and the on-call duty that goes with it.
\item The three offers are three different jobs (research tools, a pricing library, a trading system) whatever their title
and code. Choose by the product and the clock you want to work to, and compare pay within the employer kind and level.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-developer:1}
Find whether it is arithmetic (order of operations, floating-point summation, a different library function) or data (a
different input on those days): log both versions' intermediate values on a failing day and compare step by step; then
decide the tolerance with the researcher and put the case in the regression tests.
\iqlookfor{bisecting the computation, and a tolerance agreed and tested.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-developer:2}
Version the code, the data (point-in-time snapshots or a data version), the configuration and the environment
(dependency pins, random seeds), record them with the result, and rerun from the record as a test.
\iqlookfor{code, data, configuration and environment all versioned.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-developer:3}
Profile before changing anything: allocation and copying per call, repeated work (curves rebuilt each call), cache misses,
then the numerical method; batch calls across trades; parallelise across cores last.
\iqlookfor{measurement first; repeated work and memory before arithmetic.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-developer:4}
Compare latency histograms of the two releases on the same replayed data; bisect the release's changes; look for new
allocations, locks, logging or branches on a rare path, and for changes in thread placement or garbage collection.
\iqlookfor{tail latency needs replay and bisection, not averages.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-developer:5}
A typed specification of inputs with their timestamps, the output's units and range, reference cases with expected
outputs that both versions run in CI, and a live check that compares the two on shadow data.
\iqlookfor{shared reference cases and a live comparison.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-developer:6}
Golden values for a fixed portfolio against the previous release and an independent benchmark, with tolerances by product;
identities (parity, limits) and Greeks against finite differences; performance tests; every past bug as a test; and a
report of every changed number for the desks before release.
\iqlookfor{golden-value regression with tolerances, independent benchmarks and a change report.}
\end{solution}
