\begin{solution}{exo:in:quant-researcher:8}

\begin{solution}{pb:in:quant-researcher:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A person who finds, tests and maintains tradable regularities and turns them into what systems run; high frequency
(seconds to minutes), medium frequency (days to weeks), asset management (months to years).
\item Deep, independent research that shapes strategies, execution and position management, from quick ideas to longer
investigations, designing and optimising trading algorithms.
\item It works mainly on pricing and risk models rather than on alpha (chapter~18).
\item The rate at which the role's work produces evidence that tells skill from luck.
\item Employers: systematic funds, market makers, platforms, banks, asset managers; horizon seconds to months; shares the
P\&L through credit; reports to a head of research or a portfolio manager; SOC 13-2099.01, 15-2041, 15-2031.
\item $\sqrt{(1+s^2/2)/n}$ for the per-period Sharpe ratio $s$ after $n$ periods.
\item $s/\mathrm{SE}\ge z$ gives $n=z^2(1+s^2/2)/s^2$.
\item $n/m\approx z^2/S^2$ years, since $s^2=S^2/m$ and the $s^2/2$ term is small.
\item $S\approx\mathrm{IC}\sqrt{Nm}$: shorter horizons give more bets a year and higher Sharpe ratios for the same skill.
\item 0.7 days (minute), 43 days (hour), 0.77 years (day), 3.73 years (week), 16.2 years (month), 48.5 years (quarter).
\item The portfolio manager; the firm, centrally; the desk or the firm, shared.
\item A signal with its specification, data and code or implementable specification, the tests and research log, and a
monitoring plan.
\item Mathematics, statistics, physics, computer science, economics; graduate school for most of the occupation.
\item Medians from \$107\,100 (exchanges) to \$220\,000 (systematic funds); at banks a ladder from \$88\,300 to \$200\,000.
\item Offers far above the \omterm{def:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:lca}{prevailing wage} are not ordered by the stated level.
\item 0.97 years (Sharpe 2, daily), 3.88 years (1, weekly), 15.53 years (0.5, monthly).
\item Lengthen them, by the factor that inflates the Sharpe ratio's variance.
\item Use more history out of sample, more breadth in tests, and careful research controls; learn from the fast parts of the
job (execution, data quality) while the slow ones accrue.
\item In the fast job, results will judge her within a year; in the slow one, her first two years say little about her
skill, and her process and reviews carry more weight.
\item The high-frequency job will tell her within a year whether her ideas work; the monthly fund will not for many years,
so choose it only if she values the kind of work more than the speed of the verdict.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-researcher:2}
Many small independent bets a day raise the Sharpe ratio; but each bet's edge is small and the liquidity at that horizon is
limited, so the profit cannot scale with capital.
\iqlookfor{breadth versus market impact.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-researcher:3}
With 200 trials the best t-statistic of 3.5 is weaker than it looks: correct for multiple testing (for example a Bonferroni
or a deflated Sharpe ratio) and test it out of sample before believing it.
\iqlookfor{multiple testing and out-of-sample discipline.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-researcher:4}
Set in advance a statistical test on live results against the expected distribution (for example a drawdown or a running
t-statistic beyond a bound), check its inputs and implementation first, and decide on evidence, not on one bad month.
\iqlookfor{a pre-registered stopping rule.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-researcher:5}
Inputs and their timing, every transformation with parameters, the universe, the output's meaning and scale, reference
values for test cases, and the conditions under which it should not trade.
\iqlookfor{reproducibility from the document alone.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:quant-researcher:6}
Attribute by a stated rule, for example the marginal contribution of each to the combined portfolio (with and without it),
or a Shapley value over the two; say which, since the shared positions make any split a choice.
\iqlookfor{marginal or Shapley attribution, and honesty that it is a convention.}
\end{solution}
