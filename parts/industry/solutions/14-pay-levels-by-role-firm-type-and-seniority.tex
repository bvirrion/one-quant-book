\begin{solution}{pb:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The attestation a US employer files before sponsoring a worker, with the offered wage and the \omterm{def:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:lca}{prevailing wage}, the
legal minimum for the occupation, area and level; the offered \omterm{def:in:how-pay-works:base}{base salary}.
\item It includes base, guaranteed pay, commissions and production bonuses, and excludes nonproduction bonuses; it marks
wages of \$115 an hour or \$239\,200 a year and above with \#.
\item Replacing values above a threshold by a marker; not publishing statistics from too few observations; no cell under
ten applications or three employers.
\item The total pay of a listed firm's median employee; a person paid \euro1 million or more a year in EU banking
statistics.
\item Filings: offered base, sponsored positions; survey: wages without discretionary bonuses, all employees of an
industry and occupation; accounts: staff cost per head, one entity's employees; pay ratio: median employee's total pay,
one listed firm; remuneration reports: counts and pay of those above \euro1 million, banks and investment firms.
\item As the method: stream, keep certified full-time, classify employer and role, annualise, group and summarise with
suppression.
\item Banks 7\,298 (9 employers), market makers 367 (10), systematic funds 210 (7), platforms 102 (3), exchanges 462
(4); the asset manager's 369 come from one employer and are suppressed.
\item All roles: \$215\,000, \$190\,500, \$175\,000, \$154\,300, \$130\,800 (systematic, platform, market maker, bank,
exchange); quantitative researchers \$220\,000, \$190\,000, \$175\,000, \$158\,100, \$107\,100; software engineers
\$205\,500, \$190\,000, \$175\,000, \$155\,700, \$140\,400.
\item Up 22.9\% (systematic funds, banks), 18.7\% (platforms), 16.7\% (market makers), 7.9\% (exchanges), against
18.8\% inflation: flat to slightly up in real terms, down at the exchanges.
\item At the banks the median rises from \$100\,000 at level I to \$169\,900 at level IV; at the market makers it jumps from
\$84\,800 to \$180\,000--185\,000 and then stays flat, because offers far above the floor are not ordered by it.
\item Staff cost per head over the same kind's median offered base; it overstates the typical ratio (social costs,
benefits, deferred awards, a mean over a skewed distribution) and mixes populations; it understates the top where
partners are outside \omterm{def:in:reading-a-trading-firms-accounts:staff}{staff costs}.
\item Market maker 4.4 to 12.0; \omterm{def:in:quantitative-hedge-funds-and-systematic-managers:sys}{systematic manager} 19.1; bank 2.2 to 2.6.
\item Intercontinental Exchange 1.35, Nasdaq 1.43, Morgan Stanley 2.58 times \omterm{def:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:median}{median-employee pay}.
\item Software developers' median in securities \$163\,290 (banks \$136\,620) against the filings' \$140\,400 to \$175\,000:
the same range.
\item The tail's size and shape by kind of firm (variable over fixed 0.99 in banks, 8.14 in investment firms dealing on
own account); not how many are near the threshold, nor anything outside EU-authorised institutions.
\item Market maker 4.4 to 12.0, \omterm{def:in:quantitative-hedge-funds-and-systematic-managers:sys}{systematic manager} 19.1, bank 2.2 to 2.6 times the kind's median offered base; upper
bounds for the average employee, from one entity or firm each.
\item The mean over a skewed distribution: a few very large awards raise the per-head cost far above a typical employee's
pay.
\item Median total pay by role at private firms, which none publishes.
\item The base, the bonus range paid in the last three years at her level, the deferral and its vesting, and the share of
people in the role who received more than the base again.
\item At the bank, expect total pay of roughly two to two and a half times base on average; at the market maker the
average is several times larger but spread far wider, and the typical first years sit much nearer base than the average
suggests.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:1}
Multiply by 1, 12, 26, 52 or 2\,080 hours; for a range, record both ends and use the lower end as the guaranteed wage,
reporting the range's width as information about pay setting.
\iqlookfor{units as data, and a stated convention for ranges.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:2}
The median's sampling distribution depends on the data's density at the median, which is unknown and often lumpy (round
salaries); the bootstrap estimates it from the data without a formula.
\iqlookfor{no parametric form for the median's error; lumpy data.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:3}
Stream rows with a read-only reader, keep a filter and running aggregates, write a small cache of kept rows; cap memory at
the operating-system level. Risks: a header change between files, units and blanks, duplicate cases across files, one
row blowing up the parser, and personal fields reaching output.
\iqlookfor{bounded memory by construction, schema checks, and privacy by column selection.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:5}
The EU's cap limits variable pay to 100\% of fixed (200\% with approval) at banks, which raises fixed pay and compresses
the ratio; the cap has not applied to investment firms since 2021, and a firm dealing on its own account pays from its
trading result.
\iqlookfor{the regulatory cap, and who it covers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:pay-levels-by-role-firm-type-and-seniority:6}
Sponsored workers may be hired into particular roles, levels or locations, and may accept different offers; the ranges
then differ from the firm's. Compare the filings' ranges with the occupational survey for the same industry, occupation
and area, and with offers of the same employer to non-sponsored hires if any are public.
\iqlookfor{selection into sponsorship, and an external benchmark.}
\end{solution}
