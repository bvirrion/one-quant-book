\begin{solution}{iq:in:locations:1}
Convert each to net pay after tax and contributions, subtract housing and other costs that differ, adjust for price levels
and for the structure of pay (bonus, deferral, equity), and then weigh the job itself.
\iqlookfor{net, housing, price level and structure, in that order.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:locations:2}
Engineers who work on the trading systems need to reach the colocated machines, the exchange's staff and the traders
during trading hours; time zone and access matter more than distance.
\iqlookfor{time zone and operational access.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:locations:3}
Yes: equal average rates at one package can hide different marginal rates, and social contributions, exchange rates and
deductions can differ; the disposable difference then includes both tax and rent effects.
\iqlookfor{average against marginal, and more than one difference.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:locations:4}
Bisection on the difference of disposable pay over a bracket where it changes sign; the difference can have several roots
(New York and London have two), can be flat, and can jump at tax thresholds, so scan a grid first and bracket each root.
\iqlookfor{several roots and discontinuities.}
\end{solution}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:locations:6}
The London numbers, converted at a weaker dollar, would be worth more dollars: a package fixed in dollars converts to
fewer pounds and leaves less after London's tax and rent. A London employee paid in pounds sees no change at home, but
more dollars if converted.
\iqlookfor{which currency the package is set in.}
\end{solution}
