\begin{solution}{exo:in:leadership-roles:8}

\begin{solution}{pb:in:leadership-roles:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item As in the chapter's definitions.
\item The desk's P\&L, risk and people, within the firm's risk appetite and the agreed revenue budget.
\item SMF4 (chief risk officer), SMF24 (chief operations), SMF2 (chief finance), among others.
\item Pay becomes a share of the firm's profit, part of it becomes capital at risk, and the partner answers for the firm.
\item Through a strategy or technology that works and the capital and people to scale it, rather than through titles.
\item $s_t=p/P_t$; draw $\rho s_t\max(\Pi_t,0)$; retention $(1-\rho)s_t\max(\Pi_t,0)$; a loss $s_t\Pi_t$ reduces capital.
\item Profit normal with mean \$300 million and standard deviation \$200 million; 30 partners of 100 points; 30 points
and \$1 million for the new partner; 60\% payout; three more partners in year three.
\item \$8.93 million, 10th--90th percentile \$5.79--12.14 million.
\item \$2.15 million after year one rising to \$6.67 million after year five; 5th percentile \$3.32 million.
\item In 9.4\% of paths at some year-end.
\item 3, 6 and 17.
\item The regulator's approval, and a \omterm{def:in:leadership-roles:smf}{statement of responsibilities}.
\item The FCA's PS26/6 reforms of the regime: most phase 1 changes from 24 April 2026.
\item Registration as a principal, with the Series 24 exam.
\item Chief executives' median wage \$431\,020 in the securities industry; 586 management-body members of EU banks paid over
one million euros, variable 0.73 of fixed.
\item Expected draws of \$8.93 million over five years and a capital account of \$6.67 million at risk; break-even at about
20 points, 0.66\% of the partnership, against \$1.2 million a year.
\item As wealth at risk, repaid on leaving, not as income.
\item The partner function: approval, a \omterm{def:in:leadership-roles:smf}{statement of responsibilities}, and accountability for failures in his area.
\item The points and how they grow, the contribution, the payout ratio, admissions, and capital repayment on leaving.
\item At 30 points the draws alone beat his salary by about half in expectation, and the capital builds a stake in the
firm. Accept if he can carry the capital at risk and the accountability; negotiate the points and the repayment terms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:leadership-roles:1}
Understand why (opportunity, risk taken, capacity, a failing strategy), protect the desk's risk discipline rather than
chase the budget with more risk, and tell management early with a plan and a revised estimate.
\iqlookfor{not taking more risk to meet a budget.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:leadership-roles:2}
From the firm's risk appetite and capital: allocate a share of the firm's loss tolerance to the desk by its expected
profit and volatility, check it against stress losses and the desk's history, and review it on a fixed schedule.
\iqlookfor{top-down from appetite, checked bottom-up.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:leadership-roles:3}
Reward risk-adjusted contribution: profit relative to the risk and capital used, the quality of the process, and the
behaviour; say so in advance, so that traders know risk is priced.
\iqlookfor{risk-adjusted pay and stated rules.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:leadership-roles:4}
Incidents and their causes, deployment frequency and failure rate, latency and capacity headroom, the bus factor of
critical systems (Book~16, chapter~21), and cost.
\iqlookfor{reliability and risk before new projects.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:leadership-roles:5}
When managing the desk's risk and people takes the attention trading needs, or when trading personally creates a conflict
in allocating limits and credit; many desks keep a small book for the head to stay close to the market.
\iqlookfor{attention and conflicts.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:leadership-roles:6}
For: partners are rewarded and retained, and the firm may not need the capital. Against: less loss-absorbing capital, a
higher chance that capital falls below requirements after a bad year (the chapter's model: 13.1\% against 9.4\%), and
less capacity to invest.
\iqlookfor{payout against capital resilience.}
\end{solution}
