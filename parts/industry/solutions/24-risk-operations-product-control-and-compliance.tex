\begin{solution}{exo:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:6}
Product control teaches every desk's products, pricing and daily P\&L from outside, and the controls around them; the
desk analyst's job teaches one desk's trading from inside. Product control is easier to leave for another desk or
function because it has seen many; the desk job leads more directly to trading.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item As in the chapter's definitions.
\item Risk management, compliance and internal audit.
\item Sets limits, watches positions against them and escalates breaches and unmodelled risks.
\item Handles the exceptions: breaks, fails and missed corporate actions, and automates what repeats.
\item Through surveillance models of market abuse in order and trade data.
\item Predominantly fixed pay; variable pay set separately from the businesses controlled, on control objectives; a
significantly lower variable-to-fixed ratio.
\item So that the controller is not paid for the risks she should limit.
\item The FCA's required compliance oversight and money laundering reporting functions.
\item Two exams and two years of relevant experience.
\item A flatter path: more of it in base, less in bonus, less dependent on the businesses' results.
\item 0.855 (0.807 to 0.903) overall; 0.68, 0.79, 0.85 and 0.83 at levels I to IV.
\item 0.965 (0.925 to 1.151), from only 17 filings at 7 employers.
\item Variable pay 0.88 of fixed in \omterm{def:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:control}{control functions} against 1.23 in investment banking: fixed pay 53.3\% and 44.8\% of
the total.
\item Financial risk specialists \$133\,070, accountants and auditors \$105\,160, \omterm{def:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:compliance}{compliance officers} \$100\,160,
brokerage clerks \$70\,070.
\item From level (1.01) in fiscal 2021 to 0.86 in fiscal 2025, because the front office's offers rose.
\item Banks 0.855 (0.807 to 0.903); trading firms 0.965 (0.925 to 1.151).
\item Bonus, which is larger and more variable in the front office.
\item The whole firm's risk, its models from outside, and the controls a firm needs.
\item Market risk to risk trading or strategy; model validation to the desk's quant teams; product control to the desks.
\item In base pay the market-risk job costs about a seventh at a bank, and much more once bonuses are counted; the
market maker's research job pays more and judges faster. Choose market risk only if you want to see the whole firm, and
plan the move you would make from it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:1}
Missing or late market data, a trade booked wrongly or not at all, a model or curve change, a corporate action, and a
Greek computed on the wrong position; then the desk's explanation, in writing.
\iqlookfor{data and booking before models.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:2}
Record and escalate the breach as the policy requires, whatever the trader says about the model; separately, review the
model if there is evidence, and change the limit only through the proper approval.
\iqlookfor{independence and process.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:3}
Consensus pricing services, recent trades in similar instruments, broker quotes, model prices from observable inputs,
and a valuation reserve for the uncertainty (Book~6, chapter~27).
\iqlookfor{several independent sources and a reserve.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:4}
Flag orders placed and cancelled quickly on one side near the touch while the same trader executes on the other side,
with thresholds on size, time and ratio. Legitimate market making cancels often too, so most alerts are false; rank them
and review the top with context.
\iqlookfor{the pattern and the false-positive trade-off.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:5}
A calm period left the historical window (or a volatile one dropped out), a correlation changed, or data errors in the
window; check the scenario set and the inputs before believing it.
\iqlookfor{the measure moves with its window, not only with positions.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:risk-operations-product-control-and-compliance:6}
Profits on both sides of what should be a hedge suggest mismarked or fictitious positions, or a transfer price between
desks that is not at market. Check the internal trades against market prices, confirm external positions, and look at
who approved the internal trades.
\iqlookfor{internal trades as a place to hide losses.}
\end{solution}
