\begin{solution}{pb:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A multilateral system for clearing, settling or recording transactions: payment systems, central securities
depositories, securities settlement systems, central counterparties, trade repositories.
\item Trading, clearing, data, index licences and connectivity; engineers for matching, data and clearing systems;
quants for margin, index and surveillance models.
\item About 3\,875; 1\,661; 12\,844; 9\,525.
\item CME Group \$1.68m and \$1.09m; Cboe \$2.84m and \$0.88m; ICE \$0.98m and \$0.38m; Nasdaq \$0.87m and \$0.24m.
\item Transaction-based expenses (rebates and fees paid to participants) counted in revenue and deducted below.
\item 64.9\%, 31.1\%, 39.0\%, 28.2\%.
\item $1.09/0.24=4.5$ times.
\item Fixed income and data services and mortgage technology; capital access platforms and financial technology.
\item Access, execution, financing and clearing; collected, cleaned and redistributed data and evaluated prices.
\item Salaries and equity, following a software company's economics, not a share of trading profit.
\item A published range of basic pay for a grade, with supplements and any cap.
\item Grade minimum and maximum, times one plus the locality rate, each capped; compare by overlap.
\item \$115\,489--\$195\,672; \$157\,698--\$267\,168; \$180\,094--\$292\,300.
\item SK-16 and SK-17, at \$292\,300.
\item They cover progression within a grade; new hires are placed near the bottom.
\item Operating income per head \$0.24 to \$1.09 million; SK-14 in New York \$157\,698 to \$267\,168.
\item The employer's post-employment and conflict-of-interest rules, set out in its own codes.
\item The market's plumbing, rules and consolidated data, from the inside.
\item Chapter~14, against labour-condition filings for the same occupations.
\item Compare the work (engineering for a market against for a regulator) and pay at the same seniority, reading the
exchange's pay from filings and the regulator's from its band; the regulator's is published and capped, the exchange's
includes equity.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:1}
Fairness and determinism: every participant's order processed in the stated priority, identical behaviour under load, no
lost or duplicated messages, recovery without disadvantaging anyone, and regulatory audit. A trading firm cares about its
own speed and P\&L.
\iqlookfor{the venue's obligations, not its speed.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:2}
Set margins that cover a defaulting member's potential loss over the close-out period at a high confidence, and size
the default fund for extreme but plausible losses; if it is wrong, the default waterfall spreads losses to surviving
members' contributions and the clearing house's capital (Book~1, chapter~5).
\iqlookfor{coverage, close-out period, and the waterfall.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:3}
Equal-length cross-connects or delay equalisation to a common point, hardware timestamps at the hand-off, and continuous
measurement of the spread of arrival times across ports, published or audited; test with synchronised captures on every
port (Book~14).
\iqlookfor{physical equalisation and measurement.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:4}
Message-level data with timestamps for every order, cancel and failure, including failed orders; first measure races:
groups of messages from different firms targeting the same quote within a short window after a price move.
\iqlookfor{message data including failures, and a definition of a race.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:5}
From comparable bonds' trades and quotes (issuer curve, sector spreads, rating), adjusted for maturity and liquidity;
check against later trades (back-testing the evaluated price) and dealers' quotes.
\iqlookfor{a model from comparables, and out-of-sample checks.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:exchanges-brokers-vendors-and-regulators-as-employers:6}
Rebates paid to liquidity providers are a cost of generating volume: gross revenue overstates what the exchange keeps.
Compare revenue net of transaction-based expenses, operating margins, and revenue per contract or share traded.
\iqlookfor{net revenue and unit economics.}
\end{solution}
