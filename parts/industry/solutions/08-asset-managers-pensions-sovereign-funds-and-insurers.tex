\begin{solution}{pb:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An institution investing on behalf of beneficiaries or the state; a government-owned special-purpose fund for
macroeconomic purposes investing in foreign assets; a plan promising a fixed benefit at retirement.
\item Management by the owner's own staff; 95\%.
\item Allocation, risk, liability matching, factor and quantitative equity, index and implementation.
\item 3.8 and 23.1 basis points; one includes external managers' fees and the other excludes them, with different
denominators.
\item Personnel, custody, IT and data, research and legal, other and allocated costs, and external managers' base and
performance fees.
\item 3.5 and 81.8 basis points.
\item 2.3 basis points on internally managed assets; 27.7 on external mandates.
\item $59.7/81.8=73\%$.
\item NOK 3.65 million; \$352\,000.
\item \$3.0 billion.
\item Internal cost over the fee rate.
\item \$5.02 million a year.
\item \$5.02, \$2.51 and \$1.255 billion.
\item $15/2.51=6.0$ times.
\item \$30 million a year.
\item \$5.02 billion, \$2.51 billion and \$1.255 billion.
\item The same return, net of costs, as the external manager.
\item What the costs buy: asset mix, strategies, and net returns.
\item It creates portfolio-management, trading and research jobs; it reduces manager-selection and oversight work for the
mandate.
\item At 20 basis points the mandate is six times the break-even, so \omterm{def:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:internal}{internal management} is far cheaper if the team can
match the manager's net return; the decision rests on that capability, not on cost.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:1}
An owner answers to beneficiaries or a government and chooses what to manage and whom to hire; a manager is paid fees
to run mandates. At an owner a quant works on allocation, liabilities, risk and manager selection; at a manager on
producing the mandated return.
\iqlookfor{principal against agent, and the work that follows.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:2}
Liabilities rise by about 18\% per 1\% fall in rates and the bonds by about 6\%, so the funding ratio falls. Extend the
duration of assets or add interest-rate swaps (receive fixed) to hedge part of the gap.
\iqlookfor{duration mismatch and the hedge.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:3}
Net excess to the owner is $x-50-0.2x$ basis points against $-5$ for the index portfolio: hire if $0.8x-50>-5$, that
is $x>56.25$ basis points a year, before any risk adjustment.
\iqlookfor{fees on excess return, and a clean inequality.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:4}
The allocation sets most of the portfolio's risk and so the spread of its returns; selection within asset classes adds
a smaller, less certain amount. The best quants should inform the allocation and risk, not only security selection.
\iqlookfor{risk decomposition drives where effort pays.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:5}
Curves, duration and convexity, rates derivatives and hedging, XVA and collateral transfer; to learn: liability models,
mortality and lapse, and the solvency capital rules.
\iqlookfor{transferable rates skills and the new liability side.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:in:asset-managers-pensions-sovereign-funds-and-insurers:6}
Regress the team's monthly excess returns net of its costs on the benchmark (and factors), estimate alpha with a
heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-robust error, and ask whether ten years give power: with a tracking error of
2\% and a true alpha of 0.5\%, the t-statistic after ten years is about 0.8.
\iqlookfor{net of cost, robust errors, and power.}
\end{solution}
