\begin{solution}{exo:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:4}
Stratification balances the difficulty mix across brokers but leaves each order's difficulty in the noise; random allocation already balances it on
average, and with heavy-tailed difficulty the extreme orders within the top bucket still land unevenly. Adjustment subtracts the difficulty itself.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:5}
Information about the other brokers: broker~2 got 5.4\% of the flow and the best-pair test fell to 88\% power, and brokers 3 to 6 are barely
observed, so a change in any of them would go unseen. A desk that needs to monitor every broker, or expects effects to drift, prefers the uniform wheel
or a bandit with share floors.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:6}
Its orders were the hardest fifth, whose difficulty alone costs far more than the effects between brokers. The adjustment assumes the difficulty
model is right (here, cost rises one for one with the pre-trade estimate) and extrapolates it to orders that only broker~1 received.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:7}
Adjusted: 3.9 months; raw ($\sigma=\sqrt{15^2+25^2}=29.2$): 14.8 months. With less noise, the difficulty dominates the raw comparison and adjustment

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:8}
A quarter is 1\,800 orders, 300 a broker: the standard error of a difference between two brokers is about $30\sqrt{2/300}=2.4$ basis points, so 2
basis points is noise, and the true order here is the opposite.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:1}
\textbf{1.} The OMS holds orders, decisions and positions; the EMS the \omterm{def:mx:the-almgren-chriss-framework:parent}{child orders} and fills. TCA needs the decision time and price from one and the
executions from the other.
\textbf{2.} High touch: a person at the broker works the order; low touch: the broker's electronic channels. Large, sensitive orders go high touch.
\textbf{3.} A broker given harder orders pays more whatever its skill.
\textbf{4.} A rule that allocates orders among brokers; the order.
\textbf{5.} 600 orders a month, difficulty lognormal (mean 15, s.d. 25 basis points), noise $t_4$ with s.d. 30; effects 0, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8.
\textbf{6.} $2\sigma^2(z_{0.975}+z_{0.8})^2/\delta^2$ orders each: 1\,570 for $\delta=3$, $\sigma=30$.
\textbf{7.} Its noise includes the orders' difficulty ($\sigma=39.1$).
\textbf{8.} \emph{Named result}: with difficulty adjustment 16 months (15.7 by the formula), without it 24 months in simulation (26.6 by the formula).
\textbf{9.} A slightly more precise raw comparison: s.d. 1.51 against 1.59 after a year.
\textbf{10.} It samples each broker's posterior adjusted cost and sends the order to the lowest.
\textbf{11.} 87.2\% to broker~1 (97.5\% in the last month), 0.54 basis points excess, power 88\%; uniform: 16.7\%, 4.33, 100\%.
\textbf{12.} A floor on each broker's share and a forgetting factor in the posteriors.
\textbf{13.} Their true difference is 1 basis point with standard errors of 0.8 to 0.9: their order is not established.
\textbf{14.} Raw, broker~1 ranked last, 35.1 basis points worse than broker~2; adjusted, first, 3.8 better than the next.
\textbf{15.} Some brokers deliver better executions consistently over time.
\textbf{16.} See the definitions.
\textbf{17.} UK joint payments allowed again since 1 August 2024; EU choice of joint or separate payment applied from 6 June 2026; US no-action relief
expired on 3 July 2023.
\textbf{18.} Bundled payments tie the allocation to research; unbundled, it can follow execution quality.
\textbf{19.} Adjust for difficulty, run the wheel uniformly for about a year and a half before ranking the top two, and use a floored bandit only once
the ranking is clear.
\textbf{20.} So that the brokers' orders differ only by chance and their costs can be compared.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:1}
When the order is large relative to liquidity, information-sensitive, or needs a natural counterparty or the broker's capital: a block in an
illiquid name, a trade around an event.
\iqlookfor{Size and sensitivity; blocks and capital.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:2}
Randomise the allocation, fix the benchmark (arrival) and the difficulty model in advance, measure all orders including cancelled ones (opportunity
cost), and look at reversion and fill rates, so that a broker cannot pick easy orders, finish only the easy parts, or trade to its benchmark.
\iqlookfor{Randomisation; a fixed benchmark; completeness.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:3}
With 600 orders a month over six brokers and a noise of 30 basis points, about 16 months adjusted; fewer brokers, more flow, better difficulty
adjustment (less residual noise) or variance reduction with pre-trade covariates shorten it.
\iqlookfor{The power calculation; the levers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:4}
The decision time and price, the order's instructions and changes, the broker and algorithm with parameters, every \omterm{def:mx:the-almgren-chriss-framework:parent}{child order} and fill with venue
and time stamps, and the wheel's allocation decision and its random seed.
\iqlookfor{Decision data; the allocation record.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:5}
It saves cost once the best broker is clear, but it starves the others of flow (their estimates decay), confounds time trends with allocation, and
brokers may react to their share. Use floors, forgetting, and a uniform period to start.
\iqlookfor{Exploration versus exploitation; drift; incentives.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:the-buy-side-trading-desk-and-its-brokers:6}
Ask for the order-level data and the method, show that one quarter cannot separate brokers a few basis points apart (standard errors), check the
difficulty of the orders you received, and fix whatever the data show (reversion, fill rates, venue choices) rather than argue the rank.
\iqlookfor{Statistical humility; order-level evidence.}
\end{solution}
