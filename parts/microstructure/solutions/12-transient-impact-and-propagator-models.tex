\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:1}
With $\E[\varepsilon_t\varepsilon_{t+1}]=0.27$ and signs $\pm1$, $\Pr(\text{same})-\Pr(\text{different})=0.27$, so $\Pr(\text{same})=(1+0.27)/2=63.5\%$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:2}
With a constant kernel each trade's impact is permanent, and persistent signs keep adding impacts of the same sign: the price would drift in the direction
of the flow, predictably, for as long as the signs' memory lasts.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:3}

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:4}
$\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&e^{-1}\\e^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}$; the round trip costs $\tfrac12(1+1-2e^{-1})=1-e^{-1}=0.632$, positive. To sell one unit,
$x\propto\Gamma^{-1}\mathbf 1$, which is symmetric: half at each time.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:5}
Without memory, each trade's impact decays and nothing replaces it on average: price changes over long lags are smaller than the sum of short ones, so
the variance per lag falls (0.32 to 0.18).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:6}
$\alpha-1=0.5$. The measured 0.62 is steeper because the fundamentalists' orders, whose signs follow the gap to the value rather than long runs, dilute
the noise takers' memory, and the fit uses lags 2 to 50 of a sample of 10,764 orders.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:7}
The eigenvector of the most negative eigenvalue ($-0.181$ on the round-trip subspace, a profit of 0.091) alternates buys and sells in seven legs: $+0.20$
at the start, $-0.26$ at the fourth trade, $+0.33$ at the seventh, $-0.37$ at the eleventh, and so on symmetrically. The kernel stays near its full value
for about $\tau$ and then falls abruptly, so its spectrum is negative at the frequency of this oscillation: each leg trades against the price its
predecessor left before that impact has decayed, and after it has, in a pattern that earns more than it pays.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:8}
Positive definiteness rules out profitable round trips but not transaction-triggered manipulation: a kernel that is positive definite but not convex
($1<\kappa\le2$ in the chapter) makes the cheapest sale include buys. The safe condition is a convex non-increasing decay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:1}
\textbf{1.} Order signs with a power-law autocorrelation, exponent about 0.6 (Hurst 0.7).
\textbf{2.} Splitting power-law metaorders into equal pieces: $C(\ell)\sim\ell^{-(\alpha-1)}$.
\textbf{3.} 0.27, 0.106 and 0.023; an exponent of 0.62.
\textbf{4.} 0.52, 1.05 and 2.3 ticks.
\textbf{5.} $p_t=\sum_{s<t}G(t-s)\varepsilon_s$; $\E[\varepsilon_{t-k}\Delta p_t]=\sum_nK(n)C(|k-n|)$.
\textbf{6.} 0.541, 0.452 and 0.419 ticks.
\textbf{7.} Market 1.98 falling to 1.35; model 0.29 rising to 1.34; shuffled 0.32 falling to 0.18.
\textbf{8.} The kernel's decay exactly compensates the signs' memory, leaving prices diffusive.
\textbf{9.} $\tfrac12x^\top\Gamma x$, $\Gamma_{ij}=G(|t_i-t_j|)$.
\textbf{10.} Minimise $\tfrac12x^\top\Gamma x-\mu(\mathbf 1^\top x-X)$: $\Gamma x=\mu\mathbf 1$.
\textbf{11.} Blocks of $X/(\rho T+2)$ at both ends and a rate $\rho X/(\rho T+2)$ between: the first block uses the existing book, the last uses the refilled
book with no later trade to pay for it.
\textbf{12.} 1,000 shares, 4,000 an hour, 1,000 shares.
\textbf{13.} A round trip with negative expected cost; a cheapest execution with opposite trades.
\textbf{14.} Impact must decay as a convex non-increasing function of time.
\textbf{15.} $-0.136$, $-0.042$, $-0.008$, $-0.002$, $+0.028$, $+0.091$, $+0.213$.
\textbf{16.} $\kappa=1.5$ and 1.8 (two buys each), and every $\kappa>2$.
\textbf{17.} The Gaussian kernel's matrix is singular on a fine grid: the boundary of positive definiteness.
\textbf{18.} \emph{Named result}: with $G(t)=e^{-(t/0.3)^\kappa}$ on 21 trades, the best round trip earns 0.028, 0.091 and 0.213 for $\kappa=2.2$, 2.5 and 3 and
loses for every $\kappa\le2$; manipulation of the transaction-triggered kind starts at $\kappa>1$, where convexity is lost.
\textbf{19.} Flat then dropping is $\kappa>1$: at least transaction-triggered manipulation, and profitable round trips if the drop is sharp ($\kappa>2$).
\textbf{20.} Convex and non-increasing.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:1}
Each trade's impact decays (transient impact), and the decay offsets the predictable continuation of order flow; liquidity providers who see the flow's
persistence adjust quotes so that prices stay close to a martingale.
\iqlookfor{Transient impact; compensation; liquidity providers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:2}
Signs and pre-trade mids; the sign autocorrelation and the cross-correlation of signs with later mid changes; solve the Toeplitz system for the kernel's
increments (Levinson); check that the fitted model makes prices diffusive.
\iqlookfor{The linear system; a diagnostic.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:3}
The quadratic form of the kernel must be non-negative on round trips (positive definiteness); to exclude transaction-triggered manipulation too, the decay
must be convex and non-increasing.
\iqlookfor{Positive definiteness; convexity.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:4}
The first block takes the liquidity already in the book; the continuous part trades at the book's refill rate; the last block takes liquidity that will
never have to be paid for by later trades, since the execution ends.
\iqlookfor{Resilience; end effects.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:5}
$\tfrac12x^\top\Gamma x$ is $O(n^2)$; for an exponential kernel a running sum makes it $O(n)$, for a sum of exponentials $O(nk)$, and for general kernels an
FFT convolution on a regular grid is $O(n\log n)$.
\iqlookfor{Complexity; recursive or FFT tricks.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:transient-impact-and-propagator-models:6}
Probably not a bug: the fitted kernel is not convex (or not positive definite), and the optimiser is exploiting it. Fix the model, by fitting within
convex kernels or projecting the fit, rather than the optimiser.
\iqlookfor{Transaction-triggered manipulation; model constraints.}
\end{solution}
