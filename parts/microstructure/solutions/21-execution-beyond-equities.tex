\begin{solution}{exo:mx:execution-beyond-equities:2}
Selling 200 fronts: 150 at 4,999.75 and 50 at 4,999.50, an average of 4,999.6875; buying 200 backs: 100 at 5,010.25 and 100 at 5,010.50, an average of
5,010.375. The spread is sold at $-10.6875$ against a mid of $-10.25$: 0.4375 points a contract.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:execution-beyond-equities:4}
An implied-in price is built from the outright books, so it is the legging price; the direct spread book was already better at its top. Implied
liquidity makes the spread and outright books one pool and lets the roll trade atomically: no risk that the second leg's price moves after the first.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:execution-beyond-equities:5}
Last-look streams quote tighter because they can decline the requests that would lose them money; the client pays for that only on the rejected
requests (0.007 basis points here). It would not be cheaper if rejections were frequent, holds long, or the rejected requests' information were used
against the client.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:execution-beyond-equities:6}
The pool's marginal cost is its fee plus about twice the price move per dollar ($2x/R$); it equals the exchange's marginal impact cost after about
USD~1.4 million of a USD~1 billion pool.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:execution-beyond-equities:7}
Two dealers, 47.76 basis points: higher leakage makes each extra dealer cost more than the competition it adds.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:execution-beyond-equities:8}
The quote paid is only the accepted request's price; the rejected requests' lost moves (and the re-requests at the next stream) are the cost of last
look, 0.007 basis points on average here: 0.087 in all, not 0.080.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:mx:execution-beyond-equities:1}

\textbf{2.} Implied in: a spread price from the two outrights; implied out: an outright price from the spread and the other outright.
\textbf{3.} 0.88 basis points by legging, 0.45 through the spread book.
\textbf{4.} Atomic execution and one pool of liquidity; no better price here.
\textbf{5.} See the definitions.
\textbf{6.} Transparency and disclosure; last look as a risk control, not to gather information.
\textbf{7.} The provider observes the move over its hold; above its threshold it rejects, and the client pays that move at the next stream: 0.007
basis points on average.
\textbf{8.} Rejections of 7.7\% of requests; 0.007 basis points.
\textbf{9.} See the definition.
\textbf{10.} 49.27 of markup, $-6.77$ of competition, 3.0 of leakage: 45.50.
\textbf{11.} A fourth dealer adds 1.46 basis points of competition, less than its 1.5 of leakage.
\textbf{12.} USD~1.375 million to the pool and the rest to the exchange; 0.36 basis points.
\textbf{13.} On strategies that send many orders and cancels (100 orders in ten seconds on a large exchange), not on a twelve-slice schedule.
\textbf{14.} \emph{Named result}: all-in 1.81 basis points in the index future (the roll through the spread book, 25\% of it), 0.54 in EUR/USD (last look,
1.3\%), 45.50 in the corporate bond (RFQ competition net of leakage saves 8.3\%) and 21.14 in bitcoin (the pool leg saves 1.7\%).
\textbf{15.} The order's size relative to each market's depth: from 0.025\% of a day's volume to 250\%.
\textbf{16.} A \omterm{def:mx:the-limit-order-book:orders}{limit order} in a book, a request to a stream, an auction among dealers, a swap against a pool.
\textbf{17.} One scheduler (\texttt{firm.acexec}) and one adapter per market implementing ``trade this slice''.
\textbf{18.} The volumes and the bond dealers' markup.
\textbf{19.} Electronic trading was 59\% of FX turnover in April 2025, and a customer could choose among more than 15 disclosed multi-dealer platforms.
\textbf{20.} What a \omterm{def:mx:the-almgren-chriss-framework:parent}{child order} is, and what it costs to find the other side.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:execution-beyond-equities:1}
Through the calendar-spread book (and implied prices), not by legging, over the roll period's liquid days, watching the spread's value against its
fair carry; the risks are legging, a crowded roll window, and the spread moving against a large position.
\iqlookfor{Spread book; timing; legging risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:execution-beyond-equities:2}
Record, per request, the price move from request to rejection and the price obtained on the retry; the cost is the average of those moves over all
requests, plus the information the rejected requests may have leaked; compare with the provider's disclosed hold and symmetry.
\iqlookfor{Measuring the lost moves; disclosure.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:execution-beyond-equities:3}
Until one more dealer's expected improvement (competition) is smaller than the leakage it causes; it depends on the dispersion of dealers' prices,
the leakage per losing dealer, and the bond's liquidity.
\iqlookfor{The trade-off; its parameters.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:execution-beyond-equities:4}
A scheduler asks for a quantity by a time; the adapter implements ``trade this slice'' (place and manage orders, request streams, run an RFQ, swap) and
reports fills and costs in a common format. Keep market-specific state inside the adapter.
\iqlookfor{Separation of schedule and mechanism; a common fill record.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:execution-beyond-equities:5}
Equalise marginal costs: the exchange's marginal impact against the pool's fee plus price move ($2x/R$ for a constant-product pool), including gas and
the risk of front-running on chain.
\iqlookfor{Marginal costs; on-chain risks.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:execution-beyond-equities:6}
Estimate the cost of unwinding it (spread plus impact at the bond's volume, over the days needed), the inventory risk while holding it, hedges
available (rates futures, index CDS), the client's information, and the competition for the trade; quote a price that covers them.
\iqlookfor{Unwind cost; risk and hedges; competition.}
\end{solution}
