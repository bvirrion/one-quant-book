\begin{solution}{exo:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:2}
The cost has the term $-\tau\alpha x$: holding shares while the drift is positive earns the drift, so a seller keeps more of the position when $\alpha>0$
and sells less; when $\alpha<0$ it sells faster.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:4}
Acting on a forecast means trading faster or slower than the schedule, which costs temporary impact; a forecast that decays within a few steps changes the
price by little before it is gone, so there is little to capture for the impact spent: 0.19 basis points at a twentieth of a day and USD~0.5 a day, 3.0 even
at USD~2.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:5}
It changes speed so much after moves that it pays heavy impact in bursts: its mean cost rises to 81.7 basis points, more than the risk it removes is worth
at this risk aversion (certainty equivalent 103.6 against 101.9).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:6}
It trades more while the market is liquid and waits while it is dry (60\,000 against 20\,000 shares at mid-order), paying the dry regime's quadruple impact
less often; Almgren--Chriss trades on schedule whatever the regime.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:7}
Acting on the noisy observation as if it were exact saves 8.3 basis points (standard error 2.0) instead of 41; weighting it by one half, its signal-to-total
variance ratio (the Bayes estimate of $\alpha$), saves 20.3. Noise makes the policy trade on errors; shrinking the forecast recovers half the value.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:8}
The saving depends on the signal's strength and half-life relative to each order's duration and size, it is measured on the backtest's orders, and it
assumes the signal is as good out of sample and observed without noise (exercise~7: noise cuts it from 41 to 8 basis points); and the schedule takes more
risk. Value it per order type, out of sample, with the risk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:1}
\textbf{1.} Minimise $\E\sum(\eta u^2/\tau+\lambda\sigma^2\tau x^2-\tau\alpha x)$; state $(x,\alpha)$.
\textbf{2.} Quadratic cost and linear dynamics: the value function is quadratic, the policy linear; Riccati recursion backward.
\textbf{3.} With $\phi=0$ and no drift the gains reproduce the discrete Almgren--Chriss trajectory.
\textbf{4.} Half-life 0.05: 0.19, 0.75, 2.98; 0.2: 1.1, 4.5, 17.2; 1: 3.1, 11.5, 41.0 basis points for USD~0.5, 1 and 2 a day.
\textbf{5.} Faster when the price has moved in the order's favour.
\textbf{6.} 60.9, 90.5, 101.9 basis points.
\textbf{7.} Means 61.2, 62.2, 66.2, 81.7; deviations 88.7, 86.4, 80.4, 66.2; certainty equivalents 100.5, 99.4, 98.6, 103.6.
\textbf{8.} After a gain, cutting the remaining risk is worth some impact; the static schedule cannot do it.
\textbf{9.} 0.0893 and 0.0834; blocks of 0.0876 (formula 0.0833).
\textbf{10.} Resilience of 10 a day makes impact decay fast compared with the day, so spreading evenly is nearly optimal.
\textbf{11.} Normal and dry (four times the impact), staying 0.9 and 0.7; 60\,000 against 20\,000 shares at mid-order.
\textbf{12.} 73.2 against 91.5 basis points: 18.3 saved.
\textbf{13.} Signal (up to 41 for a strong, persistent one), liquidity (18), transient impact (6.6\% of impact), adaptation (3.2\% of the certainty equivalent).
\textbf{14.} The signal and the liquidity regimes.
\textbf{15.} It raises it: 212 against 173 basis points of standard deviation for the strongest signal.
\textbf{16.} Split real orders by the signal's value at arrival and compare schedules with and without it on matched orders, out of sample.
\textbf{17.} \emph{Named result}: on the block, a drift forecast with a half-life of a twentieth of a day saves 0.19 to 3.0 basis points for standard deviations of
USD~0.5 to 2 a day, and one with a half-life of a day saves 3.1 to 41; the saving grows with strength and faster than linearly with persistence.
\textbf{18.} About a fifth of a day (1 to 17 basis points) for these strengths; shorter signals are worth little.
\textbf{19.} Liquidity regimes: an 18-basis-point saving from data the desk sees in real time.
\textbf{20.} The chance to trade when trading is cheap and the price is on its side, at the cost of more risk.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:1}
Sell faster now, before the expected fall, at the cost of more impact; how much faster depends on the forecast's size and horizon against my impact.
\iqlookfor{Direction; the trade-off with impact; horizon.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:2}
State (holdings, signal), control the trade; cost impact plus risk minus drift times holdings; dynamics linear with the OU persistence; solve by Riccati.
\iqlookfor{State, cost, dynamics, solution method.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:3}
A first block uses the book as it is, the continuous part trades at the refill rate, and the last block uses liquidity no later trade will pay for.
\iqlookfor{Resilience; end effects.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:4}
Speed up, in the mean--variance sense: the gain pays for cutting risk (aggressive in the money); moderately, since strong adaptation pays too much impact.
\iqlookfor{Direction; moderation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:5}
Same orders, same market paths (common random numbers), many repetitions, costs against the same arrival price, report means with standard errors and the
risk.
\iqlookfor{Common random numbers; errors; risk.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:beyond-almgren-chriss:6}
Estimate the signal's predictive power for returns over execution horizons, simulate the policy on historical orders with the signal as known at arrival,
and compare with the schedule without it, out of sample and net of the extra impact.
\iqlookfor{Out of sample; horizon matching; net of impact.}
\end{solution}
