\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:mx:smart-order-routing} --- Smart Order Routing}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:1}
The farthest venue is 360 microseconds away: A's piece waits $360-50=310$ microseconds, C's $360-200=160$.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:2}
The spray still fills at A first (50 microseconds). The provider hears of it 95 microseconds later, at 145; its cancels reach C at 150, B at 195, D at
195 and E at 245. The spray reaches B at 120, before the cancel, and C, D and E at 200, 280 and 360, after it: it finds the fast quotes at A and B,
1\,600 of the 2\,500 shares (64\%).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:3}
At the ask, 1\,300 shares pay only fees: $500\times0.30+200\times(0.30+0.10+0.30-0.10)=270$ cents. The clean-up pays a cent more on 1\,200 shares
(1\,200 cents) and fees of $1\,000\times0.30+200\times0.30=360$ cents. Above 100.01: $(270+1\,200+360)/2\,500=0.73$
cents a share.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:4}
The race is lost because A's fill always comes first and the cancels are faster than the router's later pieces. Random latencies sometimes make a far
piece arrive before A's fill has been reported, so it finds the fast quote; they help the spray exactly as they hurt the synchronised sweep.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:5}
Each venue fills only part of its order on most scenarios, so posting more than needed raises the expected shares executed toward $X$. The over-fill
penalty (1.2 cents a share, more than the 0.7 or so each passive fill earns) makes the scenarios in which most venues fill costly, which bounds the
overbooking (1\,778 shares posted).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:6}
It rests everything at the venue with the 0.25-cent rebate, which has the longest queue (2\,500 shares): only 22.0\% fills, and the penalty for the rest
makes it cost 0.77 cents a share, almost as much as a \omterm{def:mx:the-limit-order-book:orders}{market order}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:7}
Posting pays: each passive fill earns more than the 0.5-cent over-fill penalty. The optimum posts about 4\,030 shares (1\,000 at A, B and D, 751 at C,
277 in the dark venue) and ``costs'' $-0.24$ cents a share out of sample, a gain that exists only because the penalty undervalues the risk of buying
too much.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:mx:smart-order-routing:8}
E's 500 shares were there when the router looked; 300 of them belonged to a provider who cancelled after the router's fill at A reached it. The
router's own spray caused the loss. Synchronised, E filled 100\% and, with the taker rebate, became the cheapest venue.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:mx:smart-order-routing:1}
\textbf{1.} It splits an order across venues and chooses venue, price, size, type and timing of each piece.
\textbf{2.} A to E at 50, 120, 200, 280 and 360 microseconds; take fees 0.30, 0.30, 0.10, 0.30 and $-0.10$ cents; each shows 200 slow and 300 fast
shares at 100.01 and 1\,000 at 100.02; the fast provider sits at A with links of 45 to 195 microseconds and cancels everywhere after a fill.
\textbf{3.} See the definitions.
\textbf{4.} The consolidated book was deeper after the entrant arrived, and it supplied less liquidity where its better quotes were traded through.
\textbf{5.} Spray arrives at 50, 120, 200, 280, 360; cancels at 100, 150, 200 and 250 at B to E.
\textbf{6.} 52\%, and 0.73 cents a share above the ask with the clean-up and fees.
\textbf{7.} Each piece waits the farthest latency minus its own: 310, 240, 160, 80 and 0 microseconds.
\textbf{8.} \emph{Named result}: a spray fills 52\% of the 2\,500 displayed shares and costs 0.73 cents a share fee-adjusted; synchronised, it fills
100\% at 0.18 cents, the average take fee.
\textbf{9.} Up to 10\% jitter nothing changes; at 20\% the synchronised sweep fills 99.3\% (the whole level on 94\% of trials) and at 40\% 90.3\%; the
spray rises to 68.6\%.
\textbf{10.} A 100\%, B 44\%, C 41\%, D 40\%, E 40\%.
\textbf{11.} Spraying: A, E, C, B, D (0.30 to 0.72 cents); synchronised: E, C, then A, B, D ($-0.10$ to 0.30): a ranking measures the router's own
routing.
\textbf{12.} By fee (E, C): 99.8\% sprayed, 100\% synchronised, about zero cost; by distance (A, B): 71.2\% sprayed at 0.59 cents, 100\% synchronised at
0.30.
\textbf{13.} Its first fill came from far venues, from which the fast provider's cancels could not overtake the other piece.
\textbf{14.} $(h+f)M-\sum_k(h+r_k)\min(L_k,(\xi_k-Q_k)^+)+\lambda_u(X-A)^++\lambda_o(A-X)^+$.
\textbf{15.} 0.80 (100\%), 0.77 (22.0\%), 0.74 (38.7\%), 0.20 (61.3\%) and 0.03 cents (76.4\%).
\textbf{16.} 0.03 cents a share (0.06 without it).
\textbf{17.} \omterm{def:mx:the-limit-order-book:orders}{Limit orders} sent to the venues paying the largest rebates executed worse: routing for rebates does not maximise execution quality.
\textbf{18.} Synchronised arrivals (\omterm{def:mx:smart-order-routing:spray}{quote fade} after a first fill), minimum fill quantities in the dark (pinging), randomised child sizes and timing
(pattern detection).
\textbf{19.} Synchronise the sweeps, rank venues on measured fills, not displays, and allocate passive orders by expected fills, not rebates.
\textbf{20.} Around quotes that will not be there and queues that will not clear.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:smart-order-routing:1}
\omterm{def:mx:smart-order-routing:spray}{Quote fade}: pieces arrived one after another, the first fills alerted fast providers who cancelled elsewhere. Check the arrival times per venue and
the cancels just before them; synchronise.
\iqlookfor{Arrival-time spread; the provider's reaction.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:smart-order-routing:2}
Measure one-way latency per venue (from acknowledgements, continuously), hold each piece by the difference to the slowest, and add a margin for jitter.
It fails when latencies vary more than the reaction time of the fastest provider, or when a venue's gateway queues.
\iqlookfor{Latency estimation; jitter as the limit.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:smart-order-routing:3}
From the router's own orders: fills against time resting, conditional on queue position and size; the difficulty is censoring (cancelled orders),
selection (the router only posts where it expects fills) and the dependence on its own behaviour.
\iqlookfor{Censoring and selection; conditioning on queue.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:smart-order-routing:4}
Choose a \omterm{def:mx:the-limit-order-book:orders}{market order} $M$ and limits $L_k$; each limit fills what leaves the front of its queue beyond the queue ahead, up to $L_k$; minimise the
expected fee-adjusted cost plus penalties for under- and over-execution (Cont and Kukanov), solved on scenarios or by stochastic approximation.
\iqlookfor{Fill as $\min(L,(\xi-Q)^+)$; penalties; convexity.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:smart-order-routing:5}
Answer with the routing logic (expected fills and cost, not rebates) and show it: fill rates, time to fill and markouts by venue for the client's own
orders, against the rebates, as Battalio, Corwin and Jennings's analysis would.
\iqlookfor{Conflict of interest; evidence by venue.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:mx:smart-order-routing:6}
Per venue, symbol group and order type: shares sent, filled, time to fill, fees, latencies and markouts; decayed with a half-life, with a prior for
new venues; the router ranks venues by expected cost including non-fills and explores a little to keep estimates fresh.
\iqlookfor{Fields; ageing; exploration.}
\end{solution}
