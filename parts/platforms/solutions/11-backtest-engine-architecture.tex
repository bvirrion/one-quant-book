\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:2}
A vectorised backtest has no orders, queues or fills, so a quoting strategy run there would trade nothing and return zero: a result that
looks like an answer. Refusing makes the mismatch between the strategy and the level visible.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:3}
The level, a parameter (latency, queue model, bar width, inventory limit), or the engine's code hash: the identifier hashes the whole
manifest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:4}
No: $-12 \pm 13$ is within one standard error of zero. The gap is: level~4 is below level~3 in all twenty sessions, by \$90 on average

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:5}
A shared fill is the same trade in both runs: the same side, price and moment, against the same aggressive order of the session; what the
price does in the next ten seconds is mostly the session's, so the markouts should agree. If they did not, the classification would be
pairing different trades, or the two runs' markets would have drifted apart before the fills --- either way the decomposition could not be
read.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:6}
The strategic response of the other participants: the recorded limit orders do not requote around the strategy's orders, market makers do
not widen or narrow, and informed traders do not change their timing. An agent population (Book~10's \texttt{firm.agentmkt}) running in the
simulator would add it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:7}
For seed~3 with the limit of three lots: \$417, $-\$95.5$ and $-\$246$ at levels~2, 3 and~4 (1\,462, 583 and 690 fills); with ten lots:
\$350, $-\$110$ and $-\$472$ (1\,580, 670 and 818 fills). More inventory costs a little at levels~2 and~3 and a great deal at level~4. The
reactive-only fills' share of the markout gap rises from 129\% to 147\% (above 100\% because in this session the replay-only fills lose
too): with a larger limit the strategy keeps quoting after it has been run over, and at level~4 those quotes are real.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:8}
With two hand ports, the difference between the runs has three candidate causes --- the market model, the ports, and the backtesters'
conventions --- and the claim picks one without evidence. Run one strategy object through one engine at both levels so the ports cannot
differ, then compare the event logs: the first differing fill says whether the simulator matched differently or whether the strategy was
simply in the book (a reactive-only fill). A simulator that is worse because the strategy's orders are real is not a bug.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item One strategy interface, one \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:event}{event model} and clock, one \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:dal}{data-access layer} and one \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:results}{results store} across the levels.
\item A target rule for level~1 (no orders), run bar by bar at level~2; a quoting strategy with market and fill callbacks for levels~2
to~4.
\item The order of simultaneous events: market, then fills, then the strategy's orders and cancels, so that equal inputs give equal runs and
levels are comparable.
\item Level, strategy and engine code hashes, data hashes, parameters, seed and summary results; hashing it makes equal inputs one
identifier and any change a new one.
\item So that the run records which data it read, whatever the files are called, and ``same data'' can be checked.
\item The touch at the end of each one-second bar with trades; a quote fills in full when the bar trades at its price.
\item A shadow takes a place in the recorded queue and fills when recorded executions reach it, without changing the recording; a level-4
order rests in the simulator's queue and trades against the replayed orders, changing what they meet afterwards.
\item Every recorded addition as a limit order, cancellation as a cancel, trade as a market order of its size.
\item The strategic reaction of other participants.
\item By the simulator standing in for the venue, as Book~7 used an agent inside the synthetic market as the stand-in for live trading.
\item Level~2: \$228 ($\pm 29$) from 841 fills; level~3: $-\$12$ ($\pm 13$) from 333; level~4: $-\$102$ ($\pm 18$) from 434.
\item Level~2 against level~3, and level~3 against level~4 (paired over sessions); level~3 is not distinguishable from zero.
\item All twenty; by \$90 an hour ($\pm 7.4$).
\item Within their first five fills in every session, at a median of 11~seconds into the hour.
\item A touch fill needs only a trade at the price, never a place in a queue.
\item \textbf{Named result.} P\&L per hour \$228 at level~2, $-\$12$ at level~3 and $-\$102$ at level~4; the reactive-only fills explain 101\%
of the \$68.4 ten-second-markout gap between replay and \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:reactive}{reactive simulation}, the shared fills none, and the other \$22 of the \$90 gap is
inventory marked later.
\item The 241 reactive-only fills per session, which lose \$69.2 over ten seconds: trades that happen only because the strategy's orders are
real, in the queue or left at the touch.
\item About \$22 an hour: the P\&L of the inventory beyond ten seconds.
\item That it has no edge: it breaks even at best behind the queue and loses where its orders count; the bar backtest's profit is the touch
fill's.
\item It makes the difference between two levels a property of the markets, because the strategy object, \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:event}{event model} and data are the same.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:1}
At the fills: how many, where and when, at each level. Bar backtests with touch fills ignore queue position and adverse selection; the
order-book simulation adds both. Check that it is the same strategy code, then decompose the difference by fills and markouts.
\iqlookfor{Fill models, queues and adverse selection; a decomposition, not a guess.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:2}
By a fixed, documented rule --- for example market data before fills before the strategy's own actions --- and then by arrival order, so
that runs are deterministic and levels comparable. Never by the order of a hash map.
\iqlookfor{Determinism by a total order.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:3}
A manifest per run: code hashes of the strategy and engine, data snapshot hashes, parameters, seed and environment; an identifier derived from
it; outputs stored under it with a query API; data read only through a layer that returns hashes.
\iqlookfor{Content identity of code and data, not file names or dates.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:4}
Its own impact: its orders take fills that went to others, stay visible at the touch, and change what later orders meet. Run it in a
reactive simulator on the same session and decompose the difference into shared fills, replay-only fills and reactive-only fills by
markout.
\iqlookfor{Shadow orders against real orders; a counterfactual measured on the same data.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:5}
Two strategy forms (a target rule and an event-driven strategy) over one context; adapters per backtester, the backtesters unchanged; one
\omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:event}{event model} with a total order; a \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:dal}{data-access layer} with content hashes; a \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:results}{results store} keyed by manifest hashes; tests that each adapter
reproduces the backtester called directly.
\iqlookfor{Adapters over existing engines, one context, reproducible runs.}
\end{solution}
