\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:pl:post-trade-systems} --- Post-Trade Systems}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:1}
The keys decide which confirmation is compared with which trade; a tolerance there would pair a trade with someone else's confirmation and
then report a false difference, or a false match. The compared fields are where a difference is information --- and only the price has a
legitimate reason (precision) to differ slightly.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:2}
Rounding to four decimals moves the price by at most half a unit in the fourth decimal, \$0.00005; at that tolerance every correct confirmation
matches, which is where the plateau of the chapter's figure begins.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:3}
91 of the 138 breaks were fixed in the evening windows; 47 failed, 34\%.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:4}
A wrong account on one confirmation breaks one trade and is caught by matching. A wrong account in the SSI table is copied into every
instruction for that counterparty and market, matches on both sides because both use it, and fails every such trade until the agent rejects
them.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:5}
By a one-to-many group: the custodian's key is mapped to the two allocations, whose quantities are summed to 3\,000 before the comparison, so
the two sides agree and no break is raised.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:6}
Because only trades due to settle by Friday are in Friday's positions. Under T+2 the trades of Thursday and Friday settle the next week, and 6 of
the 10 fails were among them; the other 4 each made one position break.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:7}
47, 35, 22, 14, 11 and 10 fails for 3 to 8 people: eight people are needed to match T+2's 10 exactly, seven to come within one.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:post-trade-systems:8}
The extra percentage point is five wrong prices now accepted as matches: the breaks did not go away, they were hidden until the cash fails
to agree. A match rate is only meaningful at a tolerance derived from the counterparties' precision, and should be reported with the number of
breaks found later by \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:recon}{reconciliation}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:pl:post-trade-systems:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Security, side, counterparty, trade date and fund are keys and must be exact; price, quantity, settlement date and account are compared,
the price within a tolerance.
\item The investment manager's agreement that the terms are right, which lets the trade settle without further intervention.
\item They round the average price to four decimals, so their correct prices differ from the firm's by up to \$0.00005; at \$0.0000001, 60.4\%
of trades match.
\item From \$0.00005 to \$0.01, at 95.8\%.
\item Five wrong prices are accepted, and the rate rises to 96.8\%.
\item A deliver versus payment of 9\,400 shares for \$917\,317.25 on the settlement date: security, quantity, amount and date from the trade,
the fund's account from the canonical model, the broker's agent and account from the SSI table.
\item Proposed, approved by a second person, effective from a date, never edited in place.
\item The keys: one-to-many and aggregated positions and identifiers that differ between the two sides.
\item A kind, an owner and an age.
\item A break that survives several \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:recon}{reconciliations} is one nobody understands, and it is where losses and misstatements accumulate.
\item The next working day (08:00--17:00) under T+2, the trade date's evening (17:00--21:00) under T+1: the cut-offs quoted in the chapter.
\item About 26 hours a day; the day gives three people 27, the evening 12.
\item 10 trades, \$2.74 million, under T+2; 47, \$19.17 million, under T+1.
\item Wrong accounts and missing confirmations, 18 each.
\item 4 position breaks, all one to three days old, under T+2; 24 under T+1, 7 under a day, 12 one to three days and 5 three to seven.
\item \textbf{Named result.} At a price tolerance of \$0.0000001, 60.4\% of trades match automatically, from \$0.00005 to \$0.01 95.8\%, at
\$0.05 96.8\% with five wrong prices accepted. Of 138 breaks in the week, 10 remain unfixed at the cut-off under a two-day cycle and 47 under a
one-day cycle, failing \$2.74 million and \$19.17 million of trades.
\item Seven people bring the fails to 11, eight to 10.
\item Fails fall to 13 with the same three people.
\item Halving the errors: it costs reference-data and workflow work once, instead of four more salaries every year, and it lowers every later
cost of the breaks, not only the fails.
\item It shortens the time to fix a break without changing how many there are.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:post-trade-systems:1}
An item on which two records that should agree differ. The report gives the key, both values, the kind, the owner, when it was opened and its
age bucket, sorted by value and age.
\iqlookfor{Kind, owner, age; value first.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:post-trade-systems:2}
Exact keys; zero tolerance on quantities, dates and accounts; a price tolerance per counterparty from the precision it uses; then check the match
rate against the breaks found later, to make sure the tolerance does not hide errors.
\iqlookfor{Tolerance from precision, per counterparty.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:post-trade-systems:3}
Map each fund and strategy position to the custodian account through reference data, sum the firm's positions into the custodian's keys
(one-to-many), compare, and raise breaks with owners; keep the mapping versioned so yesterday's \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:recon}{reconciliation} can be rerun.
\iqlookfor{Key mapping and one-to-many groups.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:post-trade-systems:4}
Allocation, confirmation and \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:confirmation}{affirmation} move to the trade date; the time to fix a break shrinks to hours, so fewer breaks (SSIs, early
allocation) matter more than more people; the \omterm{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:service}{position service} must track settled and unsettled positions and funding moves forward a day.
\iqlookfor{Fewer breaks, earlier.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:post-trade-systems:5}
The canonical trade from booking; a matching engine with per-counterparty tolerances; \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:confirmation}{affirmation} tracking against the cut-off; an approved,
dated SSI store generating instructions; \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:recon}{reconciliations} of positions, cash and trades with one-to-many and aggregate matching; one break queue
with kinds, owners and ageing; a straight-through rate by counterparty as the measure.
\iqlookfor{One break queue, measured upstream.}
\end{solution}
