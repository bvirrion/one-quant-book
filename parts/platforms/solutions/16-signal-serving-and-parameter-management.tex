\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management} --- Signal Serving and Parameter Management}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:1}
50 basis points of \$4 million is \$20\,000, 200 shares at \$100: two lots. 50 percent is \$2 million, 20\,000 shares: two hundred lots.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:2}
The raise is a step of 25 basis points (0.0025), above the 20 that need a second approver; the correction from 75 to 60 is a step of 15, below
it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:3}
0.7, version 3, fresh: the latest value was published at 60~seconds, 340~seconds earlier, within the five-minute bound.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:4}
All at once, ten strategies had the error and the first of ten monitors fired after five minutes; staged, only the canary's monitor could fire,
and its quiet session took fifteen. But all at once, ten strategies accrued exposure until detection, staged only one: 1.35 million
dollar-minutes against 53\,000.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:5}
What the strategy used is the value as known then (75 basis points): that is what explains its behaviour. What it should have used is the value
as known now (60): that is what the correction and any restatement of risk or P\&L need.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:6}
A drift check comparing what each server runs with what is declared would have shown the eighth server's old code; retiring flags instead of
repurposing them, and a staged rollout watched per server, would have limited the damage. A schema on parameter values would not: the flag's
value was valid, its meaning on that server was not.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:7}
With q10 as the canary, its monitor does not fire within the thirty minutes, the other nine receive the value at forty minutes, and the first of
their monitors fires five minutes later: detection 35 minutes after the change, a peak of \$1.31 million and 5.44 million dollar-minutes --- worse
than all at once. A canary whose monitor cannot see the error only delays the rollout; the canary must be a strategy on which the error would
show.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:8}
Versioned and reviewed, yes; typed and checked, no: a reviewer sees ``50'' in a diff and nothing says in what unit, whether it is in bounds or
how far it moved; the file says what is declared, not what each strategy runs; and a merge goes everywhere at once. A schema, approvals by
size, a drift check and a flag-based rollout are still needed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A canonical unit (a fraction of capital), accepted units (basis points, percent, fraction), bounds 0 to 2\%, a largest step of 50 basis
points, and two approvers above 20.
\item Refused (no unit); refused (outside the bounds and too large a step); accepted as 0.005.
\item Anyone but the author; one, or two above the threshold, distinct.
\item Valid time and knowledge time: what was in force at a moment, as known at another moment.
\item That any edit of a recorded line is detected, and where.
\item The declared fallback, with the status ``stale-fallback'' (or ``missing-fallback'' if never published).
\item The signal's owner, when the signal is designed.
\item Rolling a change out to chosen strategies and back; a source of drift and of latent behaviour.
\item One strategy running 0.5 where the store declares 0.005.
\item Orders carrying a flag repurposed for new code triggered old code on a server the new code had not reached.
\item Two lots and two hundred lots.
\item It fires in the first five-minute window after the change whose fills exceed 1.3 times the usual count; it knows each strategy's
counterfactual count, which a real monitor does not.
\item After 5 minutes for five strategies, 15 for three, 30 for one and 50 for one.
\item Five minutes; \$410\,000 at the peak and 1.35 million dollar-minutes.
\item Fifteen minutes; \$30\,000 and 53\,000 dollar-minutes.
\item \textbf{Named result.} All at once: detected after 5 minutes, with \$410\,000 of excess inventory at the peak and 1.35 million
dollar-minutes; staged: 15 minutes, \$30\,000 and 53\,000 dollar-minutes; schema check: refused at entry, no exposure.
\item Detection counts only after the damage; what limits damage is how many strategies hold the error until then, and whether it can enter
at all.
\item The schema with units: it costs least and stops the whole class of error.
\item Tighter bounds per strategy, a maximum step relative to the current value, and a comparison of the new value's effect in a simulator
before the rollout.
\item A unit, bounds, an owner, approvals, a history and a rollout plan.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:1}
Make the unit part of the value: typed parameters with a canonical unit, conversion on entry, bounds and step limits in a schema, and refusal
of anything without a unit.
\iqlookfor{Types and schemas, not care.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:2}
The signal goes stale; the service serves the declared fallback with a status the strategy can act on, and an alert goes to the signal's owner.
The fallback and the freshness bound are part of the signal's contract.
\iqlookfor{Freshness bounds and declared fallbacks.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:3}
Through the \omterm{def:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:store}{parameter store} with two approvals, behind a flag: to one canary strategy on which the change's effect is visible, watched against
its usual behaviour, then to a few, then to all, with automatic rollback when a monitor fires.
\iqlookfor{Staged rollout with a meaningful canary and rollback.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:4}
The difference between declared and running configuration; detect it by having every process report the configuration it loaded (with a
hash) and comparing with the store, continuously and at start-up.
\iqlookfor{Running configuration reported and compared.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:5}
A store of typed, schema-checked, bitemporal values with owners; proposals and approvals by change size; a hash-chained audit trail; flags for
staged rollouts with rollback; drift detection against running processes; a \omterm{def:pl:signal-serving-and-parameter-management:service}{signal service} with versions, freshness and fallbacks; and
post-mortem queries of what was live when.
\iqlookfor{Types, approvals, history, rollout, drift.}
\end{solution}
