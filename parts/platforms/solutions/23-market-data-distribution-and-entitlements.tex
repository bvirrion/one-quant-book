\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements} --- Market-Data Distribution and Entitlements}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:1}
Because subscribers consume at different speeds: with a queue per subscriber a slow one only delays itself, and each can have its own policy
(every message or conflated) and its own \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlement} check. A shared queue per topic would hold every subscriber to the pace of the slowest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:2}
Two: the venue counts each unique user identification and password combination (or each device), not each person.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:3}
22 servers at \$412 each, \$9\,064 a month; 45 servers fall in the 40 to 99 tier, a flat \$16\,490.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:4}
The screen, the risk service and the research dashboard: each wants the current state of each instrument, not its history. The strategy
reacts to every change of the book and may need the sequence (queue position, trades); it must be fast enough to keep up instead.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:5}
The feed brings about 400 messages a second and it can process 100: its queue grows by about 300 a second for the whole session, to 180\,267,
and a message published late in the session waits behind all of them. The age is set by the backlog, not by the 10~ms of work.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:6}
Cache the \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlement} decision per subscription on the platform, have the \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlement system} push each grant and revocation to the platform,
and invalidate or cut the subscription on a revocation; the check at delivery is then a flag lookup.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:7}
\$235\,664: the whole non-display shortfall remains, since display use was only \$22\,932 of the \$258\,596.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:8}
The distributor fee pays for the right to receive and distribute the data inside the firm; each use is licensed separately, by display
subscriber and by non-display subscriber or server. Every application that computes on the data is a non-display use to be counted and paid.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item It receives the feed once, gives each consumer its own queue and policy, checks \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlements}, and logs every use.
\item Strategy 20~$\mu$s, risk service 2~ms, research dashboard 10~ms per message, the screen 50~ms per refresh; the feed brings about 400
messages a second (240\,267 in ten minutes).
\item Its queue grows to 180\,267 messages, the mean age is 891~seconds, and it finishes half an hour after the session.
\item The risk service's largest age falls from 2.4~s to 32~ms; the dashboard's mean age from 891~s to 67~ms, processing 60\,012 messages
instead of 240\,267.
\item 1\,199 messages: the opening snapshot of every instrument's book, published at one instant.
\item A subject (user identifier or application), a source, and a use.
\item The subject keeps receiving until it next subscribes: 6\,005 updates to the revoked screen in the chapter.
\item By caching the decision per subscription and invalidating it when the \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlement system} pushes a change.
\item Display: each device or unique user identification; non-display: each device (usually a server) that receives and benefits from the
data, or each person who can modify the application in real time.
\item Servers that only transport, disseminate, aggregate, normalise or store the data --- provided the firm can identify them.
\item 40 display subscribers and no non-display use every month; the log shows 43 display identifiers and 4 servers in the first month, 53
and 22 in the last.
\item \$84 per professional display subscriber; non-display \$412 per subscriber up to 39, then \$16\,490 per firm up to 99.
\item \$1\,900 and \$10\,156.
\item \$258\,596: \$22\,932 for display and \$235\,664 for non-display.
\item Interest is added, and the underreporting (87.5\% of the reported units) is far above the 10\% at which the subscriber agreement adds the
audit's costs.
\item \textbf{Named result.} Reconciling three years of usage logs with the licences held finds a display shortfall of 3 to 13 user
identifiers and a non-display shortfall of 4 to 22 servers a month; back-billed at the venue's 2026 fees, \$258\,596, of which \$235\,664 for
the non-display use of the eleven applications.
\item At least as long as the look-back (three years here), in the venue's \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:unit}{unit of count}: identifiers and servers.
\item No: it is the venue's data delivered less often, and needs the same licence.
\item Log every subscription in the venue's units, check \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlements} by use, and reconcile the log with the licences every month.
\item Each use of the data, counted in the venue's unit, not the data's arrival at the firm.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:1}
Keeping only the latest update per key (instrument) in a consumer's queue, so that a consumer that cannot process every message sees current
state with bounded delay. Use it for screens, risk and monitoring; not for consumers that need the sequence.
\iqlookfor{Latest value per key; bounded age.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:2}
Give each consumer its own queue so it only delays itself; conflate it if it wants state; otherwise bound its queue, measure its lag, and
disconnect or alert when it falls too far behind rather than applying back-pressure to the feed.
\iqlookfor{Isolate, conflate, bound.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:3}
Display is a person viewing the data, non-display a machine using it; venues license and count them separately, so every program a developer
connects to a feed is a licensed use that must be registered and counted.
\iqlookfor{Every program is a use.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:4}
Three years of usage logs in the venue's units of count, the list of \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlements} with grants and revocations, the list of servers by role
(distribution against use), the reports filed, and the licences held.
\iqlookfor{Logs in the venue's units, for the look-back.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:5}
Feed handlers into a bus with topics and per-subscriber queues and policies; an \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlement system} with grants, revocations and uses, pushed
to the bus; a usage log in each venue's units; monthly reports and a \omterm{def:pl:post-trade-systems:recon}{reconciliation} with licences; a derived-data register; capacity per
subscriber monitored.
\iqlookfor{Distribution, \omterm{def:pl:market-data-distribution-and-entitlements:entitlement}{entitlement} and evidence together.}
\end{solution}
