\begin{solution}{exo:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:3}
$811\,160 / 56 = 14\,485$ records. With \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:partition}{partitioning} by instrument the query reads $811\,160 / 14\,477\,960 = 5.6\%$ of the day;

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:4}
Only the two \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:raw}{raw captures} say which line lost which packet and when each copy arrived: the question of a network or latency
investigation (was line B slower this morning? did the outage cause the gap?). Any question about the market itself (the book
at 10:03, the trades of instrument~2) is answered equally well by the arbitrated records, which contain each message once.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:5}
$311\times10^9 \times 11.9 = 3.70\times10^{12}$ bytes, 3.70~TB a day; $\times 252 = 0.93$~PB a year.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:6}
Five years is 4.67~PB, $4.67\times10^6$~GB; at USD~0.023 a GB-month that is about USD~1\,288\,000 a year, 6.6 times the tiered
policy's USD~194\,000. Almost all of the saving comes from moving the four older years to the archive.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:7}
The ratio falls slightly, from 4.70 to 4.67. Sorting by instrument makes the price column's steps smaller (no jumps between the
two instruments' price levels), but it breaks the columns that were regular in arrival order: sequence numbers no longer step by
exactly one, receive and exchange times jump back at the boundary and advance by larger, irregular amounts. On this session the
two effects nearly cancel. The lesson is the chapter's: measure the layout on the data.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:8}
A capture must keep up with the feed's peak rate, not its average, and it writes each byte once. On the laptop zlib at level~9
compresses at 3.0~MB/s against 34.5 at level~6 and 220 for zstd at level~1 in a columnar file, for 2\% more ratio than level~6;
at a full feed's peak it would fall behind within seconds and drop packets, and packets per call are too small to compress well
anyway. Compress the capture fast, in blocks, and recompress for ratio at night.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item 503\,024 packets; 258\,535 messages.
\item 255\,899: every message is sent on both lines, so almost every packet has a second copy on the other line; the difference
from exact halves is the packets each line lost and the duplicated packets.
\item Eight messages in four gaps. A live handler would request them from the retransmission service or rebuild the book from
the snapshot channel.
\item Arbitration and normalisation discard the duplicates, which line delivered first, the packet boundaries, the later copies'
arrival times and any field without a place in the record.
\item The executed-with-price message's printable flag (or the add order's symbol and tracking number).
\item 61.0, 56.0 and 11.9 bytes a message.
\item 3.32 and 4.70.
\item In arrival order it increases by exactly one at every record, so its deltas are a constant that compresses to almost
nothing.
\item The \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:raw}{raw capture} compressed fast for as long as investigations need it (weeks to months); the columnar, delta-encoded
records for the retention period (five years).
\item Framing, message sizes and redundancy are properties of the feed; the simulator sends one message per packet.
\item 37.95~TB a day.
\item 47.8~PB.
\item 3.70~TB a day, 0.93~PB a year, 4.67~PB in five years.
\item About USD~1\,989\,000 a year for \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:raw}{raw capture} of both lines and USD~194\,000 for the compressed columns.
\item It is the capacity the feed's operator plans for, a ceiling, not an average day.
\item \textbf{Named result.} At the options feed's planned capacity, full capture takes 9.56~PB a year as raw packets on both lines
(USD~1.99 million a year to keep five years in three tiers) and 0.93~PB a year as delta-encoded columns (USD~0.19 million): a
petabyte a year only in the lightest layout.
\item On both, by comparable factors: the layout divides bytes by about ten against \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:raw}{raw capture} of both lines, tiering divides the
cost of the same bytes by about six against standard storage.
\item It reads 5.6\% of the day instead of all of it; with millions of series, partitions per series become millions of tiny
files whose opening costs more than their reading, so the key must be coarser (the underlying, the date).
\item The fast one at capture time, where the feed's peak sets the pace; the slow one at night, when the day is rewritten once
for years of reading.
\item The \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:raw}{raw capture} briefly, for investigations, and the arbitrated events for years, in compressed, partitioned columns, in the
cheapest tier that its readers can tolerate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:1}
Raw data is the bytes as received, with their arrival times and duplicates; normalised data is one event per message in the
firm's layout. The normaliser can have bugs or lack a field, and only the \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:raw}{raw capture} lets you rerun it; normalised data is what
everyone reads.
\iqlookfor{Normalised is a function of raw, not the reverse.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:2}
Merge by sequence number: the first copy wins, later copies are dropped; hold a gap open for a short time in case the other line
fills it; then ask the retransmission service, or rebuild from a snapshot, and in a research capture record the gap's range.
\iqlookfor{Sequence numbers, duplicates, a timeout, and recovery or recording of the gap.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:3}
Messages a day (from the feed's published statistics, and its planned capacity) times bytes a message in each layout (measured
on a captured sample, not assumed) divided by the \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:ratio}{compression ratio} (measured on the same sample), times copies, times trading
days and years; then price it by tier and retention. Measure bytes a message and the ratio first.
\iqlookfor{Measurement over assumption; copies and tiers counted.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:4}
From the filters queries use: the date almost always, then the instrument or underlying. Too fine a key creates many small files,
each with a fixed cost to list and open, so queries that span many keys become slow and the file system strains.
\iqlookfor{Pruning against per-file overhead.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:5}
Consecutive messages share most of their content: the same instruments, nearby prices, times that advance by small amounts,
sequence numbers that step by one. Stored as columns, with differences for the monotone and slowly moving fields, those become
long runs of small values; sequence numbers and timestamps compress best, order references least.
\iqlookfor{Columns and deltas; which fields are regular.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:6}
Wire capture of both lines at the colocation site, kept weeks, fast compression; arbitrated normalised events written intraday
in arrival order; nightly rewrite into columns partitioned by date and underlying, compressed for ratio, kept five years or
more; hot tier for the recent month, infrequent access for the year, archive beyond. Size from the feed's planned capacity and
measured bytes a message: petabytes over the retention period, a cost in the hundreds of thousands to millions a year depending
on layout and tiers.
\iqlookfor{Capture point, layout, \omterm{def:pl:capturing-and-storing-tick-data:partition}{partitioning}, tiers and an order-of-magnitude cost with its assumptions.}
\end{solution}
