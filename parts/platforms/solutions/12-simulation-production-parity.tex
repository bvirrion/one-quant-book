\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:pl:simulation-production-parity} --- Simulation--Production Parity}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:1}
Neither 99.99 nor 0.01 has an exact binary representation, and their difference is stored as
\texttt{99.97999999999999}; ten thousand times it is just
below 999\,800, and \texttt{int()} truncates. Keep prices as integer counts of the smallest unit (999\,900 $-$ 100); if a float must be
converted, round to the nearest unit, never truncate.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:2}
The timers that fired in production are inputs recorded in the journal, at the times they fired. Setting them again in the replay would
deliver each timer twice, or at a different time if the strategy computed its delay differently; the replay must reproduce what the
strategy received.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:3}
In production the machine's clock is synchronised with the venue's, so the machine's time and the host's time agree and the defect has no
visible effect there; it shows wherever the two differ --- every simulation and every replay.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:4}
Every quote change that arrives before the acknowledgements makes the strategy believe it has no working order and send another, and each
new order is itself a quote change for the next round; the storm grows with the quote rate inside the forty-microsecond round trip. The
gateway's worst-case exposure limit stops it: it counts every order that might still fill and refused 8\,406 of the sends.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:5}
When the strategy cancelled an order whose fill had arrived in the same instant but not yet been delivered, the gateway refused the cancel
locally, while the in-process adapter sent it; the venue rejected it as too late, the strategy received a reject that production never
sent, and the venue's extra message delayed the next order by 500 nanoseconds. The gateway was right: a cancel that cannot succeed should
not reach the venue; the in-process adapter now refuses it too.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:6}
The two comparisons pair production's outputs with different second runs: the simulator trades its own history after the divergence
(different fills, different quotes), while the replay receives production's inputs and differs only where the strategy's decisions differ.
A defect whose effects compound through the market affects more outputs against the simulator.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:7}
No: the first differences depend on the session --- when the first price needs a float subtraction that lands off the grid, when a fill and
a quote change first coincide --- except for the wall clock, whose first difference is the timer at thirty seconds in any session with
quotes before it, and the ack semantics, which diverges at the first quote change after the first orders.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:pl:simulation-production-parity:8}
The test compares the backtest with itself: any input the backtest environment supplies wrongly (a clock, a price type, an event order, an
acknowledgement rule) is supplied wrongly both times, and the orders agree. Parity must compare the backtest with production's inputs
(replay a production journal) and a production-shaped environment with the simulator on the same session.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:pl:simulation-production-parity:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The host's clock, market data and order entry, through its context and callbacks; nothing else.
\item So that inputs of one instant are delivered in the \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:event}{event model}'s order whatever order the environment produced them in.
\item Book~13's gateway (state machine, throttle, exposure), encoding and framing on a byte stream, and the input journal.
\item Every input as delivered, with its receive time, in Book~13's input-journal format: 8 bytes of time and a 48-byte record.
\item Records them without sending them anywhere: the replay's outputs are compared, not executed.
\item In the simulator the timer never fires in the hour and the opening lasts fifty minutes; in the replay the journalled timer fires but the
strategy reads its own machine clock and requotes wide, parting at 30.1~seconds.
\item $99.99 - 0.01$ becomes 999\,799, off the tick grid; the venue rejects it.
\item When a fill and a quote change arrive in the same instant.
\item Whether a sent but unacknowledged order counts as working; the strategy re-sends at every quote change inside the round trip.
\item None of the four here: each also changed what the replay host delivered or read (its clock, its integer prices, its event order, its
semantics); a defect shared by the backtest and production environments would be missed.
\item 175 (wall clock), 126 (float prices), 203 (callback order), 2 (ack semantics).
\item 539 of 553, 1\,686 of 1\,690, 387 of 463 and 8\,449 of 8\,452 outputs against the simulator.
\item Ack semantics at the second input; callback order the slowest, after 203.
\item Cancels of orders already finished: the gateway refused them, the in-process adapter sent them.
\item 8\,887 outputs from 188\,079 inputs over ten sessions, with no difference in either comparison.
\item \textbf{Named result.} The wall clock diverges after 175 inputs, float prices after 126, callback order after 203 and ack semantics
after 2, affecting 539, 1\,686, 387 and 8\,449 outputs in the hour; with the fixes, production-shaped, simulator and replay runs agree on
every output of ten sessions.
\item That it reads only the host's clock and inputs, and passes the harness against production's journal and the simulator on the same
session.
\item Integer prices, delivery in the \omterm{def:pl:backtest-engine-architecture:event}{event model}, and one set of order-entry semantics, tested by the harness.
\item Two environments on the same session catch what a replay shares with production; a replay of production's journal catches what two
simulators share.
\item Because the host fixes time, prices, order and semantics for every strategy, and the harness tests them once for all.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:simulation-production-parity:1}
At the first order that differs, and at the inputs just before it, from a journal of production's inputs replayed into the backtest: then at
what the strategy reads that is not an input (clocks, randomness, iteration order), and at how the environments differ in types, event order
and order-entry semantics.
\iqlookfor{Journal, first difference, then the usual suspects.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:simulation-production-parity:2}
Because most decimal prices have no exact binary representation, so arithmetic and conversions produce values off the tick grid, and equality
tests fail; integers in the smallest unit are exact, compare exactly and hash identically in every language.
\iqlookfor{Representation error, truncation, off-grid orders.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:simulation-production-parity:3}
Give it one injected clock --- time of the current input and timers as inputs --- and forbid every other time source; then journal the timer
firings in production and replay them, and test with a harness whose machine clock differs from the host's.
\iqlookfor{Injected clock, timers as inputs.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:simulation-production-parity:4}
By a fixed rule the platform applies everywhere --- the chapter's delivers market data first, then order entry, then timers --- because
otherwise the same instant produces different decisions in different environments; which rule matters less than that there is one.
\iqlookfor{A total order, the same in every environment.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:pl:simulation-production-parity:5}
One host and one strategy interface; adapters for the backtest, the simulator and production; production's inputs journalled; a harness that
compares outputs event by event against the replay of the journal and against the simulator on the same session, reports the first
difference and its inputs, and runs in continuous integration with planted defects that it must find.
\iqlookfor{Journal, two comparisons, first difference, planted defects as tests of the harness.}
\end{solution}
