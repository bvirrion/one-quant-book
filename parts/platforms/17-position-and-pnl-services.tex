\chapter{Position and P\&L Services}\label{ch:pl:position-and-pnl-services}

Thirty-three minutes into the session, the order-entry session dropped for four seconds. Four executions happened while it was down, and
when it came back the strategy's own execution reports put it 500 shares less short than the venue's drop copy did. The \omterm{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:service}{position service} had
to decide which to believe. The answer is neither, until they agree: count every execution any source reports, once, and say that the
position is pending until the sources match. Every downstream number --- risk (chapter~18), limits, P\&L, the books that close at night ---
starts from positions, and positions start from executions that arrive twice, late, out of order, and sometimes are taken back.

\section{Positions from executions}

\begin{definition}[Position service]\label{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:service}
A \emph{position service}\index{position service} maintains the firm's positions and P\&L by account and instrument from its executions,
merged from every source that reports them, and serves them as of any time, by trade date or settlement date, with a status that says
whether its sources agree.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Execution identifier]\label{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:execid}
An \emph{execution identifier}\index{execution identifier} is the venue's unique identifier of one execution (in the simulator, its match
number with the order's client identifier); it is what makes an execution reported by two sources, or twice by one, recognisably the same.
\end{definition}

The chapter's service (\texttt{firm.posservice}) stores executions keyed by that identifier and never stores a position: every position is
computed by replaying the executions in time order through Book~1's exact keeper (\texttt{firm.pnl}), in which quantity and cash are
integers, so that cash plus quantity times the mark, less fees, holds to the ten-thousandth of a dollar. Storing the events rather than their
sum makes arrival order irrelevant, makes duplicates harmless (idempotent processing, Book~13, chapter~24) and makes every past position
recomputable.

\section{Two feeds of the same truth: sessions and drop copies}

The order-entry session reports the executions of the orders it sent; the drop copy (Book~13, chapter~21) sends the firm a copy of every
execution of its sessions on a separate connection, for exactly the moment the first one fails. A session that drops and logs back in asking
only for new messages never receives the reports generated while it was down; a drop copy that runs a little late (50~milliseconds here) has
them. The service takes both, counts each execution once, and compares the two sets: when they differ, the position it serves is the union,
and its status is pending.

otherwise, with the executions each source is missing.}\label{lst:pl:position-and-pnl-services:status}

\section{Busts, corrections and late fills}

\begin{definition}[Trade bust]\label{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:bust}
A \emph{trade bust}\index{trade bust} is the cancellation of an execution by the venue after it happened --- for example under its rules on
clearly erroneous trades (Book~1, chapter~31) --- so that for the clearing statement the trade never took place.
\end{definition}

A bust arrives minutes or hours after the execution; a correction changes an execution's price or quantity. The service applies both as
events with their own times, so that the position as of any moment can be computed as it was known then (the bust not yet received) or as it
is known now (the trade never happened). A late fill --- an execution report that arrives after the position has been read --- is only a late
event: the replay puts it in its place.

\section{Intraday P\&L and end-of-day marks}

\begin{definition}[Intraday P\&L, end-of-day mark]\label{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:marks}
\emph{Intraday P\&L}\index{intraday P\&L} is the P\&L of positions marked at current market prices during the day, for monitoring and limits.
The \emph{end-of-day mark}\index{end-of-day mark} is the price at which positions are valued for the day's official P\&L and books, usually
the official closing price, set by the valuation policy rather than by the last print the strategy happened to see.
\end{definition}

The chapter marks intraday at the feed's mid and at the close at the venue's last trade, which stands for the official close. The P\&L is
exact: \texttt{firm.pnl} keeps cash and quantity in integers, and the realised and unrealised parts under average cost (Book~1, chapter~7)
add up, with fees, to the total at the mark.

\section{Start of day and the books that close}

\begin{definition}[Start-of-day position, trade-date position, settlement-date position]\label{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:sod}
The \emph{start-of-day position}\index{start-of-day position} is the position a day begins with: the previous day's end-of-day position,
reconciled. The \emph{trade-date position}\index{trade-date position} counts every execution from the moment it trades; the
\emph{settlement-date position}\index{settlement-date position} counts it only from its settlement date (one business day later in US
equities, Book~1, chapter~5), which is what custody and cash reflect.
\end{definition}

At the end of the day the service records each position with its \omterm{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:marks}{end-of-day mark} and total; the next day's \omterm{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:sod}{start-of-day position} is that
record. The clearing firm's statement --- the executions it cleared, busts removed --- is the check: the service reconciles its positions
against it and lists every break before the next day starts.

\section{Four seconds without a session}

The chapter's experiment runs a quoting strategy (500 shares a side, an inventory limit of 5\,000 shares, orders left working if the session
drops) for an hour on ten \texttt{firm.tape} sessions of Book~10's simulator, each with a drop-copy session. At a seeded minute the order-entry
session drops for four seconds; the client logs back in asking for new messages only, and operations request a replay of the missed reports a
minute later. At minute~50 the venue busts the execution nearest minute~30. Three services are compared with the clearing statement: session
reports only; session and drop copy merged; merged with the bust applied.

\begin{example}[Seed 3]\label{ex:pl:position-and-pnl-services:seed3}
The session drops at minute~33 and four executions happen in the four seconds; the session-only service is 500 shares off from then on. The
merged service has them 50~milliseconds after they happen, from the drop copy, and reports its position as pending for 62~seconds, until the
replay brings the session into agreement. Both carry the execution of minute~30, which the venue busts at minute~50; only the service that
applies the notice agrees with the clearing statement at the close. At the close the statement shows $-3\,300$ shares and $-\$3\,493$; the
session-only service is 600 shares and \$252 off, the merged service 100 shares and \$47 off, the merged service with the bust exact.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[What pending buys]\label{rem:pl:position-and-pnl-services:pending}
A pending status turns a silent error into a visible one. The merged service's position was right during the outage, but only the
disagreement between its sources said that something had happened to the session; the session-only service was wrong and confident. Risk
(chapter~18) should treat a pending position as the union of what any source reports --- the larger exposure --- until it agrees.
\end{remark}

\section{Tutorial: one day, three sources, one statement}\label{tut:pl:position-and-pnl-services:tut}

\begin{tutorial}
\textbf{Goal.} Build positions from two execution feeds and a bust, watch them disagree and agree, and reconcile with the clearing statement.
\textbf{End state:} \cref{fig:pl:position-and-pnl-services:timeline} and \cref{tab:pl:position-and-pnl-services:close}.
\begin{tutsteps}
\item \textbf{A day}: \texttt{pl\_posservice.day(seed)} runs the simulator with a drop-copy session and a scheduled outage.
\item \textbf{Services}: \texttt{services(d)}; \texttt{status} and \texttt{missing} during the outage.
\item \textbf{The statement}: \texttt{truth(d)}; \texttt{reconcile(service, statement)}.
\item \textbf{The close}: \texttt{at\_close()} over ten seeds; \texttt{end\_of\_day} and \texttt{start\_of\_day} for the roll.
\end{tutsteps}
\textbf{What to change next.} Cancel orders on disconnect and see what the outage costs then; delay the drop copy by a second and watch the
pending windows grow.
\end{tutorial}

\section{Build: the position service}\label{bld:pl:position-and-pnl-services:posservice}

\begin{build}
\bfield{Purpose} One source of positions and P\&L for risk, limits, reporting and the books, correct under duplicate, late and missing
reports, busts and corrections, and honest about when it is not sure.
\bfield{Interface} \texttt{Execution}; \texttt{PositionService(sources)} with \texttt{on\_execution}, \texttt{on\_bust},
\texttt{on\_correction}, \texttt{position(account, symbol, as\_of, view, date)}, \texttt{status}, \texttt{missing}, \texttt{pnl},
\texttt{end\_of\_day}, \texttt{start\_of\_day}; \texttt{reconcile}.
\bfield{Rules} Executions are stored, positions computed; an \omterm{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:execid}{execution identifier} counts once; busts and corrections are events with times;
integer quantities and cash (\texttt{firm.pnl}); pending whenever sources disagree.
\bfield{Acceptance tests} \texttt{code/firm/posservice/tests/}: idempotent merge and status; exact P\&L and independence from arrival order;
busts and corrections as of times; trade-date and settlement-date views, the end-of-day roll, and reconciliation breaks.
\bfield{Stretch} Many accounts and instruments with allocations from an average-price account; corporate actions on positions (chapter~6);
the service's events journalled and replayed (chapter~12).
\end{build}

\item One Quant Book~1, chapters 5, 7 and 31 (settlement, positions and P\&L, erroneous trades); One Quant Book~13, chapters 21 and 24 (drop
copies, idempotent processing); One Quant Book~10, chapter~26 (the simulator's drop copy and session faults).
\end{omsources}

\section{Problem: Four Seconds Without a Session}

\begin{problem}[{Weekend problem --- positions from two feeds and a bust}]\label{pb:pl:position-and-pnl-services:1}
The chapter's strategy, ten sessions, a four-second outage and a bust each.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part I --- The service.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item What does the service store, and how does it compute a position?
\item What makes two reports the same execution?
\item What does the status say, and when?
\item How does the service handle a bust and a correction?
\item What keeps the P\&L exact?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part II --- The sources.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Why does a session lose reports in an outage?
\item What does the drop copy add, and at what cost?
\item What is a replay, and why is it requested?
\item What does the clearing statement represent?
\item When does each service agree with the statement?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part III --- The day.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item In how many sessions did the outage lose executions, and how many?
\item How long was the merged service pending?
\item What were the errors at the close in seed 3?
\item What were the mean errors over the ten sessions?
\item Which error does the drop copy remove, and which not?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Part IV --- The verdict.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item State the \emph{named result}: the position and P\&L error at the close without the drop copy, with it, and with it plus the bust, and
the time until the service agrees with the clearing statement.
\item What should risk do with a pending position?
\item What should happen at the start of the next day?
\item What would you monitor on the \omterm{def:pl:position-and-pnl-services:service}{position service} itself?
\item In one sentence: what makes a position trustworthy?
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}
