\begin{solution}{exo:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:8}
Four errors: the notice's bits are before the Ethernet framing (\qty{59.4}{\giga\bit\per\second} on the wire); the allowance adds 10\%;
the 10-millisecond peak is an average, and the busiest millisecond runs at \qty{234}{\giga\bit\per\second} in the chapter's simulation;

\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item As the notice counts it, \qty{44.0}{\giga\bit\per\second} over 100 milliseconds and \qty{50.1}{\giga\bit\per\second} over 10; on the
  wire 52.2 and \qty{59.4}{\giga\bit\per\second}; both streams with the allowance 114.8 and \qty{130.6}{\giga\bit\per\second}.
\item \qty{48.0}{bytes}: the notice's 352 bytes a packet taken as UDP payload, framed to 418 bytes on the wire, over 8.68 messages a packet.
\item 4, 12 and 44.
\item Lines are packed whole and each line has its own bursts, up to \qty{6.8}{\giga\bit\per\second} in one millisecond, in different
  milliseconds; so nearly every line needs a link of its own, against a bound of 26 a stream from the busiest millisecond alone.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item 16 a stream, for 156.2 million messages a second.
\item 449\,500 messages, cleared in \qty{2.8}{\milli\second}.
\item 64: the busiest millisecond holds 609\,500 messages, and 64 cores process 640\,000 a millisecond.
\item Its busiest 100 milliseconds, 13.56 million messages against the notice's 13.575 million.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item 2.4 million quotes, 48\,000 bulk messages and \qty{0.31}{\giga\bit\per\second}.
\item One for throughput (a port carries 50\,000 messages a second); twelve, one per engine, by the venue's structure, and the firm
  uses the two per engine it is allocated, 24.
\item \qty{10.0}{\milli\second}, \qty{10.2}{\milli\second} and \qty{15.0}{\milli\second}.
\item For up to that long some of its quotes are stale, and anyone faster can trade against them; the firm needs purges and an order
  of refresh that removes the most dangerous quotes first.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{Named result}: quoting 1\,200 classes of 250 series at four updates a second a side takes 2.4 million quotes and 48\,000
  bulk messages a second, which one port could carry but twelve engines turn into 24 quoting ports (two an engine), refreshing every
  quote in 10 to \qty{15}{\milli\second} depending on the risk layer; taking the consolidated feed in full, both streams, takes 44
  links of \qty{10}{\giga\bit\per\second} at the 99.99th percentile of milliseconds (12 at the 99th) and 16 handler cores a stream.
\item Published: the capacity projection, the busiest-millisecond metric, the line count, the port rules and fees, the gateway
  latencies. Assumed: line shares, the burst model's shape, bytes as payload, cores' speed, the round trip, the software layer, the
  firm's load and the venue's twelve engines.
\item The busiest millisecond needs 32 instead of 178, and the 99.99th percentile 16 instead of 44; fewer, faster links with the
  same switch ports.
\item The full-refresh time, and so how long its quotes are stale after a move; one message's latency matters less.
\item Most dangerous first: the sides most likely to be hit (near the money, short-dated, those the move made cheap), purging what
  cannot be updated in time.
\item The feed plan (links, cores, queue) from the new projection, the fit to the latest metrics, and the port prices.
\item In the equities and options rows: links and cores for the feed, ports per venue, the risk layer, and their monthly costs.
\item Because the binding constraints are the feed's bursts and the ports' round trips, which set how many links, cores and ports the
  firm must buy before any microsecond is saved.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:1}
Because receivers are sized for bursts: a one-second average hides peaks several times higher, and a switch port or a handler that
cannot keep up for a few milliseconds drops or delays data. Even 10 milliseconds hide the millisecond peaks.
\iqlookfor{Averages against peaks; buffers; the planning window.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:2}
Take the operator's capacity notice and metrics, convert to wire bits, double for both streams, add the retransmission allowance;
model the millisecond bursts per partition, choose a planning percentile, pack partitions onto links; size handler cores on the peak
and check queues; re-run at every notice.
\iqlookfor{Published capacity; framing; percentiles; partitions; handlers.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:3}
The number of quotes to change, the quotes per message, the ports per engine and the round trip per message (including the risk layer),
and the venue's throttles. Measure by timestamping each bulk message and its acknowledgement per port, and the time from a market event
to the last quote updated.
\iqlookfor{Messages and round trips; ports; risk layer; measurement from event to last quote.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:4}
The latency added each way at the percentiles that matter, with and without load; the checks it performs and how they are configured;
failure behaviour; capacity per port; and who operates it. Compare by the full-refresh time and the round trip each implies for the
firm's quoting, not only by one message's latency.
\iqlookfor{Latency distribution under load; checks; failure modes; effect on throughput.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:5}
The quotes most likely to be traded against at stale prices: near the money, short-dated, large sizes, and sides the move made too
generous; purge what cannot be updated in time rather than leave it.
\iqlookfor{Adverse selection; ordering by risk; purges.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:equities-and-options-connectivity:6}
Both feed streams on links sized at a chosen millisecond percentile, with switch buffers and drop monitoring; handler cores sized with a
queue budget; quoting ports on every engine, purge ports, a drop copy; a risk layer measured for its effect on refresh time; and
monitoring of gaps, queue delays, acknowledgement times and port limits, re-planned at every capacity notice.
\iqlookfor{Both budgets; percentiles and queues; ports per engine; risk layer; monitoring.}
\end{solution}
