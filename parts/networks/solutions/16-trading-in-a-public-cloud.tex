\begin{solution}{exo:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:3}
The time a virtual CPU wanted to run but the hypervisor gave the physical CPU to something else. For a trading program it is time during which a
market event waits unprocessed: a delay invisible to the program's own clocks except as a gap, and a sign that the host is shared and busy.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:4}
Crossing zones adds distance and switching hops to every packet: a fixed cost that moves the whole distribution. A \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:steal}{noisy neighbour} takes resources only
now and then, so most round trips are untouched and a few are much longer: it moves the tail, not the body.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:5}
It removes the hypervisor and the other tenants on the host (steal time, shared caches, shared network card queues). It does not remove the shared
network beyond the host, the provider's own card and software, or the distance to the venue's zone.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:6}
Launch machines in every zone of the region, measure round trips to the venue's endpoints from each with hardware timestamps, and keep the zone with the
lowest and most stable figure (chapter~17); read the zone identifiers of the machines, which are physical, and record the result with its date.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:7}
Above a spike probability of about 0.092\% (one round trip in about 1\,090) the noisy-neighbour case's 99.9th percentile jumps into the spikes and exceeds
the cross-zone case's \qty{851}{\micro\second}: the tail percentile is decided by how often the neighbour strikes, not by how hard.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:8}
The venue's documentation names the zone in its own account; the same name in the firm's account is a different physical zone with probability
$1 - 1/n$. Only the zone identifier locates the venue; the firm must compare identifiers, or measure.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $1/6$.
\item $(1 - 1/6)(555 - 270) = \qty{237.5}{\micro\second}$ on average; with three zones, \qty{190}{\micro\second}.
\item us-east-1e and us-east-1f: the only names that point to the same identifiers (use1-az3 and use1-az5) in both accounts.
\item As us-east-1b: A's us-east-1a is use1-az4, which is B's us-east-1b.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item Within a zone \qty{396}{\micro\second} and \qty{743}{\micro\second}; across zones \qty{755}{\micro\second} and \qty{1908}{\micro\second}.
\item Within 2\% (743 against 760) and 0.2\% (1\,908 against 1\,905).
\item It takes it from \qty{463}{\micro\second} to \qty{7474}{\micro\second}, sixteen times more.
\item For the median race, the zone; for the tail (the races lost to a stall), the neighbour. A racing strategy needs both right.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item $4 \times 0.714 \times 730 = 2\,084.88$~USD.
\item $20\,000 \times 0.01 \times 2 = 400$~USD.
\item $2 \times 4.284 \times 730 = 6\,254.64$~USD a month: less than one Nasdaq Ultra High Density Cabinet's monthly fee (7\,230~USD, chapter~9) before
  its power and connections.
\item The zone mistake costs nothing on the bill and \qty{285}{\micro\second} on the median round trip; bare metal costs thousands a month and removes
  only the neighbours. The question is wrong because the zone is free to fix and the neighbours are not.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{Named result}: two accounts' same-named zones are the same physical zone with probability $1/n$ ($1/6$ for six zones); trusting names costs
  $(1 - 1/n)(t_c - t_s)$ per round trip on average, \qty{237.5}{\micro\second} with six zones and the published medians.
\item By reading its own zone identifiers and measuring round trips to the venue from every zone, keeping the fastest.
\item So that customers who all choose ``a'' do not all land in the same physical zone, which would concentrate load and failures.
\item The venue should publish the zone identifier of its servers (or offer endpoints per identifier); the firm should ask for it and verify it by
  measurement.
\item Documentation: the random mapping, the identifiers, the placement tools, the prices. Measurement: the published round trips. Assumptions: the
  uniform shuffle, the \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:steal}{noisy neighbour}, the traffic volume.
\item After every launch and relaunch, after the venue's maintenance, and continuously for the round trips (chapter~17).
\item In a different form: the hall's position of a cabinet (chapter~9), which equalisation hides; a \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} customer knows its building, while a
  cloud customer must discover its zone.
\item Nothing about where you are, unless you translate it into an identifier.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:1}
One of the isolated groups of data centres that make up a region, kilometres from the others. Machines in one zone talk faster than machines in two:
in the published measurement, \qty{270}{\micro\second} against \qty{555}{\micro\second} of median round trip.
\iqlookfor{Zones as buildings; names against identifiers; the order of magnitude.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:2}
At the tail's causes: steal time and \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:steal}{noisy neighbours} on the host (check steal, move to dedicated or bare metal), the network (timestamp packets to
separate network from host), cross-zone paths, the program's own pauses (garbage collection, page faults, interrupts; One Quant Book~13).
\iqlookfor{Measure before guessing; separate host, network and program; hardware timestamps.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:3}
Find the venue's zone identifier (published or measured), launch in that zone by identifier, use a cluster \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:pg}{placement group}, choose instances with
enhanced networking or bare metal, and keep the few that measure best.
\iqlookfor{Identifier, \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:pg}{placement group}, instance type, measurement; chapter~17's lottery.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:4}
With a provider that exposes a PTP hardware clock and hardware packet timestamps (AWS documents both in its precision time \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:pg}{placement groups}):
synchronise the clock with the hardware clock, read packet timestamps from the network card, and check the error budget as in chapter~4.
\iqlookfor{Hardware clock and timestamps; documented accuracy; verification.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:5}
Machines are cheap by the hour; the hidden costs are data transfer across zones and regions and out to the internet, bare metal or dedicated hosts
for predictability, the engineering to measure and re-place, and the latency tail of shared infrastructure.
\iqlookfor{\omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:dtc}{Data-transfer charges}; predictability as a cost; comparison with \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} fees.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:6}
Only if the venue itself ran in that region (so that the cloud is the \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation}), the firm could place by zone identifier next to it, and the tail
could be controlled (bare metal, measurement). Against venues in their own data centres, a \omterm{def:nw:trading-in-a-public-cloud:cloud}{public cloud} is a remote site: fine for research and
slow strategies, not for races.
\iqlookfor{Where the venue is decides; the tail; honesty about the use case.}
\end{solution}
