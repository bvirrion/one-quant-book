\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:nw:the-vendor-map} --- The Vendor Map}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:1}
It receives the feeds once, decodes and normalises them, keeps the books and distributes them inside the firm. Centralising saves
duplicated decoding and servers, at the cost of a distribution network and its latency.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:2}
A company that sells trading or post-trade software to firms and connects them to exchanges on their behalf. The exchange certifies it
so that its software behaves correctly on the exchange's interfaces, which protects the exchange and every other participant.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:3}
They are not on the path from data to orders, so their failure does not stop trading. But every timestamp the regulators require
depends on them, so a long failure leaves the firm unable to prove its records.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:4}
The \omterm{def:nw:the-vendor-map:tickerplant}{ticker plant} has a parallel path through the in-house feed handlers, so removing it leaves a path from data to orders.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:5}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:6}
A common sub-supplier: two server vendors with the same network cards, firmware or chips fail together for the same defect. The map
should record the parts' makers, not only the sellers.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:7}
AMD and Blackcore; Equinix drops out, as do the \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} and \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:mmr}{cross-connects} from the single components, and the index falls to 1\,104.

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:the-vendor-map:8}
Share of spend is not dependence: a vendor with a small share can supply a component on the only path from data to orders, and the
largest vendor here is also a point of failure whose exit takes half a year.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:nw:the-vendor-map:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{Colocation} and \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:mmr}{cross-connects} (Equinix), a \omterm{def:nw:switches-and-layer-1-devices:l1}{layer-1 fan-out} (Arista), switches (Cisco), a \omterm{def:nw:the-vendor-map:tickerplant}{ticker plant} (Exegy), servers
  (Blackcore), network cards (AMD), order management (Trading Technologies), grandmasters (Meinberg), analytics (Pico), a microwave route
  (Quincy Data), and its own feed handlers and gateway.
\item A venue's certified vendor list, the vendors' own product pages, and the earlier chapters' rows.
\item USD 3.19 million; Equinix 22.6\%, Quincy Data 15.0\%, in-house work 14.1\%, Exegy 12.5\%.
\item 1\,149.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{Colocation}, \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:mmr}{cross-connects}, the strategy servers, their network cards and the gateway.
\item AMD, Blackcore and Equinix.
\item Each has a parallel component from another vendor or built in house.
\item 26, 12, 6, 4 and 0 weeks: 48 in all.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item The index falls to 1\,119 and Blackcore leaves the vendor points; the servers remain a single component.
\item A second network card maker on some servers, or cards of another make in the second server vendor's machines.
\item The \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} site is where the exchange is; a second site means another operator's building, its own \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:mmr}{cross-connects} and a
  longer path, and half a year to move.
\item Nothing to automated trading; manual and agency flow stops, and records and monitoring degrade.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{Named result}: the example firm's stack has a concentration index of 1\,149 on spend, and five components whose single vendor
  (or the firm itself) stops trading: \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} and \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:mmr}{cross-connects} (Equinix), the strategy servers (Blackcore), their network cards (AMD)
  and the in-house gateway; the vendor points are AMD, Blackcore and Equinix.
\item Published: the vendors and what they sell. The firm's own: the stack, its graph, spend and exit times.
\item The index counts money; the vendor points count paths. A cheap component on the only path matters more than an expensive one
  with a spare.
\item Each vendor point: how the firm keeps trading or stops safely, spares, the second site, and who calls whom.
\item What would it take to leave, how long, and has anything changed that makes the vendor a point of failure.
\item The \omterm{def:nw:the-vendor-map:tickerplant}{ticker plant} vendor drops out and in-house work rises; the firm now owns that component's failures and its exit is its own.
\item In the bill of materials and the risk register of the connectivity plan.
\item Being the only supplier of a component on the only path from data to orders.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:the-vendor-map:1}
A \omterm{def:nw:the-vendor-map:tickerplant}{ticker plant} decodes once and distributes, saving servers and duplicated work at the cost of a network hop and a central point of
failure; embedded handlers decode in each strategy, faster and independent, at the cost of duplicated work and many copies to keep up
to date.
\iqlookfor{Duplication against latency; distribution; failure domains.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:the-vendor-map:2}
Draw the path from market data to orders as a graph with every component and its supplier, remove each component and each supplier in
turn, and see whether a path survives; then test the answers in failover exercises.
\iqlookfor{Graph; cuts by component and by vendor; testing.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:the-vendor-map:3}
Price the options: upgrade with the vendor, move to another vendor, or build; plan the certification and testing each needs, and start
the one with the longest lead time now.
\iqlookfor{Exit time; certification; lead time.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:the-vendor-map:4}
For spares, drivers, skills and consistent performance; not when that vendor becomes the only supplier of the servers on the trading
path, where a second vendor for some machines removes a point of failure.
\iqlookfor{Standardisation against dependence.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:the-vendor-map:5}
Automated strategies with their own gateways keep trading; manual orders and allocations stop. Work through the broker or the exchange's
own tools for urgent manual orders, and reconcile from drop copies afterwards.
\iqlookfor{What is on the path; fallbacks; reconciliation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:the-vendor-map:6}
Buy the commodity layers (\omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation}, hardware, certified connectivity software), build what differentiates (strategies, execution
gateway), dual-source the components on the trading path where it is cheap, record every vendor with its exit time, and review the map
at each renewal.
\iqlookfor{Buy against build; trading path; exits.}
\end{solution}
