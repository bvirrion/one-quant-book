\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:nw:futures-connectivity} --- Futures Connectivity}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:1}
A Convenience Gateway session reaches every market segment, the gateway routing each message; a Market Segment Gateway connection
reaches one segment, a group of products on one engine, and the firm connects to each segment's gateway with the same session
identity.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:2}
$6 \times 260 + 4 \times 520 = 3\,640$~EUR a month, for $10 \times 150 = 1\,500$ transactions a second.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:3}
A packet missed on one feed is usually present on the other, so processing both avoids most gaps; a gap on both means every book on
the channel must be resynchronised, which costs time exactly when data is busiest.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:4}
All copies of each firm's order reach the one gateway's card together, and its queue is first-in first-out: the first copy is
served no earlier than a single one would be, so the winner is the firm whose order arrives first, decided by ±2.5~ns of cable,
uniformly among ten equally fast firms.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:5}
Sending the start of a message before its end is ready lets a firm begin its transmission early, a way to arrive first that the
design did not mean to reward. The session is delayed at least \qty{3}{\micro\second} and a complete message from another session
may reach the engine first.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:6}
That all four may execute: its position limits must count four times the order's size until the other copies are cancelled or
expire, and its reconciliation must expect up to four executions.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:7}
Five Full and nine Ultra sessions, EUR 8\,320 a month; twenty Full sessions cost EUR 8\,840, 520 more, because fourteen of them
are at the price from the seventh.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:futures-connectivity:8}
If the others copy, the gain disappears (one in ten again) while everyone's latency rises, and adding sessions only restarts the
race at a higher cost; and the exchange, whose system carries the load, has every reason to change the design, as Eurex did.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:nw:futures-connectivity:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item 10.0\% and 59.7\%.
\item 8.4 points (EUR 840 a month at the chapter's values), and 1.8 points (EUR 180.5).
\item The minimum of independent delays falls with every copy (\cref{prop:nw:futures-connectivity:copies}).
\item The gateways' independent queues and jitter relative to the differences between firms; with no jitter there would be nothing
  to gain.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item It returns to one in ten (10.2\% in the simulation).
\item It rises from \qty{25.4}{\micro\second} to \qty{35.8}{\micro\second}.
\item The copies loaded its gateways at the busiest moments and made latency higher and less predictable for every participant.
\item Deterministic networking, FIFO in the gateways, then a single partition-specific entry point; equal-length connections; and
  prices that rise with the number of sessions.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item 9.8\% with either.
\item The order in which messages reach the gateway's card, and so the firm's own speed to the exchange, to within ±2.5~ns of cable.
\item The firm's own reaction: market data handling, decision, and its path to the card.
\item To remove the advantage of splitting a message across packets to start sending it before it is complete.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{Named result}: with parallel, jittery gateways a firm's chance of winning a ten-firm race rises from 10.0\% with one
  session to 59.7\% with twelve, while under a segment gateway it stays at 9.8\%; at 20\,000 races a month and EUR 0.50 a race, the
  eleven extra sessions are worth EUR 4\,973 a month, EUR 452 a session on average, but each session beyond the sixth is worth less
  than the EUR 520 it costs, and the twelfth only EUR 180.5.
\item That the fee from the seventh is set where the marginal copy is worth less than it costs: the schedule enforces what the
  design no longer needs.
\item Published: gateway designs, path lengths, variances, cable equality, session limits and fees. Assumed: the model's delays,
  queues, service times, the number of firms, races and their value.
\item Parallel: sessions up to where their marginal value meets the fee, knowing the gain vanishes if others copy. Segment: sessions
  for throughput, segments and failover only.
\item Measure: send timestamped probes through several sessions and gateways and compare the exchange's receive and matching
  timestamps; if order at the engine differs from order at the network card, the design is not FIFO.
\item Up to $k$ executions per order to reconcile per session, and positions that must assume all copies fill.
\item In the futures rows: segments, sessions and their fees, failover, clearing limits, drop copies, and both feeds by separate paths.
\item That the order of arrival at the exchange's edge, not the luck of a path inside it, decides who is first.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:futures-connectivity:1}
A gateway dedicated to one group of products on one engine, so that all orders for them enter through one path in one sequence. It
removes routing inside the exchange and the variance of parallel paths, and so the reward for sending copies.
\iqlookfor{Segments; one sequence; fairness; routing removed.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:futures-connectivity:2}
Parallel gateways with independent queues and jitter make the firm's arrival the minimum of its copies' delays. It stops working
when the exchange orders messages at one entry point, when everyone copies, or when fees and throttles make copies cost more than
they win.
\iqlookfor{Order statistics; independent paths; equilibrium; exchange response.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:futures-connectivity:3}
Send pairs of messages at known times through different sessions and gateways, and compare the exchange's timestamps at its network
edge with the order of matching; measure variance on each path; check that equal cables and equal ports give equal times.
\iqlookfor{Controlled probes; exchange timestamps; order inversion; variance.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:futures-connectivity:4}
A common cause: the same switch, buffer, NIC or host receiving both feeds, or a burst above what that shared element can carry; less
likely, a loss at the exchange. Check whether the feeds share any element and move them apart.
\iqlookfor{Correlated losses; shared elements; recovery.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:futures-connectivity:5}
Flatten or hedge what can still be traded within the allowed actions, talk to the clearing member's risk desk about the limit and
the breach, and reconcile positions from the drop copy; tomorrow, size strategies to the limit with headroom, and ask for limits
per product where they fit the risk better.
\iqlookfor{Immediate risk; communication; reconciliation; limits in the plan.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:futures-connectivity:6}
One session identity per the exchange's model, connected to each segment's gateway, primary and backup, with throttles sized per
segment; no copies under a FIFO design; failover tested in the exchange's windows; clearing limits known and monitored; drop copies
reconciled per session; both market data feeds on separate paths.
\iqlookfor{Segments; throughput; failover; risk; reconciliation; feeds.}
\end{solution}
