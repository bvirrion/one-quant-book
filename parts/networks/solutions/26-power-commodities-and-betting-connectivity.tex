\begin{solution}{exo:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:4}
With three slots the firm must only beat the third-fastest arrival, and at \qty{10}{\milli\second} it is almost always ahead of the 20,
40 and \qty{80}{\milli\second} rivals; with one slot it must beat the \qty{5}{\milli\second} rival too, which it rarely does.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:5}
The same capacity cannot be sold twice: the auction allocates it across the border for the auctioned periods, so the continuous market
must stop allocating it until the auction is completed or cancelled.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:6}
3\,500: each placement counts once with its successful cancellation, and the 500 failed cancellations count as failed transactions;
below the 5\,000 threshold.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:7}
About \qty{5}{\milli\second}, the fastest rival's median: 51.3\% at \qty{4.9}{\milli\second} and 49.3\% at \qty{5.0}{\milli\second}.

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:8}
The market runs for hours, but scarce cross-border capacity is allocated first come, first served at the moment of the match: on a
binding border, a few milliseconds decide who gets it, and the whole value of the spread goes to the first.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Implicitly, with each match, first come, first served, the best prices served first.
\item It is removed from the capacity management module at once; the next match sees what is left.
\item Sixty minutes before delivery; the reform aims at thirty.
\item Continuous cross-zonal allocation stops on that border until the auction is completed or cancelled; trading within each zone
  continues.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item 98.5\% and 4.5\%.
\item About \qty{5}{\milli\second}.
\item At \qty{10}{\milli\second}, 95.4\% instead of 4.5\%.
\item Winning the last slot means beating the fastest rival, so the fall of the curve sits where the firm's and that rival's
  distributions overlap; their spreads set how steep it is.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item EUR 11.82 and EUR 0.54 per megawatt-hour offered.
\item Allocation is by arrival: the first gets the capacity and the second gets nothing, however close.
\item Where and when capacity binds: congested borders, hours of large price differences between zones.
\item Where the shared book is, how long each local trading system takes, and its rivals' latencies; it can measure its own times and
  infer its rank from which contests it wins.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{Named result}: in the chapter's model, the chance of obtaining the last capacity across a scarce border falls from 98.5\% at
  a median of \qty{2}{\milli\second} to the \omterm{def:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:sob}{shared order book} to 4.5\% at \qty{10}{\milli\second}, crossing one half at the fastest
  rival's \qty{5}{\milli\second}; at an average spread of EUR 12 per megawatt-hour it is worth EUR 11.82 against EUR 0.54 per
  megawatt-hour offered.
\item Published: the coupling's rules and structure, the ranking rule, the gate closure and auction suspension. Assumed: every latency,
  the spreads, the number of rivals and the scarcity.
\item The one with the fastest measured path to the shared book, for the borders where capacity binds.
\item A common delay adds to everyone's arrival equally and changes no one's rank; only differences matter.
\item Its own latency per local system, contests entered and won per border and hour, and the spreads captured.
\item Every intraday trade must reach a schedule before the grid operator's deadline, over a slower, acknowledged channel.
\item In the power rows: local trading systems and their latency, borders and hours, schedules, and the futures exchanges' \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation}.
\item When capacity across a border is scarce and allocated by arrival.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:1}
A pan-European continuous intraday market: operators' local trading systems feed one \omterm{def:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:sob}{shared order book}, and a cross-border match is
possible while capacity remains in the capacity management module, which the match then consumes.
\iqlookfor{Shared book; local systems; implicit allocation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:2}
Because capacity is allocated with each match in arrival order: when only one order's worth is left, the first order to arrive gets it.
\iqlookfor{First come, first served; scarcity; winner takes all.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:3}
Generate schedules from the trade records, send them over the grid operator's platform with acknowledgements, reconcile acknowledged
schedules against trades and positions, and alert well before each deadline on any gap.
\iqlookfor{Reconciliation; acknowledgements; deadlines; alerting.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:4}
Streaming: each change arrives once, when it happens; polling costs requests under a weight limit and delivers data half an interval
old on average. Polling only for occasional snapshots.
\iqlookfor{Weights; staleness; the venue's own recommendation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:5}
From the desk's own history: contests entered, won and lost on binding borders, the spreads, and its latency at each; model the win
probability against latency and multiply by the spreads captured.
\iqlookfor{Scarcity events; win model; value per megawatt-hour.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:power-commodities-and-betting-connectivity:6}
Colocated futures connectivity; local trading systems for intraday with measured paths to the shared book; a scheduling service with
acknowledged delivery and reconciliation; clocks for gate closures and auctions; monitoring of contests won and schedules delivered.
\iqlookfor{Three connections, three clocks; latency where capacity binds; reconciliation.}
\end{solution}
