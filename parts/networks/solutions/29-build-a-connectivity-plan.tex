\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan} --- Build a Connectivity Plan}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:1}
The itemised list of everything the plan buys; each row carries the item, supplier, quantity, monthly and one-time price, the ledger row
that publishes the price and the date it was read, or a flag that the price is an assumption.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:2}
$8 \times 1\,200 = 9\,600$ USD a month, plus a 5\,000 USD initial fee for the cabinet.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:3}
Its price has no published source: the carrier does not publish it, so the plan carries an assumption, and the check that every price row
names its source fails on it until the firm has a quote.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:4}
USD 293\,000 a year, 28.8\% of the budget; its monthly price rose from 22\,000 to 24\,000 USD, by 9.1\%.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:5}
Its round trip, \qty{0.80}{\milli\second} at the 99th percentile, is under one millisecond, but traffic between the zones is charged each
way per gigabyte on top of the endpoint's hours, and the extra round trip leaves less margin.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:6}
A second 10 Gb LCN connection, USD 366\,250 a year; it buys full bandwidth through a connection failure, which the 1 Gb backup already
covers at reduced bandwidth for 38\,542 a year; its value depends on what trading at 1 Gb for a few hours a year costs.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:7}
\qty{6.90}{\milli\second}, against \qty{7.48}{\milli\second} at 1.3.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:8}
One row is an assumption, not a price: the wavelength must be quoted, and at 50\% above the assumption the budget rises by USD 152\,083.
The missed Tokyo target must be fixed, and the contracts' service levels are not yet in the plan.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:1}
\textbf{Part I.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item NYSE \qty{35.6}{\micro\second} against 40; CME data \qty{3.986}{\milli\second} against 4.2; CME orders \qty{7.48}{\milli\second}
  against 8; Binance \qty{2.40}{\milli\second} against 1.
\item The cage and software path (\texttt{firm.cagenet}, \texttt{firm.wirepath}); the published radio route; \texttt{firm.fibreroute} on
  the geodesic; \texttt{firm.privlink}.
\item Published: the radio figure and the software stages' measurements. Modelled: the cage, the cards, the fibre \omterm{def:nw:the-north-american-map:factor}{route factor}, the cloud
  path's edge time.
\item The Tokyo round trip, 240\% of target, because the public endpoint adds an \omterm{def:nw:where-the-crypto-venues-live:as}{edge network}'s two milliseconds.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part II.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item A \omterm{def:nw:private-connectivity-to-crypto-venues:endpoint}{private endpoint} in the instance's zone, \qty{0.44}{\milli\second}; one in another zone, \qty{0.80}{\milli\second}.
\item USD 122.64 a year of endpoint hours, plus its traffic charge.
\item That the endpoint has an interface in the instance's physical zone, by zone identifier, not name (chapter~16).
\item It removes an \omterm{def:nw:where-the-crypto-venues-live:as}{edge network} from the path rather than buying distance; the rest of the plan pays for distance and access.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part III.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item USD 68\,812 a month, 39\,500 one-time, 1\,017\,289 a year.
\item Connectivity 567\,167, disaster recovery 178\,375, \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} 146\,333, market data 109\,667, cloud 15\,747.
\item The wavelength's price is unsourced: get carrier quotes and replace the assumption before signing.
\item Bandwidth and redundancy on the equity connection and the wavelength's price move the budget; the cloud barely does.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Part IV.}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \emph{Named result}: USD 1.02 million a year all in (1\,017\,289), with NYSE at \qty{35.6}{\micro\second} of a
  \qty{40}{\micro\second} target, CME data at 3.986 of 4.2 and CME orders at 7.48 of \qty{8}{\milli\second}, and Binance at
  \qty{2.40}{\milli\second} against one; a \omterm{def:nw:private-connectivity-to-crypto-venues:endpoint}{private endpoint} in the instance's zone meets it at \qty{0.44}{\milli\second} for USD 123 a year.
\item Availability from 99.900\% to 99.982\% under the chapter's assumptions, at reduced bandwidth during a failure.
\item The order route shrinks to a \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:mmr}{cross-connect}, the data route reverses (New Jersey prices to Chicago), and a second \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation}
  footprint enters the budget.
\item Which prices are quotes and which assumptions, which targets come from which strategies, and what each sensitivity is worth to the
  business.
\item At every price change, venue move or new strategy, and at least before each contract renewal.
\item The exported cost table, with each line's source and date, to budget the firm's technology tiers.
\item The latency table's component for that venue, then the path it assumed: route, zone, endpoint.
\item To know, before signing, what the firm will get at each venue and what it will pay.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:1}
The venues and what each strategy needs from them; sites, routes, connections and services; their latency, service levels, recovery
arrangements and cost, each price dated and sourced; the checks that keep it current.
\iqlookfor{Requirements; latency; \omterm{def:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:plan}{bill of materials}; service levels; recovery; budget.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:2}
From the two sites' coordinates: the geodesic, the fibre floor at the glass's \omterm{def:nw:the-north-american-map:floor}{group index}, a \omterm{def:nw:the-north-american-map:factor}{route factor} from published routes, and
equipment; compare with published radio figures for the same corridor.
\iqlookfor{Geodesic; \omterm{def:nw:the-north-american-map:floor}{group index}; \omterm{def:nw:the-north-american-map:factor}{route factor}; published comparisons.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:3}
List the alternatives that could meet it, compute each one's latency with the same models, price each, and pick the cheapest that meets the
target with margin; check whether the target itself is right.
\iqlookfor{Alternatives; same models; cost ranking; margin.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:4}
Every price is a dated row with its source; a check fails rows older than a limit and rows without a source; the plan is re-run when
schedules change.
\iqlookfor{Dated sources; staleness checks; automation.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:5}
Estimate how often and how long the primary connection fails, what trading at the backup's bandwidth loses in those hours, and compare that
expected loss with USD 366\,000 a year; include what the firm's \omterm{def:nw:disaster-recovery-and-regulatory-requirements:resilience}{impact tolerance} requires.
\iqlookfor{Failure rates; loss while degraded; tolerance.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:nw:build-a-connectivity-plan:6}
Requirements per strategy; \omterm{def:nw:colocation-products-and-how-they-are-sold:colo}{colocation} at the equity venue; radio data and a fibre route for futures, or a second footprint near the futures
exchange; instances in the crypto venue's cloud zone with a \omterm{def:nw:private-connectivity-to-crypto-venues:endpoint}{private endpoint}; service levels and a recovery site; a budget with sources,
sensitivities and checks, re-run as prices change.
\iqlookfor{All the book's pieces; alternatives; sources; checks.}
\end{solution}
