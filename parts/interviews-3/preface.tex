\chapter{Preface}

This book is for training. It holds interview questions in quantitative finance and full answers to them, for
quantitative researchers and traders, traders, analysts, data scientists, quantitative developers, software and
low-latency engineers, and risk quants, at market makers, crypto firms, banks and hedge funds.

The questions were written for this book. They follow the topics, the level and the form that candidates report in
public, and they are not leaks: no question here is presented as coming from a particular firm or a particular
interview.

It is the third of three interview books. It trains algorithms, languages, low latency, systems and data. Book 18
covers speed, probability and mathematics, and Book 19 covers markets, trading games and the final round. A developer
will usually want Books 18 and 20.

\section*{How to use this book}

Answer each question out loud or on paper before you look at its answer, and always finish with a clear conclusion.
Answers are at the back of the book, so that you do not see them by accident. Each question links to its answer, and
each answer links back to its question.

Stars give the time a prepared candidate should need: $\star$ two or three minutes, $\star\star$ about seven,
$\star\star\star$ about ten, and $\star\star\star\star$ fifteen or more. The tags beside the stars name the roles and
the types of firm where the question fits, and whether it suits a junior or an experienced candidate. Skip what is not
for you.

An answer gives the result and the derivation. Where they earn their place, it also gives the classic wrong answer, the
follow-ups an interviewer is likely to ask, and why the question matters on a desk.

Every listing in this book comes from a file that was compiled and tested, and a question states the language standard,
compiler or processor its answer depends on. Code printed as buggy really is buggy, and its test shows the bug.
