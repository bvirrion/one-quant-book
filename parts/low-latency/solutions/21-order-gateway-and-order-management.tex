\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management} --- Order Gateway and Order Management}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:1}
The fill takes it from \emph{pending cancel} to \emph{filled}; the ``too late'' answer leaves it \emph{filled}. Before the fill
arrived, the gateway counted the whole remaining quantity (the cancel might fail); after it, nothing remains.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:2}
At 0: two tokens, so the first two go through and the third is refused. At \qty{1}{\milli\second} the bucket holds 0.3 of a
token: refused. At \qty{4}{\milli\second}, 1.2: it goes through, leaving 0.2. At \qty{5}{\milli\second}, 0.5: refused. So
through, through, refused, refused, through, refused.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:3}
The worst long exposure is $200 + 100 + 100 = 400$. The replace would raise one order's worst remaining quantity from 100 to
300, adding 200, for 600 above the limit of 500: refused. The largest new quantity that fits adds 100: a buy of 200.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:4}
$1 - e^{-50 \times 350 \times 10^{-6}} = 1 - e^{-0.0175} \approx 1.7\%$, so about 347 races in 20\,000 cancels (if the orders
have rested at least \qty{350}{\micro\second} when cancelled).
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:5}
The two executions have the same match number: they are one execution delivered twice, and the gateway, with 60, is right.
The reconciliation should key executions by their match number and count each once; a duplicate is itself worth reporting,
since the \omterm{def:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:dropcopy}{drop copy} is supposed to be the independent record.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:6}
About \qty{1.6}{\micro\second} per new order, some three hundred times the gateway's own work of 4 to \qty{5}{\nano\second} per
message, and more than the rest of the order's path through the machine. Running sums remove it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:7}
Every open order of the side enters \emph{pending cancel} when the request is sent (one request, one throttle token); the
exposure keeps counting each in full until its own cancellation, or a fill, arrives. Test against the engine with fills
crossing the mass cancel: every order must end filled or cancelled, the \omterm{def:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:dropcopy}{drop copy} must reconcile, and the position must stay
under the limit throughout.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:8}
A fill that crossed the cancel arrives for an order the gateway no longer knows: it is ignored (or treated as an error), so
the position is wrong and the exposure understated, which is exactly the double position of the chapter's opening. An order
stays in the table until a terminal report (filled, cancelled, rejected), and the table is sized for it.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A fill before $c - d$ was reported before $c$, so the strategy knew it when it cancelled; a fill after $c + L$ finds the
order already cancelled. A fill between the two happens while the cancel is being decided or travelling, and its report
arrives after the cancel was sent.
\item The window has length $L + d$ and the fills are Poisson at rate $\lambda$: the probability of at least one is
$1 - e^{-\lambda (L + d)}$.
\item $1 - e^{-0.006} \approx 0.60\%$, $1 - e^{-0.024} \approx 2.4\%$ and $1 - e^{-0.204} \approx 18.5\%$.
\item $1 - e^{-x} \approx x - x^2/2$: the relative error is about $x/2$, under 5\% when $\lambda(L + d) < 0.1$.
\item 0.65\%, 2.1\% and 11.7\%.
\item The orders are held a millisecond on average, about as long as the path: many are cancelled before they have rested
$L + d$ at the venue, and some that would have raced were filled before the strategy decided to cancel.
\item The window stops growing at the holding time, so the probability tends to that of a fill during the holding time; the
exact curve of the figure flattens (16\% at \qty{3}{\milli\second}), where the simple formula keeps rising.
\item $6 / 245 = 2.4\%$, against 2.2\% for the exact probability: about 5.4 races expected, with a standard deviation of about
2.3, so yes.
\item The old order fills (100), the replacement is sent because the old order was counted as gone, and it fills too: 200
lots, twice the limit.
\item $n$ times the order's quantity, $100\,n$ lots above the limit.
\item It keeps counting the old order in full until its cancellation or its fill is reported, and refuses the replacement
until then: the firm never exceeds the limit, and the strategy waits one round trip before it can reuse the quantity.
\item $40\,000 \times 2.4\% \approx 950$ (about 880 with the exact probability for orders held a millisecond).
\item \textbf{Named result.} A cancel races a fill with probability $1 - e^{-\lambda (L + d)} \approx \lambda (L + d)$ for an
order resting longer than $L + d$ (2.4\% at \qty{100}{\micro\second}, 200 fills a second and \qty{20}{\micro\second} of report
delay); without in-flight accounting, each race can put one more order's quantity on the position, beyond the limit.
\item The one-way latency $L$ and the report delay $d$ (both paths through its own systems and lines) and how often it cancels;
not the fill rate, which is the market.
\item The window is $L + d$: halving $L$ halves only one of its terms.
\item Both sides followed the protocol: the venue filled a live order and then correctly answered that the cancel came too
late.
\item Reports about it can still arrive, and a fill for an unknown order would be lost.
\item A replace races in the same way, and while it is pending either the old or the new quantity may fill: the gateway counts
the larger.
\item Count, from the journal, the fills that arrived while their order was pending cancel, divide by the cancels, and compare
with $\lambda (L + d)$ using the measured latencies and the fill rate of the day.
\item Count every order for the worst it can still do until the venue says otherwise.
\end{enumerate}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:1}
Pending new, live, partially filled, pending cancel, pending replace, then filled, cancelled or rejected. The exchange knows the
truth; the firm knows what it asked and what it heard back, so it needs states for ``asked, not yet answered'', during which
the order can still fill.
\iqlookfor{the pending states, and why they exist.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:2}
The fill happened at the venue before the cancel arrived: a \omterm{def:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:inflight}{cancel--fill race}. Apply the fill (the order is filled or
partially filled), expect the cancel to be rejected as too late or to cancel only what remains, and never treat the fill as an
error or the order as gone before the terminal report.
\iqlookfor{the race explained, and worst-case accounting.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:3}
A \omterm{def:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:bucket}{token bucket} in integer units, set below the venue's limit, checked before each message. Cancels reduce risk, so they get a
reserved part of the budget or priority over new orders; a refused cancel must be retried at once and reported to the
strategy.
\iqlookfor{a bucket, and cancels treated differently.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:4}
Nothing certain: orders may have filled or been cancelled (by cancel on disconnect, if enabled). Count them all in full, stop
new orders, log in again asking for the reports missed, apply the replay, reconcile with the \omterm{def:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:dropcopy}{drop copy}, and only then resume.
\iqlookfor{worst case while blind, replay by sequence number, reconciliation before resuming.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:5}
Keep running sums of the worst remaining quantity per side (and per instrument), updated whenever an order changes: remove its
old contribution, apply the change, add the new one. The check is one addition and one comparison.
\iqlookfor{incremental aggregates rather than scans.}
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:ll:order-gateway-and-order-management:6}
One gateway per venue session with its state machine, throttle and worst-case exposure; a firm-wide aggregation of exposures
above them for limits across venues; the venue's reports as the source of truth for fills, checked continuously against drop
copies and daily against the broker; every break investigated, never overwritten.
\iqlookfor{per-venue truth from reports, independent copies, and reconciliation.}
\end{solution}
