\section*{Chapter \ref{ch:rs:order-book-replay-simulation} --- Order-Book Replay Simulation}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:1}
The shadow joins behind 750. The 300 executed leave 450 ahead; the cancellation of 100 leaves 350; the order of 400 joins behind. Of the 450 executed,
350 consume the two orders still ahead and the last 100 execute against the order that joined behind: the shadow fills all 100.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:2}
The share ahead is $600/800 = 0.75$: 300 of the cancelled 400 come from ahead and 100 from behind. New position: 300 ahead, 100 behind.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:3}
The order was decided on a book 3 milliseconds old and reaches the exchange 5 milliseconds later: it meets a book 8 milliseconds newer than the one it was
priced on.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:4}
The front model fills the shadow on every execution at its price, including the many small executions after which the price turns back: benign fills. The
fifo model fills it only after the queue ahead is consumed, which happens mostly when the flow is heavy and the price is about to move through: adverse
fills. Latency makes it worse, because the orders that still fill late are the ones faster traders did not want.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:5}
$522 \times 100 \times 0.10/2 = 2\,610$ ticks of a cent: \$26 an hour.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:6}
Shadows fill only by recorded executions at their price, and a price with no resting orders has none: an aggressor that would have hit the better-priced shadow
is recorded executing elsewhere. Strategies that improve the price, or take liquidity in size, need a model of the aggressors' response (a reactive simulator)
or live evidence; the replay understates their fills.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:7}
\texttt{rs\_lobreplay.see\_or\_act(1.0)}: with only a one-second \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:latency}{order-entry latency} the quoter fills 376 lots an hour with a mark-out of $-0.075$ ticks and loses
\$133; with only a one-second \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:latency}{market-data latency} it fills 416 lots with $-0.132$ ticks and loses \$173. Seeing late is worse here: a stale view keeps orders at
prices the market has already left, and the fills that come are the ones that have become bad.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{exo:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:8}
Filling on every print at the quote's price is the front-of-queue model: it assumes the quote was first in every queue and ignores the orders ahead of it, taking
every benign fill a real order would miss. On the simulated tape that model turned a strategy with no edge into \$1\,106 an hour. Replay with \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:queue}{queue positions} and
latency, look at the mark-outs, and bound the impact.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{pb:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:1}
\begin{enumerate}
\item 129\,303. The identifier of every resting order, so the orders ahead of a shadow are known exactly; market-by-price gives only each level's total size, and the
shadow's place must be modelled.
\item It can be filled by recorded executions and changes the strategy's position and cash; it does not change any recorded message: no impact.
\item The strategy's events (its orders' arrivals and cancellations, its view of the market) act before a market message at the same time.
\item On a shared fixture (8\,513 messages, 75 shadow orders): the Python engine, the Python reference, the C++20 core and the Rust twin all give the same 29 fills.
\item Front: \$1\,106 an hour, 61\% of traded volume, 3\,195 lots. Fifo: $-\$76$, 10\%, 522 lots.
\item $-\$49$ an hour and 499 lots: executions consume the quantity ahead first in both, and proportional cancellation is a fair guess when cancellations fall anywhere in
the queue, as they do in the simulator.
\item No real order is first in every queue: the model takes the benign executions a real order behind the queue never gets.
\item Front: $+0.39$ ticks after 10 seconds; fifo: $+0.008$.
\item Lots from 522 to 182 an hour; mark-out from $+0.008$ to $-0.115$ ticks.
\item The mark-out turns negative at 50 milliseconds ($-0.001$); the P\&L is within its noise of zero at every latency.
\item That the value of predicting liquidity-consuming flows is eroded by latency when there is not enough time to cancel and reinsert the order.
\item Its profit falls from \$1\,106 to \$192 an hour and its share of volume from 61\% to 11\%, but its mark-out stays near $+0.4$: it still takes only benign
fills, just fewer.
\item \textbf{Named result.} Front of queue: \$1\,106, 778, 672, 498 and 192 an hour at 0, 50 ms, 200 ms, 1 s and 5 s. Fifo: $-\$76$, $-94$, $-89$, $-146$ and
$-8$. Prob: $-\$49$, $-42$, $-41$, $-26$ and $+33$. With \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:queue}{queue positions} the quoter has no edge at any latency, and its mark-out turns negative at 50 milliseconds.
\item 344 lots a day (47\% of its orders), $-\$239$ a day, a five-minute mark-out of $-0.77$ ticks; the \omterm{def:rs:event-driven-backtests:fill}{penetration fill} was closer than the \omterm{def:rs:event-driven-backtests:fill}{touch fill}.
\item About \$26 an hour with no latency (\$9 at five seconds): small against this strategy's noise, but decisive for one whose edge is tens of dollars.
\item The market's reaction to the strategy (impact, and other traders' responses) and its real latency and fills: a reactive simulator (Book~10) and live trading at
small size (chapters 19 and 21).
\item Quote only when the queue is short or the imbalance favourable (chapter~8), and cancel faster when the flow turns toxic (chapter~9).
\item Each order's send, acknowledgement and fill times, the exchange's \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:queue}{queue position} reports where available, and the market's executions at the order's price, to

\item So that research and production fill orders by the same rule, checked on one fixture, and a discrepancy is a bug rather than a debate.
\item Where the strategy's order stands among the orders at its price, and how that changes as the book does.

\begin{solution}{iq:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:1}
They assume fills a real order would not get: first place in the queue (fills on every touch or print), no latency, no impact. The fills the model adds are the
benign ones (the price turns back), so the \omterm{def:rs:vectorised-backtests:backtest}{backtest} understates adverse selection; on the chapter's tape, front-of-queue fills made \$1\,106 an hour of a strategy
that loses with \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:queue}{queue positions}.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:2}
Start at the level's total size when the order arrives; move it up by every execution at the price; on each size decrease without an execution, attribute part of it
to the orders ahead with a probability that depends on the position (proportional to the share ahead, or a power or log function of it, as hftbacktest's
probabilistic models do); calibrate against live fills or a market-by-order feed.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:3}
A single event queue merging market messages and the strategy's delayed events; a book rebuilt by order identifier; shadow orders with \omterm{def:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:queue}{queue positions} under a
pluggable model; latency queues for data and orders. Invariants: recorded messages are never altered; nothing acts before its timestamp; the strategy's events at a
time act before the market message at that time; runs are deterministic; the fill rule is the same as production's.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:4}
Fewer fills (orders arrive later in the queue and are cancelled later), and worse ones: the fills that remain are those faster traders let through, so mark-outs fall.
On the chapter's quoter the mark-out goes from $+0.008$ ticks with no latency to $-0.1$ at one second; seeing late costs more than acting late.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:5}
The strategy's orders and trades would have moved prices and changed other traders' behaviour; a replay keeps the recorded messages. Bound it with an impact
coefficient (\omterm{def:rs:trade-flow-features:lambda}{Kyle's lambda} times the strategy's traded size), check the strategy's share of the volume at its prices, and confirm at small size live.
\end{solution}

\begin{solution}{iq:rs:order-book-replay-simulation:6}
Write the fill rule once as a specification, implement it in each language, and check them on shared fixtures (messages, orders with their times, expected fills)
bit for bit in continuous integration, as the chapter's Python, C++20 and Rust implementations are; add live fills to the fixtures as they arrive.
\end{solution}
