# 22. Stress Testing and Scenarios — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In March 2021 a family office defaulted on its swap margin calls and its prime brokers lost more than ten billion dollars between them; one lost five and a half billion on positions no value-at-risk model had flagged.
- **Sections.** What value at risk cannot see; Historical scenarios; Hypothetical scenarios and their consistency; Reverse stress tests; Supervisory stress tests.
- **Defines.** stress test, historical scenario, hypothetical scenario, scenario conditioning, reverse stress test, supervisory stress test.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** value at risk (ch21), expected shortfall (ch21), risk factor (ch21), historical simulation (ch21), total return swap (B1.6), prime broker (B1.6), liquidity spiral (B1.31), margin spiral (B3.28), dash for cash (B2.4), liability-driven investment (B2.7), procyclicality (B1.20), covariance matrix (B4.22).
- **Tutorial.** Apply the 1987, 2008, March 2020 and September 2022 scenarios to a multi-asset book; fill the unshocked factors of a hypothetical scenario by conditioning; find the most plausible scenario that costs a given loss.
- **Build.** `firm.stresstest`: scenario library, conditional scenario expansion, reverse stress by constrained optimisation.
- **Weekend problem.** The family office from the broker's side — named result: the market move at which a concentrated swap book exhausts its margin, found by reverse stress.
- **Facts to verify.** Archegos: Credit Suisse special committee report (Paul Weiss, July 2021); 2022 UK LDI gilt episode (Bank of England); Fed supervisory stress test scenarios (latest year, dated); EBA EU-wide stress test (latest, dated); October 1987 crash size (public).
