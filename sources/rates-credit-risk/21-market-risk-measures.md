# 21. Market-Risk Measures — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In the early 1990s a bank's chairman asked for one number, delivered at a quarter past four each afternoon, that said how much the firm could lose tomorrow; in 1994 the method was published for everyone.
- **Sections.** Loss distributions and value at risk; Value at risk three ways; Expected shortfall and coherence; Backtesting the measure; Risk contributions.
- **Defines.** risk factor, value at risk, expected shortfall, historical simulation, filtered historical simulation, parametric value at risk, Monte Carlo value at risk, delta--gamma approximation, coherent risk measure, square-root-of-time rule, VaR exception, Kupiec test, Christoffersen test, traffic-light test.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** realised volatility (B1.25), P\&L attribution (B1.7), GARCH model (B4.18), extreme-value theory (B4.15), bootstrap (B4.13), Monte Carlo (B4.26), hypothesis test (B4.12), gamma (B1.26), bucketed sensitivity (ch3), Euler allocation (ch20).
- **Tutorial.** Compute historical, parametric and Monte Carlo VaR and expected shortfall of a small multi-asset book on public rates and FX data, backtest them over four years and place them in the traffic-light zones.
- **Build.** `firm.varmodel`: VaR and ES by three methods, delta--gamma approximation, backtests (Kupiec, Christoffersen, traffic light), Euler contributions.
- **Weekend problem.** The quarter-past-four report — named result: the 99 per cent VaR and 97.5 per cent ES of a desk, its exceptions over 250 days, and the capital multiplier they imply.
- **Facts to verify.** RiskMetrics Technical Document 1994/1996 (decay 0.94); Basel 1996 market-risk amendment (99%, 10 days, multiplier 3, traffic light); BCBS FRTB expected shortfall at 97.5%; Artzner, Delbaen, Eber, Heath 1999 (paper); Kupiec 1995; Christoffersen 1998 (papers); FRED H.10 FX and Treasury yields (public domain).
