# 28. Operational Risk and Rogue Trading — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** On 26 February 1995 Barings, a bank more than two centuries old, failed because the losses one trader had hidden in an account numbered 88888 had grown larger than its capital.
- **Sections.** Operational risk and its measurement; The documented cases; The control failures they share; Detecting fictitious and hidden trades.
- **Defines.** operational risk, operational loss event, rogue trading, segregation of duties, fictitious trade, block leave.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** three lines of defence (ch26), unexplained P\&L (ch27), product control (ch27), independent price verification (ch27), position limit (B1.27), margin call (B1.6), settlement (B1.5), P\&L attribution (B1.7).
- **Tutorial.** Run detection rules on a synthetic trade blotter (late bookings, cancel-and-amend around reporting dates, off-market prices, internal counterparties, forward-dated trades that never settle) and measure hit and false-alarm rates.
- **Build.** `firm.tradecontrol`: blotter surveillance rules, alert scoring, audit log.
- **Weekend problem.** January 2008 — named result: the position size implied by the reported unwinding loss and market move, and the first control in the chain that would have caught it.
- **Facts to verify.** Barings: Board of Banking Supervision report 1995 (loss GBP 827m); Daiwa 1995 (USD 1.1bn), Sumitomo 1996 (USD 2.6bn) (court/regulatory records); Allfirst: Promontory (Ludwig) report 2002 (USD 691m); Société Générale: Mission Green report and Commission Bancaire 2008 (EUR 4.9bn, EUR 50bn position); UBS 2011 (USD 2.3bn): FINMA and FSA 2012 decisions; Basel operational-risk definition and standardised approach (2017; implementation dated).
