# 2. Switches and Layer-1 Devices — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A store-and-forward switch cannot forward a 1,500-byte frame before it has received all of it, 1.2 microseconds at 10 Gb/s; a layer-1 fan-out device copies the same signal to dozens of ports in a few nanoseconds without reading a single bit of it. The fastest switch in a trading cage is the one that does not look at the packet.
- **Sections.** Store-and-forward against cut-through; Layer-1 devices: fan-out, multiplexing and patching; Taps and mirror ports; Designing the network inside one cage.
- **Defines.** store-and-forward switching, cut-through switching, layer-1 switch, layer-1 fan-out, layer-1 multiplexer, network tap, port mirroring, out-of-band management network, direct-attach cable.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** latency (B13.1), jitter (B13.1), tail latency (B13.1), wire-to-wire latency (B13.1), tick-to-trade latency (B13.1), latency budget (B13.1), Ethernet frame (ch1), serialisation delay (ch1), line rate (ch1), egress queue (ch1), microburst (ch1), multicast group (ch1), redundant feed lines (B10.26), order-entry session (B10.26), hardware timestamp (B13.5).
- **Tutorial.** Add cut-through forwarding, a layer-1 fan-out and a layer-1 multiplexer to firm.netsim; send the feed and the orders of chapter 1 through three cage designs (two commodity switches in series, one low-latency cut-through switch, layer-1 fan-out for data and a layer-1 multiplexer for orders) and measure the path latency by frame size and the multiplexer's contention when two servers send at once. End state: a chart of forwarding latency against frame size for each device type, and a table of feed-in and order-out latency per design.
- **Build.** `firm.cagenet`: a cage network as data (devices with their forwarding mode and per-hop latency parameters, ports, cables with length and medium), path latency from exchange handoff to each server and back, a redundancy check (no single device or cable between line A or line B and any server, and between any server and each order-entry handoff), a capture-point check (every handoff tapped); wraps firm.netsim; Python. Consumed by chapters 5 and 29.
- **Weekend problem.** Three designs for one cage -- named result: the order-entry path latency of each of three cage designs, the nanoseconds each saves, and the cost per nanosecond saved from list-price inputs.
- **Facts to verify.** forwarding latency of a named cut-through switch and of a named layer-1 device from their datasheets (dated); signal velocity in fibre and in twinax copper (datasheet: nominal velocity of propagation); a passive optical tap's split ratio and insertion loss (vendor datasheet, dated); Linux / switch documentation on port mirroring limits (oversubscribed mirror ports drop).
- **Data.** Parameters from datasheets (ledger rows); simulated traffic from chapter 1.
