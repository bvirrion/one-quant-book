# 14. Long-Haul Wireless — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** When a storm crosses the line of towers between Chicago and New Jersey the microwave links fade and, for a few hours, the fastest route is fibre again: researchers who matched weather records to trading data found that adverse selection and trading costs fell on exactly those afternoons.
- **Sections.** Microwave links: line of sight, Fresnel zones and towers; Weather, rain fade and availability; Millimetre waves and lasers; Shortwave radio across oceans; Licences, towers, bandwidth and what is sent.
- **Defines.** microwave link, line-of-sight path, Fresnel zone, earth bulge, link budget, fade margin, rain fade, link availability, millimetre-wave link, free-space optical link, skywave propagation.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** geodesic distance (ch10), latency floor (ch10), group index (ch10), route factor (ch10), latency arbitrage (B11.9), adverse selection (B1.1), latency (B13.1), dark fibre (ch13), forward error correction (ch13).
- **Tutorial.** Compute a hop's geometry (earth bulge with the 4/3 earth-radius factor, first Fresnel zone radius, tower heights for clearance), its link budget and fade margin, and the rain attenuation from the ITU-R P.838 model at 6, 11, 18 and 80 GHz; run a labelled simulation of a storm cell crossing a chain of hops between Chicago and New Jersey, with failover to fibre. End state: a chart of specific attenuation against rain rate by frequency, and a timeline of route latency through the storm.
- **Build.** `firm.radiolink`: hop and chain models on firm.geomap (geometry, clearance, link budget, fade margin), ITU-R P.838 rain attenuation (coefficients as data with their source), availability from a rain-rate distribution, and a route selector that fails over to a firm.fibreroute path; Python. Consumed by chapters 15, 19 and 29.
- **Weekend problem.** The storm between Chicago and New Jersey -- named result: the rain rate at which a hop of a given length and frequency loses its fade margin, and the minutes of fibre-only latency that a storm crossing the route costs.
- **Facts to verify.** Shkilko and Sokolov 2020, 'Every cloud has a silver lining: fast trading, microwave connectivity, and trading costs' (Journal of Finance); ITU-R P.838-3 coefficients k and alpha; ITU-R P.530 (terrestrial line-of-sight design: Fresnel clearance, k-factor); FCC Universal Licensing System as the public record of microwave paths; a published Chicago-New Jersey microwave latency (dated); a published source on shortwave (HF) trading links and their licences (dated); free-space optics and millimetre-wave between exchange data centres: a public filing or paper (e.g., the NYSE wireless filings) (dated).
- **Data.** ITU-R coefficients (ledger rows); storm is a labelled simulation.
