# 4. Time Synchronisation — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** European rules require a high-frequency trader's business clocks to stay within 100 microseconds of UTC and to timestamp to the microsecond; a server's quartz oscillator, left alone, drifts by tens of microseconds every second. Staying within the rule is a control loop, and proving it is a record.
- **Sections.** Clocks, oscillators and drift; Two-way time transfer: NTP and PTP; Grandmasters, satellite references and holdover; Boundary and transparent clocks: removing the network's error; The regulatory requirements and proving your clock.
- **Defines.** clock offset, fractional frequency offset, Allan deviation, holdover, Network Time Protocol, Precision Time Protocol, grandmaster clock, boundary clock, transparent clock, path asymmetry, clock servo, GNSS time reference, traceability to UTC.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** hardware timestamp (B13.5), time-stamp counter (B13.2), invariant time-stamp counter (B13.5), exchange timestamp (B1.28), receive timestamp (B1.28), jitter (B13.1), egress queue (ch1), network interface card (ch1), microburst (ch1).
- **Tutorial.** Model a server's oscillator (frequency offset, random-walk frequency noise), run PTP's four-timestamp exchange over firm.netsim with queueing and a path asymmetry, discipline the clock with a PI servo, then add transparent clocks that correct residence time; compute the offset against true time over an hour for each set-up and the Allan deviation of the free-running and disciplined clocks. End state: a chart of offset over time (NTP-style over a loaded path, PTP without and with transparent clocks) against the 100-microsecond band, and an Allan-deviation plot.
- **Build.** `firm.clocksync`: oscillator models (quartz, oven-controlled, rubidium parameters as data), the PTP delay-request-response exchange with hardware or software timestamps, transparent and boundary clocks, a PI servo with step and slew, holdover, and a compliance report of offsets against a regulatory tolerance (maximum divergence from UTC, timestamp granularity); Python, with a C++20 servo kernel checked on a fixture. Consumed by chapters 5, 20 and 28.
- **Weekend problem.** A hundred microseconds to UTC -- named result: the end-to-end error budget from a satellite receiver to a trading server's timestamps, and the hours a given oscillator can hold over after losing its reference before it breaches the 100-microsecond limit.
- **Facts to verify.** Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/574 (RTS 25), annex tables 1-2: 100 microseconds and 1 microsecond granularity for high-frequency algorithmic trading; 1 ms for other algorithmic trading; FINRA Rule 6820 and the CAT NMS Plan clock synchronisation tolerance (50 ms for industry members; tighter for exchanges); IEEE 1588-2019 (PTP) message exchange and clock types; RFC 5905 (NTPv4); GPS Standard Positioning Service performance standard: UTC time-transfer accuracy; typical frequency stability of quartz, OCXO and rubidium oscillators (manufacturer datasheets, dated); linuxptp (ptp4l, phc2sys) documentation; White Rabbit (CERN) sub-nanosecond synchronisation, for contrast.
- **Data.** Simulated; oscillator parameters from datasheets (ledger rows).
