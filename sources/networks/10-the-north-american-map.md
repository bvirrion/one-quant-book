# 10. The North American Map — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Three data centres in New Jersey, a few tens of kilometres apart, hold most of the U.S. equity and options matching engines; a fourth, outside Chicago, holds the futures. Light in a vacuum needs about four milliseconds to cross from Chicago to New Jersey, light in glass about six, and the history of North American trading networks is the story of closing that gap.
- **Sections.** The New Jersey triangle; The Chicago-area futures site; Toronto and the rest of the continent; Distances, light-times and route factors; Method: a site table you can defend.
- **Defines.** geodesic distance, latency floor, group index, route factor.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** colocation (ch9), cross-connect (ch9), proximity hosting (ch9), synchronised routing (B10.18), latency arbitrage (B11.9), national best bid and offer (B1.9), securities information processor (B1.9), direct feed (B1.9), latency (B13.1).
- **Tutorial.** Load the site table (coordinates of each named data centre, each a ledger row with its source), compute every pairwise WGS-84 geodesic distance with Vincenty's inverse formula (checked against published geodesic test values), and the one-way latency floor in vacuum, the time in air and the time in fibre along the geodesic; divide published route latencies by the fibre time to get route factors; project the sites for a TikZ map. End state: the map of the New Jersey triangle with Chicago, drawn from computed coordinates, and a latency table (distances, floors, published routes, route factors).
- **Build.** `firm.geomap`: sites as data (id, operator, venues, latitude, longitude, source row), the WGS-84 geodesic (Vincenty inverse with a convergence guard, tested against GeographicLib's published values), latency floors for vacuum, air and fibre, a route-factor calculator, local projections and TikZ-ready coordinate exports to figdata; Python with a C++20 geodesic twin on the same test set. Consumed by chapters 11-14, 17, 19, 21, 24, 28 and 29.
- **Weekend problem.** Carteret to Aurora -- named result: the one-way latency floor between the Nasdaq and CME data centres in vacuum, in air and in fibre along the geodesic, and the route factor implied by a published fibre round trip.
- **Facts to verify.** addresses and coordinates of the NYSE (Mahwah), Nasdaq (Carteret), Equinix NY4/NY5 (Secaucus), CME (Aurora, CyrusOne) and TMX (Toronto-area) data centres, from company pages or SEC filings, coordinates from a cited geocoder (dated); which venues match in which building (dated box, each row sourced); speed of light in vacuum (SI definition, 299 792 458 m/s); group index of standard single-mode fibre at 1550 nm (Corning SMF-28 datasheet); refractive index of air near the ground (about 1.0003); GeographicLib / Karney 2013 geodesic test data; a published Chicago-New Jersey fibre or wireless latency (dated).
- **Data.** Site coordinates (ledger rows, committed as a small CSV); all distances and times computed.
