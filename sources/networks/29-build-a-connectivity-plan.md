# 29. Build: A Connectivity Plan — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A new firm will trade three venues -- an equity exchange in New Jersey, a futures exchange outside Chicago and a crypto venue in a Tokyo cloud region -- and has to say, before it signs anything, what latency it will get at each, what it must buy, from whom, on what terms, and what it will cost a year.
- **Sections.** Requirements: strategies, venues and latency targets; The latency table; The bill of materials; Contracts and service levels; The annual budget and its sensitivities.
- **Defines.** connectivity plan, bill of materials.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** geodesic distance (ch10), latency floor (ch10), group index (ch10), route factor (ch10), colocation (ch9), cross-connect (ch9), proximity hosting (ch9), cloud region (B3.26), availability zone (ch16), availability-zone identifier (ch16), service-level agreement (ch15), financial extranet (ch15), route diversity (ch15), latency budget (B13.1), wire-to-wire latency (B13.1), disaster-recovery site (ch28), recovery time objective (ch28), private endpoint (ch18), order-entry port (ch22), market segment gateway (ch23), build against buy (B16.20), data budget (B16.22).
- **Tutorial.** Run the plan end to end from one configuration file: requirements, the latency table from the book's components (geomap, fibreroute, radiolink, cagenet, wirepath, cloudplan, privlink), the bill of materials (serverspec, portplan, vendormap), contracts (slamodel), disaster recovery (drplan) and the budget (colobill, cloudplan); read the report and change one requirement. End state: the plan's latency table, bill of materials and annual budget, with every price row dated and sourced.
- **Build.** `firm.connplan`: the connectivity plan -- inputs (venues, strategies and their latency targets, footprints), outputs (latency table, bill of materials, contracts with service levels, disaster-recovery design, annual budget with sensitivities, a list of unmet targets and the cheapest change that meets each), and checks (every target met or flagged; every price row has a ledger id and a date no older than 60 days); Python on the book's components. The book's capstone.
- **Weekend problem.** Three venues, one budget -- named result: the annual all-in connectivity cost of entering the three venues, the latency achieved at each against its target, and the cheapest change that meets the one missed target.
- **Facts to verify.** every dated price, product and location from chapters 9-28 (re-verified in Phase C); a public example of a new firm's venue-entry costs if one exists (otherwise none).
- **Data.** Ledger rows of earlier chapters; computed plan.
