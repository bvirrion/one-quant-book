# 13. Long-Haul Fibre — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In 2010 a new cable between Chicago and New Jersey took a round trip of about 13 milliseconds by digging straighter than the railway rights of way the older routes followed; within a few years microwave towers had beaten it, and the cable's buyers had learnt that glass is slower than air.
- **Sections.** Light in glass: attenuation, amplifiers and dispersion; Route straightness: the Chicago-New York story; Subsea cables; Dark fibre against leased wavelengths; Faster glass: hollow-core fibre.
- **Defines.** optical amplifier, chromatic dispersion, dispersion compensation, forward error correction, dark fibre, lit service, wavelength service, indefeasible right of use, cable landing station, hollow-core fibre.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** geodesic distance (ch10), latency floor (ch10), group index (ch10), route factor (ch10), latency (B13.1), latency arbitrage (B11.9), cross-connect (ch9).
- **Tutorial.** Model a fibre route as spans (length, group index, amplifier and dispersion-compensation per span, FEC and terminal equipment), compute its one-way latency, and invert published route latencies (the 2010 Chicago-New Jersey route; a transatlantic low-latency cable) into implied route factors and equipment shares; then replace the glass with hollow-core fibre and recompute. End state: a stacked bar chart of latency components per route and the hollow-core saving.
- **Build.** `firm.fibreroute`: fibre routes as spans on firm.geomap coordinates, latency by component, implied-route-factor inversion from a published latency, and a dark-fibre (IRU plus operations, amortised) against lit-wavelength (monthly) cost model with a break-even term; Python. Consumed by chapters 14, 15, 19 and 29.
- **Weekend problem.** Thirteen milliseconds -- named result: the implied route factor and the split of the 2010 Chicago-New Jersey round trip into glass, equipment and excess route length, and the milliseconds a hollow-core fibre on the same path would save.
- **Facts to verify.** Laughlin, Aguirre and Grundfest 2014, 'Information transmission between financial markets in Chicago and New York' (Financial Review): route latencies and dates; Spread Networks' Chicago-New Jersey route round trip (about 13 ms, 2010) from press or the paper; a transatlantic low-latency cable's published round trip (e.g., Hibernia Express, 2015) (dated); Corning SMF-28 datasheet: group index, attenuation; ITU-T G.652 (single-mode fibre) and G.709 (OTN FEC); hollow-core fibre: a published paper on latency (group index near 1.0) and deployment; indefeasible right of use as a telecom contract form (a regulator's or a filing's definition).
- **Data.** Published route latencies (ledger rows); computed components.
