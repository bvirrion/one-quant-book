# 5. Capturing and Monitoring the Network — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The strategy's own log said that it answered a price change in two microseconds; the capture taken at the cage's taps said the order left the building four microseconds after the packet came in. The difference -- the card, the kernel path, the switches -- was the part nobody had measured.
- **Sections.** Taps, capture appliances and capture files; Measuring wire-to-wire latency: matching orders to their trigger packets; Detecting gaps, drops and microbursts from a capture; Monitoring in production: counters, telemetry and alarms.
- **Defines.** packet capture, packet capture file, capture appliance, timestamp trailer, trigger packet, streaming telemetry.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** latency (B13.1), jitter (B13.1), tail latency (B13.1), wire-to-wire latency (B13.1), tick-to-trade latency (B13.1), latency budget (B13.1), hardware timestamp (B13.5), network tap (ch2), port mirroring (ch2), microburst (ch1), multicast group (ch1), message gap (B13.16), slow consumer (B13.12), latency attribution (B13.26), instrumentation point (B13.26), latency histogram (B13.5), clock offset (ch4), stale-book flag (B13.18).
- **Tutorial.** Write a nanosecond-resolution pcap file from a simulated session: the venue's feed frames and the firm's order frames, timestamped by a simulated tap on the cage network of chapter 2 with the software stages taken from Book 13's measured tick-to-trade stages; read it back with a pure-Python pcap reader, match every order to its trigger packet, and decompose wire-to-wire latency into network, card and software parts; detect sequence gaps per multicast group and microbursts per port. End state: a stacked wire-to-wire budget (median and p99) and a gap and burst timeline, from the capture alone.
- **Build.** `firm.wirecap`: pcap and pcapng writer and reader with nanosecond timestamps and an optional timestamp trailer, Ethernet/IPv4/UDP/TCP encapsulation of the simulator's MoldUDP64 and order-entry messages, trigger matching, per-group gap detection, per-port burst detection and a report; Python with a C++20 reader checked on the same file. And `firm.wirepath`: Book 13's tick-to-trade path (firm.ticktotrade's stages, from its measured CSV or a firm.latbudget budget) extended with NIC receive and transmit, serialisation, cable and switch hops from firm.cagenet, composed into a wire-to-wire budget with firm.latbudget; Python. Both consumed by chapters 7 and 29.
- **Weekend problem.** The two microseconds nobody logged -- named result: the decomposition of wire-to-wire latency at the median and at the 99th percentile into switch, card and software stages, and which stage owns the 99th percentile.
- **Facts to verify.** pcap file format (tcpdump/libpcap: the nanosecond magic number 0xA1B23C4D) and pcapng (IETF draft-ietf-opsawg-pcapng); a published timestamp-trailer format of a capture-capable switch or layer-1 device (vendor documentation, dated); named capture-appliance vendors from their public product pages (dated box); OpenConfig / gNMI streaming telemetry documentation; RTS 25's requirement to timestamp reportable events (pointer to chapter 4).
- **Data.** Simulated capture built from Book 10's simulator and Book 13's measured stage CSV (read-only).
