# 22. Equities and Options Connectivity — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A firm that wants the whole U.S. options market must take a consolidated feed split across dozens of multicast partitions, sized by its operator for bursts rather than averages, and send its quotes through ports whose message budgets it cannot exceed: connectivity for options is capacity planning before it is latency.
- **Sections.** Gateway and port types; Consolidated options-feed bandwidth; Risk-check layers under sponsored access; Quote-traffic engineering.
- **Defines.** order-entry port, feed partition, sponsored-access risk layer.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** sponsored access (B1.4), direct feed (B1.9), securities information processor (B1.9), mass quote (B11.19), message throttle (B11.10), pre-trade risk check (B11.27), market access rule (B11.27), drop copy (B13.21), order-entry session (B10.26), microburst (ch1), line rate (ch1), colocation (ch9), feed handler (B13.18).
- **Tutorial.** From a consolidated feed's published capacity projections (dated) and chapter 1's microburst factor, compute the links, ports and handler cores needed to take the feed; from a venue's port limits (dated) and a quoting load, the order-entry and quote ports needed; add the latency of a broker's risk layer. End state: a capacity table and a chart of required 10 Gb/s links against the planning percentile.
- **Build.** `firm.portplan`: a port and bandwidth planner -- venue port types and limits as dated data, quoting load to ports, feed partitions to links, handler cores and servers with microburst headroom from firm.netsim; Python. Consumed by chapter 29.
- **Weekend problem.** Quoting every series -- named result: the ports and 10 Gb/s links needed to quote a given number of option classes at a given update rate and to take the consolidated feed in full.
- **Facts to verify.** SIAC / OPRA capacity projections and operating metrics (dated); an options venue's order-entry and quote-port limits and fees (dated); SEC Rule 15c3-5 (pointer B11.27); a named risk-check appliance or broker risk layer with a published latency (dated); equity venues' port types (order entry, drop copy, market data) from their connectivity documents (dated).
- **Data.** Published capacity figures (dated rows); computed plan.

## EXCLUDED

- A per-port message throughput for any options venue: not published in the documents read; the chapter derives a port's capacity from the synchronous interface and an assumed round trip.
- The number of MIAX matching environments today: filings of different years give different counts; the chapter assumes twelve.
- A published latency for a broker's software risk layer: none found; the chapter assumes 5 microseconds one way.
- Equity venues' port-type lists from their connectivity documents: not re-read for this chapter; ports types are described through the options documents and Book 10's order-entry sessions.
