# 1. Networking for Trading — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A feed whose one-second average used a few per cent of a 10 Gb/s port lost packets at the open: over the busiest ten microseconds its rate was above the port's line rate, and the switch's egress buffer, not the handler, dropped them. An average over one second says nothing about a microsecond.
- **Sections.** The Ethernet frame and what a byte costs on the wire; IP multicast: groups, membership and snooping; Datagrams against streams: why feeds are UDP and orders TCP; Congestion: egress queues, incast and microbursts.
- **Defines.** network interface card, Ethernet frame, line rate, serialisation delay, maximum transmission unit, multicast group, Internet Group Management Protocol, IGMP snooping, head-of-line blocking, Nagle's algorithm, microburst, egress queue, incast, oversubscription ratio.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** latency (B13.1), jitter (B13.1), tail latency (B13.1), wire-to-wire latency (B13.1), tick-to-trade latency (B13.1), latency budget (B13.1), market data feed (B1.4), sequence number (B1.28), multicast (B13.16), redundant feed lines (B10.26), line arbitration (B13.16), message gap (B13.16), message burst (B13.18), receive buffer overrun (B13.18), retransmission request (B10.26), snapshot recovery (B10.26), order-entry session (B10.26), M/M/1 queue (B4.8), exchange simulator (B10.26).
- **Tutorial.** Encapsulate a recorded minute of Book 10's simulated feed (MoldUDP64 packets) in Ethernet/IPv4/UDP frames and compute each frame's serialisation delay at 1, 10 and 25 Gb/s; measure the feed's peak rate over windows from one second down to one microsecond; push several such feeds through a simulated switch egress port (firm.netsim) with a finite buffer and count queue depth and drops against buffer size. End state: the 'microburst curve' (peak rate against averaging window, log-log) and a chart of drops against buffer size for fan-in of 1, 4 and 8 feeds.
- **Build.** `firm.netsim`: a discrete-event packet network model -- links (rate, propagation delay, MTU), hosts, switches with store-and-forward or cut-through forwarding, egress queues with finite byte buffers and tail drop, multicast replication by group, per-hop timestamps and drop accounting; Python reference with a C++20 egress-queue kernel checked on a shared fixture. Consumed by chapters 2, 4, 5, 7 and 29.
- **Weekend problem.** The open that did not fit -- named result: the minimum egress buffer (bytes) that carries the opening burst of eight feeds into one 10 Gb/s port without loss, and the drop count at a published buffer size of a low-latency switch.
- **Facts to verify.** IEEE 802.3 frame format: preamble and start delimiter 8 bytes, inter-frame gap 12 bytes, minimum frame 64 bytes, standard maximum 1518 bytes (1522 with a VLAN tag); RFC 768 (UDP), RFC 791 (IPv4), RFC 9293 (TCP) header sizes; RFC 896 (Nagle's algorithm) and the TCP_NODELAY socket option (Linux tcp(7)); RFC 1112 / RFC 3376 (IGMPv3) and RFC 4541 (IGMP snooping considerations); OPRA or another consolidated feed's published peak bandwidth per line (dated); packet buffer size of a named low-latency switch from its datasheet (dated).
- **Data.** Book 10's firm.exchsim recorded minute (regenerated by script, not committed); no external data.
