# 16. Trader — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In 2021 the largest US derivatives exchange group announced that most of its open-outcry pits would not reopen; nearly all its futures volume was already electronic. The trader's job had moved from shouting to clicking to supervising algorithms, and at a market maker today one trader may watch strategies quoting thousands of instruments.
- **Sections.** Three traders: market maker, bank flow desk, hedge fund; From click trading to supervising algorithms; Controls and accountability: registrations, certification, kill switches; A documented day.
- **Defines.** risk trader, execution trader, click trader, algorithm supervisor, certification regime.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** market maker (B1.1), sales-trader (B1.2), flow trading (B1.2), franchise P\&L (B9.24), kill switch (B11.27), loss limit (B11.27), pre-trade risk check (B11.27), algorithmic trading (B10.24), open outcry (ch2), floor trader (ch2), labor condition application (ch14), prevailing wage (ch14), wage level (ch14).
- **Tutorial.** The role's pay evidence: LCA base-salary ranges (10th-90th percentile, median) for the role family by employer type and wage level from data/industry/lca_ranges.csv through firm.paydata, cells under ten filings suppressed, beside the OEWS range for the matching occupation in the securities industry. Then the supervisor's load with firm.roles: alerts from a set of strategies as a Poisson stream, handling times from a stated distribution, and the waiting-time tail of a single supervisor (queueing results from Book 4 ch. 8). End state: the pay-range chart and the number of strategies one supervisor can watch at a stated alert-response standard.
- **Build.** `firm.roles` (first increment): the role registry (role card: firm types, horizon, P&L ownership, reporting line, SOC codes and title patterns for LCA matching, the series books that teach it) and the supervision-load model; Python; each later chapter of Part III adds its role card and one analysis.
- **Weekend problem.** One trader, many algorithms -- named result: the largest number of strategies one supervisor can oversee with at most a 1 per cent chance that an alert waits more than a minute, under the chapter's stated alert and handling rates, and its sensitivity to the alert rate.
- **Facts to verify.** CME Group release on open-outcry pits (2021) and electronic share of volume (dated); FINRA Series 57 Securities Trader Representative requirement (dated); FCA SM&CR certification functions including the algorithmic trading certification function (dated); MiFID II RTS 6 (Delegated Regulation 2017/589) staff and kill-functionality requirements; firms' own published descriptions of a trader's day (careers pages; dated); EU/UK certification of traders at banks (FCA Handbook SYSC 27) (dated).
- **Data.** data/industry/lca_ranges.csv; synthetic alert streams.
