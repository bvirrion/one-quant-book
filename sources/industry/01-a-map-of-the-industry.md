# 1. A Map of the Industry — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In one fiscal year's US labour-condition filings the job title quantitative researcher appears under employers of at least five business models -- electronic market makers, systematic funds, multi-manager platforms, banks and asset managers -- with offered base salaries spread over a factor of about three. The title names a task, not a job; the employer's business model decides what the task is for. (Numbers from the chapter's own LCA table.)
- **Sections.** Five questions that place an employer: whose capital, who pays, horizon, product, size; The taxonomy: business models and their sub-types; Size: headcount, capital and revenue bands; How the series maps onto the industry; Reading an employer from outside: codes, registrations and filings.
- **Defines.** principal trading firm, industry classification code, employer taxonomy.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** market maker (B1.1), proprietary trading firm (B1.1), hedge fund (B1.1), multi-manager platform (B1.1), asset manager (B1.1), assets under management (B1.1), sell side (B1.2), buy side (B1.3), broker-dealer (B1.2), franchise (B1.2), flow trading (B1.2), commodity trading advisor (B1.3), electronic market maker (B11.1), commodity trading house (B3.1), centralised exchange (B3.15), legal entity identifier (B2.28), exchange (B1.4), broker (B1.4).
- **Tutorial.** Classify a committed sample of about sixty employers (public identifiers only: SIC code from EDGAR, NAICS code from their LCA filings, regulator registrations -- SEC adviser, FINRA broker-dealer, NFA, FCA permissions -- and LEI) into the taxonomy with firm.industrymap's rules; compare the code-based class with the business-model class sourced in the ledger. End state: a taxonomy tree with counts per class, and a confusion table showing where industry codes misplace trading firms.
- **Build.** `firm.industrymap`: the employer taxonomy as data (business model, sub-type, size band, products), classification rules from public identifiers (NAICS, SIC, registration categories), the map from employer types to the series' books, and an agreement score between code-based and sourced classes; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Classifying from the outside -- named result: the share of the sample that industry codes alone place in the right business model, and the smallest set of public registrations that raises it above 90 per cent.
- **Facts to verify.** NAICS 2022 structure for 5231, 5239, 5221, 5242 and 5415 (US Census Bureau) and the 2017-to-2022 recoding of 523110/523120 into 523150; SEC SIC codes 6211, 6282, 6199, 6798 (EDGAR company search definitions); FIA Principal Traders Group's description of principal trading firms (dated); FCA register permission names: dealing in investments as principal, managing investments (dated); SEC Form ADV and FINRA BrokerCheck as public registration records; GLEIF LEI data licence (CC0); EU NACE Rev. 2.1 class 64.99/66.12 equivalents (Eurostat).
- **Data.** data/industry/employer_sample.csv (public identifiers, ledger ids); counts from data/industry/lca_ranges.csv.
