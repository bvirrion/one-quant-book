# 15. Pay Regulation and Tax by Location — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** From 2014 the European Union capped a bank risk-taker's bonus at twice fixed pay; the United Kingdom removed the cap in October 2023, citing its effect of raising fixed pay. The same package also leaves very different amounts after tax in London, New York, Amsterdam, Zurich, Singapore, Hong Kong and Dubai, and a special regime for incomers can change the answer in some of them.
- **Sections.** Pay regulation: the bonus cap, deferral rules and who they cover; Income tax on salary and bonus; Expatriate regimes; Deferred and equity pay: when it is taxed; What a number is worth after tax.
- **Defines.** bonus cap, marginal tax rate, average tax rate, expatriate tax regime, tax residence.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** bonus pool (B16.10), formulaic payout (B16.10), discretionary bonus (B16.10), deferred compensation (B16.10), vesting schedule (B16.10), malus (B16.10), clawback (B16.10), material risk taker (B16.10), deferral buyout (B16.10), investment firm (B16.17), carried interest (ch13), restricted stock unit (ch13), base salary (ch13), total compensation (ch13).
- **Tutorial.** Compute after-tax pay with firm.aftertax on sourced 2026 schedules for eight locations: net pay for packages from 150 thousand to 3 million in local-currency equivalents with a range of base shares, the marginal rate on the last unit of bonus, and the effect of each expatriate regime where one exists. End state: a chart of average tax rate against gross pay (log axis) for the eight locations and a table of net pay for one package.
- **Build.** `firm.aftertax`: tax and social-contribution schedules as dated data with a ledger id per parameter (brackets, allowance tapers, contribution ceilings, local taxes, expatriate regimes), net pay, marginal and average rates, and bonus-against-salary treatment; tests against hand-computed cases and the authorities' published worked examples; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** A million, eight ways -- named result: net pay from a package of one million (30 per cent base, 70 per cent bonus) in each of the eight locations, and the ratio of the highest to the lowest.
- **Facts to verify.** Directive 2013/36/EU art. 94(1)(g) as amended; Directive (EU) 2019/2034 art. 32 (investment firms); PRA PS9/23 and FCA PS23/15 (cap removed from 31 October 2023); US Dodd-Frank s. 956 proposals (2016; 2024 re-proposal) and their status (dated); HMRC income tax and NIC rates 2026/27; IRS 2026 brackets; New York State and City rates; Illinois flat rate (dated); Netherlands box 1 2026 and the 30 per cent ruling changes; Italy impatriate regime (2024 reform); France impatrie regime (CGI art. 155 B); Switzerland Zurich and Zug; Singapore resident rates (YA2026); Hong Kong two-tier standard rate from 2024/25; UAE absence of personal income tax (dated); UK non-dom abolition and the FIG regime from 6 April 2025 (dated); OECD Taxing Wages 2026 as a cross-check (licence to verify).
- **Data.** data/industry/tax_2026.csv (parameters, source id each).

## EXCLUDED

- Italy's impatriate regime and France's impatrie regime: not modelled; their current texts not read.
- US Dodd-Frank section 956 incentive-compensation proposals (2016, 2024): status not verified; not stated.
- OECD Taxing Wages 2026 cross-check: not used.
- Singapore CPF: not modelled (the chapter's employee is a foreign national; the coverage rule was not read in a primary source, so the text says only that the model includes no Singapore contributions).
- Hong Kong's deduction for MPF contributions: not modelled (MPF is counted as a cost, not deducted).
- Swiss deductions other than social contributions (occupational pension, professional expenses), and church tax: not modelled.
- New York: the 2025 schedule and a simplified benefit recapture are used; the 2026 cut below USD 215,400 changes the tax on the chapter's smallest package by at most a few hundred dollars.
