# 2. Proprietary Market Makers and High-Frequency Firms — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** One electronic market maker files a Form 10-K every February; firms of comparable or larger size file nothing in the United States but the audited statement of financial condition of their broker-dealer, the accounts of their UK subsidiary at Companies House, and -- when they borrow -- a rating agency's or a newspaper's account of what their lenders were shown. Most of what is public about the industry's largest private firms comes through such side doors.
- **Sections.** What the public record holds for a private trading firm; Origins: from the floors to the screens; Profiles: products, ownership, headcount and offices; Culture and technology as the firms describe them; Size and concentration: what venue and regulator data show.
- **Defines.** open outcry, floor trader, firm profile.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** market maker (B1.1), proprietary trading firm (B1.1), principal trading firm (ch1), electronic market maker (B11.1), revenue capture (B11.1), high-frequency trading (B10.24), latency arbitrage (B11.9), wholesaler (B1.10), payment for order flow (B1.10), designated market maker (B1.29), limited liability partnership (B16.2), technology treadmill (B16.2), net trading revenue (B16.1).
- **Tutorial.** Build ten firm profiles from sourced facts with firm.profiles: load data/industry/profiles/*.csv (one row per fact: field, low, high, as-of date, ledger id), validate that every fact is sourced, dated and a range, flag facts older than the staleness limit, and render the comparison table and a chart of founding year against headcount range (log axis). End state: the profile table and the chart.
- **Build.** `firm.profiles`: a sourced-fact store for firm profiles (Fact(firm, field, low, high, unit, as_of, ledger_id)), validation rules (no fact without a ledger id and a date; point values only where the source gives one; ranges widened to the source's rounding), staleness checks, and LaTeX and CSV renderers; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** What can the public know? -- named result: the disclosure gap, the share of a standard profile's fields that public sources fill for listed against private firms, and the median relative width of the private firms' headcount ranges.
- **Facts to verify.** Virtu Financial Form 10-K for 2025: headcount, offices, business lines (dated); X-17A-5 audited statements of financial condition of US broker-dealers of Jane Street, Citadel Securities, Hudson River Trading, Jump Trading, Tower Research, DRW (EDGAR; dated); Companies House accounts of UK subsidiaries (Jane Street Europe, Citadel Securities (Europe), HRT Europe, Jump Trading International, XTX Markets Ltd, Tower Research Capital Europe): average employees, turnover (dated); firms' own publications on founding, offices and technology (careers and technology blogs, conference talks, open-source releases: OCaml at Jane Street, Firedancer at Jump) (dated); Citadel Securities' own statement of its US retail volume share (dated); reputable press on reported revenues of private market makers (Bloomberg, FT, WSJ; context only, dated); Aquilina, Budish and O'Neill (2022, QJE) on latency races and the concentration of race wins; CME floor history and the 2021 closure of most open-outcry pits (CME Group release).
- **Data.** data/industry/profiles/*.csv (sourced facts).
