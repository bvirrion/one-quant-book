# 20. Software Engineer — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A listed electronic market maker of about a thousand people states in its annual report what share of its staff works in technology; a large technology company employs a hundred times as many people. At a trading firm an engineer is one of a few hundred, a few steps from the P&L, and on call while markets are open.
- **Sections.** Core, low-latency, infrastructure, hardware and network engineering; How a trading firm differs from a technology company; Pay and progression compared with technology firms; Public engineering: blogs, talks and open source.
- **Defines.** low-latency engineer, FPGA engineer, site reliability engineer.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** latency (B13.1), tail latency (B13.1), kernel bypass (B13.13), colocation (B14.9), on-call rotation (B15.28), blameless review (B15.28), service-level objective (B15.28), platform team (B16.19), product team (B16.19), research engineer (ch19), trading-system developer (ch19), labor condition application (ch14), prevailing wage (ch14), wage level (ch14), Standard Occupational Classification (ch14), small-cell suppression (ch14).
- **Tutorial.** The role's pay evidence: LCA base-salary ranges (10th-90th percentile, median) for the role family by employer type and wage level from data/industry/lca_ranges.csv through firm.paydata, cells under ten filings suppressed, beside the OEWS range for the matching occupation in the securities industry. Then the comparison with firm.roles: SOC 15-1252 filings at trading firms against a sourced list of large technology employers, by wage level, as a ratio of medians with bootstrap intervals. End state: a chart of the ratio by wage level.
- **Build.** `firm.roles` (increment): the engineering role cards and a two-population range comparison (ratio of medians, bootstrap interval, cell suppression); Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Trading firm or technology company? -- named result: the ratio of median offered base at trading firms to large technology employers for software developers at each wage level, with bootstrap intervals.
- **Facts to verify.** Virtu 10-K and Flow Traders annual report: share of staff in technology (dated); large technology employers' 10-K headcounts (dated, for scale); firms' engineering blogs, talks and open-source releases (Jane Street, HRT, Optiver, IMC, Jump) (dated); Book 13-15 pointers.
- **Data.** data/industry/lca_ranges.csv; data/industry/tech_employers.csv (sourced list).
