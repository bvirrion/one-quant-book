## Conventions of this book

- Teaching modules `mx_*.py`; running project `code/firm/<component>/firm_<component>.py`.
- Prices on the wire and in the engine are integers (1/10,000 currency unit, as Book 1's feed); time is
  integer nanoseconds since midnight (simulated clock). Figures convert to ticks or cents in the caption.
- Libraries: numpy, pandas, scipy, statsmodels (batch ruling 1). Seeds fixed; statistics over many seeds.
- Timings printed only as "measured on a laptop (Intel Core Ultra 7 155H) under WSL2, no isolated cores";
  tests assert orderings and ratios with wide margins, never absolute times (batch ruling 2).
- Data: simulated (firm.tape, firm.exchsim, firm.agentmkt) plus public-domain SEC MIDAS metrics; the
  "Data:" clause of each brief's tutorial says which.
- `make_briefs.py` has no field for "data needed": it is the tutorial string's final "Data:" clause.
- Rerunning `make_briefs.py --book 10` regenerates `DEFINITIONS.md` and drops its header notes: re-add them.

## Traps met so far

- **Memory (user limit 2026-09-25):** the first `make_recorded_day.py` held a whole 5 x 6.5 h day as Python objects
  (8 GB RSS) and was killed. Stream everything to disk; keep every process under ~1.5 GB and one core; one heavy job
  at a time; no parallel builds or test runs; `latexmk` only when a chapter is ready.
- Chapter labels use the filename slug without `NN-` (`ch:mx:the-limit-order-book`), not a shortened slug.
- Never hand-write `\omterm` (cross-book links are undefined references): write the plain word.
- A `\label` after a `tabular` in `center` points at the section: do not `\cref` tables.
- Zero-intelligence market without price improvement has an unbounded spread (sides drift apart): a useful ablation,
  not a usable market.
- firm.tape's P* does not react to trades: the realised-spread impact at long horizons is dominated by P*'s own moves
  (SE grows as sqrt(h)); chapter 5 subtracts eps (P*_{t+h} - P*_t), which only a simulation can.
- Listings: lines longer than ~95 characters wrap in the PDF (the ruff limit is 120): keep listed code short.
- A listing caption shorter than a line, followed by a long unbreakable code path, is typeset as a stretched line:
  write captions that run past one line.
- firm.exchsim's TapeBackground scales tape prices by the instrument tick; for a coarser grid build on a one-cent
  instrument and round (chapter 6's CoarseTape), do not change the tick.
- Replaying firm.tape's flow across two venues does not work: the tape's executions land on different orders than
  the venues', so either ghost orders pile up or cancelling them depletes the book twice. Chapter 7 drives two
  Engine instances with its own reactive flow (mx_fees.Flow) instead.
- Bisection for an equilibrium on a noisy simulated value difference is unreliable (non-monotone, tipping);
  report seed-averaged values with standard errors instead.
- Read Book 1's dated boxes before writing a regulatory date: the June 11, 2026 order (34-105656) deferred the
  half-penny tick and access fee cap to November 2027, superseding the October 2025 press release's November 2026.
- pgfplots legends split entries at commas: brace an entry that contains one.
- "protected quote" (B10 ch. 8, defined) overlaps Book 1's unindexed "protected quotation" (ch. 9): ch. 8 defines
  it generically and points to Book 1; report at Phase D.
- A price-impact model where informed orders also move P* creates a runaway feedback (informed trade toward P*,
  which their impact moves further): only uninformed flow should carry permanent impact.
- Paired "with/without the leak" differences are noisy once endogenous paths diverge; account the known costs
  (lambda x shares ahead x shares still to fill) inside one run instead.
- A replayed background (TapeBackground) cannot show impact: its providers repost where the recording did. Impact
  needs a reactive population (firm.agentmkt), and noise from the population makes metaorder impact expensive to
  resolve: pre-draw the exogenous flow and difference with/without runs, and still expect SE of 1-3 ticks per six runs.
- A reactive market with a thin book is chaotic: counterfactual paths diverge after the first child order.
- A benchmark study measured against each algorithm's own benchmark flatters the algorithm: the VWAP and the close
  include the algorithm's own trades. Run every day again without the algorithm (common random numbers) and report
  the impact cost against that day's mids.
- A dynamic volume model that treats a busy day's level as a transient deviation front-loads on every busy day
  (worse than static): separate the day's level (lasting) from the AR deviation (fading), e.g. by GLS on the bins seen.
- Participation arithmetic: trading r of the whole last-bin volume reaches participation r; following only the others
  needs r/(1-r). Two followers each pace at r/(1-2r) of the natural volume.
- Execution agents should top up to the plan from their fills, not from what they sent: IOC children on a thin book
  fill partially and the order ends short.
- Rounding ties (x.x5 exactly in binary, e.g. 5.25 minutes over 8 seeds) print half-even: take the test's value.
- A symmetric-drift simulated market makes passive placement dominate (Harris-Hasbrouck's finding): with repricing, an up-move costs a passive buy
  only as much as crossing did at the start; crossing wins only for tiny, very urgent orders. Report the absence of a threshold, do not tune for one.
- Rules whose best cut is ambiguous (ties) need an explicit tie-break (here: the most patient rule), or a zero-weight region picks an arbitrary cut.
- firm.exchsim client order ids are numbered per session: a multi-venue agent must set Order.cl_ord_id itself (unique across venues), or its
  reports cannot be told apart (on_report does not say which venue).
- Cont-Kukanov as an LP with the fills as free variables bounded by min(L, room) is a relaxation that overbooks without limit when over-fill is
  penalised: the fills are outcomes, not choices. Minimise the scenario average of the true cost instead.
- `make test-code CH=...` goes RED on a duplicate module name from other books (code/firm/mlinfer/make_fixture.py and code/firm/lobfeat/make_fixture.py,
  Books 12/13): not Book 10's; the chapter's own steps are green. Reported for the batch.
- Realised volatility from tick-level mid changes is dominated by bid-ask bounce (flat across regimes): measure a regime's volatility as the dispersion
  of the mid's move over the order's horizon.
- Execution-cost attribution must use the mid just before each fill (ref_at(t - eps)): the tape's top at the fill's timestamp already includes the
  fill's own move, which shifts spread into impact.
- pgfplots `xtick={0,...,6}` trips the drafty "..." gate: list the ticks.
- EUR-Lex throttles scripted downloads (HTTP 202, empty body) after a few requests: legislation.gov.uk serves the EU texts as adopted
  (eudr/, eur/ ... /adopted), and the WebFetch tool read EUR-Lex pages when curl could not.
- A difference-in-differences example needs a comparable control group: treating the least active stocks makes the market-wide shock hit the treated
  harder and breaks parallel trends (a good exercise, a bad main design).
- In firm.exchsim a band (control R) rejects orders priced outside it and stops matching at it: it is a barrier, so an LULD-style simulation finds the
  band repelling the price more than any anticipation attracts it. Report the decomposition (behaviour vs barrier), not a tuned magnet.
- firm.halts stamps engine trades with the time of the check that sees them (the engine keeps no trade times); a dynamic reference that averages
  them moves at the next check, which can move the band before a limit state is seen: LULD(update_pct) keeps the reference until it moves enough.
- A C++ test binary left in cpp/bin can go stale when the codec changes (the N control): mx_exchsim rebuilds it when older than the sources.
- firm.exchsim's FeedConfig impairments apply to both lines independently: a "lossy line A" scenario also loses on B.
- A market maker that computes its quotes from the top of book including its own quotes walks them: a long maker skewing down sets each new bid
  below its own previous one. Take the reference from the others' orders (exclude own references).
- Trade-sign autocorrelation on prints counts one aggressive order sweeping several resting orders as several same-sign trades (0.4-0.5 at lag 1
  with independent signs): collapse prints with the same time and sign to one order first.
- Mid-return kurtosis in a deep, sticky simulated book explodes (mostly zero returns, rare jumps): check the facts' scale on the target before
  choosing the sampling interval, and weight by the target's dispersion across days.
- `pkill -f name` inside a Bash command whose own command line contains `name` kills that shell: kill by PID.
- An execution algorithm on several venues must take its touch from the consolidated book: reading only the first venue's top, it froze
  (returned every second) once that venue's ask side was swept empty.
- Compare execution algorithms all-in: a passive algorithm's rebates and a taker's fees moved the chapter's A/B result from -0.47 bp to -0.15 bp.
  firm.tca's total includes fees; a shortfall from prices alone does not.
- A passive child resting while a catch-up cross is sent must be counted as cancelled at once (pending cancels), or the pre-trade "never beyond
  the order" control refuses the cross.
