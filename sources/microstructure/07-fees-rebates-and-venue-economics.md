# 7. Fees, Rebates and Venue Economics — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The same stock is bid at the same price on two exchanges. One pays the resting order a rebate and has twenty thousand shares queued ahead; the other charges the resting order a fee and has eight hundred. A trader who wants to be filled should pay to stand in the short line.
- **Sections.** Maker-taker, inverted and flat pricing; Fee-adjusted prices and the effective tick; Is the fee split neutral?; Tiers and the venue's business; Evidence from fee experiments.
- **Defines.** fee-adjusted price, effective tick size, fee neutrality, flat pricing.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** maker-taker pricing (B1.9), inverted venue (B1.9), access fee cap (B1.9), volume tier (B1.29), non-display fee (B1.29), market data feed (B1.4), payment for order flow (B1.10), exchange (B1.4), tick size (ch1), queue value (ch6), passive fill probability (B7.18), adverse selection (B1.1), mark-out (B2.15), difference-in-differences (ch6).
- **Tutorial.** Run two venues of firm.exchsim with the same flow and different fee schedules (maker-taker and inverted); let the background liquidity providers choose where to post by fee-adjusted price and queue length; measure queue lengths, fill rates, mark-outs of passive fills and the spread net of fees on each venue. Data: simulated; published fee schedules.
- **Build.** `firm.venuefees`: fee-adjusted prices, effective tick, the passive and aggressive venue choice under tiers (reusing Book 1's firm.feesched), and a fee-neutrality check of any two-venue result; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** The rebate that bought a queue -- named result: the fill rate and the mark-out of passive orders on the maker-taker and the inverted venue, and the rebate at which a liquidity provider is indifferent.
- **Facts to verify.** Colliard and Foucault 2012 trading fees and efficiency in limit order markets (RFS); Malinova and Park 2015 subsidizing liquidity: the impact of make/take fees on market quality (JF); Battalio, Corwin, Jennings 2016 can brokers have it all? (JF); SEC Transaction Fee Pilot 2018 and its vacatur by the DC Circuit 2020; SEC 2024 amendments to the access fee cap (dated); exchange fee schedules: a maker-taker and an inverted venue (dated); exchange revenue mix from listed exchange groups' annual reports (dated).

## EXCLUDED

- The hook's queue sizes (twenty thousand against eight hundred shares) are an illustration, not a fact about a market.
- Exchange revenue mix from listed exchange groups' annual reports: not needed once the chapter's own venue simulation was written; not quoted.
- Colliard and Foucault's neutrality argument: the chapter proves the neutrality proposition itself and cites the paper for the model of fees and fill rates.
- All simulated statistics (fill shares, mark-outs, queue lengths, values per posted share, volume shares) are computed on two firm.exchsim engines and tested.
