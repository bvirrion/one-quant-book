# 24. Market Quality and Regulation — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Twenty years of automation have narrowed spreads and multiplied messages, and regulators still disagree about whether markets are better for it. The evidence is abundant; what it measures, and what it cannot, is the subject of this chapter.
- **Sections.** Measuring liquidity and market quality; Algorithmic and high-frequency trading in the evidence; The flash-crash literature; Latency races and speed bumps in the evidence; Rules on algorithms and order-to-trade ratios.
- **Defines.** market quality, algorithmic trading, high-frequency trading, order-to-trade ratio, hot-potato trading.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** flash crash (B1.31), stub quote (B1.31), limit up--limit down (B1.31), effective spread (B1.10), realised spread (B1.10), Rule 605 report (B1.10), Amihud illiquidity (B7.7), Kyle's lambda (B7.9), VPIN (B7.9), market maker (B1.1), book resilience (ch3), quoted spread (ch5), difference-in-differences (ch6), frequent batch auction (ch10), latency arbitrage (B11.9, forward), speed bump (B11.9, forward), clustered standard errors (B4.16), spoofing (B9.29).
- **Tutorial.** Build a panel of market-quality measures on simulated stock-days with and without fast liquidity providers (firm.exchsim with the tape's background flow); plant a regulatory change on half the stocks and estimate it by difference-in-differences with clustered errors; show the wrong answer without a control group. Data: simulated; SEC MIDAS metrics.
- **Build.** `firm.mktquality`: liquidity and market-quality measures on a stock-day panel (quoted, effective and realised spreads, depth, price impact, Amihud, variance ratios, resilience, order-to-trade ratio), event studies and difference-in-differences with clustered errors; Python (statsmodels).
- **Weekend problem.** Did the rule make the market better? -- named result: the difference-in-differences estimate of a planted change in spreads with its standard error, against the before-after estimate that ignores the control group.
- **Facts to verify.** CFTC-SEC 2010 findings regarding the market events of May 6, 2010; Kirilenko, Kyle, Samadi, Tuzun 2017 the flash crash: HFT in an electronic market (JF); Hendershott, Jones, Menkveld 2011 does algorithmic trading improve liquidity? (JF); Brogaard, Hendershott, Riordan 2014 high-frequency trading and price discovery (RFS); Menkveld 2013 high frequency trading and the new market makers (JFM); Aquilina, Budish, O'Neill 2022 quantifying the high-frequency trading arms race (QJE); MiFID II article 4(1)(39)-(40) definitions of algorithmic and high-frequency algorithmic trading; RTS 6; RTS 9 order-to-trade ratio (dated); SEC 2010 concept release on equity market structure.
