# 8. Fragmentation and Routing — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** An order to buy 5,000 shares at the best offer finds 1,200 on the venue it was sent to. The other 3,800 at that price sit on four other venues, and by the time a second order reaches them some have gone.
- **Sections.** Many venues, one stock; Consolidated and direct views of the market; Protected quotes, locked and crossed markets; Latency between venues; Where is the price discovered?.
- **Defines.** market fragmentation, protected quote, locked market, crossed market, price discovery, information share, component share.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** national best bid and offer (B1.9), securities information processor (B1.9), direct feed (B1.9), order protection rule (B1.9), trade-through (B1.9), intermarket sweep order (B1.9), consolidated tape provider (B1.11), best execution (B1.11), alternative trading system (B1.4), multilateral trading facility (B1.4), cointegration (B4.20), vector error-correction model (B4.20), Hayashi--Yoshida estimator (B4.21), lead--lag relationship (B7.10), market-data latency (B7.18), latency arbitrage (B11.9, forward).
- **Tutorial.** Run three venues of firm.exchsim with inter-venue latencies; build the consolidated book from each venue's direct feed and from a delayed consolidated feed; measure how often the two views disagree, the frequency of locked and crossed markets, and the information share of each venue by a VECM with Hasbrouck bounds. Data: simulated; SEC MIDAS metrics.
- **Build.** `firm.consolidated`: consolidated book across venues with per-venue latency (reusing Book 1's firm.nbbo), locked and crossed detection, direct-against-consolidated comparison, information shares (Hasbrouck bounds) and component shares; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Where is the price found? -- named result: the information shares of three simulated venues, and the share of time the direct-feed and consolidated best prices differ, against the consolidated feed's delay.
- **Facts to verify.** O'Hara and Ye 2011 is market fragmentation harming market quality? (JFE); Degryse, de Jong, van Kervel 2015 the impact of dark trading and visible fragmentation on market quality (Review of Finance); Hasbrouck 1995 one security, many markets (JF); Gonzalo and Granger 1995 (JBES) component share; Bartlett and McCrary 2019 how rigged are stock markets? (JFM) or Ding, Hanna, Hendershott 2014 (RFS); SEC 2020 market data infrastructure rule and its status (dated); SEC 2026 proposal to rescind the order protection rule and its status (dated; see Book 1 ch. 9 trap); number of US equity exchanges and ATSs (dated).
