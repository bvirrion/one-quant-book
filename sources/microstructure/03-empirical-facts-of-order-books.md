# 3. Empirical Facts of Order Books — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Average the depth of a liquid stock's book at each distance from the best price and the curve is not highest at the best quote: it rises for a few ticks and then falls. The book's shape, its flows and the way its spread lives through the day repeat across markets with a regularity no single trader designs.
- **Sections.** Depth profiles; Arrival and cancellation rates by distance; Spreads and the spread-volatility relation; Intraday shapes; Order lifetimes, fleeting orders and resilience.
- **Defines.** fleeting order, order lifetime, relative limit price, book resilience, volatility per trade.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** limit order book (ch1), tick size (ch1), price level (ch1), zero-intelligence model (ch1), stylised fact (B7.5), intraday seasonality (B7.5), intraday volume profile (B7.5), depth profile (B7.8), cancellation rate (B7.8), queue depletion rate (B7.8), bid--ask spread (B1.1), heavy-tailed distribution (B4.15), tail index (B4.15).
- **Tutorial.** Measure on firm.tape, and on the public SEC MIDAS market-structure metrics, the depth profile, arrival and cancellation rates against the relative limit price, the spread distribution, order lifetimes, the intraday profiles of spread and volume, and the book's recovery after large trades; set each beside its published counterpart. Data: firm.tape (simulated); SEC MIDAS metrics by exchange (US government, public domain).
- **Build.** `firm.lobstats`: the stylised-fact toolkit for any market-by-order stream in the firm.tape format (depth profile, rates by distance, lifetimes and fleeting share, spread histogram and spread-volatility fit, intraday profiles, resilience after large trades), the yardstick chapter 27 calibrates the agent-based market against; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Is the simulator a market? -- named result: the table of stylised facts measured on the simulated market against their published values, and the facts it fails.
- **Facts to verify.** Bouchaud, Mezard, Potters 2002 statistical properties of stock order books (QF); Zovko and Farmer 2002 the power of patience: relative limit prices (QF); Hasbrouck and Saar 2009 technology and liquidity provision: fleeting orders (JFM); Wyart, Bouchaud, Kockelkoren, Potters, Vettorazzo 2008 relation between bid-ask spread and volatility (QF); Biais, Hillion, Spatt 1995 an empirical analysis of the limit order book (JF); Large 2007 measuring the resiliency of an electronic limit order book (JFM); SEC MIDAS market structure metrics: cancel-to-trade and hidden-volume statistics (dated).
