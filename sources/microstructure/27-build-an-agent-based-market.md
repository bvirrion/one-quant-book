# 27. Build: an Agent-Based Market — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** An exchange simulator with nobody on it is an empty room. Populated with a few kinds of simple traders -- some who know nothing, some who know the value, some who follow trends and some who make markets -- it starts to look like a market, and which of them it needs to look like one is itself a finding.
- **Sections.** Agents and their information; Liquidity providers and their inventory; Execution agents and metaorders; Calibrating to stylised facts; What agent-based markets can and cannot tell you.
- **Defines.** agent-based model, fundamentalist agent, chartist agent, market-making agent, method of simulated moments.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** exchange simulator (ch26), zero-intelligence model (ch1), informed trader (ch4), noise trader (ch4), stylised fact (B7.5), fleeting order (ch3), book resilience (ch3), metaorder (B7.9), square-root impact law (B7.27), Hawkes process (B4.7), calibration (B4.24), method of moments (B4.11), depth profile (B7.8), order-sign autocorrelation (B7.9), Glosten--Milgrom model (ch4), inventory (B2.30).
- **Tutorial.** Populate firm.exchsim with agents (zero-intelligence noise, informed on a hidden value, fundamentalists and chartists, inventory-skewing market makers, execution agents with metaorders); run a day; check the stylised facts with firm.lobstats; calibrate the population by the method of simulated moments; switch each agent type off in turn to see which fact it carries. Data: simulated.
- **Build.** `firm.agentmkt`: the agent library and population runner on firm.exchsim (deterministic, seeded per agent), with a calibration routine and output in the firm.tape format, so that Book 7's tools and the tutorials of Books 11 and 12 run on it; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** A market that looks like a market -- named result: the distance between simulated and target stylised facts after calibration, and the fact each agent type is responsible for (ablation).
- **Facts to verify.** Chiarella and Iori 2002 a simulation analysis of the microstructure of double auction markets (QF); Farmer, Patelli, Zovko 2005 the predictive power of zero intelligence in financial markets (PNAS); Lux and Marchesi 1999 scaling and criticality in a stochastic multi-agent model of a financial market (Nature); Franke and Westerhoff 2012 structural stochastic volatility in asset pricing dynamics: estimation and model contest (JEDC); Byrd, Hybinette, Balch 2020 ABIDES (ACM SIGSIM PADS); Vyetrenko et al. 2020 get real: realism metrics for robust limit order book market simulations (ICAIF).

## EXCLUDED

- Chiarella and Iori (2002): the abstract does not say which trading rules the agents combine; the chapter cites the paper only for the order-driven
  heterogeneous-agent design (a later paper by Chiarella, Iori and Perello mixes fundamentalist, chartist and noise components; not used).
- Real-market values of the stylised facts: the target is firm.tape (Book 7's synthetic market), measured by the chapter's code, so no external
  numbers are needed; the text says the method applies unchanged to a real tape.
- All numbers of the calibration, ablation, sign curves and market-maker study are computed by mx_agents and tested.
