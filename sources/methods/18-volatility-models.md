# 18. Volatility Models — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The risk report uses the equally weighted standard deviation of the last 250 daily returns. Exactly 250 trading days after a crash, the crash leaves the window and the reported risk falls off a cliff, on a day when nothing happened in the market.
- **Sections.** The conditional-heteroskedasticity family; Realised volatility; Heterogeneous autoregressive models; Forecasting and evaluation.
- **Defines.** volatility clustering, conditional heteroskedasticity, ARCH model, GARCH model, volatility persistence, EWMA volatility, leverage effect, GJR-GARCH model, integrated variance, realised variance, HAR model, QLIKE loss, Mincer--Zarnowitz regression, Diebold--Mariano test.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** realised volatility, variance, quasi-maximum likelihood, sandwich variance, autoregressive process, half-life, heavy-tailed distribution, quadratic variation, HAC estimator.
- **Results (named theorems, not terms).** stationarity condition alpha + beta < 1 and the unconditional variance of GARCH(1,1); half-life of a volatility shock ln(1/2) / ln(alpha + beta); EWMA as integrated GARCH; consistency of QMLE for GARCH under Gaussian likelihood; realised variance converges to integrated variance (no noise); MSE and QLIKE are robust to a noisy volatility proxy (Patton).
- **Tutorial.** Fit GARCH(1,1) with Student t innovations by quasi-maximum likelihood to daily EUR/USD returns; compute the persistence and half-life; compare one-day forecasts of GARCH, EWMA with decay 0.94 and a 250-day window under QLIKE; plot the ghost of the window.
- **Build.** `firm.volfcst`: volatility forecasting (GARCH and GJR-GARCH with Gaussian or t innovations and robust standard errors, EWMA, HAR on realised variance, QLIKE and MSE evaluation, Diebold-Mariano test); Python.
- **Weekend problem.** The ghost in the risk report — named result: the half-life of a volatility shock in EUR/USD under the fitted GARCH(1,1), and the size of the one-day drop in the 250-day volatility on the day a shock leaves the window.
- **Facts to verify.** ECB euro reference rate USD per EUR (dataset, licence); Engle 1982 (Econometrica) ARCH; Bollerslev 1986 (J. Econometrics) GARCH; Glosten, Jagannathan and Runkle 1993 (Journal of Finance); Nelson 1991 (Econometrica) EGARCH; J.P. Morgan/Reuters RiskMetrics Technical Document 1996, decay 0.94 for daily data; Black 1976 leverage effect; Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Labys 2001 (JASA) / 2003 (Econometrica); Barndorff-Nielsen and Shephard 2002 (JRSS B); Corsi 2009 (J. Financial Econometrics) HAR; Patton 2011 (J. Econometrics) robust loss functions; Diebold and Mariano 1995 (JBES); Mincer and Zarnowitz 1969.

## EXCLUDED

- Nelson 1991 EGARCH and Black 1976 (planned): EGARCH only named in the Build Stretch field; Black not cited.
- The 11 Nov 2022 EUR/USD move: restored in Phase C as timing only (first fixing after the 10 Nov US CPI release, 7.7%), new last F row; no causal attribution beyond the sequence of events.
- The realised-variance and HAR results use a simulated market (no intraday data licence in hand). Brief deviation: HAR is fitted to simulated RV, not to EUR/USD.
- QLIKE is used in the form proxy/h + ln h (Patton's QLIKE up to terms free of h), because 55 of the 7,098 ECB returns are exactly zero.
