# 27. Finite-Difference Methods — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The morning after a grid refinement, a new pricer's gamma near the strike shows a sawtooth: the prices have converged, their second derivative oscillates from node to node, and the hedger's limits flash red for no market reason.
- **Sections.** Discretising a parabolic equation; Explicit, implicit and theta schemes; Boundaries and non-uniform grids; Stability and oscillations; Alternating directions.
- **Defines.** finite-difference method, method of lines, truncation error, consistent scheme, stable scheme, von Neumann stability analysis, explicit scheme, implicit scheme, theta scheme, Crank--Nicolson scheme, CFL condition, Rannacher time-stepping, upwind scheme, M-matrix, non-uniform grid, Richardson extrapolation, alternating direction implicit method.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** Feynman--Kac formula (result), Kolmogorov backward equation, infinitesimal generator, tridiagonal matrix algorithm, gamma, barrier option, call option, variational inequality, condition number.
- **Results (named theorems, not terms).** Lax equivalence theorem (stated); von Neumann amplification factors of the theta schemes; explicit stability dt <= dx^2 / sigma^2; Crank-Nicolson is A-stable but not L-stable: the oscillation mechanism; an M-matrix scheme is monotone and positivity-preserving; Rannacher start-up restores second-order convergence for non-smooth data (stated with source); Douglas ADI stability for mixed derivatives (stated).
- **Tutorial.** Solve the backward equation of a geometric Brownian motion for a kinked and a digital payoff with explicit, implicit and Crank-Nicolson schemes; show the explicit blow-up past the stability limit, the Crank-Nicolson sawtooth in the second difference, and its removal by a few implicit start-up steps; tabulate convergence orders.
- **Build.** `firm.pde`: one-dimensional theta-scheme solver (non-uniform grids, general linear boundary conditions, Rannacher start-up, Richardson extrapolation) and a two-dimensional Douglas ADI solver; Python, with the one-dimensional kernel also in C++20 and Rust.
- **Weekend problem.** The sawtooth gamma — named result: the number of implicit start-up steps that removes the oscillation, and the convergence order it restores, measured by the error ratio per grid halving.
- **Facts to verify.** Crank and Nicolson 1947 (Proc. Cambridge Phil. Society); Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy 1928 (Math. Annalen); Lax and Richtmyer 1956 (Comm. Pure Appl. Math.); Rannacher 1984 (Numerische Mathematik); Peaceman and Rachford 1955; Douglas and Rachford 1956; Craig and Sneyd 1988; in 't Hout and Foulon 2010 (IJNAM) ADI for Heston; Pooley, Vetzal and Forsyth 2003 (J. Computational Finance) convergence remedies; Giles and Carter 2006 (J. Computational Finance) Crank-Nicolson convergence; Richardson 1911.

## EXCLUDED

- Stability theorem for the Douglas scheme with mixed derivatives: the brief asked for it "stated"; the precise conditions on theta could not be checked in the primary texts (paywalled), so the chapter states only that the conditions were analysed (Craig-Sneyd 1988; in 't Hout-Welfert 2007) and cites in 't Hout-Foulon 2010 for the comparison, without quoting a condition.
- Margrabe's exchange-option formula is used as a known closed form (Book 5 defines exchange options); not a ledger fact, as it is derived in the tests from the lognormal ratio.

## Brief deviations

- The 2-D ADI demonstration uses the exchange option (closed form available) rather than Heston; its error is first order (kink along the diagonal), reported as measured.
- Rannacher start-up needs four half steps (two replaced CN steps) to restore second order for gamma; two half steps give first order. The brief's named result is this measured count.
