# 17. Regulators and Licences — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Before 2018 a firm trading only its own money on European exchanges could, in most cases, do so without a licence. Since the second Markets in Financial Instruments Directive, one that uses a high-frequency algorithmic trading technique needs one, however small it is and whoever its clients are: it has none.
- **Sections.** Why a trading firm needs permission; The United States: the SEC, the CFTC and the self-regulatory organisations; Europe and the United Kingdom; Asia-Pacific; Rules for algorithmic trading.
- **Defines.** regulatory perimeter, authorisation, dealing on own account, investment firm, passporting, self-regulatory organisation, swap dealer, commodity pool operator, introducing broker.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** broker-dealer (B1.2), exchange member (B1.4), futures commission merchant (B1.30), clearing member (B1.30), commodity trading advisor (B1.3), high-frequency trading (B10.24), algorithmic trading (B10.24), market access rule (B11.27), crypto-asset service provider (B3.24), Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (B3.24), ancillary activity exemption (B3.13), legal entity identifier (B2.28), own funds (ch2), K-factor requirement (ch2), net capital rule (ch2), market abuse (ch16).
- **Tutorial.** The regulatory map as data: activities (own-account dealing on exchanges, market making, managing outside money, swap dealing, crypto) by jurisdiction, mapped to regimes, registration categories and key references, each row dated and sourced; query it for three firm profiles. End state: a table per profile of the regimes its activities touch, each with the reference to the rule -- information about where the rules are, not advice.
- **Build.** No build in the running-project sense: the subject is law. Analytical tool instead: `firm.regmap`, the map as data with a schema check (every row has a reference and an as-of date), a query by activity set and jurisdiction, a stale-row report, and the algorithmic-trading obligations as a checklist with references; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Three offices -- named result: the regimes a firm's activities touch as it adds a second and a third jurisdiction, and the longest statutory decision period on its path to trading.
- **Facts to verify.** Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II) arts 2(1)(d), 4(1)(39)-(40), 17 (dated); Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589 (RTS 6) (pointer to Book 11 ch. 27); UK FSMA 2000 s.19 and s.55V determination periods (dated); US Exchange Act ss.15(a), 15(b)(8); SEC Rule 15b9-1 as amended (2023) and its status (dated); CFTC and NFA registration categories (FCM, IB, CPO, CTA, SD) (dated); Investment Advisers Act s.203 (dated); MAS capital markets services licence; SFC regulated activities; Japan FIEA registration (dated); MiCA authorisation (pointer to Book 3 ch. 24).
- **Data.** data/desk/regmap.csv (dated rows with references).
