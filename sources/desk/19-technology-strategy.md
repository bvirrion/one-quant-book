# 19. Technology Strategy — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A listed electronic market maker's filings show a communication and data bill that has barely moved in a decade of rising and falling revenue: the firm does not choose each year whether to be fast. Which latency tier a firm pays for is a decision about which businesses it will be in, and it is taken once and paid every year.
- **Sections.** Architecture as strategy; What speed is worth, strategy by strategy; Latency tiers and the arms race; Platform teams and product teams; The technology budget.
- **Defines.** latency tier, latency arms race, platform team, product team, run-the-bank spending, change-the-bank spending.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** latency (B13.1), tick-to-trade latency (B13.1), latency budget (B13.1), latency arbitrage (B11.9), latency race (B11.9), stale-quote sniping (B11.9), revenue capture (B11.1), colocation (B14.9), connectivity plan (B14.29), fixed cost base (ch1), technology treadmill (ch2).
- **Tutorial.** A firm with five strategies, each earning revenue that depends on its latency rank against competitors (a race-win model with parameters from cited studies), chooses one of three latency tiers for each, with the tiers' annual costs as inputs; find the profit-maximising tiers, then let competitors upgrade and find the arms-race equilibrium in which everyone pays and capture falls. End state: a chart of profit by tier for each strategy, and equilibrium spending against the spending if no one upgraded.
- **Build.** `firm.techtier`: strategies' latency sensitivity (race share against rank), tier costs as data (reads Book 14's connectivity-plan budget where present), the tier choice per strategy, the n-firm arms-race game solved by best response, and a run/change budget split; Python; reads firm.latbudget's budgets.
- **Weekend problem.** The fastest tier -- named result: the profit-maximising tier for each of the five strategies, and the share of the industry's speed spending that the arms-race equilibrium wastes against no one upgrading.
- **Facts to verify.** Budish, Cramton and Shim 2015 (QJE), The high-frequency trading arms race; Aquilina, Budish and O'Neill 2022 (QJE) (pointer to Book 11 ch. 9 and Book 13 ch. 1); Baron, Brogaard, Hagstromer and Kirilenko 2019 (JFQA) (pointer to Book 11 ch. 1); Virtu Financial Forms 10-K: communication and data processing over ten years (dated); Flow Traders annual reports: technology expenses (dated); a bank's published technology budget and its run/change split (annual report or shareholder letter) (dated); Skelton and Pais 2019, Team Topologies (platform teams).
- **Data.** data/desk/tech_spend.csv (derived from filings); synthetic strategies.
