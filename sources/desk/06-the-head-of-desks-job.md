# 6. The Head of Desk's Job — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The budget meeting in December sets next year's revenue target at this year's plus ten per cent and leaves the desk's risk limits where they were. With the desk's Sharpe ratio, those limits meet that target in fewer than half the years: the head of desk has been handed a plan that fails more often than it succeeds, before a single trade.
- **Sections.** The budget: revenue, costs and the plan; Risk appetite: from a loss the firm can bear to limits; Product scope and new-product approval; The rhythm: the day, the week, the year.
- **Defines.** revenue budget, allocated cost, risk appetite, risk appetite statement, product scope, new-product approval.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** risk limit (B6.29), risk hierarchy (B6.29), value at risk (B6.21), expected shortfall (B6.21), stress test (B6.22), Sharpe ratio (B4.11), maximum drawdown (B7.22), P\&L attribution (B1.7), daily P\&L attribution (B8.1), loss limit (B11.27), franchise (B1.2), return on equity (ch1), allocated equity (ch5).
- **Tutorial.** The desk's plan as data: three businesses with expected revenue, volatility and correlation, and a cost budget including allocations. Simulate the year to get the probability of meeting the target, the distribution of the worst drawdown, and the risk the desk must run to meet the target with a chosen probability. End state: a fan chart of cumulative P&L against the target line, and a table of required risk by target probability.
- **Build.** `firm.deskplan`: the annual plan (revenue by business with volatility and correlation, costs and allocations, capital usage), the Monte Carlo of the year, the risk-appetite cascade (loss tolerance to an annual volatility budget to VaR and stop limits by business), and a monthly plan-against-actual report; Python; uses firm.perf.
- **Weekend problem.** Plus ten per cent -- named result: the probability that a desk of Sharpe ratio S meets a target set at a multiple of its expected revenue (closed form, checked by simulation), and the extra risk needed to meet it three years in four.
- **Facts to verify.** FSB Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013); Senior Supervisors Group, Risk management lessons from the global banking crisis of 2008 (2009); OCC Bulletin 2017-43 on new, modified or expanded products (dated); a bank's published description of its risk appetite framework (annual report or Pillar 3) (dated).
- **Data.** Synthetic.
