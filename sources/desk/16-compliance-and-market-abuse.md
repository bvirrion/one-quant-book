# 16. Compliance and Market Abuse — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The UK regulator fined a bank for years in which its trade surveillance did not cover whole classes of its trading: the alerts that would have found abuse were never generated, so nobody could have read them. Compliance is a function that must see the trading before it can judge it.
- **Sections.** The rulebook a trader lives under; Surveillance as a function; Personal dealing; Information barriers, insider lists and wall-crossing.
- **Defines.** market abuse, inside information, insider dealing, suspicious transaction and order report, personal account dealing, pre-clearance, restricted list, watch list, information barrier, wall-crossing, insider list.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** spoofing (B9.29), layering (B9.29), marking the close (B9.29), momentum ignition (B9.29), benchmark manipulation (B9.29), wash trading (B3.16), three lines of defence (B6.26), segregation of duties (B6.28), trade surveillance system (B15.30), surveillance alert (B15.30), alert triage (B15.30), communications archive (B15.30), front office (ch13).
- **Tutorial.** A year of employees' personal trade requests and the firm's trades, with a restricted list and a watch list that change through the year: run pre-clearance; then send a surveillance alert stream (firm.surveil) through a triage queue with fixed analyst capacity and measure the share of true cases found by alert threshold. End state: pre-clearance decisions by reason, and a chart of true cases found against analyst hours by threshold.
- **Build.** `firm.compliance`: restricted and watch lists through time, a pre-clearance engine for personal trades (lists, holding periods, blackout windows), an insider-list register with wall-crossing records, and an alert-triage capacity model on firm.surveil's alerts; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** The alert queue -- named result: the alert threshold that maximises true cases found for a fixed number of analyst hours, and the share missed at the default threshold.
- **Facts to verify.** Regulation (EU) No 596/2014 (MAR): arts 7, 8, 11, 12, 14-16, 18 (dated); UK MAR as onshored and the FCA's Market Watch on STORs (dated); US Exchange Act s.10(b), Rule 10b-5, s.15(g); Advisers Act Rule 204A-1 (dated); Commodity Exchange Act s.4c(a)(5)(C) (spoofing) (pointer to Book 9 ch. 29); an FCA final notice on trade-surveillance failures at a bank or broker (e.g. Citigroup Global Markets, 2022) (dated); an SEC or FCA enforcement action on personal account dealing (dated).
- **Data.** Synthetic; alerts from firm.surveil.
