# 1. The Economics of a Trading Firm — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In a year of high volatility a listed electronic market maker's revenue roughly doubled while its pay and technology bills barely moved; in the quiet year before, the same cost base had left it little. A listed quantitative asset manager, paid on assets and performance, lived a different cycle entirely. Reading the two sets of filings side by side is reading two cost structures.
- **Sections.** The income statement of a trading firm; Costs that scale and costs that do not; Pay: the largest line, and how much of it is variable; Capital, financing and return on equity; The cycle: volatility, volume and revenue.
- **Defines.** net trading revenue, volume-driven cost, fixed cost base, compensation ratio, operating margin, return on equity, non-GAAP measure.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** proprietary trading firm (B1.1), asset manager (B1.1), assets under management (B1.1), management fee (B1.3), performance fee (B1.3), revenue capture (B11.1), electronic market maker (B11.1), net capture (B1.1), payment for order flow (B1.10), leverage (B1.6), risk-weighted assets (B1.2), mark-to-market (B1.7).
- **Tutorial.** Load committed derived tables of five years of filings for three listed firms of different types (an electronic market maker's annual reports on Form 10-K, an exchange-traded-product market maker's annual reports, a listed quantitative asset manager's annual reports) and one bank's markets segment; map each filer's lines to a standard chart of accounts; compute compensation ratio, operating margin, fixed-cost cover and revenue per head; regress each firm's annual revenue on the year's average of a volatility index (Cboe history as derived statistics, as in Book 9). End state: a stacked cost-structure chart by firm type, and a table of revenue sensitivities to volatility and operating leverages.
- **Build.** `firm.firmecon`: a standard chart of accounts for a trading firm (revenue lines; volume-driven, fixed and compensation costs, pay split into fixed and variable; financing; tax), mapping tables from each filer's reported lines, the ratios, a cycle scenario that moves revenue with volatility and variable pay with the pool, and break-even revenue; Python; wraps firm.mmecon's break-even and operating leverage.
- **Weekend problem.** Three firms, one bad year -- named result: the fall in revenue that takes each firm's operating profit to zero, with variable pay flexing and with pay held fixed, and each firm's operating leverage.
- **Facts to verify.** Virtu Financial Forms 10-K 2021-2025: revenue lines, direct costs, compensation, communication and data, headcount, adjusted net trading income (non-GAAP); Flow Traders annual reports 2021-2025: net trading income, fixed and variable employee expenses, technology, FTEs; Man Group annual reports 2021-2025: net revenue, management and performance fees, compensation ratio, AUM, headcount; a bank's markets segment: revenue, expenses, allocated equity and return (JPMorgan Chase or Goldman Sachs Form 10-K) (dated); Cboe VIX index daily history, annual averages as derived statistics; SEC Regulation G and Item 10(e) of Regulation S-K on non-GAAP measures; IFRS 8 operating segments.
- **Data.** data/desk/filings_*.csv (derived figures with filing and page), data/desk/vix_annual.csv; LICENSES.md.
