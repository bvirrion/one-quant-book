# 2. The Proprietary Market-Making Firm — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The filed accounts of a London trading firm with a few hundred staff show a profit per employee that most banks' markets divisions do not reach, a large share of it paid out to its owners, and a capital base that has grown almost entirely out of retained profit. What the owners keep, what they leave in the firm, and why the technology bill has to keep rising, decide whether the firm is still there in ten years.
- **Sections.** Ownership: partnerships, private companies and listed firms; Capital: what it is for and how much is required; Reinvestment and payout; The technology treadmill.
- **Defines.** limited liability partnership, members' capital, retention ratio, own funds, fixed overheads requirement, K-factor requirement, net capital rule, technology treadmill.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** proprietary trading firm (B1.1), market maker (B1.1), revenue capture (B11.1), capture decay (B11.28), latency arbitrage (B11.9), clearing member (B1.30), initial margin (B1.5), broker-dealer (B1.2), fixed cost base (ch1), compensation ratio (ch1), return on equity (ch1).
- **Tutorial.** Simulate a trading partnership over ten years: capture decays at a rate set by competitors' speed unless the firm's technology spending keeps pace; profit is split between payout and retention; retained capital sets the risk the firm may run under an activity-based capital requirement, and so its volume. Compare retention ratios of 20, 50 and 80 per cent. End state: paths of capital, capture and payout, and the retention ratio that maximises the partners' discounted payout.
- **Build.** `firm.partnership`: members' capital accounts (contributions, profit allocation by points, drawings, retention, repayment on departure), a capital-requirement function (the higher of a permanent minimum, a quarter of fixed overheads and activity-based charges; parameters labelled illustrative), the treadmill (capture as a function of relative technology spending) and the ten-year simulation; Python; uses firm.firmecon.
- **Weekend problem.** Keep it or pay it out -- named result: the steady-state technology spending that holds capture constant, and the retention ratio that maximises the partners' discounted payout over ten years.
- **Facts to verify.** Regulation (EU) 2019/2033 (IFR) and Directive (EU) 2019/2034: own funds requirement as the highest of permanent minimum, fixed overheads requirement and K-factors (K-DTF, K-NPR, K-CMG, K-TCD) (dated); UK MIFIDPRU equivalents (FCA Handbook) (dated); SEC Rule 15c3-1 net capital (dated); UK Limited Liability Partnerships Act 2000; SORP on members' remuneration; filed accounts of one or two London trading firms at Companies House (revenue, profit, staff, dividends or members' profit, capital) (dated); a listed market maker's technology and communication costs over ten years (Virtu or Flow Traders filings); Budish, Cramton and Shim 2015 (QJE): the arms race.
- **Data.** data/desk/filings_*.csv (derived); synthetic simulation.
