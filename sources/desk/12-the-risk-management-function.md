# 12. The Risk-Management Function — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** The court-appointed examiner's report on a failed investment bank describes a firm that raised its own risk limits as its positions grew, left large positions out of its stress tests and sidelined the people whose job was to object. The failure was not of measurement: the numbers were there. It was of a function that could not make them count.
- **Sections.** Independence: where the risk function reports; Committees and the risk officer's tools; Escalation: breaches, excesses and exceptions; What the risk officer looks for.
- **Defines.** chief risk officer, risk committee, escalation procedure, risk exception, four-eyes principle, risk culture.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** three lines of defence (B6.26), effective challenge (B6.26), model risk (B6.26), model validation (B6.26), operational risk (B6.28), segregation of duties (B6.28), value at risk (B6.21), stress test (B6.22), risk appetite statement (ch6), limit framework (ch7), limit breach (ch7), hard limit (ch7), soft limit (ch7), limit utilisation (ch7).
- **Tutorial.** A year of daily limit utilisation for twenty desks (synthetic), with breaches, temporary increases and model changes planted: run the escalation rules, measure time to resolution and how each breach was resolved (position cut, limit increase, model change), and flag the pattern of the public record -- limits raised or models changed while a breach is open. End state: a table of breaches by resolution type, and a chart of one desk's utilisation around a model change.
- **Build.** `firm.escalation`: the breach register and escalation workflow as data (severity by size and duration, who is told within what time, approval authority for increases, expiry), resolution tracking, audit metrics, and red-flag rules (a limit raised or a model changed during an open breach; repeated temporary increases); Python; reads firm.limitalloc's framework.
- **Weekend problem.** Raised to fit -- named result: in the simulated year, the share of breaches resolved by raising the limit and the median time to resolution, and how both move when increases need the risk committee's approval.
- **Facts to verify.** Report of Anton R. Valukas, Examiner, In re Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc. (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2010): risk appetite limits, stress-test exclusions, role of risk management; JPMorgan Chase Management Task Force report (16 January 2013) and US Senate PSI report (15 March 2013): limit breaches and the VaR model change (pointer to Book 6 ch. 26); BCBS, Corporate governance principles for banks (2015): the chief risk officer; Federal Reserve Regulation YY, 12 CFR 252.33: risk committee and chief risk officer of large bank holding companies (dated); Senior Supervisors Group, Observations on risk management practices during the recent market turbulence (2008).
- **Data.** Synthetic; public-record figures in the ledger.
