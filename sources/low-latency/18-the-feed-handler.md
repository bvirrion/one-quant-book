# 18. The Feed Handler — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** At the open the message rate jumped a hundredfold; the handler fell behind, the socket buffer overflowed, line A lost a packet that line B lost too, and the firm's book was stale for tens of milliseconds while it recovered from a snapshot.
- **Sections.** Decode and arbitrate; Detect gaps and recover; Normalise and publish; Bursts, buffers and slow consumers; Monitoring a feed.
- **Defines.** feed handler, message burst, stale-book flag, receive buffer overrun, recovery latency.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** market data feed (B1.4), sequence number (B1.28), normalisation (B1.28), market-by-order (B1.19), market-by-price (B1.19), level 1 (B1.28), level 2 (B1.28), level 3 (B1.28), exchange simulator (B10.26), matching engine (B10.1), line arbitration (ch16), message gap (ch16), snapshot channel (ch16), retransmission request (B10.26), ring buffer (ch12), slow consumer (ch12), M/M/1 queue (B4.8).
- **Tutorial.** Run the C++ handler on the simulator's recorded A and B lines with injected loss, reordering and a burst; watch arbitration, gap detection, the retransmission request, snapshot recovery and the stale-book flag; compare the normalised output with the clean run's. End state: a timeline of staleness intervals and the per-packet handling-time histogram, measured on this laptop.
- **Build.** `firm.feedhandler`: A/B arbitration by packet sequence, gap detection with a timeout, recovery by retransmission then snapshot, normalisation to the firm's event record, publication on firm.ring, counters for monitoring; C++20 and Rust with a Python reference; acceptance: identical normalised output on clean and impaired lines once recovered, in all three languages.
- **Weekend problem.** The opening burst -- named result: the receive-buffer size a handler with a given service time needs to absorb a burst of a given size and rate without loss, and the staleness a snapshot interval implies when it does lose.
- **Data.** Book 10 simulator's recorded lines with seeded impairments.
- **Facts to verify.** socket(7) and the net.core.rmem_max / SO_RCVBUF behaviour (the kernel doubles the value); a published peak message rate of a major feed (for example OPRA capacity figures, dated); CME MDP 3.0 market-recovery procedure (dated).
