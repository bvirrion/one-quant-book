# 22. Pre-Trade Risk and Kill Switches — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** On 1 August 2012 a broker-dealer's order router sent millions of unintended orders in forty-five minutes (One Quant Book 7, chapter 21, tells the story). Read as a requirements document, the regulator's order lists the checks, limits and kill switch this chapter implements.
- **Sections.** The requirements document: the 2012 incident and the rules it produced; Checks in nanoseconds; Limits in a hierarchy, updated live; Kill switches: block, cancel, flatten; Where the checks sit: gateway, separate process, broker, exchange.
- **Defines.** risk gate, worst-case exposure, mass cancel, fail-closed design.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** kill switch (B11.27), pre-trade risk check (B11.27), price collar (B11.27), risk limit (B6.29), risk hierarchy (B6.29), sponsored access (B1.4), canary deployment (B7.21), in-flight order (ch21), order gateway (ch21).
- **Tutorial.** Implement the checks in C++ with precomputed per-instrument limits in a dense table and branch-light code; measure the cost per check; replay a runaway child-order generator of the 2012 shape against the simulator with and without each check. End state: the position and order count reached before each check trips, and the per-check cost, measured on this laptop.
- **Build.** `firm.riskgate`: price collar, order size and notional, position limits including in-flight orders, order and message rates, duplicate detection, a limit hierarchy updated atomically between events, kill switch (block, mass cancel, optional flatten), audit records; C++20 and Rust with a Python reference; acceptance: every check at its boundaries, the runaway stopped within a stated number of orders, zero allocations, fail closed on stale limits.
- **Weekend problem.** Forty-five minutes -- named result: orders sent and position reached before each check trips for a runaway generator at the 2012 incident's rates, against the incident's actual totals.
- **Data.** Book 10 simulator; runaway rates from the SEC order.
- **Facts to verify.** SEC Release 34-70694 (2013), In the Matter of Knight Capital Americas LLC: orders, executions, shares, positions, loss; SEC Rule 15c3-5 (17 CFR 240.15c3-5), market access controls; Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589 (RTS 6): pre-trade controls and kill functionality (articles 12 and 15); FIA best practices for automated trading risk controls (dated); exchange-provided kill switches (dated).
