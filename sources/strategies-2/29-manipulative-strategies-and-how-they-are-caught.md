# 29. Manipulative Strategies and How They Are Caught — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A trader placed large orders he never meant to fill, cancelled them within seconds, and traded the other side of the price move they caused; the pattern was clear enough in the order log to convict him.
- **Sections.** Spoofing and layering; Momentum ignition and marking the close; Wash trades and benchmark manipulation; Sandwiching and its legal status; Surveillance: detectors and their errors.
- **Defines.** spoofing, layering, momentum ignition, marking the close, benchmark manipulation.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** wash trading (B3.16), sandwich attack (B3.22), front-running (B3.22), cancellation rate (B7.8), best execution (B1.11).
- **Strategy files.** spoofing; layering; momentum ignition; wash trades; marking the close; benchmark manipulation; sandwiching; detector: cancellation-pattern surveillance.
- **Tutorial.** On firm.tape order logs, plant spoofing, layering and marking-the-close episodes among legitimate market-making, build detectors from public enforcement descriptions, and measure their true and false positive rates.
- **Build.** `firm.surveil`: surveillance detectors (order-to-trade and cancellation patterns around executions, close-window price impact, self-matching) with labelled synthetic episodes and ROC evaluation; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** In the order log — named result: each detector's true-positive rate at a fixed false-positive rate, and how legitimate market making triggers them.
- **Facts to verify.** US v. Sarao (CFTC/DOJ 2015) and Coscia (7th Cir. 2017) spoofing cases (dated); Dodd-Frank Act section 747 anti-spoofing provision (dated); EU Market Abuse Regulation Annex I indicators (dated); FCA and CFTC benchmark (LIBOR/FX) enforcement (dated); SEC marking-the-close cases (dated).
