# 1. The Business of Market Making — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A listed electronic market maker reports its trading income per day to the cent, and the same filing shows what the machines, the data and the exchange memberships that earn it cost; divided by the volume it trades, the revenue per share is a fraction of a cent.
- **Sections.** What a market maker sells; Revenue: capture per unit traded, volume, volatility; Costs: inventory, adverse selection, fees, technology, people; What the public statements reveal; The shape of the business: fixed costs and scale.
- **Defines.** high-frequency trading, electronic market maker, revenue capture.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** market maker (B1.1), bid--ask spread (B1.1), adverse selection (B1.1), mid price (B1.1), inventory (B2.30), proprietary trading firm (B1.1), maker-taker pricing (B1.9), designated market maker (B1.29), net capture (B1.1), P\&L attribution (B1.7), mark-out curve (B7.23), spread capture (B7.23), adverse-selection cost (B7.23), inventory P\&L (B7.23), fill rate (B7.23).
- **Tutorial.** Build firm.mmecon and read two public market makers' annual figures (trading income per day, volume, cost lines) into a unit-economics table; then run a first market maker through firm.mmharness on firm_tape and decompose its day into capture, adverse selection, fees and inventory.
- **Build.** `firm.mmharness` (the strategy harness: a `Quoter` protocol with on_market / on_fill / on_timer, run on firm_tape's agent API now and on Book 10's exchange simulator later, with a common fill log) and `firm.mmecon` (unit economics: capture per share, break-even volume, fixed-cost leverage); Python.
- **Weekend problem.** A fraction of a cent — named result: the break-even daily volume of a small market maker at a given capture and cost base, and its operating leverage.
- **Facts to verify.** Virtu Financial 10-K FY2025: trading income, adjusted net trading income per day, segment data, costs (dated); Flow Traders annual report 2025: net trading income, value traded (dated); Menkveld 2013 High frequency trading and the new market makers (JFM); Baron, Brogaard, Hagstromer, Kirilenko 2019 Risk and return in high-frequency trading (JFQA); Brogaard, Hendershott, Riordan 2014 High-frequency trading and price discovery (RFS).
