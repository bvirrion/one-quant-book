# 5. Phone and Technical Screens — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Twenty minutes into a video screen the candidate announces an expected value of 3.5 for a game whose payoff is never above three. The interviewer says nothing. The candidate who says 'that cannot be right: the payoff is capped at three' and finds the error in a minute has had a better screen than one who never made it, and the notes will say so.
- **Sections.** What a screen is for: a pass-or-fail filter with a short rubric; Thinking aloud: structure, pace and silence; Sanity checks: limits, units, symmetry and small cases; Recovering from an error; asking for a hint; the last five minutes.
- **Defines.** technical screen, think-aloud protocol, sanity check.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** interview rubric (ch1), interview loop (ch1), technical interview (ch1), online assessment (ch4).
- **Question bank.** 8 questions, 3/3/2. Families: questions with a planted trap where the sanity check is the point (an expectation exceeding a bound, a probability above one, a variance that should shrink); a short derivation to be done aloud (the transcript of a good and a bad answer in the solution); the small-case test of a conjecture; a coding screen question with an off-by-one to find (Python, tested); what to say when stuck. Roles: trader 3, researcher 3, developer 3, bank 1. Firms: any 6, market maker 2, systematic fund 1, bank 1.
- **Facts to verify.** Ericsson and Simon, Protocol Analysis: Verbal Reports as Data (1993): the think-aloud method; research on interviewer evaluation of recovery or error handling, if a citable study exists (otherwise EXCLUDED and stated as the book's method).
- **Data.** Figures: none planned. Code: iv_screen.py (each trap question's correct value and the planted wrong one, and the off-by-one coding answer against an oracle); exact tests.
