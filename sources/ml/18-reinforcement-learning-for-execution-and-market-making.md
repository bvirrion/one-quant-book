# 18. Reinforcement Learning for Execution and Market Making — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Execution and market making both have closed-form optimal policies in their textbook models; the reason to learn a policy instead is everything the textbook model leaves out, and the risk is that a learned policy exploits what the simulator gets wrong.
- **Sections.** State, action and reward design; Deep Q-networks and their stabilisers; Learning to execute; Learning to make markets; What works in the published evidence.
- **Defines.** deep Q-network, experience replay, target network, reward shaping, action masking, domain randomisation.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** Almgren--Chriss model (B10.14), child order (B10.14), exchange simulator (B10.26), agent-based model (B10.27), Avellaneda--Stoikov model (B11.3), reservation price (B11.3), implementation shortfall (B7.19), arrival price (B7.19), queue position (B7.18), mark-out curve (B7.23), inventory P\&L (B7.23), sim-to-real gap (ch17), off-policy evaluation (ch17), Markov decision process (ch17), policy (ch17), reward (ch17), action-value function (ch17), Q-learning (ch17), value function (B4.9), dynamic programming (B4.9), multilayer perceptron (ch7), Adam (ch7), early stopping (ch5), stochastic gradient descent (B4.24), reverse mode (B4.28).
- **Tutorial.** Train a DQN to liquidate in an Almgren-Chriss environment and compare it with the closed-form schedule and TWAP; add a passive-order action whose fills come from queue position in firm.tape (then Book 10's simulator) and see what the agent learns; train tabular and deep Q-learning market makers in an Avellaneda-Stoikov environment and compare with the closed form; retrain with domain randomisation and evaluate every policy in a world with different parameters. Data: synthetic.
- **Build.** `firm.rltrade`: environments with the rlcore protocol (Almgren-Chriss liquidation, Avellaneda-Stoikov market making, and a wrapper over Book 10's simulator agent API), a small DQN with experience replay and a target network, domain randomisation, and evaluation against the closed forms on common random numbers; Python on PyTorch.
- **Weekend problem.** Beating a closed form -- named result: the learned policy's shortfall against the Almgren-Chriss optimum in the model world and in a mis-specified world, with and without domain randomisation.
- **Facts to verify.** Nevmyvaka, Feng and Kearns 2006 reinforcement learning for optimized trade execution (ICML); Ning, Lin and Jaimungal 2021 double deep Q-learning for optimal execution (Applied Mathematical Finance); Spooner, Fearnley, Savani and Koukorinis 2018 market making via reinforcement learning (AAMAS); Hambly, Xu and Yang 2023 recent advances in reinforcement learning in finance (Mathematical Finance); Kolm and Ritter 2020 modern perspectives on reinforcement learning in finance (J. Machine Learning in Finance) or equivalent; Almgren and Chriss 2001 (J. Risk); Avellaneda and Stoikov 2008 (Quantitative Finance); van Hasselt, Guez and Silver 2016 double Q-learning (AAAI); Tobin et al. 2017 domain randomization (IROS).
