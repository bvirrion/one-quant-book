# 14. Large Language Models in Finance — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A language model asked in 2026 whether a 2022 earnings headline was good news for the stock answers correctly nine times in ten; asked about headlines published after its training data ended, it does little better than a coin. The first number measures its memory, not its judgement.
- **Sections.** Language models and how they are trained; Filings, calls and news at scale: retrieval; Evaluation without leakage from the training cut-off; Agents in research workflows; What a desk can and cannot delegate.
- **Defines.** language model, large language model, prompt, context window, retrieval-augmented generation, BM25, training cut-off, memorisation, anonymisation test, hallucination, language-model agent, tool call.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** look-ahead bias (B7.3), point-in-time data (B7.3), material non-public information (B7.12), research log (B7.1), holdout set (B7.1), transformer (ch8), attention mechanism (ch8), tokenisation (ch13), word embedding (ch13), entity linking (ch13), pre-training (ch10), fine-tuning (ch10), target leakage (ch3), prediction target (B7.6), forecast horizon (B7.6), information coefficient (B7.6), in-sample (B7.20), out-of-sample (B7.20), cross-validation (B4.16).
- **Tutorial.** Train a tiny word-level transformer in seconds on the Chapter 13 corpus; build BM25 and embedding retrieval over synthetic filings and answer questions by extraction; show look-ahead contamination by training one model on documents up to a cut-off and another on all of them, and measure the memorisation premium and the anonymisation test; run a deterministic agent loop whose tools (retrieval, calculator, firm.vecbt) sit behind a firm.pit guard that refuses any data after the as-of date. No network access, no downloaded weights. Data: synthetic corpus.
- **Build.** `firm.llmeval`: BM25 and dense retrieval, a tiny local language model for tests, the point-in-time evaluation protocol (cut-off split, anonymisation and entity-swap tests, memorisation premium), and an agent harness with a tool registry, a point-in-time sandbox and a full audit log; Python on PyTorch.
- **Weekend problem.** The model that knew -- named result: the memorisation premium (accuracy before the cut-off minus after) and the accuracy lost when names are anonymised, for the contaminated and the clean model.
- **Facts to verify.** Vaswani et al. 2017; Radford et al. 2019 language models are unsupervised multitask learners (GPT-2 report); Lewis et al. 2020 retrieval-augmented generation (NeurIPS); Robertson and Zaragoza 2009 the probabilistic relevance framework: BM25 and beyond (FnT IR); Lopez-Lira and Tang 2023 can ChatGPT forecast stock price movements? (arXiv 2304.07619); Glasserman and Lin 2023 assessing look-ahead bias in stock return predictions generated by GPT sentiment analysis (arXiv 2309.17322); Sarkar and Vafa 2024 lookahead bias in pretrained language models (SSRN); Wu et al. 2023 BloombergGPT (arXiv 2303.17564); Kim, Muhn and Nikolaev 2024 financial statement analysis with large language models (arXiv/SSRN); Yao et al. 2023 ReAct (ICLR); Carlini et al. 2021 extracting training data from large language models (USENIX Security); regulators' current statements on AI in trading and investment advice (SEC, FCA, ESMA; dated).
