# 1. Why Financial Machine Learning Is Different — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A new model forecasts the next day's return of a thousand stocks with an out-of-sample R-squared of 0.4 %, and the desk is pleased: in the simulated market whose true expected returns are known, the best possible forecast scores 0.6 %, and the same model class, given a task with a strong signal, gets 95 %.
- **Sections.** Learning from data: the vocabulary; Signal-to-noise near zero and the R-squared ceiling; Non-stationarity and adversarial feedback; Small effective samples; What carries over from the rest of machine learning, and what does not.
- **Defines.** supervised learning, unsupervised learning, training set, validation set, test set, hyperparameter, generalisation error, overfitting, bias--variance decomposition, out-of-sample R-squared, predictability ceiling.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** signal-to-noise ratio (B4.19), effective sample size (B4.14), stationary process (B4.17), holdout set (B7.1), backtest overfitting (B7.20), post-publication decay (B7.13), crowding (B7.28), stylised fact (B7.5), prediction target (B7.6), forecast horizon (B7.6), information coefficient (B7.6), in-sample (B7.20), out-of-sample (B7.20), cross-validation (B4.16).
- **Tutorial.** On firm.synthmkt, whose planted alpha is the truth, compute the predictability ceiling; fit ridge, a gradient-boosted model and a small network to twenty features, and compare in-sample and out-of-sample R-squared with the ceiling; repeat on firm.mlsynth tasks whose signal-to-noise ratio is raised a thousandfold; plot learning curves against sample size. Data: synthetic only (firm.synthmkt, firm.mlsynth).
- **Build.** `firm.mlsynth`: synthetic learning tasks with a known Bayes-optimal predictor -- controlled signal-to-noise ratio, nonlinearity and interactions, a drift schedule, cross-sectional correlation and overlapping labels -- plus adapters that turn firm.synthmkt panels and firm.tape sessions into (X, y, time, group) datasets; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** The 0.4 % model -- named result: the predictability ceiling of the task, the fitted model's share of it, and the number of days needed to tell the model from zero at two standard errors.
- **Facts to verify.** Gu, Kelly and Xiu 2020 empirical asset pricing via machine learning (RFS): monthly out-of-sample R-squared magnitudes; Israel, Kelly and Moskowitz 2020 can machines learn finance? (J. Investment Management); Lopez de Prado 2018 Advances in Financial Machine Learning (Wiley); Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman 2009 The Elements of Statistical Learning (bias-variance); Geman, Bienenstock and Doursat 1992 bias-variance dilemma (Neural Computation).
