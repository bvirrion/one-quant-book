## Plan for Phase B

- **Order.** Chapters in order, one full cycle each (sources -> code -> text -> figures -> exercises, problem,
  interview questions -> solutions -> chapter gates -> figure checks). The firm components that later chapters
  consume are written in their chapter: `tickcap` (2) and `tickstore` (4) before anything that reads the store;
  `btengine` (11) before `strathost` (12); `jobgraph` (13) before `riskgrid` (20) and `cloudcost` (26);
  `posservice` (17) before `rtrisk` (18); `tradecap` (21) before `posttrade` (22) and `survpipe` (30).
  Chapter 24's `eventlog` is used by chapters 17 and 21 only through a thin in-memory queue until it lands (the
  text of 17 and 21 points forward to chapter 24 in prose; no code dependency backwards).
- **Calibration.** ~450-550 body lines and ~150-180 solution lines a chapter, aiming at 10.5-12 pages all-in
  (Books 10-13 landed at 10.3-11.6). Projection: 30 x 11 + ~15 = ~345 pages against the outline's ~398 (-13 %).
  Checkpoints at chapters 10 and 20: a projection more than 15 % under the outline means the chapters are being
  compressed.
- **Figures.** ~3-4 a chapter: architecture schematics (TikZ) for the systems chapters, pgfplots charts from
  `figdata/platforms/NN-*/` CSVs written by `fig_*.py` scripts. No photographs are planned; no AI illustrations.
  Measured charts (ch. 5, 8, 9, 10, 14) follow Book 13's rule: `bench_*.py` -> `measured_*.csv` + `.meta`,
  the caption names the laptop (Intel Core Ultra 7 155H, WSL2, no isolated cores), tests assert only
  machine-independent properties, and a final pass re-measures on a quiet machine.
- **Tests.** Every chapter directory: a fast unmarked set well under 20 s with a `test_small_runs`; full-size
  results (a simulated day, a big sweep, the grid) are `@pytest.mark.reference` from the start; no heavy work at
  import time; BLAS/torch threads 1.
- **Data.** Everything synthetic or generated by script: firm.exchsim recorded days (tests at seconds, the
  chapter's day once, late, under `nice -n 19` and a cgroup cap), firm.tape seeds, synthetic vendor files,
  logs and archives. Nothing licensed is committed; nothing larger than a few MB is committed.
- **Resource rule.** `systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryHigh=2G -p MemoryMax=4G nice -n 19 <cmd>` for
  anything heavy; `free -g` first; one heavy job at a time.

## Notes and traps (Phase A)

- `tools/make_briefs.py` does not write a brief's `data` field into the ledger; it was appended by hand to each
  `sources/platforms/NN-*.md` ("- **Data.**" line).
- Re-running `make_briefs.py --book 15` regenerates `DEFINITIONS.md` and drops its hand-written "Checks" and
  "Contested" sections: re-append them.
- `firm.recon` is Book 1's index-*reconstitution* predictor, not a reconciliation engine (the batch file lists it
  among the post-trade components to build on); chapter 22 builds reconciliation in `firm.posttrade`.
- Book 7 defines fidelity level 4 as live trading at small size or paper trading (`parts/research/16-*.tex`
  lines 41-44); chapter 11 presents the reactive simulation on `firm.exchsim` as that level with the simulator
  standing in for the venue, exactly as Book 7 ch. 19 used `firm.tape` as its "live" stand-in -- pending the
  sync's confirmation.
- `firm.pricing` has no algorithmic-differentiation engine; chapter 20's adjoint sensitivities are a vectorised
  hand-written adjoint of a Monte Carlo engine, checked against Book 4's `firm.aad` tape on small inputs, and
  offered to firm.pricing through the optional `greeks` override of an engine registered by `firm.riskgrid`.
- `firm.surveil`'s detectors read its own synthetic account-day dictionaries (`n_small`, `n_large`, `f_small`,
  `f_large`, `c_large`, `linked`, ...); chapter 30 builds those columns from firm.exchsim orders and trades.
