# 13. Half-Life, Decay and Stability — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A published anomaly returns about a quarter less out of sample than in the paper, and more than half less after publication; the question for a desk is whether its own signals are on the same path.
- **Sections.** Decay across horizons; Stability across years; Structural breaks; Post-publication decay; Measuring the half-life of a predictor card.
- **Defines.** IC decay curve, signal half-life, signal autocorrelation, IC stability, structural break, CUSUM test, post-publication decay.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** half-life (B4.4), Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process (B4.4), autoregressive process (B4.17), bootstrap (B4.13), power of a test (B4.12), information coefficient (ch6), predictor card (ch6).
- **Tutorial.** Measure the IC decay curves of the chapter 7--10 predictors, fit their half-lives with bootstrap intervals, and detect a planted break in a rolling IC with the CUSUM test.
- **Build.** `firm.decay`: IC decay curves, exponential and AR fits of the half-life with bootstrap intervals, signal autocorrelation and turnover link, CUSUM and sup-F break tests; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Did it die, or was it unlucky? — named result: the probability that a signal whose measured IC halved over five years has truly decayed, from a power analysis on the IC's sampling error.
- **Facts to verify.** McLean and Pontiff 2016 (JF) 26% out of sample, 58% post-publication; Chordia, Subrahmanyam, Tong 2014 (J. Accounting and Economics); Brown, Durbin, Evans 1975 CUSUM (JRSS B); Andrews 1993 sup-F (Econometrica); Bai and Perron 1998 (Econometrica); Hou, Xue, Zhang 2020 replicating anomalies (RFS).
