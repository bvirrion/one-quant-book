# 22. Performance Measurement — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Two strategies have a Sharpe ratio of 1.0 over five years. One has lost 40 per cent peak to trough twice; the other sells out-of-the-money puts and has never had a losing quarter.
- **Sections.** The Sharpe ratio and its standard error; Drawdowns; Turnover, hit rate and holding period; The shape of outcomes; Benchmark-relative measures.
- **Defines.** annualisation, autocorrelation-adjusted Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, drawdown duration, Calmar ratio, Sortino ratio, Omega ratio, hit rate, profit factor, return smoothing, market beta.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** Sharpe ratio (B4.11), standard error (B4.11), delta method (B4.11), HAC estimator (B4.11), drawdown (B2.29), information ratio (B1.3), tracking error (B1.3), benchmark (B1.3), alpha (B1.1), deflated Sharpe ratio (B4.12), portfolio turnover (ch16).
- **Tutorial.** Compute the tear sheet of firm.perf for four synthetic return streams (trend, short volatility, a smoothed illiquid book, a market maker) and read what each number hides.
- **Build.** `firm.perf`: performance module on BacktestResult (Sharpe with iid, HAC and Lo standard errors, drawdown statistics, Calmar, Sortino, Omega, hit rate, profit factor, turnover and holding period, alpha and beta with HAC errors, unsmoothing), a text tear sheet; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** Two Sharpe ratios of one — named result: the probability of a 30% drawdown within five years for each strategy, and the Sharpe ratio's standard error under the smoothed book's autocorrelation.
- **Facts to verify.** Lo 2002, the statistics of Sharpe ratios (FAJ); Getmansky, Lo, Makarov 2004 serial correlation and illiquidity (JFE); Magdon-Ismail, Atiya, Pratap, Abu-Mostafa 2004 maximum drawdown of Brownian motion (J. Applied Probability); Sortino and Price 1994 (J. Investing); Keating and Shadwick 2002 Omega; Young 1991 Calmar ratio (Futures); Sharpe 1966 (J. Business); Sharpe 1994 (JPM); Goetzmann, Ingersoll, Spiegel, Welch 2007 (RFS).
