# 28. Capacity, Decay and Crowding — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** In the second week of August 2007 many quantitative equity market-neutral funds lost more in three days than in a normal year, with no news on the stocks they held. On the fourth day most of it came back.
- **Sections.** Capacity curves; Alpha decay in production; Detecting crowding; August 2007.
- **Defines.** strategy capacity, capacity curve, profit-maximising size, crowding, crowded trade, comomentum, unwind.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** liquidity spiral (B1.31), short interest (B1.16), days to cover (B1.16), market impact (ch18), square-root impact law (ch27), post-publication decay (ch13), IC decay curve (ch13), risk model (ch24).
- **Tutorial.** Draw the capacity curve of chapter 27's strategy; then simulate several funds holding overlapping books when one is forced to sell, and measure the losses of the others as a function of the overlap and the fraction sold.
- **Build.** `firm.capacity`: capacity-curve solver (net P&L and Sharpe ratio against size under the impact model), crowding monitors (return co-movement of long and short legs, comomentum, holdings overlap) and an unwind simulator; Python.
- **Weekend problem.** The week of 6 August 2007 — named result: the peak loss of the non-selling funds against the fraction of the overlapping book sold, and the size at which the strategy's net Sharpe ratio halves.
- **Facts to verify.** Khandani and Lo 2011, What happened to the quants in August 2007? (J. Financial Markets); Lou and Polk 2022 comomentum (RFS); Pedersen 2009, When everyone runs for the exit (IJCB); Stein 2009 presidential address (JF); Berk and Green 2004 (JPE); Fama-French factor returns in August 2007 (derived statistics).
