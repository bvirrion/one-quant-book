# 6. Power Markets II: Trading — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** Forty-five minutes before delivery a wind farm's forecast falls by a third; its trader buys the missing megawatt-hours on the continuous intraday market at three times the day-ahead price, or pays the imbalance price instead.
- **Sections.** Continuous intraday markets; Imbalance settlement and balancing responsibility; Transmission rights and virtual bids; Renewable forecasts, capture prices and power purchase agreements; Batteries and flexible assets.
- **Defines.** intraday market, gate closure, imbalance volume, imbalance price, balancing responsible party, financial transmission right, virtual bid, power purchase agreement, capture price, shape risk, round-trip efficiency.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** day-ahead market, market time unit, bidding zone, locational marginal price, congestion rent, merit order, price-time priority, ancillary service, dynamic programming (Book 4).
- **Tutorial.** Settle a wind farm's day: day-ahead schedule, intraday corrections, metered output and the imbalance cost at the imbalance price; then compute the solar capture price from SMARD hourly prices and generation (CC BY 4.0).
- **Build.** `firm.balancing`: balancing-group position keeper (schedules from day-ahead and intraday trades, forecast and metered volumes, imbalance volume and cost per market time unit); uses `firm.dayahead`.
- **Weekend problem.** A battery's year — a 100 MW / 200 MWh battery arbitrages German day-ahead prices; named result: its arbitrage revenue per MW-year with one cycle a day and a stated round-trip efficiency, against the perfect-foresight bound.
- **Facts to verify.** Single Intraday Coupling (XBID) go-live 12 June 2018 and its gate closure (60 minutes cross-border); German imbalance price (reBAP) method (TSOs' publication); 15-minute imbalance settlement period (EU Electricity Balancing Guideline); PJM financial transmission rights auctions; virtual bids (increment offers and decrement bids); German solar capture rate for a recent year (SMARD or a public study); installed battery capacity (Germany, ERCOT or CAISO) from an official source; corporate PPA volumes (a public market report).
