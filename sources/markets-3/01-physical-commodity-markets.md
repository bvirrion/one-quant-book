# 1. Physical Commodity Markets — brief and source ledger

## Brief

- **Hook.** A tanker loads 600,000 barrels of North Sea crude on a Tuesday; neither buyer nor seller will know the price until five days of published assessments around the bill-of-lading date have been averaged.
- **Sections.** Physical and paper: the two layers of a commodity market; Trading houses and the physical value chain; Delivery terms and the physical contract; Price-reporting agencies and assessment windows; Pricing formulas, netbacks and hedging a physical deal.
- **Defines.** physical market, paper market, commodity swap, commodity trading house, Incoterms, free on board, cost insurance and freight, bill of lading, pricing period, price-reporting agency, assessment window, price differential, netback, basis risk.
- **Uses (defined earlier).** hedger, speculator, futures contract, physical delivery, exchange for physical, basis, mark-to-market, benchmark, calendar spread.
- **Tutorial.** Price a physical cargo from a pricing period of daily assessments plus a differential, allocate freight and insurance under FOB and CIF, and hedge the priced days with futures sold across the pricing period; show the residual basis P&L.
- **Build.** `firm.physdeal`: physical deal model (pricing formula, Incoterm cost allocation, netback, per-day price exposure for the hedge); posts to `firm.pnl` and `firm.ledger`.
- **Weekend problem.** The cargo that priced late — a trading house buys a cargo FOB priced around loading and sells it CIF priced around discharge; named result: the per-day barrel exposure of the mismatched pricing periods and the margin per barrel locked in by the futures hedge.
- **Facts to verify.** Incoterms 2020 (ICC): FOB, CIF, DAP definitions; Platts market-on-close assessment window (London 16:00-16:30) and methodology; Argus and Platts as the main price-reporting agencies; IOSCO Principles for Oil Price Reporting Agencies (2012) and their reviews; largest commodity trading houses' volumes and revenues from their annual reports (Vitol, Trafigura, Glencore, Gunvor, Mercuria as reported); size of the physical oil market (IEA: world demand, barrels per day); typical cargo sizes (Aframax, Suezmax, VLCC) from a public source.
