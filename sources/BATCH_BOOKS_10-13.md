## User rulings for this batch (2026-09-25)

1. **Libraries.** The "numpy + pandas only, no scipy" rule of Books 3–9 is
   lifted for this batch: `scipy`, `scikit-learn`, `lightgbm`, `statsmodels`
   and `torch` (CPU build) are installed in `.venv`. Book 12 may use all of
   them (and should: the reader will use the real libraries); Books 10, 11 and
   13 may use `scipy` and `statsmodels`. Anything else needs the main
   session's approval (it must go into `make venv`). No GPU exists: every model
   is CPU-sized. Tests stay deterministic (fixed seeds;
   `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` where it matters;
   `torch.set_num_threads(1)` or 2 in tests and figure scripts) and each
   chapter's tests should run well under a minute. The machine (22 logical
   cores, 19 GB RAM) is shared by four agents: never train anything that needs
   more than ~2 GB or several minutes; cache heavy intermediate results under
   your own `figdata/<slug>/` or `data/<slug>/` only if they are small and
   regenerated by a script.
2. **Measured timings (Book 13, and any chapter that prints a latency).**
   Numbers are measured on this machine and the caption says so: "measured on
   a laptop (Intel Core Ultra 7 155H) under WSL2, no isolated cores". Tests
   assert only machine-independent properties (an ordering between two
   designs, an allocation count, a tail-to-median ratio bound with a wide
   margin, zero heap allocations on the hot path), never an absolute time.
   Absolute figures for tuned production servers come from cited sources
   (talks, papers, vendor documents) through the ledger. `perf` hardware
   counters, `isolcpus`, IRQ affinity and real-time priorities are not
   available here: the text explains them, and the tutorials that need them
   measure what can be measured (`rdtscp`/`std::chrono::steady_clock`,
   `taskset`, allocation counters) and say what the reader would see on a
   tuned box. The toolchain is g++ 11.4 (`-std=c++20`; no clang), Rust 1.97,
   CPU flags up to AVX2/AVX-VNNI (no AVX-512).

## Ownership rules (in force at all times)

1. **Write only your own files:**
   - anything under `parts/<slug>/`, `code/<slug>/`, `figdata/<slug>/`,
     `sources/<slug>/`, `images/<slug>/` and `data/<slug>/`;
   - your own `tools/briefs/book<N>.py` and `tools/term_config/book<N>_en.py`;
   - your entry file;
   - the `code/firm/<component>/` directories reserved for you at the sync
     (none before it).
2. **Never edit** files belonging to Books 1–9 or to another batch book. Put
   the defects you find there in your report, with file:line and the fix.
   Books 1–9 are committed, but this batch's work (and the shared Step 0
   edits) is not: its only copy is this working tree.
3. **Shared files are append-only**, and only when unavoidable:
   `styles/onequant.sty` (a new package or macro) and `tools/firm_names.txt`.
   Append at the end under a `% Book <N>:` (or `# Book <N>:`) comment. Never
   change or reorder existing lines. Re-read the file just before editing,
   because others append too. Keep appended macros few and prefixed or
   clearly scoped, and build your book right after.
4. **Do not edit** `tools/gates.sh`, `tools/termlink/`, `tools/test_code.sh`,
   `tools/figdata.sh`, the other `tools/*.py`, `Makefile`, `latexmkrc`,
   `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `OUTLINE.md`, `outline/*.md`, `WRITING_A_QUANT_BOOK.md`,
   `code/firm/INTERFACES.md`, any `CLAUDE.md`, or this file. If a tool is
   buggy or a doc must change, report it (and work around it locally if you
   can). New traps and calibration go in your `sources/<slug>/PROGRESS.md`;
   the main session merges them into the shared docs.
5. **Git is read-only:** `git status`, `git diff` and `git log` only. Never
   run `checkout`, `stash`, `reset`, `clean`, `restore`, `add` or `commit`. A
   repo-wide checkout destroyed a whole batch of shared work once in a sister
   series.
6. **Build only your own entry file:** `latexmk <your entry file>`. Never run
   a bare `latexmk` or `make`/`make all`/`make gates`, which build or check the
   other books mid-write. Never run two `latexmk` on your entry at once and
   never wrap it in a short timeout (a killed run corrupts `.aux`/`.toc`;
   recover by deleting your entry's `.aux .toc .out .fdb_latexmk` in `build/`).
7. **Scope tests to your slug:**

   - `tools/gates.sh book <slug>`; `tools/gates.sh log <slug>`.

   carry on.
8. **Teaching modules** (Python files under `code/<slug>/`) start with your

9. **Cross-book references are prose only:** "One Quant Book 10, chapter 26",

## Carry-over traps (read WRITING_A_QUANT_BOOK.md §9 in full; these bite most)

- A model's memory is not a source; search for the most recent release of any
  rule a chapter leans on (Reg NMS order protection was proposed for
  rescission in 2026).
- Planted effects are too strong at first, every time: tune to the public
  record and say when a synthetic number is an upper bound.
- Execution at the signal is a free lunch: rerun every rule a tick (or a
  day) later. A single seed is not a test.
- A simulation a caption leans on gets a time-step-divided-by-four stability
  test and an ablation of every mechanism the text credits.
- Listing ranges drift: `omcode_ends.py` after every code edit and every
  `ruff --fix`; listings ≤ 40 lines; ASCII only in listed files.
- Captions are written from the rendered figure; legends below the axis;
  write tick lists out (no `...`); brace commas in `\legend` entries.
- Print numbers from the test's rounding; a product of rounded factors must
  equal the printed factors' product.
