# Books 1–3 — Markets

Pages are all-in (chapter + its share of solutions). Each book adds ~20 pages
of front and back matter.

---

## One Quant Book 1 — Markets I: The Ecosystem and Exchange-Traded Markets (written: 374 pp; outline estimate was ~428)

**Part I — The ecosystem**

1. **What a trading firm does** (12 pp) — The business models side by side: market maker, prop firm, multi-manager hedge fund, asset manager, bank desk; who earns what from whom, and why each exists.
2. **The sell side** (12 pp) — Bank markets divisions: flow trading, structuring, prime services, research, primary markets; the franchise model and its economics.
3. **The buy side** (12 pp) — Asset managers, pensions, insurers, hedge funds, CTAs, retail; mandates, benchmarks, and the reasons each of them trades.
4. **Exchanges, brokers and venues** (14 pp) — The exchange as a business, membership, direct and sponsored access, broker roles, alternative trading systems and multilateral facilities.
5. **Clearing and settlement** (14 pp) — Novation, the margin waterfall, default funds, settlement cycles, delivery versus payment, depositories, fails; what happens when a member defaults.
6. **Financing: repo, securities lending, prime brokerage** (14 pp) — How positions are actually funded; short selling, locates, rehypothecation, margin financing, synthetic prime.
7. **P&L and the accounting of a position** (12 pp) — Mark-to-market, realised and unrealised, carry, funding, fees, currency translation. Build: a position and P&L keeper.

**Part II — Equities**

8. **Shares, corporate actions and indices** (14 pp) — Share classes, dividends, splits, rights, spin-offs, adjustment factors; how index providers weight and maintain indices.
9. **US equity market structure** (16 pp) — Order protection and the national best bid and offer, consolidated tape versus direct feeds, the exchange families, maker-taker and inverted pricing, alternative systems, speed bumps.
10. **Retail flow and wholesaling** (12 pp) — Payment for order flow, price improvement, internalisers, the economics of uninformed flow, the 2021 meme-stock episode.
11. **European equity market structure** (14 pp) — The regulatory framework, systematic internalisers, periodic auctions, dark caps, fragmentation across venues, the consolidated-tape project.
12. **Asian and emerging equity markets** (14 pp) — Japan, Hong Kong, the China connect programmes, Korea, India, Taiwan: price limits, lot sizes, short-selling rules, taxes, access.
13. **Auctions** (12 pp) — Opening, closing, volatility and IPO auctions; uncrossing algorithms, imbalance feeds, and why the close keeps growing.
14. **Exchange-traded funds** (16 pp) — Creation and redemption, authorised participants, NAV and intraday value, premiums and discounts, fixed-income ETFs, leveraged ETFs and their rebalancing flows.
15. **Index construction and rebalancing** (12 pp) — Methodologies, announcement and effective dates, float adjustments, the size of passive money and its footprint.
16. **Stock loan and short selling in practice** (12 pp) — Specials, recalls, squeezes, locate rules, dividend-related lending; the dividend-tax scandals as a case study.
17. **Delta-one instruments** (12 pp) — Total-return swaps, single-stock and index futures, depositary receipts, dual listings; what "the same exposure" costs in each wrapper.

**Part III — Futures**

18. **Futures contracts and their exchanges** (14 pp) — The major exchange groups, contract specifications, tick values, sessions, daily settlement, price limits.
19. **Matching algorithms and implied spreads** (14 pp) — Price-time, pro-rata and hybrid allocation, lead-market-maker privileges, implied-in and implied-out prices, order-by-order versus price-level feeds.
20. **Margin** (12 pp) — Scenario-based and value-at-risk-based initial margin, variation margin, portfolio offsets, intraday calls.
21. **Basis, roll and delivery** (14 pp) — Cost of carry, fair value, the roll, physical and cash delivery, exchange-for-physical, blocks, trade-at-settlement.
22. **Equity index and single-stock futures worldwide** (10 pp) — The main contracts by region, dividend futures, total-return futures.

**Part IV — Listed options**

23. **Option contracts and the options exchanges** (14 pp) — Central clearing of options, the many US venues, European venues, multipliers, exercise and assignment, expiry cycles.
24. **Options market structure** (14 pp) — Quoting obligations, pro-rata and customer priority, price-improvement auctions, complex order books, penny increments, order-flow payment in options.
25. **Volatility as a traded quantity: first contact** (14 pp) — Implied volatility, skew, term structure, put-call parity with dividends and borrow, the volatility index and its futures.
26. **Zero-day options, weeklies and retail options flow** (10 pp) — The growth of short-dated options, who trades them, what dealers' hedging does to the underlying.
27. **Options markets in Europe and Asia** (12 pp) — The European index and single-stock markets, Korea, India's index-options boom and the expiry-day manipulation case.

**Part V — Working with the market**

28. **Reading market data** (14 pp) — Feed levels, trade and quote condition codes, timestamps, symbology, reference data. Build: a feed normaliser.
29. **Getting access: equity markets** (14 pp) — Broker-dealer membership against sponsored access, clearing arrangements, exchange fee schedules and volume tiers, designated and supplemental liquidity-provider programmes and their obligations, proprietary-feed and non-display data licences, what a new entrant negotiates and with whom.
30. **Getting access: futures and listed options** (14 pp) — Choosing a clearing broker, member against non-member fees, buying or leasing a membership, exchange incentive and market-maker programmes, options market-maker appointments and quoting obligations, give-ups, data licences.
31. **Stress case studies** (14 pp) — The 2010 flash crash, 24 August 2015, the 2018 short-volatility collapse, the 2021 meme stocks: minute-by-minute mechanics.

## One Quant Book 2 — Markets II: Rates, FX and Credit (written: 363 pp; outline estimate was ~412)

**Part I — Rates: money and bonds**

1. **Central banks and the short rate** (14 pp) — Reserves, corridor and floor systems, policy rates, open-market operations, balance-sheet policy, the overnight benchmarks and the end of interbank offered rates.
2. **Money markets** (12 pp) — Bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, general-collateral repo, money-market funds, quarter-end and year-end turns.
3. **Government bonds** (14 pp) — Conventions, yield, accrued interest, duration, convexity, dollar value of a basis point, strips.
4. **The Treasury market** (16 pp) — Auctions, primary dealers, when-issued trading, on-the-run versus off-the-run, inter-dealer versus dealer-to-client platforms, principal trading firms, October 2014 and March 2020.
5. **Repo and specials** (12 pp) — General versus special collateral, tri-party, sponsored clearing, haircuts, the September 2019 spike.
6. **Bond futures** (14 pp) — Conversion factors, cheapest-to-deliver, delivery options, gross and net basis, implied repo.
7. **European and Japanese government bonds** (14 pp) — The euro-area curve and its spreads, central-bank backstops, the electronic dealer market, Japan's yield-curve control, the 2022 gilt crisis.
8. **Short-term interest-rate futures** (12 pp) — Overnight-rate futures, packs and bundles, pricing central-bank meetings, the convexity adjustment in practice.

**Part II — Rates: swaps and beyond**

9. **Interest-rate swaps** (14 pp) — Overnight-index and fixed-float swaps, conventions, risk, swap spreads, execution facilities, compression.
10. **Swap clearing and the clearing-house basis** (10 pp) — The competing clearing houses, initial margin, bilateral margin rules, why the same swap has two prices.
11. **Inflation markets** (12 pp) — Index-linked bonds, breakevens, zero-coupon inflation swaps, indexation lag and seasonality.
12. **Mortgages and agencies** (12 pp) — Pass-throughs, prepayment, the to-be-announced market, negative convexity and its hedging flows.
13. **The rates options market** (12 pp) — Caps, floors, swaptions, how the volatility cube is quoted, and who structurally buys and sells.

**Part III — Foreign exchange**

14. **The FX market** (14 pp) — Over-the-counter structure, dealer tiers, primary venues, electronic networks, single-dealer platforms, prime brokerage, turnover surveys.
15. **FX spot microstructure** (14 pp) — Streaming prices, last look and hold times, tiered and skewed liquidity, internalisation, the global code of conduct.
16. **FX swaps, forwards and the cross-currency basis** (14 pp) — Covered interest parity, forward points, short-dated rolls, basis swaps, dollar funding stress.
17. **Fixings and flows** (12 pp) — The London afternoon fix, month-end rebalancing, option expiries at the New York cut, the fixing scandal.
18. **Emerging-market FX and non-deliverable forwards** (12 pp) — Convertibility, onshore and offshore markets, pegs, bands and interventions; the 2015 franc unpeg as the limiting case.
19. **The FX options market** (12 pp) — Delta and at-the-money conventions, risk reversals and butterflies, premium-adjusted delta, broker markets, barrier-driven flows.
20. **Settlement risk** (8 pp) — The 1974 bank failure that named the risk, payment-versus-payment settlement, what remains unprotected.

**Part IV — Credit**

21. **Corporate bonds** (14 pp) — Issuance, ratings, the family of spread measures, covenants, seniority, recovery.
22. **How bonds trade** (14 pp) — Request-for-quote platforms, trade reporting, all-to-all trading, portfolio trades, the link with bond ETFs, algorithmic dealer pricing.
23. **Credit default swaps** (14 pp) — The standard contract, upfront and running, credit events and the determinations committee, settlement auctions, the cheapest-to-deliver option.
24. **Credit indices and tranches** (14 pp) — The index families, rolls, index-versus-constituents skew, tranches, index options.
25. **Loans, CLOs and securitisation** (12 pp) — The leveraged-loan market, structure of a securitisation, waterfalls, who holds which tranche.
26. **Distressed, sovereign and bank-capital credit** (12 pp) — Restructurings, collective-action clauses, holdouts, contingent-capital bonds and their 2023 write-down.

**Part V — Getting access**

27. **Getting access: FX** (12 pp) — Prime-broker credit and give-up lines, net-open-position limits, onboarding as a liquidity provider on venues and single-dealer platforms, brokerage negotiation, bilateral streaming relationships and how they are reviewed.
28. **Getting access: rates and credit** (12 pp) — Dealer status on the client platforms, inter-dealer access for non-banks, sponsored repo and clearing membership, swap clearing brokers, documentation needed before the first trade.

**Part VI — Trading as a craft**

29. **Thinking in expected value** (12 pp) — Edge and odds, the Kelly criterion and fractional Kelly, risk of ruin, sizing when the edge itself is uncertain.
30. **Market-making games** (12 pp) — Make-me-a-market, updating on trades, adverse selection around a table; the training games firms publicly describe.
31. **Reading a macro calendar and a rates screen** (10 pp) — Data releases, positioning into events, reading curve shapes and spreads at a glance.

## One Quant Book 3 — Markets III: Commodities, Energy and Crypto (~386 pp)

**Part I — Commodities and energy**

1. **Physical commodity markets** (14 pp) — Physical versus paper, trading houses, producer and consumer hedging, delivery terms, price-reporting agencies and assessment windows.
2. **Crude oil** (14 pp) — The North Sea benchmark complex, the US inland benchmark, Middle-East pricing, and April 2020's negative price step by step.
3. **Refined products and cracks** (10 pp) — Gasoline, distillates, fuel oil, refinery margins, regional arbitrage.
4. **Natural gas and LNG** (14 pp) — Hub pricing in the US and Europe, locational basis, storage seasonality, cargo arbitrage, the 2022 crisis.
5. **Power markets I: design** (16 pp) — Day-ahead auctions and their clearing algorithm, merit order, zonal versus nodal pricing, capacity and ancillary markets, negative prices.
6. **Power markets II: trading** (14 pp) — Continuous intraday markets, imbalance settlement, transmission rights and virtual bids, renewable forecasting, batteries, long-term purchase agreements.
7. **Emissions and environmental markets** (10 pp) — Cap-and-trade design, the stability reserve, certificates of origin.
8. **Metals** (14 pp) — The London metal market's prompt-date structure and warehouses, the 2022 nickel episode, the London gold market versus futures and the 2020 dislocation, iron ore.
9. **Agriculturals and softs** (10 pp) — Crop calendars, government reports, weather, daily limits, positioning reports.
10. **Forward curves, storage and convenience yield** (14 pp) — Contango and backwardation, theory of storage, roll yield, commodity indices and the footprint of their roll.
11. **Freight, weather and other corners** (8 pp) — Freight derivatives, weather contracts, what makes a market tradable at all.
12. **Commodity options and structured hedges** (10 pp) — Average-price options, calendar-spread options, swing contracts, sovereign and producer hedging programmes.

13. **Getting access: commodities and power** (12 pp) — Exchange and clearing memberships, metal-market membership categories, becoming a balancing-responsible party, grid-operator nominations, physical logistics and credit lines, the licences a paper trader needs before touching a physical market.

**Part II — Crypto and decentralised finance**

14. **Blockchains for traders** (14 pp) — Consensus, blocks, finality, gas, the mempool, base layers and rollups, sequencers, bridges, stablecoin mint and redeem mechanics, custody.
15. **Centralised exchanges** (14 pp) — Account and matching models, interfaces and rate limits, fee tiers, maker rebates and market-maker programmes, the exchange as counterparty, proof of reserves, off-exchange settlement.
16. **Spot markets** (12 pp) — Fragmentation, stablecoin quote pairs, triangular and cross-venue arbitrage, transfer latency as risk, wash trading and measuring real liquidity.
17. **Perpetual futures** (14 pp) — Index and mark price, the premium and funding formulas, linear versus inverse contracts and the convexity of the inverse, cash-and-carry and funding arbitrage.
18. **Margin, liquidation and loss allocation** (12 pp) — Isolated, cross and portfolio margin, liquidation engines, insurance funds, auto-deleveraging, socialised losses, a liquidation cascade dissected.
19. **Dated futures, options and ETFs** (12 pp) — The dominant options venue's conventions, inverse options, the crypto volatility surface, the regulated-futures basis trade, spot ETF creation and redemption.
20. **Automated market makers** (16 pp) — Constant product, concentrated liquidity, stableswap, impermanent loss, loss-versus-rebalancing, just-in-time liquidity, aggregators and intent-based routing. Build: an AMM.
21. **On-chain order books and perpetual DEXs** (14 pp) — Hyperliquid in detail (consensus, on-chain book, order-priority rules, the liquidity vault, oracle and liquidation design, incidents); app-chain and oracle-priced designs; the Solana venues.
22. **Maximal extractable value** (16 pp) — Front-running, sandwich attacks with full profit arithmetic, back-running, exchange-to-chain arbitrage, liquidation races, gas auctions, proposer-builder separation, bundles, private order flow, rollup sequencing, mitigations. Build: a sandwich simulator.
23. **DeFi credit and leverage** (12 pp) — Lending pools and their liquidations, flash loans, liquid staking and restaking, staking yield as a base rate, oracle-manipulation attacks.
24. **The crypto trading business** (12 pp) — Token market-making agreements (the loan-plus-call structure), OTC desks, treasury and custody operations, round-the-clock operations, regulation.
25. **Getting access: crypto venues** (14 pp) — Entities, onboarding and sub-accounts; fee schedules, VIP tiers and tier-matching between venues; market-maker programmes and their uptime, spread and depth obligations; maker rebates; raised rate limits; dedicated and private websocket or FIX endpoints and what unlocks them; loan lines; off-exchange settlement and custody arrangements. (Network engineering side: Book 14.)
26. **Crypto data and infrastructure** (10 pp) — Running nodes, remote-procedure and archive data, historical order-book vendors, a first look at where matching engines sit (full treatment in Book 14).

**Part III — Other markets and cross-market stress**

27. **Prediction and betting markets** (12 pp) — Betting exchanges, event-contract venues on and off chain, overround, how trading firms make markets in them.
28. **When markets break together** (12 pp) — 2008, March 2020, and 2022 as cross-asset liquidity events: margin spirals, correlations going to one, what each desk saw.
29. **A map of all markets** (10 pp) — One synoptic chapter: every market in Books 1–3 by size, structure, hours, participants and dominant strategy types.
